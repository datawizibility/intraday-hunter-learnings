### Core idea
- A chart is one of the **three equal legs** of trading: *"चार्ट ट्रेडिंग का एक इंपोर्टेंट पार्ट होता है जैसे हमारे लिए कैपिटल होता है, डिसिप्लिन होता है"* — chart-reading sits beside **capital** and **discipline**, not below them.
- ⭐ The first decision when you open a chart is the **timeframe**, and it is chosen by **what you are trying to do** — not by taste.
- ⭐ For **SL hunting** the small timeframe (he uses **1-minute**) is the right lens, because only there can you *see the pool*: the sellers parked at a level, the breakout that takes their stop-losses, and the moment momentum changes. On a bigger frame that detail disappears — *"जस्ट मैं कैंडल को देखता ही रह जाऊंगा"*.
- ⭐ The trade-off: setups that need a **candle close / confirmation** cannot be read on 1-minute — *"1 मिनट का कैंडल अगर क्लोज हो भी जाता है तो उसको कंसीडर करना और ना करना एक ना के बराबर"* — there the **5-minute close** is the meaningful one.

### Timeframe — which one, and why
| Timeframe | What it shows | What it hides | Use it for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1-minute** | smallest support/resistance, smallest breakout/breakdown; the sellers seated and their SLs being eaten; momentum turning | noise; candle-colour flips ("red candle, green candle") on every tick | **SL hunting**; seeing the pool; intraday entries |
| **5-minute** (and up) | a calm, steady-looking trend; decisions feel easy | the *change* in momentum — *"मोमेंटम चेंज होता हुआ आपको नजर नहीं आएगा"* | setups that require a **candle close**; confirmation |

- On 1-min the breakout prints clearly; on 5-min the same move can collapse into a single wide "V-shaped" candle and vanish.
- His reason for caring: he wants to **see the sellers who were sitting there and their SL being taken** — *"मार्केट ने ब्रेकआउट किया … जो भी सेलर बैठे थे उनका मार्केट ने एसएल खा लिया"* — and only then build the decision.
- ⚠️ **Preparation must match the timeframe.** *"जो मेरा काम चलता है, वो जो प्रिपरेशन मैंने कर रखा है, वो मेरे 1 मिनट के टाइम फ्रेम के लिए कर रखा है"* — a 1-minute structure and a 5-minute structure look *"बिल्कुल डिफरेंट"*. Work the frame you have prepared; on an unprepared frame the loss is near-certain.
- Practical note: some traders **switch off candle colours** (outline only) precisely so the red/green psychology stops hijacking the read.

### Momentum is read from the trend state
- Establish the trend first, then work the **opposite side** in the morning:
  - **Down trend** → *"मोस्ट ऑफ द टाइम मॉर्निंग में कॉल साइड के पोज़ीशन ज़्यादा बनने शुरू हो जाएंगे"* — the market traps: everyone *wants* to sell a down trend, so it first **lifts** (a small gap-up, or a post-gap-down bounce), traps the early sellers, then continues down. So take **call-side** in the trap, or **wait** to sell the put.
  - **Up trend** → the mirror: *"ट्रेंड अप साइड चल रहा है तो … मॉर्निंग में इंट्राडे में [पुट साइड में] बेनिफिट मिलता है"* — a gap-up first **dips** (*"साइकोलॉजी चेंज करने के लिए पहले कुछ गिरावट करेगा"*), so take the **put** on that dip, or wait for a good call entry.
  - **No trend / range** → *"मार्केट के एग्जैक्टली साथ चलने की कोशिश करते हैं"* — follow the tape exactly; do not impose a side.
- ⭐ **This is why the plan looks lopsided day to day:** the *trend* decides which side's morning trade is available. The feeling "I keep taking too many puts" is a *trend* artefact, not a strategy change.

### The plan by open
| Market state | Open | Plan (as spoken) |
|---|---|---|
| Range / no trend | Gap-up | **Buy plan** |
| Range / no trend | Gap-down | **Sell plan** (the range has broken down) |
| Range / no trend | Small gap up *or* down | **Sell plan** — work the direction the market is already moving |
| Range / no trend | **Big gap** | ⚠️ **Volatile, not directional** — *"कभी ऊपर जाएगा कभी नीचे"*; do not force a plan |
| Down trend | Gap-down | Market first lifts a little → **call side**, or wait for the put |
| Down trend | Gap-up | Market first lures, then recovers down → **call side** |
| Up trend | Gap-up | Market dips first → **put** on the dip, or wait for a good call |
| Up trend | Gap-down | Market bounces a little, net falls → **put** |

- His summary line: **down trend = call side; up trend = put side; no trend = with the market.** *"अभी तो क्या है कि मार्केट के हमें साथ ही चलना है."*
- The live example he walked through: the market gapped, sold off — **but did not cut the closing price** → **buy plan**.

### Buy / sell gates
- ⭐ **Buy only on a breakdown that FAILS** — *"जब मार्केट के अंदर कोई ब्रेकडाउन फेलियर में कन्वर्ट हो जाए"* (breakdown → failure). A **held** breakdown is sell territory; a **failed** breakdown is buy territory.
- ⭐ **Sell either (a) at the level (~round number, "500 के आसपास") or (b) after a real breakdown.** Never chase a sell into the hole — *"ब्रेकडाउन पर ट्रेड करें या फिर ऊपर मिले तब जाकर ट्रेड करें"*.
- He also flags **why he sometimes sells around a round number instead**: *"क्योंकि डायरेक्ट कैसेल वगैरह अवेलेबल नहीं थे"* — when the sell-side stops are **not stacked** at a level, the sell has no fuel, so it must be worked around the round number instead.

### Keep permanently
1. **Chart is one of three legs** — capital, discipline, chart. Treat chart-reading as a peer skill, not a garnish.
2. **Choose the timeframe by the job, not by taste.** SL hunting / seeing the pool → **1-minute**. Setups needing a candle close → **5-minute and up**; a 1-minute close is "worth nothing".
3. **On the small frame you can see the pool being eaten** — who was sitting where, whose stop got taken. That visibility is the whole reason to be there.
4. **On a bigger frame the momentum change is invisible** — the trend looks calm and decisions look easy, which is exactly the trap.
5. **Work only the timeframe you have prepared for** — a structure built on 1-min is not the same structure on 5-min.
6. **Trend decides the morning side:** down trend → call side (the market lifts first to trap sellers); up trend → put side (it dips first to trap buyers); no trend → go with the market.
7. **The morning trap is structural:** the side everyone wants (sell a down trend, buy an up trend) is the side the market teases first.
8. **Buy only when a breakdown FAILS.** A held breakdown is sell territory; a failed breakdown is buy territory.
9. **Sell at the level or after a real breakdown — never chase down into the hole.** If the sell-side stops aren't stacked, the sell has no fuel; work it at the round number instead.
10. **Do not force a plan from a big gap** — large gaps are volatile both ways; small gaps let you follow the tape.
