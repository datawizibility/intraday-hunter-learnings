### Core idea
- The session answers the objection every losing trader eventually raises — *"no method works for me; this is a rich man's game."* His answer: **no method works mechanically.** A pattern, a trendline, a breakout or a level works only under a **condition**, and the condition is **position inventory** — whether positions are already sitting on the other side for you to target.
- He walks the whole ladder of what the trader tries and abandons: read the chart and guess buy/sell here → indicators → candlesticks → chart patterns (head-and-shoulders, double top, flag) → changing timeframes → paid courses → support/resistance. Every rung gives "no result", and the conclusion the trader draws — *only people with money make money* — is the wrong diagnosis. He dismisses it directly ("get that out of your mind") and returns to the real question: **in which direction must you work so that you get a result?**
- The working tool is **projection**, and the unit of work is not the shape but the approach: at any point you intend to trade, ask **how did the market treat this very point when it first reached it?** — "the kind of approach there will be, that is the kind of market it becomes." If the approach is not read, the level/pattern/trendline means nothing.
- Two behavioural tells — the most transferable part of the session: (1) a **setup handed to you again and again** ("the market gives you a buy setup immediately, immediately again") is the market **tempting** you, and that is exactly where you must not enter; (2) a **slow, creeping continuation** is the same temptation, and the print you are meant to act on is the **momentum candle**, not the drift.
- He also explains why a *correct-looking* momentum print still loses money: **before your entry the market makes you pay your stop-loss first.** The move was real; your **entry point** was the gift.

### Why projections fail (the mechanic)
- The trader fails because he trades a **shape with no inventory behind it**: "sometimes a trendline works, sometimes it doesn't; sometimes the round number works, sometimes it doesn't; sometimes the pattern works, sometimes it doesn't." His question is the correct one — **what condition must exist** for a break or a pattern to work?
- The condition, applied to a level: you must see **how the market behaved the first time it approached that point**. On the archive chart a psychology level near **36,500** is drawn; the market gives a **breakout**, and his instruction is explicitly *do not treat that breakout as continuation* — the market goes slightly past and **immediately reverses**.
- Why that reversal is predictable to him: **many traders like to trade there.** The level is where the crowd's orders — and therefore the crowd's stops — are parked, so the print above it is made of stops, not of fresh conviction.
- Consequence he states for anyone trading that reaction: even though "good momentum" arrives, you will not make money from it, because the market takes your stop-loss before your entry order is filled.

### Pattern trading has a precondition
- On the head-and-shoulders formation he shows: if the neckline break is read correctly you do get "good momentum" — and he says plainly that **they had a position on** during it. But the teaching is not the pattern.
- The precondition: **positions must already be built** on the side you will target. Then a selling pattern earns a position, *because after that you are going to target their stops.* Without that inventory, trading patterns and levels alone is only **subscribing** — you are the liquidity, not the hunter.
- Pattern validity is also **conditional on the market's response**, not on the geometry: a **double top** would have "worked nicely" *only if* the reversal had come immediately; on the chart he shows the market did not reverse, it "folded" and continued — and *that* told him the market would continue. The pattern did not fail as a drawing; the market's response disproved it in real time.
- Same logic for a **breakout's sustainability**: at a support area, if the break happens with **no stop-losses available there, it cannot sustain**; where the market is holding around resistance, the **first** breakout seen there *can* sustain and continue. The available pool — not the shape — decides whether a break travels.

### The two tells: temptation vs the momentum print
- **Temptation (repeat):** if the market keeps handing you an easy setup at the same point — "immediately a buy setup, immediately again" — understand the condition: it is about to do the opposite. When a move repeats itself for you, the market is not rewarding you, it is collecting you.
- **Temptation (slow drift):** when the continuation comes **"slowly, slowly"**, he reads it as the market **tempting you to take a position** — do not enter there.
- **The real print:** when the **momentum** candle appears, *then* you know the market will proceed. He is explicit about the learning cost: "I am not saying from tomorrow you will recognise one candle — you have to work. I am giving you the direction." (Until you have the direction, nothing else you learn will tell you which print works.)
- **The stop-first rule:** a valid momentum move can still stop you out — the market reaches your stop-loss **before** your entry order executes. Entry placement, not read quality, is what that costs you.

### Where the stops are (the recurring comparison)
- Applied to the chart in words: "did anyone buy here? — the stop-losses **stand here**; you will not find stops *there*, you will find plenty of stops *here*." Direction comes from **comparing the two pools**, never from the pattern.
- Restated from earlier sessions: **a participant who has already exited cannot be hunted** — the market goes where the probability (of stops) is higher.
- And the trendline exercise he hands to the student: the trendline worked twice, then after the break the market reversed — *why?* Go home and work that out on the chart.

### Method and learning (his closing)
- **Drawing support/resistance is not a projection.** Marking levels alone will not produce a trade: "just putting support and resistance on the chart does not make a projection in the market." You must study how the market treated a *particular point*.
- He deliberately withholds: "I will not tell you everything — if I do all the work you will do nothing; you will watch and forget." Watching is not learning; the homework is on your own chart.
- His frame for teacher + student: **his observations plus your processor.** "You will have my mind, and you will have your own food processor: when you mix them and combine them, *then* you will achieve it." No course and no subscription can substitute for that processing.
- He repeats the timeline honesty of the March sessions: recognising these prints "will not happen from tomorrow"; it takes work, and one or two videos are not a method.

### Keep permanently
1. **No method works mechanically** — pattern, trendline, breakout, level: each works only under the **condition** that positions/stops exist on the other side to target. Check the inventory before the shape.
2. **Marking support/resistance is not a projection.** The read comes from *how the market treated that point the first time*; lines alone create no trade.
3. **⭐ A repeated gift is a trap.** If the market keeps handing you the same easy setup at the same point, it is tempting you — that is where you must not enter.
4. **⭐ Slow continuation = temptation; the momentum print = the signal.** Do not pay the drift; act on the momentum candle (and expect recognition skill to take months of chart work).
5. **Check the breakout's fuel.** A break with **no stops available** there cannot sustain; the **first** break at a level where stops are available can travel. Compare pools, not patterns.
6. **A pattern earns a trade only when positions are already built** on the side you will target — otherwise you are subscribing, i.e. you are the liquidity.
7. **Judge a pattern by the market's response**, not the drawing: immediate reversal validates it; a "fold" and continuation disproves it and predicts continuation.
8. **The market takes your stop before it fills you.** A correct read can still lose on entry placement — the entry point, not the analysis, is what stops you out.
9. **Where the stops are decides direction**: "not here — plenty here." Someone who has already exited cannot be hunted; the market goes where the stop probability is higher.
10. **Do your own processing.** A teacher gives observations, not the method; no course or subscription substitutes for working the charts yourself — watching without drawing = forgetting.
