### Core idea
- **Trading and profiting are two different skills.** Anyone can place trades; profit needs *learning the market*. Many traders say they have studied for years and still get no result — he answers that practice in the **wrong direction** produces no result, not that practice doesn't work.
- **Read who is sitting, directly off the chart.** The whole job is: find where the **sellers** are sitting and where the **buyers** are sitting, then **target them**. He repeats that this is visible "straight in the eyes" on the chart once you learn to look for it.
- **Target retail, never the operator.** You cannot hunt the operator's stops — that money does not come to you. Read **retail psychology**: a retracement followed by continued selling leaves retail sellers holding, so they are the ones you can target.
- **Depth-first learning, not breadth.** Pick the one thing you already understand ("here sellers are sitting, so I want to target them") and start working on *that* today. Waiting until you have learned the complete market before trading is why people lose years ("then take 100 days and you still won't learn").
- **"Why" beats "what".** Everyone can guess *what* may happen; the useful edge is knowing **why** the market should move — e.g. "it will fall because the people who sold here are still holding."

### The market is not deterministic (his framing)
- Charts are not arithmetic: after 2 does not always come 3 — after 2 can come **3 or 1**. The task is only to find out which of the two is the higher-probability branch, i.e. **buy or sell**. Nothing more than that needs to be solved to start earning.
- Big money doesn't guarantee the path either: "no matter how big the player, he cannot say the market will go this way."

### The two conditional plans (built before the open)
He demonstrated the same planning loop on three different charts:

| Chart | Inventory read | Flat / gap-down | Gap-up |
|-------|----------------|-----------------|--------|
| Big gap-down + continued selling | Sellers sitting, nobody bought | Target the sellers (buy side) | Sellers panic and exit in one candle → **go with the market** |
| Continuous positive momentum, buyers sitting | Buyers sitting | Target the buyers (sell side) | **Buy with market** — until closing price is crossed no buyer cuts, so there is no SL to take |
| Negative trend + retracement + selling | Sellers sitting, and they did **not** leave on the small retracement | Same plan for all three opens | Same plan |

- **Why a gap-up after a gap-down/sell day cannot be sold:** the trader who sold into weakness is *afraid*; a gap-up makes him exit in a single candle. There is no stop-loss pool left to harvest, so there is nothing in it for us — invert to the buy side and move with the market.
- **Why "map the SLs" is not enough:** the market sometimes goes *lower* first precisely to create **more** seller stop-losses, gives the sellers a fresh chance to sell, and only then targets them. The trap is manufactured before the move.
- **Decision rule he kept repeating:** flat → same plan; gap-up → the plan flips; gap-down → same plan (when sellers are the ones sitting). The open type selects *which pool* gets taken.
