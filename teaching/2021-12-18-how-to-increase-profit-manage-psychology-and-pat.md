### Core idea
- **The fear is asymmetrical — and it is backwards.** In a **losing** position he is calm: the
  chart is read normally, the whole session can be watched, and the mind starts thinking of
  **adding quantity**. In a **profitable** position he is afraid: the fear is that the profit
  will be given back, so the position is closed early and the market cannot even be watched for
  the rest of the day. *"प्रॉफिट हो रहा है लोस में तो अब हम क्या है कि आराम से अपना कॉन्फिडेंस
  बिल्कुल हाई लेवल पर रहता है"*.
- **The cure is not more confidence — it is a written system.** *"एक सिस्टम बनाना वह इसमें ऐड
  टारगेट और प्रोफिट बुकिंग तीनों आपको मिले"* — entry, stop-loss, target and profit-booking all
  defined **before** the entry. When the numbers are pre-decided, neither the profit nor the loss
  produces fear, and neither produces over-confidence.
- **A missed trade is the most expensive trade**, because it is not priced at all: the mind stops
  reading the chart and starts replaying the profit that was not taken. That replay is what drives
  the *next* mistake. *"वह प्रॉफिट यह आपसे अगली गलती है वह करवाएगा"*.
- **Patience is a system, not a mood.** It cannot be installed in one or two days; it is built
  gradually, the same way it would be built in personal life — you give yourself time and the
  behaviour follows.

### The pre-entry gate (decide before the candle closes)
- His worked example: draw the support/resistance line, then decide in advance — **if a 5-minute
  candle closes above this line, I take the buy side**. *"अगर यह प्रैंक उठाता है तो मैं यहां पर 22
  साइड की ट्रेन लूंगा"* (ASR-garbled: "22 side" = buy/one side).
- The decision is taken **before** the candle completes; when the candle does complete the plan is
  simply executed. If the decision is taken earlier — without waiting for the response — then the
  candle closing the other way immediately puts fear in the trade, and the same situation repeats
  (cut the loss, or cut the profit).
- Corollary he states: if the entry is built only after the response is received, then whether the
  trade lives or dies is already decided at entry. *"मैं पहले पोजीशन बना लें"* is the error — the
  position is created by impatience, not by the system.
- **Also stated:** if a trade has run and reversed, do not keep re-entering it — wait for the
  system's condition again rather than rebuilding the same position and cutting again.

### The four named causes of impatience / rushed entries
He lists the reasons a trader cannot hold a position, then gives one solution for all four.

1. **Loss recovery.** *"हम लोग को रिकवर करने की सोचते हैं"* — after a loss the mind tries to take
   the loss back, and that stops the trader from taking the trade the chart is actually offering.
2. **Greed measured in the wrong unit.** Wanting a ₹400-type profit from a setup that only
   supports a break-even move, or wanting in one day what the setup's arithmetic would deliver in
   ten. He states that from the entry point the trader should not price in *more* than the level
   allows, and should use a **limit profit** rather than chasing.
3. **Gambling mindset.** *"माइंडसेट आफ गैंबलिंग वाला हो गया"* — the screen must keep moving;
   profit and loss lose their meaning and the need for "entertainment" makes the trader enter
   before the setup, repeatedly.
4. **Yesterday's accounting.** Trying to recover yesterday's loss, or to protect yesterday's
   profit, on today's chart. His answer to a subscriber who regretted exiting a move early (at
   ~120 when the move later showed ~250) is explicit: *"आपको रिग्रेट उस चीज का नहीं होना चाहिए
   बिल्कुल भी नहीं चाहिए"* — **if the system's target was hit and you left, the exit was correct**;
   regretting it is what pushes you to break your own system. *"अगर आपने फ्री डिसाइड कर रखा हुआ
   कितना मुझे प्रॉफिट होना चाहिए"* — the benchmark is your own pre-decided number, not the
   market's maximum.

### The "systematic trading" prescription (his ultimate solution)
- Define and write down: **entry condition, exit, stop-loss, target, number of trades per day,
  where to enter, where to exit, where to book** — all of it, in advance.
- **Never add quantity in a losing position.** The instinct to add into a loss is named as a
  symptom (see the asymmetry of fear above), and he forbids it specifically for beginners
  (*"शुरुआती दिनों में ऐसा बिलकुल ना करें"*); later, adding can be done **only into profit** —
  and only within the system, because **not every system benefits from adding quantity**.
- **Keep the size small on purpose.** *"अपना जो नंबरों क्वांटिटी है वह भी कम से कम रखे"* — smaller
  size means the position does not shake the trader; psychology is what decides everything.
- **Keep the trade count small** — *"आपको ट्रेडिंग नहीं करनी है कभी भी आपने एक या दो ट्रेंड करने दिन
  में"* — one or two trades a day is the shape he describes, and over-trading is treated as a
  psychological failure rather than a strategy choice.
- **Enter only in the market's time.** *"पोजीशन तभी उनको करने जब मार्केट का आप का टाइम हो जाएगा"* —
  if the setup has not arrived cleanly, wait; do not manufacture an entry to satisfy the need for
  activity.
- **Follow one system for a month** before judging it (the ASR renders the horizon and the sample
  numbers unclearly — see the fidelity section); the point is a defined evaluation window, not one
  day's result.
- **Never try to recover a loss** — *"कभी भी लोस रिकॉर्ड करने की कोशिश ना करें"* (ASR: "record" =
  recover).

### Keep permanently
1. **Fear in a profitable trade is the real problem** — a calm losing trade and a frightened
   winning trade is the diagnostic of an undefined exit.
2. **Write entry, stop-loss, target and profit-booking before entering.** Undefined numbers are what
   make the profit frightening and the loss comfortable.
3. **Never add quantity to a losing position** (beginners: never at all); adding is only within a
   system, only into profit.
4. **Keep position size deliberately small** so the position cannot shake the decision — psychology
   is the binding constraint, not capital.
5. **One or two trades a day, decided in advance** — over-trading is a psychological failure first.
6. **A missed trade must be let go.** The mind replays the missed profit and that replay causes the
   next mistake; the missed move costs nothing, the chase costs money.
7. **Judge an exit against your pre-decided target, not the market's maximum.** Regret for leaving
   early is what breaks the system that just made the money.
8. **Never trade to recover a loss**, and never manage today's chart to settle yesterday's account.
9. **Enter only in the market's time** — if the setup has not arrived cleanly, waiting is the trade.
10. **Give any system a defined evaluation window (about a month** as spoken **) before judging it**;
    patience is a system that is built, not a mood that arrives.
