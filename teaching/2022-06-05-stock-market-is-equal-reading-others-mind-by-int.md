### Core idea
- If you are in the market for the **long term**, this is the one thing to tie a knot about: **first find out how the person in front of you is thinking.** The session's whole content is *how* to do that — not a philosophy. *(His aside is that the counterparty may be a newspaper, a YouTube channel, a mentor's tip — anyone whose thinking you can model.)*
- ⭐ **The method is a symmetry argument.** You cannot track two crore participants individually — **but they all follow the same handful of things**: a channel, a chart pattern, an indicator, price action. And because they follow the same few things, **price repeats on the chart.** So the chart is not a picture of the market; it is the **footprint of everybody's thinking** — and reading the chart *is* reading their minds.
- ⭐ **The law he states for it:** *"मेजॉरिटी ऑफ पीपल जहाँ काम करेंगे, उसका उल्टा मार्केट ट्रैक करेगा"* — the market tracks **opposite** to where the majority works. His reason is the profit identity: *"मेरा पैसा तब बनेगा जब दूसरे लोगों का पैसा जाएगा"* — **your money is made when someone else's money goes.** If everyone buys the same place, nobody's money is being taken and there is nothing to earn.
- ⭐ **The sequence the market runs, as he states it:** the market **first builds a selling setup** — everyone trades there — and **when the public starts selling, that is when it buys**, takes their stock, and then exits with its money. The visible setup is the invitation; the move is against the invitation.
- ⭐ And the two concrete execution rules the session produces elsewhere in the corpus for the first time: **after a good breakout, wait for its retest** (do not take the break), and **repeated selling attempts at resistance are the fuel that carries the market up**.

### The mistake he diagnoses: following one thing
- ⭐ His named cause for the standard failure — *"when our trade works, we keep trying to sell; the market does not fall; we keep waiting and it keeps going up"* — is **following only ONE chart pattern or one trendline**. When you trade on a single input, the market can be read from the other side and you become the liquidity.
- He states the stronger version of the same thing bluntly: **learning only support–resistance, trendline or indicators will not get you there** — you have to *collect* this understanding of how the market works. *(⚠️ This is a direct tension with earlier sessions — see Evolution.)*
- What is promised for the mind-reading version instead is **long-term survival**: if the market condition changes in the coming days, the trader who has understood *how the market works* stays in the game. The pattern is what changes; the behaviour does not.

### ⭐ Rule 1 — after a good breakout, wait for the retest
- He shows a chart where a breakout happened **and the market gave a proper retest**, and only then the full momentum arrived. He calls that chart the good one — and asks the obvious question: *if it was such a good chart, why did the market not give it earlier?*
- ⭐ His diagnosis of the trader's failure on that chart: **the position was there to be taken on the retest, and the trader could not take it** — because he was afraid the market would go back down. Instead, the move ran: down, up, and when the momentum came again the market **made its last test and took his stop-loss.**
- ⭐ The rule, in his words: *"अगर कोई breakout हो, अच्छा सा breakout हो जाए, तो उसके retest का wait कीजिए — तब जाकर हमें position मिलेगी."*
- The behaviour he describes instead — *"चलो ले लेते हैं"* (let's just take it now), then *"मैं हार गया, मैं लेता हूँ"* — followed by the market going down and then back up. **He files this as a discipline failure, not a knowledge failure.**
- Note the shape of the rule: he is not saying the breakout read is wrong. He is saying **the retest is where the trade becomes available**, and taking the break itself is what gives the market your stop.

### ⭐ Rule 2 — the resistance that keeps being sold is what lifts the market
- On a trending chart he shows the series of resistances overhead and explains the crowd behaviour: **as people keep trying to sell at each resistance, the market eats their stop-losses and grinds further up.** Each resistance becomes a fresh batch of stops.
- This is the corollary of the majority rule in a trend: **a market with no resistance above is not "safe"** — it is a market that has been repeatedly fed by sellers who insisted. The visible resistance is the supply of fuel, not the ceiling.

### The expiry-day trap — for the big option writer
- He moves to an expiry-day chart and describes the pattern of the **large premium seller**: someone with **≈₹12 crore** (his figure, ASR-weak) who does **not have much market knowledge**, sells because the premium looks like free money — *"इतनी free में मिल रही है?"* — and then, as the market moves, **keeps re-forming the position at the next strike**, building triangle straddles ("ट्रायंगल स्ट्रैडल") at each round strike in sequence.
- ⭐ **The trap he describes**: the market moves so that these writers **cut the profitable leg and hold the losing leg**, and then it takes **their stop-losses** too. In his summary: *"मार्केट उनको सभी को track करने के लिए, सभी को प्रॉफिट कम करने के लिए एक [trap] बनाता है, उसके बाद उसके according momentum करते हैं."*
- ⭐ The directional insight (stated as mechanism, not as a forecast): **premium sellers with capital but without a market read are the counterparty the expiry-day move is built for** — the same stop-inventory thesis, applied to the option chain on expiry.
- ⚠️ **ASR limitation:** the instrument is not reliably named and the strike figures are unreconciled (see fidelity). The *mechanism* is recorded; the *levels* are not.

### The reasons list (incomplete in the ASR)
- In one chart walk-through he enumerates **three to four reasons** why the market behaved as it did, and says these are what you must be able to justify. The ones that survive the ASR are: (1) **the market had already built good momentum there** — that is itself a reason; (2) a **gap fill** had occurred, and *"when a gap fills, a lot of people perceive it will act like resistance"* — i.e. the reason is the **crowd's belief attached to the gap**, not the gap mathematically; (3) a **repeating channel / pattern** visible on the chart. He signals a fourth.
- 📌 Recorded as a **lead, not a rule**: the enumeration is ASR-fragmented, and the fourth reason is not recoverable. What *is* clear is his framing — **a move needs a reason you can name, and the reason is usually the crowd's position or the crowd's belief, not the shape.**

### The closing boundary
- ⭐ He refuses the guru role explicitly: *"मैं तो केवल direction देने वाला हूँ — direction के बाद वह करना आपके ऊपर रहेगा, और उसी से आप खुद को एक sustainable trader बना पाओगे."* — I only give direction; applying it is on you.
- Session's own summary line: **understand how *other people* work** — that is the whole job.

### Keep permanently
1. **Read the person, not the picture.** The chart repeats because participants repeat: everyone follows the same small toolset, so their behaviour is inferable from the chart.
2. **The market tracks opposite to where the majority works** — and the reason is arithmetic, not spite: your profit requires somebody else's loss.
3. **First the market builds the visible setup; when the public piles in, it takes the other side.** The obvious trade is the invitation.
4. **After a good breakout, wait for the retest.** Taking the break itself is what gives the market your stop-loss; the retest is where the position becomes available.
5. **A market that keeps being sold at every resistance is being fuelled, not capped.** Repeated selling attempts feed the stops that carry price higher.
6. **Never trade from one input.** Following a single pattern, trendline or indicator is what makes you readable — and therefore exploitable.
7. **On expiry, the large premium seller with capital but no market read is the target.** Expect the tape to make him cut the profitable leg, hold the losing leg, and then take those stops.
8. **Before acting, name the reason for the move** — usually the crowd's position or the crowd's belief, not the shape. If you cannot name it, you have not finished reading.
9. **Learning tools is not learning the market.** Support–resistance, trendlines and indicators are inputs, not a method; the method is who is positioned and why.
10. **What survives a change of market condition is the read, not the setup** — build the durable skill, not just the durable setup.
