### Core idea
- ⭐ **The closing print decides who is sitting, and the opening decides whether you can reach them.** The session makes *both* ends of the day a psychology read — not support/resistance lines. *"मार्केट में जो ओपनिंग होता है और जो मार्केट का क्लोजिंग होता है, वह हमारे लिए कितना ज्यादा इंपोर्टेंट होता है."*
- ⭐ **The mechanism — a close above a round number fills a "domain" with buyers, and their stop-losses become tomorrow's target.** *"मार्केट ने कहीं ना कहीं 500 के ऊपर ही क्लोज किया था, तो कहीं ना कहीं बायर इस डोमेन के अंदर बैठे होंगे"* — normal traders get shaken out on a retracement, but the ones with *strong psychology and real quantity* sit there holding because *"500 को एज अ सपोर्ट कंसीडर करके मार्केट के अंदर बैठे रहते हैं."* ⇒ **their SLs are the fuel.**
- ⭐ **Round numbers are the domains.** *"जो लेवल होते हैं — 500 जैसे, 45000 हो गया, 44000 हो गया — जो भी राउंड नंबर होते हैं, वो हमारे लिए कितने इंपोर्टेंट होते हैं."* The close *relative to the round number* is what matters, not the candle shape.
- **Why the crowd is the fuel, not the signal:** the market is big and works on capital — *"पैसा लगता है, कैपिटल लगता है, उसके अकॉर्डिंग मार्केट काम करता रहता है"* — so setups come and go; only the *probability* read of who is trapped is worth having.

### The plan: sell both gaps (buyers sat above 500)
- The day's plan came from one observation: the previous close was **above 500** → buyers trapped in that domain → **target their SLs**:
  - **Gap-up** → *"जब भी मार्केट में थोड़ा बहुत गैप है [या] फ्लैट ओपनिंग … सेल का प्लान"* → **SELL**.
  - **Gap-down** → **SELL** as well — *"अगर हमें गैप डाउन देखने को मिलता है, तब भी हम सेल कर लेंगे … क्योंकि इनके एसल खाएंगे."*
- ⚠️ **The gap-up nuance (his preference):** a *good* gap-up is one where you still get a **price to sell into**; a gap-up that runs off immediately is less useful because buyers exit at once with a small profit — *"अगर हमें गैप अप मिलता है तो थोड़ा अच्छा मिलना चाहिए … बायर अगर यहां पे ओपन होता है, डायरेक्टली निकल गया तो इतना अच्छा नहीं होता."*
- **The profit-taking psychology he names:** if a buyer opens into a small gap-up and the market falls straight away, he will take his small profit and go — so you either get his SL later or you miss the move.

### Opening location: near support = a difficult market
- ⭐ **The rule he adds:** *"सपोर्ट वगैरह के आसपास ओपनिंग नहीं होना चाहिए, फिर मार्केट में प्रॉब्लम होने शुरू हो जाए"* — **if the open lands inside the support zone, the plan is fragile** because the buyers there suddenly get *a good price*, and that flips the whole psychology.
- **His worked warning (day-specific):** he had told them *"560 के आसपास ओपनिंग नहीं होना चाहिए"* — the market opened **above 560**; he still profited because the candles were strong, **but** the SLs he could have taken (below 500) were not reachable and only the *fresh* sellers got targeted:
  - *"हम टारगेट नहीं कर पाए … उसके बाद जो फ्रेश सेलर आने वाले थे, उनको हमने टारगेट किया आराम से."*
- ⭐ **Contrast — a gap-down open is the *easy* market:** *"अगर गैप डाउन की बात करें तो वो हमारे लिए इजी मार्केट हो सकता है"* — the trapped buyer from the previous close is the target, and if the market starts rising the downside risk is low.

### The 2–3 hour psychology change (why he cuts)
- ⭐ *"मार्केट इतना देर तक जब ट्रेड करता रहता है, जब दो-तीन घंटे हो जाते हैं, तो फिर चार्ट चेंज होना शुरू हो जाता है, फिर मार्केट की साइकोलॉजी डिफरेंट हो जाती है"* — after ~2–3 hours a **new crowd** has arrived; the original read decays. **This is his stated reason for cutting a trade rather than holding it.**
- Cadence link: the same mechanims appears in the earlier *time-as-input* session (2022-11-20: the day is a fixed-length shift that must be filled) — today applies it to the **intraday psychology reset**.

### Breakouts trap — wait for the retracement
- ⭐ *"अगर किसी ने गलती से यहां पे ब्रेकआउट के बाद बाय किया होगा तो 1 मिनट के अंदर हालात खराब कर दिए होंगे"* — a straightforward breakout-buy is the trap. Trade the **flat/gap open or a proper retracement**, not the breakout candle.

### Panic selling on a war — wait for the reaction, then buy
- The week was war-driven (Oct 2023). His rule: *"अगर सेलिंग आना शुरू हो जाए, तो जब तक बाइंग में मार्केट रिएक्ट करना ना शुरू करे, कोई अच्छे के अच्छे कैंडल ना बनाए, तब तक हमें रिएक्ट नहीं करना है"* — even when he planned to buy, **no reaction until the market reacts first.**
- After the panic leg he waited, and *"जैसे ही अच्छा अपसाइड का मोमेंटम है, उसके बाद हमने अच्छे से काम किया."*

### His own loss, told plainly (the 44,000 support sell)
- Set-up: post-open, market **took support at 44,000**; he planned a sell, waited *"4 घंटे तक"* for the breakdown.
- What broke it: the market stopped taking the support and started **taking resistance from above** — fresh sellers arrived, call-holders got scared of the war narrative, and the trap closed on him:
  - *"मार्केट ने एक काम और कर दिया, ऊपर से रेजिस्टेंस लेना शुरू कर दिया … तो कहीं ना कहीं सेलर भी आना शुरू हो जाते हैं … यहां पे हम ट्रैप हो गए, हमारा यहां पे लॉस हो गया."*
- ⭐ **His framing, kept honest:** *"नेट में ऐसा नहीं कि हमेशा प्रॉफिट ही होगा या हमेशा लॉस ही होगा"* — the analysis was exactly right and the trade still lost; both sit in the same week's ledger.

### Number-of-the-day context (a second worked example)
- After a close near **200**, he says a gap-up would be *difficult* (*"बहुत बड़ा प्रॉब्लम"*) but a gap-down *easy* — because a buyer at 200 gets trapped in a gap-down, or a seller gets trapped on a recovery; *"200 का सपोर्ट"* was being retested. Sitting above a round number = buyers' domain — same rule as 500.

### Keep permanently
1. **The closing print tells you who is sitting; the opening tells you whether you can reach them.** Read them as a pair before you write a plan.
2. **A close above a round number fills a domain with buyers** — strong-psychology, real-quantity buyers stay put *because* the round number held. Their stop-losses are the next day's target.
3. **Round numbers are the domains.** 500, 20,000, 44,000, 45,000 — the close *relative to* them matters more than the candle shape.
4. **An opening *inside* the support zone is a difficult market.** It hands the parked buyers a good price and flips the psychology — stand down or demand a much better setup.
5. **A gap-down open can be the *easy* market** when the prior close trapped buyers: the trapped side is the target, and downside risk is small if the market starts rising.
6. **Do not buy a breakout candle.** A flat/gap open or a proper retracement is the entry; the breakout buyer is the fuel (2023-09-10 rule, restated).
7. **When events (war/panic) drive selling, do not react first.** Wait until the market reacts and prints a good counter-candle before working the buy side.
8. **After ~2–3 hours the crowd changes and the psychology resets** — that is a reason to **cut**, not to hold a stale read.
9. **A perfectly read analysis can still trade as a loss** — keep the process, log the loss, do not rewrite the method on one outcome.
10. **The gap size and where it opens are the plan's gate**, not a fixed direction — always ask whose stop-losses the open is going to reach.
