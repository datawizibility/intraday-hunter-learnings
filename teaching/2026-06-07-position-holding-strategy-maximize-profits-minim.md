### Core idea
Finding the buy or the sell is not what makes the money — **holding the position is**. The session's whole weight sits on one asymmetry: the trader runs out of a *profit* the moment it feels like the market may turn, hunts a "second-best" then a "third-best" trade, and then parks his full attention on the *loss* — where he sits relaxed, adds quantity, and waits for a cover. The cure he gives is not a chart rule but a **symmetry law plus a size ladder**: run out of a loss as fast as you run out of a profit (the SL limit is the only waiting licence on the loss side), never read one candle — read the momentum budget — and, when you want to learn to hold, **hold a fraction of the quantity you trade**, starting from the smallest size at which you feel no fear, then scaling 50 → 100 → 200 → 400. Holding is licensed by a *reason* (visible momentum, expiry, a reversal, a big gap-down open), and the number of trades must be capped, because an uncapped trade count is exactly what makes the "second-best trade" search possible.

### The disease: cutting a profit to hunt the second-best trade
- The opening states the money-making function as a person, not a technique: the profitable trader overall is *"जो अपनी पोजीशन को होल्ड करना जानता है"*. The complaint is stated as a near-universal defect — *"वो अपनी पोजीशन होल्ड नहीं कर पाती"*.
- The mechanism, step by step as he narrates it: a trade is in profit, a feeling arrives that the market can turn here, and then — *"इस ट्रेड को कट कर लेता हूं और सेकंड बेस्ट ट्रेड ढूंढने की कोशिश करता हूं"*. The second trade may also run into profit, but *"मार्केट एक तरफा तो चलता नहीं"* — retracement, rejection and positive momentum all arrive — so the trader moves on again: *"थर्ड बेस्ट ट्रेड को ढूंढने के लिए चला जाएगा"*.
- His verdict is unambiguous: *"तो यह माइंडसेट आपको नहीं बनाना है"*. The habit-forming consequence is spelled out — *"आदत हो जाती है ट्रेड को चेंज करने की"* — and it is precisely this habit, he says, that blocks the hold mindset from ever forming.
- ⚠️ Note the double-edged statement that follows: he calls **not holding a loss** a mistake *and* calls cutting a profit to chase a better trade a mistake, in the same breath — *"प्रॉफिट वाली ट्रेड में तो कट करके भागेंगे"* is the behaviour he is describing, not recommending. Both halves belong to the same complaint about the trader's default reflexes.

### The asymmetry: full brain on the loss, no brain on the profit
- The sharper failure he names next: in a loss the trader *thinks*, looking for every place the market could cover — *"कोई उसकी कैपिटल को नुकसान नहीं पहुंचा रहा"* is the reassurance he gives himself while sitting there, adding quantity and watching a hundred things to find an exit. In profit he does the opposite: the moment a little positive momentum appears he is out, on the fear that a retracement will take it back.
- His own summary of that state is the sentence to keep: in loss the person is comfortable and studying; in profit the same person has *"100 बातें"* in his head and leaves. The bottleneck is not information — it is which side of the book the attention is assigned to.
- The behavioural evidence he uses is averaging: *"लॉस में ऐसा पर्सन आपको मिल जाएगा जो एवरेज करता है"*, while *"प्रॉफिट में अगर आपको एवरेज करने की बोल दे तो देखिए कितना डर लगता है"* — the fear is imagined, not real. The instruction is the polarity flip: *"लॉस में कुछ नहीं समझना"*.

### The symmetry law — leave a loss as fast as you leave a profit
- This is the load-bearing rule of the session, and he states it as a proportion rather than as a preference: *"जितना जल्दी आप प्रॉफिट में भागने की कोशिश करते हो ना उतना ही जल्दी लॉस में भागने की कोशिश करोगे तब आप अपनी पोजीशन को होल्ड करना सीख पाओगे"*. The lesson is that holding power is **manufactured by symmetry**, not by willpower.
- The stop is still the boundary, and he does not relax it: *"एक देखिए एसएल का लिमिट होता है"* — you may wait that much, no longer: *"उतना तो आप वेट कर सकते हो लेकिन ज्यादा देर तक लॉस में डटे रहना वो गलती आपको नहीं करना है"*. So the session gives the pull-back licence a **hard stop boundary** and nothing beyond it.
- Capital protection is restated in the same paragraph as a constraint that does not conflict with holding: in profit there is no problem at all, but the capital has to be kept intact — which he converts into the loss-side rule of not holding a losing position for long.

### Never read one candle — read the momentum budget
- The second durable instruction of the session: *"दूसरी आपको बात ये ध्यान रखनी है कि मार्केट के अंदर कभी भी कैंडल के ऊपर ज्यादा फोकस ना करें"*. He pre-empts the timeframe escape — *"1 मिनट का नहीं देखेंगे, 5 मिनट का देख लेंगे, 5 मिनट का नहीं देखेंगे, 15 मिनट का देख लेंगे"* — and answers it flatly: the fear follows you to any timeframe, so the timeframe is not the fix. The fix is the unit of attention: *"एक कैंडल के ऊपर आपको फोकस करना ही नहीं है"*.
- What replaces the candle is a **momentum budget** — the estimate of how far the move can run from here in either direction: *"आपको ओवरऑल मोमेंटम को समझना है"* ⇒ *"ओवरऑल मोमेंटम को देखो"* ⇒ *"अकेले कैंडल के ऊपर फोकस मत करो"*.
- The same ban is repeated at the end of the session as an anti-flinch rule: *"एक कैंडल से डर के मारे नहीं भागना है"* — a doji or any single print is not a reason to exit when the momentum read has not changed.

### The quantity ladder — hold less than you trade (the actionable fix)
- He gives the only mechanical procedure of the session for someone who has *tried* and still cannot hold. Take any size — *"अपना मार्केट के अंदर कोई भी क्वांटिटी लेते हो"*, illustration `400 क्वांटिटी` — and drop the identity between the traded size and the held size: *"तो ऐसे नहीं कि 400 क्वांटिटी को ही आपको होल्ड करना है"*.
- Once the momentum is with you and the target is achieved, the holding exercise is done at reduced size: *"काफी हद तक अपनी क्वांटिटी कम कर देना है"* — `400` becomes `100` or `50`, and the size rule is set by the body, not by the plan: *"जितने में आपको डर ना लगे उतनी क्वांटिटी रखकर फिर राइड करने की कोशिश करो"*, then *"फिर देखो कि क्या आप अपनी पोजीशन को होल्ड कर पाते हो"*.
- The ladder is progressive and stated with its numbers: *"एक बार के लिए अगर आप 50 होल्ड करना सीख रहे हो तो बाद में 100 को भी होल्ड कर पाओगे"*, then `200`, then `400`. The general principle is imported from everything else he teaches — *"जब छोटे में परफेक्ट हो जाते हैं फिर धीरे-धीरे हम आगे बढ़ते हैं"* — and applied here to *holding*, not to capital.
- The quantity lesson is stated as a scenario requirement: *"तो इसके लिए आपको क्या करना होगा? छोटे-छोटे सिचुएशन बनाने होंगे"*, with the inverse case named — *"पूरी क्वांटिटी लेके बैठोगे नहीं बैठ पाओगे"* (and he adds that this even degrades the exit: a full-size position leaves with less profit or gives the stop). The scaling permission is explicit: *"वो वेट किया जा सकता है और धीरे-धीरे उस चीज को बढ़ाया भी जा सकता है"*.

### The minimum target must be taken
- A second defect is named for the same root: traders who cut before the target — *"कुछ ट्रेडर गलती क्या करते हैं कि टारगेट तक पहुंच ही नहीं पाते"*. His correction is a scarcity claim about trades: *"जो ट्रेड आपको मिला है वही बेस्ट होता है"* — there is no second-best trade waiting, so the one in hand has to be carried to its target.
- He separates the two targets clearly: the simple/minimum target is not optional — *"हां एक आप सिंपल टारगेट तो लेके जाओगे"*, and at the close of the session, *"मिनिमम टारगेट तो आपको लेना ही है"* — while the *big* target is conditional, and the condition is the reason test below. The holding-to-get-there instruction is stated as a rule about profit trades: *"प्रॉफिट वाली ट्रेड को कट करके नहीं भागना है"*.

### Fix the number of trades — an uncapped count is what makes the hunt possible
- The structural fix for the whole disease is his standing trade-count device, restated here with its mechanism: *"इसीलिए मैं आपको बोलता हूं कि नंबर ऑफ ट्रेड आपको कम करने चाहिए"*. The reason he gives is that a cap is what makes the trader *stop looking*: with the count fixed he knows he cannot take more, whereas *"अगर आपने नंबर ऑफ़ ट्रेड फिक्स ही नहीं कर रखा है"* the next trade is always available, so the target is never reached: *"तो जो आपने टारगेट डिसाइड किया था उसके पास ही नहीं पहुंच पा रहे ना वो बीमारी यहां पे है"*.
- Note the direction of the causality he asserts: it is not that over-trading causes a small profit, it is that **an unlimited trade count removes the reason to hold**.

### Holding needs a reason — not every trade is a hold
- The most important guard against over-reading the session is stated explicitly: *"ऐसे नहीं कि हर ट्रेड को होल्ड ही करना है"*, and again, *"लेकिन ऐसे नहीं कि मार्केट के अंदर हर बार होल्ड करना है"*. Sitting through everything is the opposite error, and he names it in the trader's own voice — *"मैं तो यहां पे होल्ड ही करूं"* — which he rejects because *"मार्केट वन डायरेक्शनल कभी मोमेंटम नहीं करता"*.
- The licence to hold longer is a **visible situation**, and he lists the four he accepts: a market sideways for two-three days (*"आपको पता होना चाहिए दो-तीन दिन से साइडवेज मार्केट है"*) where momentum is possible today; an expiry day (*"आज कोई एक्सपायरी का मोमेंटम हो सकता है"*); a sudden reversal, with the reason given — *"जो रिवर्सल वगैरह होते हैं वो ज्यादा ही होते हैं"*; and a heavy selling or big gap-down open (*"बड़ा गैप डाउन वगैरह ओपन हुआ है"*).
- The test is perceptual and stated as the entry condition for the exercise: *"तो जब आपको कुछ नजर आए ना तब वहां पे होल्ड करना है"* — and the negative case is given in his own voice: *"कुछ नजर आ ही नहीं रहा। नहीं मुझे तो होल्ड करना है। वो नहीं होता"*.
- The instruction on such a day is to try it, at reduced size: *"उस दिन ट्राई करो"* — *"टारगेट के लिए तो मार्केट के अंदर वेट आपको करना ही करना है"*.

### The market's default is oscillation — holding power has to be sized to that
- The probability frame he puts behind the reason test: *"70-80% जो चार्ट अगर आप देखोगे तो ऐसे घूमते हुए चार्ट मिलेंगे"* and *"दो-तीन बार मार्केट घूमता ही घूमता है"*. A one-directional day is the exception: *"कभी-कभी ऐसा होगा कि एक मार्केट डायरेक्शन पकड़ लेगा"* — and when it happens, it should be harvested: *"वो उस चीज का भी आपको बेनिफिट उठाना है"*.
- The closing frame ties the two halves of the session together: the money is made by the person who can hold — *"पैसा वही बना पाता है जो अपनी पोजीशन को होल्ड कर सकता है"*; whether a particular call was right matters less than how long the good one was carried — *"अगर आपको कोई सही ट्रेड हाथ लग गया उसको आप होल्ड कितना कर पाते हो"*; and finding a side and holding it are two different skills: *"बाइंग सेलिंग ढूंढना एक बात होती है लेकिन उस पोजीशन को सही तरीके से होल्ड करना वो एक अलग बात होती है"*.
- The mechanism of the fix is one line, and it is a mindset claim, not a chart claim: *"बस यह काम माइंडसेट का और माइंडसेट ऐसे बन जाएगा आपका"*, with its precondition — *"ऐसा जब हम माइंडसेट बनाएंगे ना तब ये होल्डिंग पावर आती है"*. He also warns against the same-day-fix expectation: a target this large is reached gradually, not in one session.

### Keep permanently
1. **Money is made in the hold, not in the entry.** A correct direction that is abandoned early has not been traded; finding a side and carrying it are two separate skills.
2. **Do not cut a profit to go looking for a second-best trade.** There is no second-best trade waiting; the one in hand is the best one, and an uncapped trade count is what makes the search feel available.
3. **Run out of a loss as fast as you run out of a profit.** Holding power is manufactured by symmetry between the two sides, not by willpower.
4. **The stop is the only waiting licence on the loss side.** Wait inside the loss limit if you must; never sit past it, and never add quantity into the loss.
5. **Never decide from one candle.** Any timeframe; the fear travels with you. Read the momentum budget — how far the move can run from here — and act on that.
6. **To learn holding, hold less than you trade.** Cut the held quantity (e.g. 400 → 100 → 50) until it is a size at which you feel no fear, then scale the *held* size up over time: 50 → 100 → 200 → 400.
7. **The minimum target is mandatory; the big target is a conditional stretch.** Take the decided minimum whatever happens; extend only when a reason is visible.
8. **A long hold needs a stated reason** — sideways for two-three days, an expiry momentum, a sharp reversal, or a heavy gap-down open. "I feel like holding" is not a reason, and not every trade is a hold.
9. **Cap the number of trades.** The cap is not only risk control; it is what makes the target reachable, because a fixed count removes the option of another trade.
10. **Do not expect the fix in one day.** Holding a position is a large target and is built in small situations, at a size the psychology can actually carry.
