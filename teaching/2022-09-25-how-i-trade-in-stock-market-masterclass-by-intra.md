### Core idea
- This is the corpus's first **complete "how I build a trade" walkthrough**: he takes four trades from that week and narrates the
  **decision sequence** behind each one — not the outcome. *"हम ट्रेड बनाते कैसे हैं, किस प्रकार एक लॉजी को आईडेंटिफाई करते हैं."*
- Two statements frame the whole session:
  1. ⭐ **The faster the momentum, the higher the probability of continuation on that side** — *"मार्केट में जितना फास्ट
     मोमेंटम … उसी साइड मार्केट के जाने की प्रॉपर्टी बढ़ जाती है."* So you never plan the opposite of a fast move.
  2. ⭐ **What actually decides a trade is psychology + where stop-losses are available** — and the candlestick is *only* the
     tool that locates them. *"कैंडलेस्टिक का जो काम होता है वह स्टॉप लॉस को फाइंड आउट करने का ही काम होता है."*

### The trade-building sequence (the load-bearing rule of this session)
As spoken, the order he runs before any entry:
1. **What psychology / mindset is running in the market** (*"मार्केट में साइकोलॉजी किस प्रकार का चल रहा है, माइंडसेट किस प्रकार का है"*).
2. **Where the stop-losses are available — and where they are NOT** (*"इसके स्टॉपलेस कहां पे अवेलेबल हो सकते हैं और कहां पर अवेलेबल नहीं हो सकते"*).
3. **What the current candle/pattern is doing** — hammer, rejection, inverted hammer, breakout/breakdown, what trend is running.
4. Then the chart questions: *if the market crosses this level, who comes in? buyers or sellers? what will Nifty / Bank Nifty do?
   is it an expiry day?*
- His own weighting, in his words: *"जो मेरा मेजर ट्रेड होता है, वह ज्यादा डिसाइड करते हैं — साइकोलॉजी क्या है और मार्केट के अंदर
  स्टॉपलॉस कहां पे अवेलेबल है."* Candlestick reading is **subordinate**: it exists to find the stop-loss and to show what
  psychology the market is building — not to be the signal.

### Never plan the opposite of a fast / one-way move
- *"आपको कभी भी ऐसा तेजी का मोमेंटम देखने को मिले, आपको डायरेक्टली प्लान नहीं बनाना है कि मार्केट यहां से डाउन साइड चला जाएगा."*
  Same for continuous selling — never plan a recovery.
- He is explicit about *where* the exception lives: *"वो कंडीशन आपको न चार्ट के अंदर नज़र आएगी, न ऑप्शन चेन में नज़र आएगी — वो
  नज़र आएगी माइंडसेट के कारण."* The one licensed opposite trade is a **psychology point**: you believe the market
  *should not* do this, because if it crosses up *"केवल वायर मार्केट के अंदर आएंगे, सैलरी यहां पे आएंगे ही नहीं"* — i.e. only one
  side's inventory can arrive there, so the move has no fuel.
- **The risk licence attached to that exception (new and explicit):**
  - You may carry it if you are **already making profit**, or running **profit-loss-profit-loss**.
  - You may **not** carry it if you are just starting your training or are in a **loss-making** phase — *"क्योंकि यहां पर कैपिटल
    जाने की भी रिस्क ज्यादा रहती है"*, and because you have not yet watched enough market to read it.

### Time is what creates the stop pool (the sharpest new mechanism here)
- Setup he *saw and refused*: the market gave a **rejection at the closing price** (an inverted hammer / rejection print). Many
  traders would sell there, so *"स्टॉप लॉस तो यहां पर सैलरी मिल जाएंगे"* — a buy-side pool was building. **He still did not buy.**
- His reason is the rule: *"क्या सैलरी को यहां पे मार्केट में टाइम दिया?"* The market printed a rejection candle, then a red
  candle, then **converted that same candle green** — if you enter on a rejection print made in a fast up-down, you are caught.
  What he needs is a *"मेजर स्टॉप फ्लो"* — a major stop flow — **because he wants a target**, and a pool that was never given
  time to form cannot pay.
- As spoken: **give the other side time to build its positions (small opposing candles, time at the level) before trading the
  reaction**; in *"जहां पर मार्केट थोड़ा सा फास्टली नीचे-ऊपर आ रहा है, वहां पर डायरेक्टली ट्रेड बनाना गलत होगा और प्रोबेबिलिटी कम रहेगी."*
  A rejection candle alone is not a pool. **Time is the pool's raw material.**

### The four trades he narrates (chart reading, no levels asserted)
- **Trade A — selling side, then a cut.** He built a selling-side trade; the momentum came; the market held and made green
  candles, so he exited. Two reasons, one of them **cross-index**: *"निफ्टी ने एक लेवल का ब्रेकडाउन बड़ा ही जल्दी दे दिया … और मेरी
  पोज़िशन ज्यादा बैंक निफ्टी में होती है"* — his money was in Bank Nifty, Nifty broke a level down fast, and his target was met, so
  he cut *"क्योंकि मेरा बैंक निफ्टी में पूरा पैसा लग चुका है."* **The index that is not carrying the money decides the exit.**
- **Who cannot be targeted.** Pointing at the chart: *"जो यहां पर सेलिंग करके बैठा था उसको यहां तक प्रॉफिट नज़र आ चुका है … जब
  मार्केट ने रिकवरी किया होगा तो ये निकल गया होगा."* A side that has already seen its profit is **out** — it cannot be the target.
- **The 19-Sep analysis, restated as a standing plan shape:** if the market goes **directly up** → look for a **selling-side**
  trade; if it comes **down with a retracement** → sell; but **if you want to buy, only after the market has exited the
  stop-loss zone** (*"जब ये मार्केट स्टॉपलॉस से निकल जाए, तब ट्राई कर सकते हो"*), because big money may be sitting in that zone.
- **The engineered up-move.** Where he had expected the market to come down and it instead went up: *"इसका मतलब क्या है कि यहां पे
  जानबूझकर मार्केट ऊपर लेकर गया था, उसके बाद अपनी पोज़िशन बना के मार्केट को यहां तक लेकर आया."* The upward print was the
  fabrication of the seller inventory that was later eaten.
- **Level as a power test:** below the level the selling side has power; above it *"सैलरी में पावर ही नहीं है — अगर पावर ही नहीं है
  तो पैसा लगाने का क्या फायदा."* After his own loss he had extra confirmation that a seller was sitting there, so he kept the
  selling plan when price traded under the level.
- **The final buy:** after a gap-down and recovery he did **not** sell the recovery. He waited for a level's breakout → retest →
  breakout again, and for Nifty to stop at support and turn up, then bought Bank Nifty because *"अपना मेजर पैसा बैंक निफ्टी के
  अंदर लगता है."* A fast continuous momentum came; he could not even cut until the market recovered — *"कभी आपको इतना फास्ट
  मूवमेंट मिलेगा, कभी नहीं मिलेगा."* He names luck as a component, out loud.

### Same inputs, different output — the 2+2 statement

### Keep permanently
1. **Build in order: psychology → where the stops are (and are not) → candle → level/cross-index questions.** The candlestick's
   job is to locate the stop-loss; it is not the signal.
2. **Never plan the opposite of a fast or one-way move.** The faster the momentum, the higher the probability it continues on
   that side.
3. **The only licensed counter-trade is a psychology point** — something not visible on the chart or in the option chain, where
   the crossing level can only be fed by one side's inventory.
4. **Gate that counter-trade by your account state.** Only a profit-making or profit-loss-profit-loss trader may carry it;
   a beginner or a trader in a loss phase may not.
5. **Give the other side time before you trade its reaction.** A rejection print made in a fast up-down has no pool behind it —
   no pool, no target. Wait for the small opposing candles and the time at the level.
6. **A side that has already seen its profit has exited.** Do not build the trade around taking money from someone who has
   already booked it.
7. **Decide exits with the index that is not carrying your money.** When the other index breaks a level fast and your target is
   met, cut — the whole position is exposed in the index that has not broken.
8. **If you want the opposite trade, wait for the market to exit the stop-loss zone first.** Do not buy inside a zone where big
   money may be sitting.
9. **The same read can pay fully, partly, or nothing** (*2+2 = 4, 2, or 0*). You are not owed the full move; you are owed the
   read.
10. **Build your own thing.** A ready-made setup, bought or copied, pays less — *"जिस दिन आपने अपना कुछ बनाना शुरू कर दिया, वही
    दिन होगा जब आप प्रॉफिटेबल ट्रेडर बन जाओगे"* — and stop asking *when* you will be profitable, ask *how*.
