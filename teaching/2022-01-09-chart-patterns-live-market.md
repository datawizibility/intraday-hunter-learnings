## Teaching
**Video:** HOW TO FIND CHART PATTERNS IN LIVE MARKET | BEGINNER'S GUIDE — session, 09 JAN 2022

### Core idea
- **The patterns repeat, and that repetition is the edge.** His opening claim is that the screen is
  readable: *"जब आप स्टॉक मार्केट की स्क्रीन को समझ जाओगे तो आपने पूरी मार्केट को ही प्रिडिक्ट कर
  लिया"* — if you understand the structure on the screen, you have read the whole market's future.
  The reason patterns work is not convention but **repetition of structure**, which is what raises
  the probability of a trade.
- **Price action is accepted, but structure comes first.** *"जान तक मैं मानता हूं प्राइस सेक्शन
  ट्रेडिंग ठीक है"* (ASR garbles *price action* → *प्राइस सेक्शन*), and then the sequence he
  prescribes: first learn the price **structure**, then learn how to trade it — and only then, with
  experience, will you be able to capture *how* a price forms momentum.
- **⭐ The pattern that everyone can see is not a trade.** *"आपको मार्किट में आना आपको बिल्कुल क्लियर
  कुछ नजर नहीं आएगा अगर सभी को उस अ आप आप देख रहे हो वैसे ही सभी देख रहें हैं"* — if the structure is
  as obvious to you as to everyone else, there is no trade in it; you must bring your own reading.
  The instruction is *"आपको कुछ अपना दिमाग लगाना होगा और अपनी स्ट्रक्चर को फाइंड आउट करना होगा"* —
  apply your own mind and find **your own** structure.
- **Format.** He says the session carries a fixed set of structures — ASR renders it *"यह हमारा पांच
  जोन टोटल है"* (⚠️ garbled; heard as *five in total*), of which he demos Head & Shoulders, Double
  Bottom (W), Double Top (M) and touches a triangle-type structure, explicitly deferring
  **Inverse Head & Shoulders** to a later session — *"वह बिल्कुल उल्टा है जो हमने डिश किया था"* —
  because the video was already running long.
- He then walks a **one-year back-test** of the same patterns across named stocks, and closes by
  telling the audience to back-test **100 charts** themselves.

### Head & Shoulders — the structure and the left-side break
- Structure: **left shoulder → head → right shoulder**, with the **neckline** drawn across the
  reaction lows. In his own words the price *"मुमेंटम करता है … फिर एक हफ्ते करता है"* — impulse,
  then fold, then the next leg, and the pattern is the produced shape.
- **The trade is on the LEFT side.** *"आप इसमें सेलिंग की ब्रेड ले"* — you take the selling break of
  the neckline rather than waiting for a retest after the break. If you miss the left-side break you
  can wait for a **breakdown retest** and sell there, but that is the second, lower-quality entry.
- ⭐ **The textbook shape will not be there — read the momentum character instead.**
  *"यह प्रॉपर नहीं मिलेगा मार्किट में थोड़ा आपको अलग मुमेंटम मिलते हैं चार्ट में"*. So the discriminator
  is not the shape but what kind of momentum produced it: *"किस प्रकार का नहीं नहीं बनती है तो उसमें
  ट्रेड नहीं करना"* — if the momentum is not of the right character, there is no trade.
- He also notes a **descending / triangle-type** structure as the next one, and says a structure
  that has not been built on the *right* momentum is a structure you must be able to **say no to**.

### Double Bottom (W) — bought at lows
- *"वह बिल्कुल लो लेवल पर बने होते हैं किसी स्टोक्स के लो लेवल पर"* — the double bottom is built at
  the **low levels**, and that is the tool you reach for when you want to trade a stock from its low.
- Trade direction is the neckline break upward. He demos it on **Axis Bank** and **BPCL** and asks
  the audience to compare the two — the same W appearing in both, with a different quality — and to
  answer which side holds strength before he says it.
- ⭐ **The strength test he applies to the two lows.** Looking at the two lows of the structure he
  reads them as either *aligned* or *"यह लोग और यह लो हो गया डिफरेंट"* (the lows came out different),
  and the conclusion drawn from the mismatch is *"कहीं ना कहीं सैलरी के हाथ मजबूत नहीं है"*
  (⚠️ ASR renders *seller* as *सैलरी*) — **somewhere the selling side is not strong**. He cross-reads
  the same shape in **Tata Steel** to show what an aligned version looks like.
- And the trader's error on that chart: *"स्ट्रक्चर बन गया लेकिन उसने यह लो नहीं देखा"* — the
  structure formed, but **he never looked at that low**, so he could not actually read the momentum
  the chart was making.

### Double Top (M) — sold at highs
- ⭐ **The rule he states for the second top:** the second top must **not cross** the first top's
  high — *"इसे क्रॉस नहीं होना चाहिए"*. If it does, the structure is not the sell structure.
- ⭐ **The direction gate — strength must be on the side you are joining.** ASR version:
  *"अगर आपको कुछ भी चीज सेल करनी है तो वहां पर आपको सैलरी स्टैंड चाहिए और कुछ भी चीज बाइक है तो वहां
  पर क्या चाहिए बाय रोड डिस्टेंस ही"* — ⚠️ heavily garbled; read plainly, **to sell you must see
  SELLER strength and to buy you must see BUYER strength**. He repeats the same test for the sell
  side (*"सैलरी के पास ट्रेन है"*) and the buy side (*"बे अरे के पास फ्रेंड है"*) — again ⚠️ ASR
  renders *strength* four different ways across the session (*स्टैंड / ट्रेन / फ्रेंड / टाइम*).
- **What strength looks like — the footprint metaphor.** His clearest statement of the idea is not
  a definition but an image: the strong side **shows its footprint and invites you to follow it** —
  *"सैलरी अपनी फुटप्रिंट दे रहा है कि आप मेरी पार्टी को ज्वाइन कीजिए और मेरे साथ रही है और आपको अच्छा
  मुनाफा मिलेगा"*. Then *"उसकी पार्टी को ज्वाइन करना है और आप एक अच्छा प्रॉफिट यहां पर बनाओगे"*.
  **If the side is not showing the footprint, you do not join the party** — you do not take the
  trade into an unproven side.
- **What the footprint is NOT:** he warns against taking the neckline alone as the signal —
  the crowd that buys the neckline break on an M is the crowd that gets stopped (*"आप नैक लाइन पर क्या
  करोगे इसलिए करके बैठ जाओगे और फिर आपको सेल्यूट हो जाएगा"*, ⚠️ ASR *सेल्यूट* — read as *stopped*).
  The reason is the same as the footprint claim: the strong side already told you what it intends.

### Structure quality — proper swings, and the levels that must line up
- ⭐ **Do not build structures out of arbitrary highs and lows.** *"ऐसे नहीं किसी भी भाई लोगों को लेकर
  स्ट्रक्चर बनाने लग जाओ आपको प्रॉपर एक सर्विंग देखना है"* — use a **proper swing** (⚠️ ASR says
  *सर्विंग* for *swing*). He repeats that in the market you will not get a textbook structure:
  *"मार्केट में प्रॉपर कुछ नहीं मिलेगा"*.
- **Before the trade, test the structure's own lows/highs against each other** (the two-low test above,
  and the second-top-vs-first-top rule). A structure whose internal pivots disagree is the structure
  where *"सैलरी के हाथ मजबूत नहीं है"*.
- **He rejects a W he had drawn earlier in the same session.** *"यह डबल बॉर्डर हमारे काम का नहीं है
  क्यों क्योंकि यह देखिए आप रेस्टोरेंट ही नहीं दिखा रहा"* (⚠️ ASR *रहता/रिस्टैंड* → *strength*) — the
  pattern was present and still not tradeable, because the strength behind it was not shown. So a
  pattern can exist and be worthless.
- **Every structure must be re-read on its own day.** He walks a structure across a one-year window
  and says the reading only becomes available with experience — *"वह यह डेली नहीं सीख जाएगा धीरे-धीरे
  आ जाएगा"*.

### Quantity and confidence — size is a function of structure quality
- ⭐ *"स्ट्रक्चर बन गया तो कर ऊपर आप जितना हो उतना क्वांटिटी को यूज करें अपने ट्रेडिंग ऐसा नहीं होता"* —
  ⚠️ garbled negation; read plainly, **the mere existence of a structure is not a reason to size up**.
  And then: *"यूज कीजिए और धीरे-धीरे आफ एक्सपीरियंस के साथ सीख जाओगे"* — use small quantity and let
  experience build the size.
- **Nothing is 100%, and this is said to justify the size rule:** *"यह ट्रैक्टर बन गया तो प्रॉपर यह
  काम करेगा ऐसा सौ परसेंट कुछ नहीं मिलेगा मार्केट में"* — a structure forming does not mean it will
  work; nothing in the market comes at 100%.
- **The half-quantity case is stated explicitly on a live example.** On one of the back-tested names
  he says *"हाफ क्वांटिटी में क्योंकि प्रॉपर या फिर स्ट्रैट नहीं थी"* — **half quantity, because the
  trend was not proper**. He adds that if you want to invest in such a name, hold that position size
  or less, scaled to your own risk.
- **The counter-example he refuses:** *"बैंक करोगे तो … इतना मोमेंट होने के बाद कोई फायदा नहीं है
  क्योंकि यहां पर असल भूत इनक्रीज हो जाता है"* — ⚠️ garbled; read plainly, **entering after the move
  has already happened gives you nothing**, because the risk (ASR *असल भूत* — most likely *SL*) has
  already grown.

### Back-test — the method you are given to build CONfidence, not just technique
- He gives a **one-year back-test** on the patterns and says why it is not optional:
  *"बैकस्ट्रीट क्यों जरूरी है कि मैंने आपको यह बताती है कि ट्रैक्टर कैसी होनी चाहिए और कैसे ट्रेड लें
  लेकिन अगर आपको इंस्ट्रक्टर पर कॉन्फिडेंस नहीं आएगा तो आप रीड कैसे करोगे"* — the back-test tells
  you the shape, **but its real job is to give you confidence in reading it**.
- ⭐ **The homework is quantified: 100 charts.** *"100 चार्ट को बैड टेस्ट कीजिए फिर आपको प्राइस
  लर्निंग आने लगी फिर आपके जो न प्रॉफिटेबिलिटी है मार्किट में बढ़ती जाएगी"* — back-test a hundred
  charts and price-reading arrives, and with it profitability.
- ⭐ **And the effort is yours:** *"सारा का सारा काम दूसरा नहीं कर सकता आपको कुछ काम अपने आप भी करना
  होगा"* — nobody else can do the whole job for you.
- **He prices his own session honestly.** *"मैंने आपको दो के बैड टेस्ट … यह मैं जस्ट आपको लर्निंग के
  लिए बता रहा हूं"* — and closes by inviting corrections on his own shortcomings.
- **Closing line on the toolkit:** *"इंडिकेटेड इन की थी वह भी अच्छी ट्रेडिंग लेकिन साथ ही साथ में
  प्रोजेक्शन … को भी थोड़ा अपने सिस्टम में जाइए"* (⚠️ *इंडिकेटर्स*; *प्रोजेक्शन*) — **indicators
  also give good trades, but add pattern-reading to your system as well.** This is a neutral
  position, not an anti-indicator one.

### Keep permanently
1. **Read structure before you read anything else.** Patterns are not shapes to copy but repetitions
   of structure — the edge is in recognising the repetition, not in drawing the outline.
2. **If the structure is obvious to everyone, it is not a trade.** Find and apply your own reading;
   the crowd-visible pattern is the one that gets faded.
3. **Take the LEFT-side break, not the post-break crowd entry.** On an H&S / M, the selling break of
   the neckline is the entry; buying or selling the neckline *after* everyone has seen it is what
   gets you stopped. If you must have the second entry, take the **breakdown retest** at reduced
   quality.
4. **Validate the second pivot against the first.** A double top whose second top crosses the first
   high is not the sell structure; a double bottom whose two lows come out different tells you the
   selling side is not weak. **Test the internal pivots before the trade, not after.**
5. **Only join the party that has shown its footprint.** Sell only where **seller strength** is
   visible, buy only where **buyer strength** is visible. A pattern with no strength behind it may
   still exist — and is still not tradeable.
6. **Build structures only from proper swings.** Do not assemble a pattern out of arbitrary highs and
   lows; the market will not hand you a textbook shape, and the swing points are your responsibility.
7. **The mere existence of a structure is not a reason to size up.** Use small quantity and let
   experience raise it; take **half quantity** when the trend is not proper. Nothing in the market is
   100%.
8. **Do not enter after the move has already happened.** Once the momentum has played out, the risk
   you are accepting has already grown and there is nothing left to collect.
9. **Back-test 100 charts — the purpose is confidence, not technique.** You can be taught the shape
   and still be unable to read it until you have seen it a hundred times.
10. **The work is yours.** Nobody — including the person teaching you — can do the reading for you.
