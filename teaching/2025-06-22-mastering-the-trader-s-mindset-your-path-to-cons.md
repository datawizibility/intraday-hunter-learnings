### Core idea
- **A strong mindset — not capital size and not a good setup — is what actually produces profit.** His opening claim: *"जब तक आपके पास स्ट्रांग माइंडसेट नहीं है तब तक आप मार्केट के अंदर प्रॉफिट बना नहीं पाओगे"*, because *"जो मार्केट के अंदर प्रॉफिट बनता है वो स्ट्रांग माइंडसेट से ही बनता है"*.
- **Mindset is controlled by trading LESS, not by willpower.** More trades = worse mindset — *"जितना आप ज्यादा ट्रेड करोगे माइंडसेट उतना ही ज्यादा खराब हो जाएगा"*. He says 3–4 trades a day is already the ceiling, not the target.
- **Stop chasing the jackpot; take small profits.** The jackpot is a by-product — *"कभी भी आपको जैकपॉट के पीछे मत भागना है। छोटे-छोटे प्रॉफिट करने की कोशिश करो"*.
- **Trade so you survive to trade again** — preserve capital so tomorrow's opportunity still exists for you, and never use borrowed money.
- **Keep basics clear, keep backtesting, keep learning.** Trading is a probability game; the probability is found by backtesting, and the market keeps evolving so the learning never stops.
- **Patience + discipline = the real success**, and 100% does not exist — a correct setup can still have to be cut at a loss.

### Trade count is the mindset control
- The causal chain he states: control over your trading → control over your mindset → consistent profit.
  - *"जितना लेस ट्रेड करोगे उतना आपका माइंडसेट कंट्रोल में रहेगा"*.
- Even a full day at the screen yields only a few genuine opportunities: 3–4 good trades are hard to find — *"तीन से चार ट्रेड मैं कहता हूं बहुत ही ज्यादा है। इनफ है ट्रेड करने के लिए।"*
- The failure pattern he names: the over-trader takes ~20–30 trades — *"20 ट्रेड करेंगे 30 ट्रेड करेंगे"* — chasing every up-move into buying and every down-move into selling, and running several indices/stocks at once so that the number of trades is never under control. Then the setup is forgotten and the tape is traded on momentum.
- His consequence: more trades than that raises the odds of loss relative to profit, and one market-wide reason — focus is gone.

### The jackpot trap (small profits, and the 100% myth)
- His own example: he had a call trade written, sitting on it could have made a much bigger (he says double-triple) profit — but *"कभी भी आपको जैकपॉट के पीछे मत भागना है। छोटे-छोटे प्रॉफिट करने की कोशिश करो"* — because the jackpot arrives by itself: *"जैकपॉट तो ऑटोमेटिक आपका बन जाएगा"*.
- The mechanism of the damage: chasing the jackpot repeatedly costs the profit you *were* going to get — *"तो इसके पीछे भागना बंद कर दो"* — and it is worse for option buyers/sellers, where sitting through a wrong bet hurts more.
- **The 100% rule (his second admission).** He deliberately tried to take an **adjustment (option-selling) setup to 100%** — *"इसमें लॉस होने ही नहीं दूंगा"* — and it failed: even with adjustment, some days forced a real loss, and 4–5 days of profit were handed back in one day. Hence: *"एक लिमिट ऐसी भी होगी कि जहां पे लॉस लेकर आपको निकल जाना है"*.
- **Do not sit through an unbounded adverse move:** *"एक दिन के अंदर 1000 2000 पॉइंट का मोमेंटम आ जाए"* — if you refuse to cut, the loss beyond your margin becomes possible and you may not even identify it in time.
- Restated as a rule twice in the session: *"100% के पीछे आपको कभी भी मार्केट के अंदर नहीं भागना है"* and — after a sell that had to be cut — *"मैंने आपको क्या बोला 100% कुछ भी नहीं होता"*. He explicitly says there should be no regret when a cut trade later moves your way.

### Capital preservation (survive to trade tomorrow; never borrowed money)
- He frames it as the trader's horizon: the trade business spans days, not one day — *"ट्रेड आपको ऐसे करना है कि आप कल भी मार्केट के अंदर सर्वाइव कर पाओ"*.
- Therefore: *"एक ही दिन के अंदर कैपिटल को मत खत्म करना"* — do not commit the whole capital in one session.
- **Expiry days are the specific danger zone for greed:** *"एक्सपायरी के दिन बड़े लालच आते हैं"* — the doubled/tripled size that he says is what finishes the account.
- **Borrowed money is banned, flatly.** *"और लोन वगैरह लेकर है ना ट्रेडिंग नहीं होता"* — and *"आप कितने भी कुछ भी किसी से भी मिले ना यह काम मत करना"*, which he calls *"यह सबसे खराब है ट्रेडिंग में"*. Use your own money: you are not large enough to move the market anyway, so the size a borrowed account buys is not needed.

### Backtesting, journaling and continuous learning
- **Backtest is a habit, not an exercise.** *"बैक टेस्ट किया करो"* — review how the week and the month went, how often the plan was right, how often wrong. He does it himself on his own analysis/plans.
- **Why:** trading is a probability game — *"प्रोबेबिलिटी का गेम होता है"* — and *"तो बैक टेस्ट करना मार्केट के अंदर बहुत ही ज्यादा जरूरी है"*: the backtest is where the high-probability zone becomes visible.
- **Write the decision down.** *"ट्रेड करते हो तो कुछ बातें राइट डाउन भी कर लिया करो"* — so you can see the mindset you were in when you took the trade; then *"मिस्टेक को आइडेंटिफाई करना इजीली हो जाएगा"*, and learn from his mistakes as well as your own (*"जो मैं आपको ट्रेड करता हूं वहां पे भी आपको कुछ मिस्टेक मे भी नजर आएंगी"*).
- **The market evolves, so the method must be re-learned.** *"मार्केट की जो कंडीशन होती है, वह हमेशा चेंज होती रहती है"* — what worked one year or 2–3 years ago differs now (he names Bank Nifty's momentum having changed), and *"मार्केट अगर इवॉल्व हो रहा है तो आपको भी साथ की साथ में लर्निंग अपना रखना पड़ेगा"*.
- **Basics before advanced.** *"बेसिक को हमेशा क्लियर रखो"* — if option trading, learn the option chain and option mechanics first; *"अगर आपका बेसिक क्लियर होगा तभी आप आगे बढ़ पाओगे"* — advanced material on an unclear base does not get used.

### Flexible thinking (bend the plan; hold both sides)
- His definition of flexibility is behavioural, not philosophical: *"जितना आप ट्रेडिंग में फ्लैक्सिबल होगे, उतना आप मार्केट के अंदर अच्छा कर पाओगे"*.
- Worked sequence he narrates from that week: a **buy** plan on a big gap-down, then the next day a **sell** plan after good momentum, *"तो मार्केट जब ऊपर जा रहा है तो हम बाइंग के लिए भी प्रिपेयर हैं"* — the mindset is re-bent to the situation rather than locked to yesterday's side.
- **The live switch:** a day he had gone in with a **sell** plan (carried from the prior candle structure) — when the print disagreed (a big one-candle advance that *"नहीं आना चाहिए था"* given the open), he took the **call** side instead. The stated licence: read the situation (breakout, behaviour, and cross-index confirmation) and prepare for the other side — *"अगर आपको लग रहा है कि मार्केट नीचे जा रहा है तो मे बी आपको सेलिंग के लिए प्रिपेयर होना चाहिए"*.
- The failure mode he warns against is one-directional thinking — repeatedly selling into a market that keeps rising — where *"हो सकता है एक दिन मार्केट गिर जाए वहीं पे हम ट्रैप हो जाए"*: one locked side, one trapped day.
- **Patience and discipline are the frame for all of it:** *"रियल सक्सेस आपको तभी मिलेगी जब आपको मार्केट के अंदर पेशेंस और डिसिप्लिन की वैल्यू समझ में आएगी"* — no rushing in, no random buying/selling anywhere; and the closing claim that a mindset that works makes profit unstoppable (*"माइंडसेट अगर आपका अच्छे से काम करता है तो ट्रेडिंग में प्रॉफिट बनाने से आपको कोई नहीं रोकने वाला"*).

### Keep permanently
1. **Profit comes from a strong mindset, not from capital size or from a good setup** — fix the mindset first; the other two do not compensate for it.
2. **Control the mindset by trading LESS:** the trade count is the lever. Treat 3–4 trades as the ceiling (a fixed small count), never 20–30.
3. **Never chase the jackpot. Take small profits** — the jackpot is a by-product, not a target — and never size up because today you have decided you will make money.
4. **100% does not exist.** Every setup — including an adjusted option-selling setup — carries a loss branch; set a limit at which you take the loss and leave. Do not regret a cut that later moves your way.
5. **Do not sit through an unbounded adverse move.** One day can deliver a 1000/2000-point run against you; the cut is the protection, and refusing it is what takes you past margin.
6. **Preserve capital so you can trade tomorrow.** Never finish the capital in one day; the trade business is multi-day.
7. **Never trade on borrowed money.** No exception, regardless of who offers it; you are not large enough for the borrowed size to matter anyway.
8. **Backtest as a habit, and write your decisions down** — that is where probability becomes visible and where your mistakes (and mine) become learnable.
9. **Keep the basics clear before adding advanced material**, and keep re-learning: market conditions change, so a method that worked a year or two ago must be re-captured.
10. **Bend the plan, do not lock a side.** Be prepared for the other side the moment the market's own behaviour disagrees with your written plan.
11. **Patience + discipline is the success condition** — no rushing, no random entry anywhere; with a working mindset, profit-making stops being the constraint.
