### Core idea
- Divide news into **immediate/unexpected** and **already discounted/anticipated**. An immediate surprise can justify initially respecting its direction; otherwise, read the chart and available stop pools rather than trade the headline.
- Examples of immediate news include an unexpected death of a company's MD, a genuinely new policy, or an unforeseen Budget provision. A known bank fraud or only the probability of war may already be reflected in positioning.
- Even when news is immediate, he still prefers greater focus on price action. The chart reveals who acted on the narrative, where their stops sit, and whether fuel remains.
- Patterns fail when the stop pool required for continuation is absent. A trend-line break or W breakout is not causal by itself.

### Two news classes and the decision rule
| News class | Description | Response taught |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate / unexpected | New information the market could not reasonably position for beforehand | Respect the initial positive/negative direction, then continue reading price action |
| Already discounted / probabilistic | Known fraud, ongoing geopolitical probability, anticipated narrative | Do not trade the headline mechanically; use chart structure and stop availability |

- He gives war risk as a **50% probability** example: when occurrence itself is uncertain and discussed in advance, use price action rather than assume the feared outcome.
- A policy or Budget item can have immediate effect only when it is genuinely new—not merely when it appears in the news cycle.

### News matters through positioning
- The durable question is not “is the news positive or negative?” but **who bought or sold because of it, and where are their stops now?**
- A green candle consumed existing seller stops, but the market did not fully recover because the next required pool was not available in the same direction.
- Weekend profit-protection can change behaviour: traders whose large unrealised profit has been cut after a recovery may book rather than wait, removing or relocating the pool.
- This extends the sentiment-based stop category from 2022-02-13: news creates positioning; positioning creates stops; available stops influence the next move.

### Recovery percentage as inventory psychology
- He measures how much of a prior swing was recovered to reason about trapped/profitable participants—not to invoke a Fibonacci prediction.
- A recovery beyond roughly half the move can substantially reduce a short-holder's profit. Weaker holders may exit before the weekend, while stronger/professional holders may remain.
- The percentage is therefore an inventory clue: **how much profit remains, who is likely to stay, and which stops remain available?**

### Why patterns and breaks fail
- The archive chart showed a trend line and a W-pattern breakout, but continuation failed because seller stops were not available above while buyer stops remained below.
- After buyers entered, their stops were expected below the relevant swing. Price reached that pool and then reversed as sellers could become active again below/around the swing.
- Repeated W patterns or support buys can create fresh buyer stops; the pattern is useful chiefly because it reveals where participants entered and invalidated.
- A break works only when the corresponding stop pool exists. **Pattern geometry is a map of inventory, not an independent cause.**

### Position-building sequence across days
1. Existing positional stops are consumed.
2. With both obvious pools depleted, the market produces a late move/sentiment that induces fresh selling.
3. The next session can gap up against those sellers and consume the newly created stops.
4. A psychological level and later swing crossings attract fresh buyers/sellers, replenishing the sequence.
5. Late option-holder exits and professional/smart-money unwinding can create large momentum candles that are still described as stop-loss/unwinding events.

### Smart-money unwinding — a new mechanism
- He rejects the idea that “smart money” works alone. Another capable counterparty can force it into loss.
- Professional money tends to unwind quickly rather than accept a larger loss; that unwinding can produce a large momentum move.
- He still groups this with stop-loss mechanics, but it is more specific than the earlier crowd-only account: some momentum can be **position liquidation by sophisticated participants**, not merely retail stops.

### Keep permanently
1. **Classify news before acting:** genuinely immediate/unexpected versus anticipated/already discounted.
2. **Only immediate surprise earns directional deference—and even then, keep reading price action.** A headline is not a complete setup.
3. **Translate news into positioning:** who acted, where their stops sit, and which pool remains available?
4. **Patterns do not move the market.** A W, trend-line break or support retest works only when the required stop pool and participation exist.
5. **Use recovery percentage to infer holder psychology, not to predict from a golden ratio.** Ask how much profit remains and who may exit.
6. **Pool creation can span sessions.** A late move can induce fresh positions that the next session's gap targets.
7. **A consumed pool cannot fuel continuation again until participants rebuild.** After one side is taken, identify the next available pool.
8. **Professional unwinding can be momentum fuel too.** “Smart money” has counterparties and may liquidate quickly to cap loss.
9. **Do not preserve archive numbers without instrument, chart anchors and intelligible ASR.** Mechanism survives; levels do not.
