### Core idea
- A **week-in-review** whose real subject is **how to sit through a losing streak without panicking**.
- The week's direction: **first two days up-momentum, the next two days gave it all back** — so *"entry और exit, दोनों decide करते हैं"* whether money is made; a correct entry with a late exit still ends in a loss, and a wrong entry starts bleeding from the first candle.
- The stress is **not** one loss — it is **continuation**: *"loss–profit–loss–profit"* the mind can handle; *"loss–loss–loss"* (or a profit streak) builds panic because covering it looks impossible.
- **Same setup, changing situation:** over the week he sold, lost, sold again, lost, then bought, lost — the **side flipped every day; the rules never changed**. *"मैं कंटिन्यू अपने रूल को फॉलो करता हुआ जा रहा हूं, कहीं पर भी अपने रूल को ब्रेक नहीं कर रहा।"*

### The week, trade by trade
**Mon — SELL (win).** Analysis off Friday's chart: the put-buyers were already in good profit and had been flushed at the close (*"क्लोज़िंग के टाइम उनको निकाला"*), so **the nearby seller is gone → do not target him** → target *"जो गलती से बाइंग करके गए हैं"* (those who wrongly bought). Plan: **gap-up *or* gap-down ⇒ selling.**
- It gap-opened **up** ⇒ he waited (a green candle makes the nearby sellers panic; someone may cut). *"करना तो सेलिंग ही है"* — but not at the resistance (**51,200**) because *"सभी आ जाएंगे"*; he waited for the **breakout candle** and put on it. Premium was cheap during the up-move, so the turn paid well.

**Tue — SELL (loss).** No big two-way momentum, a small range, probability buyers-or-sellers. But the market had broken **51,500** and closed above **51,200** without breaking **51,000** ⇒ **buyers seated, sellers flushed** ⇒ sell (target the buyers). Entered a put on the *closing-price support*; the market stayed volatile, then broke out ⇒ **loss booked.** His read: one of those *"एक-दो मोमेंटम जो आपको समझ आएंगे भी नहीं"* — leave it.

**Wed — BUY (loss).** Market made a strong upside move. Same logic mirrored: after a run **up**, do **not** target the upside sellers — the downside *buyers* are the target. Above **52,000** the gap-down / gap-open plan was **buying**. It gapped slightly down but recovered above 52,000 ⇒ buyers safe. Then resistance at **52,200**; he read it as *"मार्केट केवल रेजिस्टेंस देखने में लग रहा है"* (would break 52,200 → 52,500) and bought a **call** — the market came down ⇒ **loss.**

**Thu — SELL (win).** Two straight losses + a short week ⇒ stress. Chart showed **momentum both sides, weak, closing near support** ⇒ *"बायर और सेलर दोनों ही बैठे हैं"* ⇒ read whose money is bigger / which operator is stronger. Plan by open: **flat-to-gap ⇒ buy** (the up-operator would commit again); **gap-down ⇒ sell** (the down-operator is stronger; and *"रेजिस्टेंस लिया, ब्रेकआउट करने वाला बाइंग कर सकते हैं, ऐसा नहीं कर सकते"* — that buy is only valid on a **flat open**). It gapped **down** ⇒ he refused the buy, waited, and put on the breakdown candle. Small heat, then downside momentum; he waited for the **51,800 breakdown** and booked a **moderate profit** — not the minimum, not the maximum, *"जस्ट हमें जो प्रॉफिट चाहिए होता है."*

### The losing-streak / panic problem
- Alternation vs continuation: *"loss–profit–loss–profit"* gives the mindset time to prepare; *"लगातार लॉस"* does not.
- The recovery trap: *"दो दिन से लॉस चल रहा है, उसको कवर करने की सोचना शुरू कर देंगे... ऐसा आपको कभी भी नहीं करना"* — sitting to **recover two days in one trade** gives back the day that was working.
- What carried the week was **discipline**: he read each day's condition and applied the same framework — no rule was broken on any of the four days.

### The "don't target the side that's already in profit" law (restated)
- Mon gap-up → the already-profitable sellers were flushed ⇒ target the **buyers**.
- Wed up-move → the already-profitable buyers were far/rich ⇒ target the **sellers' SLs** instead.
- Scaffold: the side that is **already in good profit and far away** does not supply your trade; the side that is **trapped** is the target.

### Which operator is stronger — and the open confirms it
- When both sides are seated, the tie-breaker is **whose money is bigger**: *"जो ऑपरेटर बाइंग कर रहा है वो ज्यादा स्ट्रांग है या जो ऑपरेटर सेलिंग कर रहा है"* — and *"वो फिर कहीं ना कहीं ओपनिंग से ही कंफर्म हो पाता है."*
- This week's own conditional: **flat-to-gap ⇒ buy** (the up-operator brought the market up from below and closed near support ⇒ he will commit again); **gap-down ⇒ sell** (the down-operator is the stronger one).
- The *buy-the-resistance-breakout* trade is only allowed on a **flat open** with a clean resistance level (52,000). Once the condition changed to a **gap-down below 52,000**, the same "breakout" must **not** be bought.

### Accept the unexplained momentum; never plan for it
- *"एक-दो बार आपको नॉर्मली ऐसे मोमेंटम मिल जाएंगे जो आपको समझ आएंगे भी नहीं, और मार्केट को भी नहीं पता होता कि मोमेंटम क्यों हुआ"* — leave one or two such losses as *"हमने गलती किया होगा."*
- The one-candle **150–200 point** move: *"ऐसे मोमेंटम को कभी भी ट्राई मत करो... ऐसे मोमेंटम कैप्चर करने का कभी प्लान नहीं बनाना चाहिए"* — rare, up or down, unpredictable.

### Keep permanently
1. Money is made only when **entry *and* exit** are both right — a good entry with a bad exit still ends at a loss; a bad entry bleeds from the first candle.
2. **Continuation is heavier than alternation:** loss–profit–loss is bearable; loss–loss–loss (or a profit streak) builds panic — plan for the streak, not just the trade.
3. **Never try to recover a losing streak in one trade.** Sitting to "cover two days today" hands back the day that was working; book the *needed* profit and leave.
4. **Do not target the side already in good profit / far away.** Find the side that is *trapped* — that is who supplies the SLs.
5. **Keep flipping the side with the market** when it rotates daily; the rules stay fixed even as the side flips.
6. **Which operator is stronger is confirmed by the open** — flat-to-gap can favour the up-operator (buy), a gap-down the down-operator (sell). Use it as confirmation, not a blind trigger.
7. **Don't buy a resistance breakout unless the open supports it** (flat open + clean resistance). After a gap-down below the level, that same "breakout" is a trap, not a buy.
8. **Enter where the crowd is not:** skipping the obvious resistance (everyone sells there) and taking the breakdown/breakout candle beats the "correct" level.
9. **Accept 1–2 unexplainable momentum losses** as mistakes-in-hindsight; don't over-analyse them and don't widen your plan to catch them.
10. **Never plan to capture a single-candle 150–200 point move** — rare and unpredictable, up or down.
11. **Follow the rule even on the losing days** — the week was *"पेनफुल"* yet stayed recoverable because no rule was broken.
