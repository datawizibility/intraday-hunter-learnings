### Core idea
- **The week was good ("यह वीक हमारे लिए काफी अच्छा था"), and the lesson he draws from it is an EXIT lesson, not an entry lesson.** His stated one chosen path: *"जैसे ही अच्छा प्रॉफिट नजर आए एग्जिट करके निकल जाना चाहिए"* — the moment a good profit appears, exit and walk away.
- **The injury the whole session is organised around:** the stop-loss picked off and the market then resuming in the *same* direction — *"मेरा एसल खा गया और फिर मार्केट उसी डायरेक्शन में जा रहा है"*. He names that as the day that hurts most.
- **Second half moves up a level — the analysis layer.** How the week's day-plans were built: which side (buyers or sellers) is seated, and which side's stop-losses are the target.
- **The open type is the trigger, not the plan.** The plan is pre-written for both brackets (gap-up / gap-down / flat); the open tells you which one is licensed.
- **Above the chart sits the seated trader's fear.** *"केवल चार्ट नहीं बोलेगा आपके सामने"* — the chart alone will not tell you; you must read *"ट्रेडर की फीलिंग"* behind the candle.

### Exit discipline — the week's headline lesson
- He has been hurt repeatedly by two shapes: (a) the stop hit and then the market continuing in the original direction; (b) a decent running profit left unbooked that the market then turned into a loss.
- The single fix he has adopted: **book on a good profit and leave.** *"उतना ही आपको आराम मिलेगा ट्रेडिंग के अंदर"* — that is the amount of rest trading will give you.
- Cost of not exiting: *"कुछ ना कुछ अलग ही मिलेगा और जरूरी नहीं वो आपके फेवर में हो"* — the trade goes wrong, or the market gives you something other than what you wanted, and it may not be in your favour.

### How the day-plan is built — where the market spent time
- Focus on the zones where the market spent *time*, not the zig-zag: where it lingered, ask who did more work — buyers or sellers.
- ⚠️ **ASR:** he points at a level spoken as *"235 मिनट का लेवल है अच्छा इंपोर्टेंट लेवल है साइकोलॉजी नंबर है"* ("a 235-minute level, an important level, a psychology number"). If the market *held above* that level, bet that a pool of buyers formed there — and then the market *"उन सभी के एसल वगैरह हिट कर दिए होंगे"* (hit all their stop-losses).
- With that side's stops flushed, the next-day read depends on the open: *"अगर यहां पे बायर बैठे होंगे तो मुझे ओपनिंग से पता लग जाए"* — a gap-up then lets the market run up: *"बायर आएंगे उनको निकालेगा और फिर अपना मोमेंटम देना शुरू कर देगा यह सबसे अच्छा मोमेंटम होता है"*.
- The mirror bracket: if you read *sellers* as the seated side, you need the market to open **gap-down** for them to come into good profit and for the continue-selling target to open.
- So both brackets are pre-written and the open selects — the same chart yields a plan only after you decide which side is seated.

### Continue-trend vs retracement (the week's central read)
- Falling 3–4 days makes retail feel the retracement is "confirmed" — that is the mistake. *"जब तक मार्केट कंटिन्यू ट्रेंड में जा रहा है तो उस ट्रेंड को फॉलो करने की कोशिश करो"*.
- In a continue trend the only question is whether the seated side has been removed: *"उसमें केवल आपको ये देखना है कि सेलर निकल रहा है या नहीं"*.
- **Removed ⇒ keep following the trend**, targeting the other pool: *"सेलर निकल चुका है तो बायर को हमें टारगेट करते हुए कंटिन्यू ट्रेंड को फॉलो करना है"*.
- **Not removed ⇒ a retracement becomes probable ⇒ a different plan.** A leftover seated seller is the cause: *"अगर सेलर बैठा रह गया ना फिर प्रॉब्लम करेगा आपको फिर रिट्रेसमेंट करेगा मार्केट"*.
- The number of down days is not a signal: *"10 दिन गिरे 15 दिन गिरे कोई मतलब नहीं"*, and *"गिरने दो मार्केट को पैसा बनाना है"* — money is made by checking who is still seated, not by counting down days.

### Closing-price psychology (one of the week's tells)
- A close near a support is read as probable buyer work there: *"जब भी किसी सपोर्ट के आसपास क्लोज करता है तो एक प्रोबेबिलिटी होती है कि वहां पर बायर ने काम किया होगा"*.
- He also checks the other angle — sellers can position anywhere (everyone decides per their own situation) — then argues from the *speed* of the fall that sellers did more work, that the market took their stops, and only then concludes *"यहां पे बायर बैठे होंगे इसीलिए हमने अगले दिन क्या प्लान बनाया था कि मार्केट के अंदर हम सेलिंग करेंगे"*.

### The trader's fear — the `49000` Bank Nifty case (the "perfect trade" lesson)
- Bank Nifty showed **no momentum / no side-wedge, just continue selling**, with a level at **49000**. If it breaks down, sellers would sit there; a gap then opens the continue-buy that targets their stops.
- But add the psychology: the market has fallen many days with no retracement — would that many people really be sitting sold, and *"सभी को डर लग रहा है कि कोई रिट्रेसमेंट वगैरह ना हो जाए"*? His line: *"डर दोनों तरफ लगता है"* — fear works both ways: no momentum ⇒ fear; momentum ⇒ fear too.
- Without that point the "perfect" breakdown-continuation buy can simply fail. The read is *"केवल चार्ट नहीं बोलेगा आपके सामने"*; you must read the seated trader — the seller who sold with the market falling fast was himself afraid of a retracement.
- Add it and *"मार्केट में आपको कोई नहीं रोक सकता"* (nobody can stop you in the market) — but it takes time; he adds *"मैं भी सीख ही रहा हूं साइकोलॉजी को"*.
- When is the extra psychology read needed? Intraday charts give you a lot, but watch it around special zones — all-time-high momentum, or a heavy round number like *"50000 का लेवल था बहुत ही ज्यादा इंपोर्टेंट लेवल था"*.

### Preparation before the open (process)
- Do as much work as possible *before* the open: *"जितना काम हम पहले कर सकते हैं उतना पहले कर लें"*, and at trade time do only what must be done at trade time.
- His habit: fully understand the *completed* chart first (support, resistance, momentum, where buyers/sellers sit) so the next chart is quick to read.

### Keep permanently
1. **Exit on a good profit and leave.** After a winner shows a good profit, book it — he names this as his single chosen path after being repeatedly hurt by profits that turned into losses.
2. **Do not volunteer for the worst injury** — the stop hit and then the market resuming in your direction. If the read flips, cut; every plan carries a loss branch.
3. **Read where the market spent TIME**, not the zig-zag. Where it lingered, decide who did more work — buyers or sellers.
4. **Follow a continue trend.** The question is never "how many days has it fallen", but "has the seated side been removed?" Removed ⇒ keep following and target the other pool; not removed ⇒ a retracement is likely ⇒ a different plan.
5. **A leftover seated seller is the cause of a retracement.** 10 or 15 down days mean nothing; check who is still seated before letting the down-count decide for you.
6. **Let the open select the plan.** Decide which side is seated, pre-write both brackets; gap-up / gap-down / flat then tells you which continuation is licensed.
7. **Add the seated trader's fear before trusting a continuation.** Fear is symmetric — no momentum ⇒ fear, momentum ⇒ fear too. If the breakout/breakdown looks too mechanical, ask who is still sitting and afraid.
8. **Use the extra psychology read only at special zones** — all-time-high momentum and heavy round numbers; ordinary intraday charts carry the rest of the day.
9. **A close near a support is a probability of buyer work there** — check the seller angle too, and weigh the speed of the fall.
10. **Understand the completed chart before the open;** do at trade time only what must be done at trade time.
