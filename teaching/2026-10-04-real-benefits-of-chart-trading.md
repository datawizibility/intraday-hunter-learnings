## The core idea

**Trade the chart, not the premium.** The whole session is one durable message: the option premium is an emotion-distraction device, and the only valid trigger is whether the **chart** says the setup is OK.

## The premium traps (both directions)

IH splits traders into two camps and says **neither is right** — you must not become either:

- **The momentum chaser:** premium goes from 100 → 105 → 110 and they jump in because "it's moving, I'll be right." This is a chart-less entry.
- **The discount snatcher:** they wanted a trade at 100, it moved away, and when it comes back to 90 / 80 they grab it because "I'm getting it cheaper." This is *also* a chart-less entry, and it feels safe precisely when the chart may be turning.

Analogy he uses: it does not matter whether the premium is *higher* or *lower* than where you wanted it — **if the chart isn't OK, you don't trade; if the chart is OK, the premium (higher or lower) is irrelevant.**

## Why it matters — emotion control

- If you follow the chart, your **emotions stay controlled**; you can wait for your stop-loss and you can hold a valid trade through a retracement.
- If you follow the premium, you will either **fear missing the trade** (rising premium) or **fear losing the "discount"** (falling premium), and both produce bad decisions.
- The concrete failure: you buy at 110 chasing the move, the premium falls to 100, you panic and cut a trade that the chart never asked you to cut — "you'll be left with a sour face, having bought expensive at 110."

## Worked examples from the week (his own recap)

1. **Two days of big selling (23–25 Sep):** the plan (flat / gap-down → find the sell setup) was right and the market was falling — but the **first candle was huge**. He did **not** enter on it; he waited until the market paused. Only then did the chart say OK, and he took the sell and made a good profit. Whether the premium was up or down was "no matter."
2. **29 Sep:** Nifty and Sensex were fine (selling), but **Bank Nifty's chart had a small problem** — early green/red candles, unclear. "When the chart does not say OK, it does not matter whether the market falls or rises — we wait." He waited until decent selling began, then entered. (Reinforces the multi-index gate: all three must agree.)
3. **30 Sep (flat open, buy):** no fast momentum, gradual move — the chart gave a comfortable entry. "Even if it had run up one candle we'd have waited; because the chart said OK, we worked."
4. **A losing example, stated honestly:** he took a sell, the market broke out, and he took the loss. Hindsight says waiting a little would have captured a big move — **that is the wrong thought.** "Trade where the chart tells you it's OK; after a loss, don't let the market's later move rewrite the decision."

## Team vs solo

- **More people = more mistakes.** Even a full team with all the brainpower will have losing trades; and "the more people you trade with, the more mistakes you'll make."
- The rule: **research and discuss as a team beforehand; execute the trade alone.** "What you see, discuss first, research the chart — but when you actually trade, trade your own way, from your own reading." A team cannot make the real-time decision for you.

## Number of trades / expectation setting

- In option **buying**, not every trade can be right — "however good you are, take 10 trades and some will be wrong, even with a whole team." This is the edge-not-certainty point (Sep 13 teaching) applied to execution frequency.

## Durable lessons minted this session

1. **Follow the chart, not the premium.** Neither chasing a rising premium nor snatching a falling one is a valid entry; only the chart's OK is. (Extends the "premium is a distraction device" rule.)
2. **The chart gate is multi-index.** If even one index (e.g. BN on 29 Sep) is unclear, wait — the market rising or falling does not matter until the chart says OK.
3. **Hindsight after a loss is a trap.** A later big move does not validate a wrong-time entry; judge the decision by the chart at decision time.
4. **Research as a team, execute alone.** Collaboration multiplies opinions and mistakes at entry time; the live decision must be yours.
5. **Expect losing trades in option buying.** A sample of ~10 trades contains errors by construction; process, not a single ticket, is what compounds.
