### Core idea
- **The session is built from the audience's own losing trades.** He had asked for chart screenshots and
  questions; he picked the submissions that shared **common mistakes** and walks the chart of each.
  The stated method is: the mistakes are **small and regular** (*"छोटी-छोटी मिस्टेक"*) — fix the small
  ones and the trading improves.
- Smalls mistakes are diagnosed in three places: **where the position was built** relative to the level,
  **the stock chosen**, and **how the correlated instrument was moving** at the same time.
- The opening is a **self-belief talk**, answering the two questions he says beginners always ask —
  *"will I ever become profitable?"* and *"will I ever recover my losses?"* His answer is not a
  technique: *"आपको अपने पर कॉन्फिडेंस करना है"* — and the two lines he wrote for them:
  *"पानी कभी फीका नहीं होता … आपको हो या न हो, मुझे है आप पर विश्वास."*
- His instruction for the audience's attention: **focus on learning, not on the noise** —
  *"लर्निंग पर ध्यान देना है, इधर-उधर की बातों पर कम ध्यान."*
- Format note: he says one session cannot answer every question, so the chart audits *are* the answer —
  the same mistake keeps reappearing across different people.

### Case 1 — the position was built ON the level, and got hunted
The trader built a position at a level break; his target was around **855** (as heard) and his stop was
hit. His question: *"why did my level take me to the stop-loss?"* He walks the whole chart back.

- **Read the first candle first.** *"पहले फर्स्ट कैंडल का low देख लेना है"* — the first candle's low is
  the anchor for everything that follows. He says the move came after a genuinely **good momentum
  phase** (the instrument is heard as **Sun Pharma**).
- **The trader's thinking error — extrapolating yesterday's momentum.** Near a cleared high, traders
  assume that because the momentum arrived like this yesterday, *"आज भी बैक साइड का मुमेंटम आएगा"* —
  the same back-side momentum will come again today. He calls that **the trader's thinking**, and says
  the market then **reversed the whole thing** from exactly there.
- **The character of the move flipped.** After the level broke and the next point cleared, price that
  had been *buying* into the level started **selling** — and with **no support visible underneath**,
  the trader's expectation of a full reversal became the trap.
- ⭐ **The timing error, stated as a rule:** *"अगर 10:00 बजे से पहले level clear कर देता है, तो सभी
  traders वहाँ आ जाते हैं"* — everybody arrives with their stops ready and builds positions on that
  clear. **Building a position there is not wrong** — what he objects to is building it **without
  checking the momentum the price is making at that moment and how the price behind is moving.**
- ⭐ **And what followed was stop-hunting, in his words:** *"यह केवल और केवल [SL] हुई है और [SL] हंटिंग का
  जादू है"* — followed by **back-to-back green candles**. The move against those positions had no other
  explanation.
- **The fix he offers:** if price had **folded back and then broken** the level, there would have been a
  proper swing entry — and he says he wanted to discuss the swing/no-swing distinction but the video was
  running long.
- **The operational moral:** *"पोज़िशन बनाना मतलब नहीं — देखिए price कैसे मूव कर रहा है और अपनी पोज़िशन
  को उसी तरह handle कीजिए"* — being *in* a position is not the goal; reading the way the price is moving
  and handling the position accordingly is.

### Case 2 — the entry was right, the STOCK was wrong
Second trader: correct timing, correct side, a clean position — **and still stopped**. His diagnosis is
blunt: *"एक की पोज़िशन थी, उसमें कोई कमी नहीं थी… लेकिन उनका stock selection था, उसमें कमी रह गई."*

- **The stock was a two-sided name.** *"यह पूरा का पूरा stock दो साइड मज़बूर कर रहा था"* — the chart was
  pushing both ways (whipsaw), and **on a two-sided stock the stop-loss has a much higher chance of
  being hit.** Avoid those names.
- ⭐ **The rule — morning momentum is spent momentum.** *"जो momentum मॉर्निंग में आ गया, उसके बाद stock
  में कुछ बचता ही नहीं"*: once the morning's move has played out, the larger participants are no longer
  interested, so **the later momentum never comes**. Do not buy a stock for its afternoon move because
  it moved in the morning.
- **What to pick instead:** from the large list available, read and **correlate** — choose names that
  showed a **good trend and a good morning position/momentum**, and where a tradeable structure (a
  high/resistance break, or a level that holds) actually appeared.
- **If you insist on trading a spent/choppy name**, he says you can only do it with an **entry built on
  the retest**, not by joining mid-move — but the cleaner answer is to leave the name alone.

### Case 3 — read the CORRELATED instrument before you build (Nifty vs Wipro)
He deliberately picked this pair because the two are **related**, and told viewers to open the last two
days of **Nifty** and **Wipro** side by side.

- **Wipro's chart:** a flat start, then rally / momentum, then a pullback — *"मुमेंटम करने के बाद fold कर
  फिर से सेलिंग आ सकती है"* — after a good momentum, a fold, and **selling can come again**.
- **Nifty's chart:** *"बिल्कुल उल्टा"* — the opposite. It **opened gap-down** and then **rose**
  (*"गैप डाउन ओपन होने के बाद भी इतना रिवर्सल करने लगा"*), which he says he had flagged to viewers on
  the previous day.
- **The lesson he draws:** when two related instruments disagree in structure, the confirmation has to
  come from the *other* one — check **how the back day moved** before you build a sell (or a buy) in the
  pair member. A sell position is defensible **only if the related instrument's own momentum/back-day
  behaviour supports it**; if the pair is moving the other way, the trade is unconfirmed.
- He also notes Wipro's *"रेस्ट्रिक्टेड सर्विस कंटिन्यू"* / rang-bound behaviour and that after a pullback
  it can sell again — the point being that a **previously good momentum does not persist by itself**;
  each leg has to be re-read.
- **Method note he makes about the example:** the charts are recent **on purpose** — *"मुझे इस stock को
  क्यों चूज़ किया? क्योंकि यह related था"* — because correlation can only be judged on the same days, so
  he had the viewers pull the last two days rather than a distant past period.

### Keep permanently
1. **Placement beats direction.** Being right about the side is worthless if the position is built *on*
   the level with the crowd — check **where** the entry sits relative to the level before the side.
2. **Never extrapolate a completed move.** "It moved like that yesterday, so it will move like that
   today" is named as a thinking error, not a chart error — the market reverses from the point where
   that assumption is most comfortable.
3. **A level cleared early attracts everyone.** When a level clears before **10:00 a.m.**, the crowd
   arrives with stops already placed — the very positions created there become the fuel. Enter on the
   **fold-and-break / retest**, not on the first clear.
4. **Read what the price is doing NOW, not the plan you wrote.** Position-building is not the goal;
   handling the position according to how the price is moving is.
5. **Stock selection is half the trade.** A **two-sided (whipsawing) stock** raises the probability of a
   stop-hit — leave those names alone.
6. **Morning momentum is spent momentum.** If the move has already played out in the morning, the
   participants are no longer interested and the afternoon move will not come. Don't buy a name for the
   move it *already* made.
7. **Correlate before you commit.** For related instruments, read the pair — if the correlated one's
   back-day behaviour and momentum contradict your setup, the trade is unconfirmed.
8. **Re-read each leg.** A previously good momentum does not persist by itself; every leg has to be
   checked again, including the correlated one.
9. **Confidence is a precondition, and it is yours to supply.** Both beginner questions ("will I be
   profitable?", "will I recover?") are answered with self-belief plus learning — not with a technique.
10. **Fix the small, regular mistakes.** The mistakes are small and habitual; that is why they are
    fixable, and why the same ones appear in almost every submitted chart.
