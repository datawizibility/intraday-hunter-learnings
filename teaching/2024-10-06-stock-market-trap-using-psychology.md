### Core idea
- **Traps come in two families:** (A) the ones you can **see forming on the chart**, and (B) the ones built in your **mindset / psychology** — *"दो तरह के होते हैं: एक जो चार्ट के अंदर बनते हुए नज़र आएंगे, और दूसरे जो आपकी साइकोलॉजी को हिट करते हुए बनाए जाते हैं."*
- The session maps the chart traps one by one, then the psychology traps (fear / greed / news), and for each he gives the **avoidance rule**.
- Key framing: **you cannot avoid every trap** — *"70 से 80 पर आप समझ पाओगे, 20 पर कभी-कभी समझ में नहीं आएगा"* (news, internal positions — which strikes have how many calls/puts written); there you must **take the loss**.

### Trap 1 — the sideways / no-momentum trap (साइडवेज़ मोमेंटम)
- Market neither goes up nor down — it does a little and **just sits there**; your profit never forms. *"ना ऊपर जा रहा है, ना नीचे जा रहा है… वहीं पर रुका रहता है."*
- **Why it forms:** above that price level **both buyers and sellers want a big profit and neither wants to commit** — both sides are focused on the breakout/breakdown (*"ब्रेकआउट होगा तो बाइंग करेंगे, ब्रेकडाउन होगा तो सेलिंग करेंगे"*). Since both sit waiting, the market has no need to move — and the waiting crowds lose on the chop.
- **Avoidance:** **trade only after the breakout**, and even then in **small quantity** — treat it as *hit-and-try / trial-and-error*; when it works you land a good trade, when it doesn't the loss is small. He notes that ~**10–15%** of the time the market gives no momentum at all; usually it gives *some* movement — work with that. To size up, add quantity **only after a good breakout**.

### Trap 2 — the fast-momentum (volatile) trap
- Market moves **300–400 points** one way, then **the same speed back** the other way — *"उतना ही तेज़ी से ऊपर, उतना ही तेज़ी से नीचे."* You can't tell what the market actually wants.
- **Sideways vs fast-momentum difference:** in a sideways trap a **patient trader can sit** and defend his position; in a fast-momentum trap the market **cuts your trade and then goes the other way**, and not cutting = an open-ended loss.
- **Why it happens:** the market does this when a side's trader sits thinking *"मैं अपनी पोज़िशन कट नहीं करूंगा"* or *"मुझे तो बड़ा प्रॉफिट करना है, छोटे प्रॉफिट में काम नहीं चलेगा."* The market builds a plan to **flush exactly those** sitting-after-the-breakout big-target traders — small-profit takers are let through.
- **The tell (memorise this):** if **no SL is available** above/below (e.g. *"ऊपर कोई सेलर बैठा नहीं है"*) and the market is **still** continuing in that direction, that is a **dangerous trap** — *"मार्केट में अगर एसएल नहीं है, फिर भी अगर ब्रेकआउट हो गया, तो वो एक खतरनाक ट्रैप हो सकता है."*
- **Avoidance:** **book a decent profit** — *"जैसे 53,500 का ब्रेकआउट… 500 के आसपास अपना बुकिंग करने का प्लान एक्टिवेट कर दोगे."* Do not sit for the huge target; the market will **rotate**, cut the move, and reduce what you had.

### Trap 3 — post-breakout / post-breakdown, and the failure conversion
- After a breakout **or** breakdown the market traps you. The question is **which side's break works and which converts to failure**.
- **The filter — operator position (from the 29-Sep session):** wherever there was a **turning point**, assume the **operator built a position** there (e.g. market rose **400–500 points** from below ⇒ an operator sits at the lower level).
- **Rule:** if the operator is sitting on the **SELLING** side, then **future trades should be mostly SELLING** and — *"जो ब्रेकडाउन होगा उसको सही मानकर चलेंगे; जो मार्केट सपोर्ट लेगा वो गलत है, वो ट्रैप है; जो ब्रेकआउट हो रहा है वो काम नहीं करेगा — फेलियर में कन्वर्ट होगा."*
- This is **live-verifiable on the chart he showed**: price closed below the **53,000** closing price and around the **53,200** level, then broke out above both — but gave **no sustainability**, because the operator is sitting short. Every breakout was a **trap**; every breakdown **worked**. Later a **53,500** breakout also **converted to failure**.

### Trap 4 — the mindset traps (fear & greed)
- **Fear:** after a run of big green candles, you become too scared to buy ("मार्केट इतना ऊपर आ चुका है, अब कितना जाएगा") so you **sell the retracement** — and the market prints small red candles purely to trap you.
- **Greed:** a **sudden breakout / breakdown** appears, looks like a big move, and in reality the market **just revolves in place** — *"लालच देकर मार्केट बहुत ज्यादा ट्रैप बनाता है."*

### Trap 5 — outside triggers (news / results)
- News and results (a positive or negative result coming) are a whole category of traps: *"इस प्रकार की न्यूज़ के कारण भी काफी ज्यादा ट्रैप बनाए जाते हैं."* Watch the **big events**; ignore small news.

### The psychology flip — after a long one-way move the trap side changes
- **His live example (day-specific, late Sep – early Oct 2024):** the market fell hard for **4–5 days**; he had the **selling** plan and it paid (market gave a good downside). He repeated the plan *"छोटे-मोटे गैप अप या गैप डाउन में सेलिंग; बहुत ज्यादा गैप में शायद बाइंग"* — because the closing was right and **the operator had not yet booked**.
- Then came the day he **had to take a BUY**: the market opened a **small gap-down and started taking resistance of the closing price** — *"क्लोज़िंग प्राइस का रेज़िस्टेंस लेके मार्केट नहीं गिर पाएगा"* ⇒ he bought.
- Then the **flip:** after 4–5 days of heavy selling and a **breakdown close**, the total psychology changed — *"सेलर इकट्ठा होना शुरू हो गए"* — so now the operator's game is to **trap the SELLERS**: *"अब ब्रेकडाउन को फेलियर में कन्वर्ट करेगा."* He waits for the tell — **a small breakdown that then rotates** = the game has changed from "breakout traps" to "breakdown traps" (size depends on the opening).
- ⇒ Same structure, **opposite trap side**, because the crowd's positioning flipped. *"जैसे होता हुआ नज़र आए, होने से पहले कुछ नहीं करना; जैसे ही हो जाए, समझ लो गेम चेंज हो चुका है."*

### Keep permanently
1. **Traps split into chart traps and psychology traps** — plan for both; fear and greed are the market's raw material.
2. **Sideways trap:** both sides sitting for the breakout/breakdown ⇒ no momentum. Trade **only after the breakout**, in **small quantity** (hit-and-try); add size only after a good breakout.
3. **Fast-momentum trap:** the same size moves both ways and cuts your trade — it is built for the trader who "won't cut" and wants only the big target.
4. **Tell for a dangerous trap: no SL available on that side yet the market keeps moving** — there is no one to fuel it ⇒ **book a decent profit and exit**.
5. **Don't sit beyond a limit** — especially in **option buying**, where time value forces a timed exit.
6. **Which break works is decided by the operator's side:** if he sits selling, **breakouts convert to failure** and **breakdowns work** (and vice-versa).
7. **Judge a breakout by its SUSTAINABILITY** — a breakout that cannot sustain = a trap.
8. **Last 20% is not readable** (news, internal strike-wise call/put positions) — accept the loss there; don't force a read.
9. **Watch big events, not small news** — results / major news are their own trap category.
10. **After a long one-way move the trap side FLIPS** — sellers gather after days of falling, so **breakdowns (not breakouts) start trapping**; wait for the rotation, then act.
11. **Never get greedy** — market almost always rotates and reduces the profit; a decent booking beats a dream target.
