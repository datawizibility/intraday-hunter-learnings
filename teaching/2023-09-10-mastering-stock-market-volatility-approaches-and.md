### Core idea
- ⭐ **The market itself is changing, so the method must be updated.** Population is rising, options money is harder than it used to be, costs are higher — *"मार्केट उतना ही एडवांस होता जा रहा है"*. You have to **capture the change and identify it**, not trade an old template.
- A breakout says *buy* — but not *buy immediately*. The trade is taken **in proportion / with a retracement** (*"ब्रेक आउट के बाद हम क्या करते हैं बाय करते हैं लेकिन कैसे करते हैं विद प्रोपोर्टेशन"*). If the market does not retrace and you chase a running move, the profit shows briefly and the fall forces the cut.
- The week's ledger, told against himself: **5 trades — 3 profit, 2 loss.** One loss was avoidable and he fixed it on the next trade; the other he simply *missed*.
- ⭐ **The chart does not repeat.** The same shape, one day later, did not produce the same move — *"कभी भी मार्केट आना चार्ट को रिपीट नहीं करता, बहुत ही ज्यादा साइकोलॉजी कुछ अलग चल रहा है"*. Same shape ≠ same outcome, because the psychology underneath is different.
- The week's real deliverable is a **habit, not a trade: start noticing and noting** what the market does — where the stops were taken, where momentum returned. *"पहले आप कहानी पर नोट करना शुरू कर दो"* — first write it down; later you will see it in the chart at a glance.

### Breakout → wait for proportion (the retracement condition)
- One sentence carries the session: *"ब्रेक आउट के बाद बाय करते हैं, लेकिन कैसे करते हैं — विद प्रोपोर्टेशन."*
- The failure mode is named: *"मार्केट का अगर है रिटामेंट ही नहीं करेगा और अगर किसी ने बाइक कर भी लिया ... मार्केट डायरेक्टली भाग रहा है, अच्छा मोमेंटम ... मैं यहां पर एंट्री कर लेट हूं"* ⇒ *"उसके बाद इतना तेजी से मार्केट गिरेगी की उसको अपनी पोषण को लॉस में काटना ही पड़ेगा."*
- ⇒ **A chase entry converts a correct read into a forced cut.** The breakout is not the signal; the *proportional* entry is.
- Pattern completion (head-and-shoulders) and stop-loss hunting are the same story told again — the pattern completes, the trapped side is forced to cut.

### The week — 5 trades, 3 profit, 2 loss (his own ledger)
Sequence as he shows it on the chart: **buy → loss · buy → profit · sell → profit · sell → loss · sell → profit.**

- **Loss 1 (avoidable — and he fixed it).** He bought expecting a *psychology change*: that sellers would average, believing the market had stopped falling, so further downside would pay him. It did not happen — the market converted to selling.
  - His stated cause: *"मार्केट में क्या हो गया — सैलरी में कन्वर्ट हो गया ... यहां पे वायर बहुत ज्यादा प्रॉफिट में ए चुके थे, गैप अभी उन्हें ओपन हो गया"*.
  - ⭐ **The fix, shown on the next trade:** *"अगर सेलिंग ए रहा है तो यहां पे या तो वेट करो या फिर इतना जल्दी से बाय मत करो — कुछ सपोर्ट वगैरा देख कर बाय करो, जैसे क्लोजिंग प्राइस का सपोर्ट देखकर बाय करो."* **Do not buy mid-air; buy at a logical support.**
- **Loss 2 (missed, not fixed).** The same setup appeared again; he sold expecting resistance at the closing price. It did not play out the same way — *"कभी भी मार्केट चार्ट को रिपीट नहीं करता"*. He flags it himself as *"मिस हो गया"* and says he could have avoided it **if he had remembered** that the previous day had also fallen from the closing-price resistance.
- ⇒ The two losses are the same lesson seen twice: **one is about entry location (support), one is about memory (notice the repetition of the closing-price reaction, and note it).**

### The fix he actually shows — wait for the level, then sell into it
- On the corrected trade he *"वेट किया की थोड़ा बहुत ऊपर मिलेगा तभी जाकर मैं शेर[सेल] करूं"* — because there was a ceiling above and a sell at the open would be stopped as the market pulled up.
- The rule as he states it: if you cannot get the trade where you want it, do it **around the sitting resistance**; on a big gap-up, the sell comes at the resistance.
- The confirmed version: *"मार्केट शैली में आया ... अगर गैप अप ज्यादा ओपन होता है ... आराम से यहां पे सेल किया जा सकता है"*, then *"जैसे रिजेक्शन नजर आया ... उसकी अकॉर्डिंग ट्रेड बनाए और मेरा प्रॉफिट बन गया."*

### The mistake he did NOT fix — averaging into a big candle
- *"मार्केट के अंदर इतना बड़ा लगा ये कैंडल देख कर तो मैंने यहां पे एवरेज भी कर लिया था कुछ क्वांटिटी"* — on seeing a big candle he **added quantity** to a losing position.
- ⚠️ He reports it as his own error, **not as a technique**. This sits directly on the corpus's unresolved averaging thread (see Evolution).

### “Chart मत repeat करता” — the 0.61 retracement example
- He used a Fibonacci retracement of a prior swing: when the market **retraces ~0.61**, the upside is treated as finished — *"लगभग यहां पर 0.6% तक मार्केट अगर रिप्लेसमेंट करता है तो मैं ये मानता हूं की अपार साइड हमें ऐसे वगैरा नहीं मिलते."*
- With a **small gap-down open** and the closing price acting as resistance, the sell was clean and profitable.
- The next day the shape looked the same — and the market fell from the closing-price level **differently**. The repeat failed. ⇒ **A formed shape is not a promise.**
- His instruction: *"जो भी बातें होती है कम से कम उनको याद रखो ... कहानी पर नोट करके छोड़ दो की मार्केट के अंदर क्या-क्या हुआ"* — **Notice → note → eventually you see it instantly.**

### The round-number / repeated-price logic
- *"मार्केट में सबसे इंपॉर्टेंट कम क्या किया है — 45000 की ऊपर गया, 200 के नीचे ... क्लोजिंग हुआ."*
- ⭐ **A repeated price becomes a level even when it is not a round number.** *"45 218 219 200 — तो ये भी इतना ही कम हो रहा है ... वैसे तो राउंड नंबर नहीं है, लेकिन बहुत ज्यादा इसका उसे किया जा रहा है मार्केट के लिए, तो मैं इसको भी कंसीडर कर लेट हूं."*
- His condition for the buy-after-gap move: *"मोमेंटम ऑक्साइड का आना चाहिए"* — the momentum has to actually arrive.

### Keep permanently
1. **After a breakout, buy with proportion** — wait for the retracement; a chase entry turns a correct read into a forced cut.
2. **The chart does not repeat.** Same shape, different psychology — most of the time the market will not print the same move again; if there is news, drop the shape argument entirely.
3. **A repeated price becomes a level** — even a non-round number (≈45,200 spoken repeatedly) is treated as important because it is repeated, not because it is round.
4. **When selling is on, do not buy quickly.** Either wait, or buy only at a logical support — closing-price support, or a formed pattern support; never mid-air.
5. **Notice, then note.** Where stops were taken, where momentum returned — write the day down. Missing it three times is normal; the point is that it eventually becomes visible at a glance.
6. **Never buy expecting a psychology change to bail you out** — hoping sellers will average (so your buy pays) is a guess, not a setup.
7. **The market evolves; the method must be re-captured** — options are harder and costlier than they were; identify the change instead of re-running an old template.
8. **Do not average a loser on the look of one big candle** — he did it here and flags it as his own error, not a technique (⚠️ the corpus's averaging question stays open).
