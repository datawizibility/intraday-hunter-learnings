### Core idea
- This is the week's **plan-and-execution diary** read back loud: he takes his own five decisions of the week (Friday plan → Monday → Tuesday → Wednesday → Friday) and shows the plan, the open, the trade and the result for each.
- The engine under every decision is one question: **who is seated, and whose stop-losses are therefore available?** The chart is only the evidence used to answer it — *"नंबर से देखोगे, कैंडल से देखोगे, मोमेंटम को समझोगे"*.
- Momentum is classified into three shapes before any decision: **continuation** momentum, **retracement** momentum, or **sideways** momentum — *"मोमेंटम हुआ है तो कंटिन्यू हुआ है या फिर रिट्रेसमेंट के साथ मोमेंटम हुआ है या फिर साइडवेज मोमेंटम हुआ है"*. Each shape implies a different next move.
- **No plan is a decision too.** He shows a full day of a gap-up open where he deliberately did *not* trade, and explains both reasons why. Not working is part of the method, not a lost session.
- **Both sides, always.** *"कभी भी जब आप ट्रेड बनाते हो तो एक तरफ का डिसाइड नहीं करते।"* He trades intraday specifically because both the trend *and* its retracements have to be captured — *"हम कोई ट्रेंड को फॉलो नहीं करते।"*
- A correct read still pays for itself in losses sometimes: the option premium, the clock, and the news are separate from the direction call. Capital preservation sits above being right.

### Reading the chart: who is seated (numbers, candles, momentum)
- The operator built the chart — *"चार्ट तो ऑपरेटर ने बनाया। यह तो क्लियर है।"* — so the read is not "what did price do" but **what did the operator intend by drawing it**.
- His worked example: the market went up but did not cross a level, so the question becomes *"क्या वो मार्केट को गिराना चाहता है या फिर और ऊपर जाने नहीं देगा? या फिर जस्ट इसने एक साइकोलॉजी क्रिएट किया ताकि दूसरे लोग सेल करें या फिर दूसरे लोग यहां पर बाय ना कर पाए।"* — three readings, and the plan differs by which one is true.
- The evidence hierarchy he gives is deliberately mechanical: **numbers → candles → momentum shape**. No indicator enters the description.

### The gap-up refusal (why an entire plan was skipped)
Two explicit reasons given for writing **no plan at all** on a gap-up open:
1. **The seated side is already in profit.** *"पहली बात तो क्या होता है? गैप अप में यहां पे जो बाइंग करके बैठे हैं वो प्रॉफिट में आ जाएंगे।"* Buyers sitting in profit are not a target — they are the crowd that survives.
2. **The chart would have to repeat.** *"नॉर्मली चार्ट वगैरह रिपीट मार्केट के अंदर नहीं होते"* — the prior move was a continuation sell at the same price level; a gap-up that then fell would draw the identical chart, which the market does not normally do.

- His contrast is the counterfactual he kept repeating: *"अदरवाइज अगर फ्लैट टू गैप डाउन ओपन होता ना तो थोड़ा बहुत अगर मार्केट ऊपर भी जा रहा होता ना फिर भी हम सेल कर लेते।"*
- Why flat-to-gap-down is the *preferred* open in that week's situation: rejection is more likely to appear there than in a gap open — *"एज़ कंपेयर टू अगर गैप वगैरह ओपन होगा तो वहां पे रिजेक्शन कम आने के चांसेस रहेंगे।"*
- The gap-up was not treated as a blanket veto though — in the Wednesday case a gap-up was exactly the **buy** trigger. The refusal is specific to the case where the seated side is in profit and the chart would repeat.

### The two outcomes the market can produce
- After sellers are driven out, his branch list is deliberately only two options — *"अगर सेलर भाग गए होंगे या तो मार्केट क्या करेगा? सेलर के एसएल क्रिएट करेगा फिर ऊपर जाएगा या फिर बायर को टारगेट करेगा। दो ही ऑप्शन होते हैं मार्केट के पास।"*
- Corollary he draws: the target is chosen **by who is still exposed**, not by the direction of the last candle.

### Sideways momentum as an eviction tool
- This is the session's sharpest mechanism. After a positive momentum, the market goes sideways — and sideways is not neutral, it is **work**: it removes the buyers who are sitting in profit without needing to break the price they bought at.
- *"जो भी यहां पे बाइंग वगैरह करके बैठे थे उनको मार्केट ने साइडवेज मोमेंटम देकर निकाला।"*
- The mechanism as he tells it: a floating profit of *"₹1 लाख"* stops growing, then wobbles — *"कभी 90 हो रहा है 80000 हो रहा है कभी ₹10000 हो रहा है"* — patience breaks, the trade is cut, and the buyer is out. The illustration is not a P&L claim.
- The order of operations therefore: **momentum → sideways eviction → next momentum**. He refuses the expectation of a straight line — *"तो डायरेक्टली कभी भी ऐसे नहीं कि कोई मोमेंटम होता ही रहेगा।"*
- And when others start buying, the market must manufacture new stops — *"दूसरे लोग अगर बाय वगैरह करना शुरू करेंगे उनकी साइकोलॉजी को चेंज करने के लिए उनको भगाने के लिए मार्केट फिर से कुछ ना कुछ मार्केट ट्रैप वगैरह बना देगा।"*
- Practical consequence he states: since momentum is the only thing paid for, **one momentum captured is a complete trade** — *"एक मोमेंटम कैप्चर करके हमने निकले।"*

### Entry timing: the closing price, the resistance above, and the 3:00 window
- The market taking the **closing price as a resistance right from the open** is read as an operational event, not noise: *"मार्केट ने ओपन होते ही क्लोजिंग प्राइस का रेजिस्टेंस लेना शुरू कर दिया।"* He waited for the breakout of that resistance and only then sold.
- The conditional gate he attached to the gap-up sell plan: *"जब तक इस हायर पॉइंट के आसपास यहां पे एक रेजिस्टेंस लगा हुआ था। तो मार्केट अगर इस पॉइंट के ऊपर नहीं जाएगा तो मार्केट के अंदर हम सेल वगैरह कर सकते हैं।"* — if it had opened above that point, there was no plan.
- The late window: momentum arriving after 3:00 catches the crowd off guard — *"3:00 बजे के बाद जब मोमेंटम आते हैं तो उसमें ज्यादा लोग फोकस कर नहीं पाते।"* That is why the fast late selling left trapped buyers behind.
- This is also why the fast late rejection became next-day fuel: *"इवन यहां पे जो बाइंग वगैरह करके बैठे होंगे वो ट्रैप हो गए होंगे। उनका पोजीशन मार्केट के अंदर होल्ड हो सकता है।"*

### Why a correct read can still lose (option layer + news)
- Loss is attributed to two things that are **outside the direction call**:
  - the **option instrument**: *"वो लॉस इसलिए हो पाता है क्योंकि हम ऑप्शन में काम करते हैं।"* — time works against the position, and premium does not always follow the index even when the direction is right: *"जो दूसरे फैक्टर होते हैं ऑप्शन के वो आपका साथ नहीं देते।"*
  - the **news layer**: *"एवरीडे कुछ ना कुछ ऐसा न्यूज़ आता रहता है जिस वजह से मार्केट में इंपैक्ट आपको देखने को मिल जाएगा।"*
- Because the SL is intraday-sized and small, a correct direction with a wrong micro-timing still ends as a booked loss — he accepted that as structural, not as a mistake: *"कभी-कभी ट्रेड आपको लगता है सही जाने वाला होता है फिर भी उसमें लॉस हो जाता है।"*

### Keep permanently
1. **Read the chart as an instruction, not a history** — numbers first, then candles, then momentum shape; ask what the operator intended by drawing it, and hold the three candidate intentions open until the plan separates them.
2. **Classify the momentum before deciding:** continuation, retracement or sideways. Each shape implies a different next move, and the decision follows the shape — not the last candle.
3. **Sideways is an eviction tool, not neutral time.** The market does not have to break a buyer's entry price to remove him — a wobbling floating profit removes him. Plan for the re-momentum after the sideways, not during it.
4. **A side already in profit is not a target.** If the seated side got its gap and is comfortable, the fuel underneath is gone — refuse the open rather than force the setup.
5. **A gap-up is not a direction.** It is a condition that voids one plan and can confirm another; the same week refused it for selling and accepted it for buying. Write the plan by the seated side, not by the gap's sign.
6. **Never pre-commit a side.** *"कभी भी जब आप ट्रेड बनाते हो तो एक तरफ का डिसाइड नहीं करते।"* Trend-following is not the mandate; capturing the trend *and* its retracements is, which is exactly why he works intraday.
7. **Gate the gap plan on a named point.** If the market opens above the reference resistance, there is no plan — waiting costs nothing, and the trade only becomes valid when the condition is met.
8. **Watch the open's relationship to the previous close.** The market using the closing price as resistance from the first minute is an operational event: wait for that resistance to break before acting on the day's direction.
9. **One momentum is a complete trade.** Capturing a single momentum and exiting is the finished unit of work — not a premature exit.
10. **Separate the read from the instrument.** A correct direction can still be a booked loss because of option time/premium and the news layer; size the intraday stop accordingly, protect capital, and take break-even as the first milestone before expecting profit.
