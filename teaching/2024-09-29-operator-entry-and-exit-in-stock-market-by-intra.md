### Core idea
- Three things about the **operator**: **how he enters**, **how you benefit from the entry**, and **how/when he exits** — *"आज हम ऑपरेटर की तीन बातें करने वाले हैं, जिसको जानने के बाद मार्केट काम कैसे करता है समझने में हेल्प होगी."*
- The whole session is about **locating the operator's parked money** — entry at the turning point, staying on his side until he exits, and never reading an **expiry-day** chart like a normal one.

### Retail vs operator capital (why retail never makes the momentum)
- Retail with **₹5 lakh** behaves like it is trading **6–8 lakh** of size — *"5 लाख का कैपिटल है तो 6-8 लाख के कैपिटल के अकॉर्डिंग काम करता रहता है."* Small capital can't move anything.
- Push more size and **psychological pressure** enters: as soon as one **opposite candle** forms, retail exits — so *"कभी भी रिटेल के अकॉर्डिंग मोमेंटम नहीं होता."*
- The **operator breaks his capital into UNITS** — a 10-lakh unit, a 20-lakh unit, a 50-lakh unit — and keeps **continuing to add per requirement, second-by-second / micro-second**, until the momentum he wants arrives. *"जब तक उसके अकॉर्डिंग मोमेंटम मार्केट के अंदर ना आ जाए"* — never the full capital at once.

### Where the operator ENTERS (and where he does NOT)
- **You cannot find his entry in the middle of the market** — *"अगर हम बीच में ढूंढेंगे, ऑपरेटर यहाँ बैठ गया होगा वहाँ बैठ गया होगा, तो हम समझ नहीं पाएंगे."*
- Operator entry = at a **TURNING POINT**: *"जब भी मार्केट के अंदर कोई टर्निंग पॉइंट होगा"* — wherever the market **turned** after a strong move, assume a position was built there (it was not "people like us" who turned it).
- The map: market rising from below ⇒ there are **buyers sitting at the lower level**; market falling from above ⇒ the operator built his position at the **upper level**. *"जो अपर साइड का लेवल होगा, वहाँ पे ऑपरेटर ने अपना पोज़िशन बना रखा होगा."*
- Entry mechanics on the chart: operator decides *"market must not cross this level"* ⇒ starts placing units ⇒ **green-to-red candles** begin forming ⇒ buyers thin out ⇒ a **down-side momentum** ⇒ **chain reaction**: buyers' SLs get flushed, down-side buyers book profit, fresh sellers add — and the operator takes his benefit gradually.

### The bigger operator above (operators fight operators, not retail)
- There is **always a bigger operator above and a smaller operator below**: *"ऑपरेटर के ऊपर भी बड़ा ऑपरेटर होता है और नीचे भी छोटा ऑपरेटर."*
- If the bigger operator's fund is **stuck or busy elsewhere**, the smaller one gets to make his money. But if the bigger one **arrives**, he will **flush the smaller one** — *"इसका एलिट कर देगा"* — because with far more money, only flushing him delivers the benefit.
- ⇒ **No operator builds a position for retail.** *"डायरेक्टली हमारे जैसे लोगों के लिए कोई भी ऑपरेटर अपनी पोज़िशन नहीं बना आता; ऑपरेटर आपस में लड़ते रहते हैं."* *"हमारे लिए कोई काम नहीं कर रहा; मार्केट में दूसरे लोग अपने-अपने लिए काम करते हैं, हम तो बीच में आ रहे हैं."*
- The one concession: the market **does** catch psychology sometimes — it knows many people are working at a level, so it gives a **retracement** so the operator can maximize his benefit.

### Benefiting from the entry — stay on the operator's side until he exits
- If the operator is sitting on the **SELLING** side, then this week's / this month's positions should be **mostly SELLING** — because the operator is taking the market **down overall**, so the benefit is on the sell side. *"ऑपरेटर कौन बैठा है — सेलिंग करके बैठा है… तो बेनिफिट सेलिंग करने पे ज्यादा मिलेगा."*
- **Support below a sitting seller is a TRAP.** If the market takes support (e.g. at 54,000) and closes, it looks like it can go up — but *"अगर यहाँ पे सेलिंग करके ऑपरेटर बैठा है तो ये ट्रैप ही होगा."* Take the probability on the **selling** side **as long as he is sitting there** — until he **books profit / exits**.

### Where the operator EXITS — normally after a BREAKDOWN
- *"एक्ज़िट नॉर्मली जो भी ऑपरेटर होता है वो ब्रेकडाउन के बाद ही करता है, क्योंकि ब्रेकडाउन के बाद साइकोलॉजी चेंज हो जाता है."*
- **After exit he turns buyer — but not directly.** He will **hold the market around the old level** (so it looks like *resistance*), do a **retracement**, then the next day either **gap-up open** or take it up from there.
- **Exit tell (the one to memorise):**
  - Market **gap-down open → comes back up → stops/holds at the old level = "resistance"** ⇒ he **exited** + started buying ⇒ **future trades are BUY-side**.
  - Market gives a **direct gap-up with no resistance behaviour** ⇒ the seller **has not booked yet** ⇒ the market will spend more time and go **down later** ⇒ **keep selling**.
- Also: **small up-down movements after a breakdown** (*"नीचे से ऊपर, नीचे से ऊपर"*) can be the operator **quietly removing quantity** — a slow exit. Confirmation still comes only when the next day **closes with resistance**.

### Expiry-day rule (a separate chart)
- *"एक्सपायरी दिन आपको एक गलती नहीं करना है"* — do not read an **expiry-day chart** the same way.
- On expiry, **puts/calls expire**: an operator who **bought puts** and dragged the market down, closing **directly at the low with no hold**, has **no need to book** — the position **auto-expires**. So the *next day the market can start going up directly*, and you must **not** expect the normal "resistance-hold" exit trace.
- ⇒ *"एक्सपायरी का चार्ट अलग से देखा करो; अगर उसको भी मैच कर लोगे तो बात नहीं बनेगी."*

### His live example — the Sep-2024 expiry / holiday sequence (day-specific)
- The expiry-week chart: market started **up from below with little momentum** ⇒ the read is someone did a **PUT WRITE** (a big momentum would have meant **call buying**). A written put **needs no booking** — it expiries automatically ⇒ the market looks **straight up**.
- Then he watched for the trap: **on the expiry day the operator zeroed his put-write** (position auto-expired); the **next day the market troubled the sellers**; the **day after (Friday)** the operator **built his position** — *"ऑपरेटर नॉर्मली हॉलीडे को ज्यादा यूज़ करता है, ताकि दूसरे लोग काम न कर पाएं"* (a 2-day holiday scares retail out of holding positional trades, while the operator sits down with a trade on **Friday**).
- He had **pre-announced** (in his live/analysis session) that an operator position could be here and a **54,000 breakdown** could come on Friday; the market **did not break 54,000**, so he **did not trade** — but the *read* was confirmed.
- ⇒ *"दिन के हिसाब से, टाइम के अकॉर्डिंग सब कुछ समझ कर चलोगे तो समझ में आएगा कि कहाँ-कहाँ कैसे ऑपरेटर काम करा होगा."*

### Keep permanently
1. **The operator's entry is at a TURNING POINT** — never look for it mid-chart; assume a position wherever the market turned after a strong move.
2. **Directional map:** market rising from below ⇒ **buyers sit at the lower level**; market falling from above ⇒ **the operator sits at the upper level**. Read the side he is parked on.
3. **Operators scale in UNITS, continuously** — they add per requirement until their momentum appears; they never drop the whole capital at once.
4. **Operators fight operators, not retail** — a bigger operator **flushes** the smaller one; **no operator builds a position for you**. You are in the middle; plan accordingly.
5. **Stay on the operator's side until he exits** — a seller sitting above ⇒ take the **selling-side probability**; a support below a sitting seller is a **trap**.
6. **The operator exits after a BREAKDOWN** (psychology flips); he then turns buyer.
7. **Exit tell:** gap-down → back up → **"resistance" hold at the old level** ⇒ he **exited** and is buying ⇒ **buy-side trades**. A **direct gap-up without resistance** ⇒ he has **not booked yet** ⇒ **keep selling**.
8. **Never read an EXPIRY-day chart like a normal one** — a written/bought option expiring leaves **no booking trace**; the next day can go straight up. Study expiry charts separately.
9. **The operator uses holidays** (Friday ahead of a 2-day holiday) to build positions while retail is scared to hold — expect positioning then.
10. **Track big timing events** — expiry day, holidays, major news — not small news; *"टाइम देखना बहुत ज्यादा ज़रूरी है."*
11. **A confirmed read with no trigger is still a win** — he had the read right and simply **didn't trade** because 54,000 never broke; the process is the scorecard.
