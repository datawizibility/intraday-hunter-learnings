### Core idea
- The operator's position is identified by **distance**. Whenever he builds a position he must push the market away from it — *"अगर ऑपरेटर यहाँ पे पोज़िशन बनाए तो या तो 200/300 पॉइंट मार्केट ऊपर लेकर जाएगा या फिर 200/300 पॉइंट नीचे"* — he **never** lets the market hold where the position is being built.
- Therefore: **distance at the close ⇒ operator present; no distance (close back inside the same range) ⇒ NO operator position**, only retail.
- Two market states, two different answers: in a **sideways** market the operator builds **no** position and you trade the *retail* side that got activated; in a **trending** market the distance proves he is seated, and you keep working **by his level** until the market stops holding there.
- Zero-sum framing: *"नेट सम-ज़ीरो गेम होता है"* — one side's money is the other's. When retail activates on one side, the operator takes the other.

### The two root-value points (put them in the notes first)
1. **Distance is mandatory** — 200/300/400 points one way; a breakout/breakdown is not the operator's move until it is followed by distance.
2. **He never works in a fast-running market** — *"ऑपरेटर कभी भी भागती हुई मार्केट में पोज़िशन नहीं बनाता."* With his quantity he cannot fill at speed, so **breakout/breakdown are retail territory**; he waits for a buyer/seller **activation** (double bottom, retracement, breakout after a multi-day run).

### Closing time — the operator is NOT there
- At the close the market stops; *"क्लोज़िंग के टाइम ऑपरेटर अपनी पोज़िशन बना सकता है?"* → **no**, there is no momentum to build into.
- **Exception (his honesty clause):** sometimes the last **15–20 min** (or 30–40) print a **double/triple momentum** spike — then the operator is visible, but his footprint sits **near the levels**, never at the day's extremes: *"नीचे के लेवल के ऊपर, ऊपर के लेवल के … आपको नहीं ढूँढना है."*

### Sideways market — no operator position, trade the activated retail side
- Range worked, no distance, closed back inside ⇒ *"ऑपरेटर है ही नहीं"* in that range. He only nudged the market to build a psychology / hit stops; the positions are **retail's**.
- Then read **which retail side got activated**:
  - The range worked and closed **down** (came up, broke down, closed back near the lows) ⇒ **sellers seated** ⇒ **target the sellers** ⇒ build a **BUY plan** — on gap-up AND gap-down, *"कोई बड़ा गैप डाउन भी ओपन होता है तब भी हम बाइंग कर सकते हैं"*.
  - The range worked and closed **up** (e.g. a double bottom forming) ⇒ **buyers seated** ⇒ **target the buyers** ⇒ build a **SELL plan** — on gap-up, gap-down or flat.
- Worked July references: the plan built only from this read (his analysis sessions around **10 / 11 / 12 July**); small gap-down seen, he waited for the **closing-price** resistance, read that the market would target the sellers, traded and took a good profit.

### Trending market — the distance proves he is seated
- Momentum WITH distance ⇒ the operator has built a position; from then on work **by his level** (*"जब तक ये होल्ड करा था तब हम इस ऑपरेटर के अकॉर्डिंग काम कर रहे थे"*).
- **Continuation without retracement** ⇒ he never needed to remove the other side ⇒ he gradually **books profit** and retail adds in — the market is then prone to come back.
- **Continuation WITH retracement** (the other side got removed) ⇒ the market **holds** at his level.
- If the market **stops holding** at his level and starts holding at a new place ⇒ **shift the focus** upward/downward to whoever sits at the new level; that new level is the working operator.

### Fast momentum — who can be on the other side?
- In a very fast one-way move (the worked example broke **≈52,000**) retail has no courage to buy the turn ⇒ *"बाय तो यहाँ पे कौन करेगा? मार्केट घुमाने वाला — ऑपरेटर ही."*
- If he could not get his full quantity, he brings the market back, **adds quantity** and runs the momentum again — so a fast move followed by a return to the level is the operator adding, not a failure.

### Keep permanently
1. **Operator position = distance.** If the market did not move ~200–400 pts away from where he acted, no position was built there.
2. **No distance at the close ⇒ no operator** in that range — only retail positions; read which side is activated and trade *that* side.
3. **Never expect the operator at the close**; a late-session spike is his footprint **near the levels**, never at the day's extremes.
4. **He never works in a fast-running (breakout/breakdown) market** — retail does; he waits for an **activation** (double bottom / retracement / breakout after a multi-day run).
5. **Net-sum-zero:** retail activation on one side = the operator on the other.
6. **Sideways market → trade the activated retail side:** closed **down** ⇒ sellers seated ⇒ **buy plan** (gap-up and gap-down); closed **up** ⇒ buyers seated ⇒ **sell plan** (gap-up, gap-down, flat).
7. **Trending market → distance proves he is seated;** keep working **by his level** while the market holds there.
8. **Continuation without retracement ⇒ market prone to come back** (he books profit); continuation **with** retracement ⇒ market **holds** at his level.
9. If the market stops holding at his level and holds at a new one, **shift focus** to the new level — that is the working operator now.
10. **Profit or loss doesn't matter; knowledge does** — an understood loss beats an unexplained profit (and don't deploy full capital while a setup is still being learned).
