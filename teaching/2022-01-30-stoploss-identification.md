### Core idea
- **Stop-loss identification sits *inside* a projection** — so it cannot be learned or taught in one sitting. He deliberately splits it across videos: *"इसको हम एक दिन में सीखना और सिखाना दोनों ही मुश्किल रहेगा, तो मैं आपको थोड़ा-थोड़ा करके इसको सिखाता रहूंगा"* — little by little, so attention also holds.
- **To find a stop-loss you must first understand market psychology.** Until you know how people actually work in the market you cannot find *their* stop-losses: *"तब तक आप उनके स्टॉपलॉस नहीं फाइंड आउट कर पाओगे"*.
- **The stop-loss you are hunting is always someone else's**, and it exists because of a habit: whoever wants to sell wants to sell at the **top**; whoever wants to buy wants to buy at the **bottom**.
- **The first control is support / resistance, read off the same chart:** a swing **low** works like support, a swing **high** works like resistance. He had charts of Nifty *and* Bank Nifty open, both marked, and everything in the session is read against those references.
- **Everything is explained through the SL lens**, not the chart pattern: why the breakdown happened, and why the resistance-to-breakout move **reversed**. Pattern names (breakout / breakdown) are secondary to *whose stops were sitting there*.

### Psychology first: whose stop-loss is where
- Seller's habit → wants the **top**; buyer's habit → wants the **bottom**. That habit is what places orders — and therefore what places stop-losses.
- After an **upside momentum**, the two indices stopped in different places: **Nifty folded near its support**, **Bank Nifty folded near its resistance**. Same tape, two different references — so two different SL pools.
- **Buy side:** buyers did *not* go to Nifty, which had already run a long way — *"निफ्टी आलरेडी इतना भाग चुका है"* — because little room was left in it. They went to **Bank Nifty**, which was sitting near its support, so an upside move had more room. The position therefore formed in the laggard-at-support, not in the mover.

### The two demonstrations (breakdown, and the reversed breakout)
**Why the breakdown happened (sell-side pool)**
- The sell side did not distribute itself evenly: those who wanted to sell the index **sold Nifty rather than Bank Nifty** (ASR sentence is garbled — see the fidelity section below; the reading is "one index carried the selling, the other did not").
- He **cropped matching candles from both indices** (the ASR timestamps read "09:58" and later "09:23" — treat as approximate) precisely to show the fall landing in one index while the other did not fall the same way.
- His mechanical point: the market wants to move down, but it needs fuel to do it — so it works **at the level where your stop-loss is sitting**, and the smart side collects it there so that **it gets the benefit and the momentum** from it. Stop-losses are the fuel, not a side-effect.

**Why the move out of resistance reversed**
- His verdict: **that was not actually a breakout.** *"यह ब्रेक आउट नहीं हुआ है, असल में यह केवल और केवल इनकी स्टॉपलॉस"* — it was **only and only** the stop-losses of traders parked above the resistance (the sellers who followed the top-selling habit), i.e. the print came from stops being run, not from fresh demand arriving.
- Because those stops were the *whole* content of the move, it had nothing behind it: it **reversed**.
- Same logic applied to the long side: those who had built **put positions** had their stop-loss eaten by the market, and the resulting move **again was only and only stop-loss momentum** — no participation of its own.
- He also reads order queues off the chart: traders **waiting for a retest** at a horizontal line he drew were exactly the group whose stop-loss got hit there — *"कुछ यहां पर बाय कर रहे थे, उनका स्टॉपलॉस यहां तो गया"*.
- The one line that governs the whole session: ***"बिना स्टॉपलॉस के मार्केट नहीं चल पाएगा"*** — the market cannot travel without stop-losses.

| Reference (as spoken) | Whose habit sits there | What the market does with it |
|---|---|---|
| Swing low → support (Nifty, Bank Nifty both marked) | Buyers who want the bottom | Position forms near it; that buying becomes the next SL pool to be hunted |
| Swing high → resistance | Sellers who want the top (short above the level) | A poke above the level runs their stops; the "breakout" is really their buying — no real demand follows, so it reverses |
| A horizontal line where traders **wait for a retest** | Pending retest-buyers / retest-sellers | Their entries and their stops both sit on one line — a ready-made pool |
| Resistance after the failed poke | Sellers who could not sell the other index earlier | They come and sell *here* → their SLs become available → market reverses immediately |

### Why Bank Nifty did not run with Nifty (the recurring mistake)
- The up-move was a **momentum nobody could follow** — *"एक ऐसा मुमेंटम था कि कोई फॉलो नहीं कर पाया"*. Had even a small reversal come, someone would have taken profit, and that itself would have set off a further reversal, because **Bank Nifty still looked weak**.
- The behaviour he keeps calling out: **when one index has run a long way, traders avoid the running index and buy the one that already looks weak.** His words are blunt — *"यह बहुत बार मैं आपको बताता हूं, आप फिर भी एक गलती करते मिलोगे, मुझे पता है"*. Buying the weak one instead of entering the strong one is named as the mistake, not a technique.
- He insists this was said **live**, not after the fact: *"ऐसा नहीं कि मैं चार्ट बनने के बाद आपको बता रहा हूं"* — and that once a position is on, his job is only to take it out at a loss or at a profit.
- The reversal-run he highlighted: those who **could not sell in Bank Nifty** came and sold in the other index — *"जिसने बैंक निफ्टी [में बेचना] नहीं करना था, वह यहां आकर छोड़ कर रहा है"* — which **made their stop-losses available**, and the moment the market knew that, it **reversed immediately** and a good upside momentum ran.
- Repeated rungs of "SL becoming available" produced repeated reversals in the same stretch. His explanation for the persistent sentiment: it was the **Budget scene** — the expectation that the market would go up kept pulling positions in, again and again, at those references.

### Keep permanently
1. **The market cannot move without stop-losses** (*"बिना स्टॉपलॉस के मार्केट नहीं चल पाएगा"*) — momentum is fuelled by stops; read *which side's SLs are still available* before reading direction.
2. **The stop-loss you hunt is always someone else's** — sellers want the top, buyers want the bottom; that habit is what positions the SLs you will later trade against.
3. **Support/resistance first, from the same chart:** a swing **low** = support, a swing **high** = resistance. SL reading happens *relative to these references*, never in isolation.
4. **An apparent breakout above resistance may be nothing but the stop-losses of sellers parked above it** — a print made only of stops has no participation behind it and reverses. Confirm real demand arrived before calling it a breakout.
5. **SLs are consumed in order:** once a side's stops have been run, the market has already moved on — ask which pool is *still* available, not which one just got taken.
6. **Queue lines are SL pools:** traders waiting for a **retest** or parked on a horizontal line hold both entries and stops on one line — identify that group, because the next move is spent on them.
7. **When one index has already run a long way, the room is in the one sitting near its support** — that is where the position forms (Bank Nifty at support while Nifty had extended). Do not confuse this with the mistake he calls out: entering the **weak** index just because it is weak.
8. **A momentum nobody can follow is fragile:** if even a small reversal would make holders take profit, the reversal will extend further — and a still-weak lagging index confirms it.
9. **A side that could not sell one index will sell the other** — when that happens, their SLs become available in the second index and the market reverses immediately. Availability of SLs, not the pattern, triggers the turn.
10. **Stop-loss identification sits inside a projection and is taught in parts** — a single 10-minute session deliberately cannot finish it (this is Part 1 of the Sunday Special series). Read it live, not from hindsight; he claims the analysis was spoken in the live market, and once a position is on, it is exited on loss or profit only.
