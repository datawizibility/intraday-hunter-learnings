### Core idea
- One subject only: **how to average an intraday position**. The session matters because every earlier anti-averaging rule in the corpus (2021-12-18, 2022-01-23, 2022-02-19, 2022-07-31) *forbade* averaging without ever saying what a *permitted* average would look like. Today he does the opposite thing: he concedes averaging exists, calls it a sweet slow poison — *"ऐसा मीठा जहर जो धीरे-धीरे आपकी ट्रेडिंग"* — and then supplies the terms, the conditions and the formula that turn it from poison into a tool — *"कंडीशन के साथ कुछ फॉर्मूला के साथ अगर हम"*.
- The frame he insists on is not *averaging is wrong* — it is *averaging without rules is wrong*: big money averages too — *"पाए लेकिन बड़ा पैसा भी Amazing करता है"* (⚠️ `Amazing` is the ASR corruption of *averaging*, written in Latin script throughout the early lines). The clip's problem statement is the fear the trader carries into the market — *"जो अवैध इनका जो डर है मार्केट के अंदर"* (⚠️ the opening clause is ASR damage; the surrounding sentences make it *the fear inside the market*, which the rules exist to remove).
- The whole session is one shape: two trader groups → how many times you may average → when → on what condition → with what frozen limit → and a personal worked example in which he does **not** average.
- **Evolution weight:** this is the corpus's first *procedure* for averaging — a budget, a quantity split and a frozen loss limit — layered onto a four-session anti-averaging ban that the clip never lifts.

### The slow poison, and the two trader groups
- He splits traders into exactly two groups (`ट्रैक्टर का ग्रुप` — `ट्रैक्टर` is the standing ASR corruption of *trader*). **Group A** is the trader whose position, after entry, shows roughly sixty percent loss — *"उनकी अपनी पोजीशन में शिफ्ट इसे 60% लॉस"* (⚠️ `शिफ्ट इसे` is ASR damage for *situation*) — after which the market recovers — *"नजर आता है उसके बाद मार्केट रिकवरी कर"* — and takes its own time to turn — *"देता हूं के अकॉर्डिंग समय लगता है और"*.
- **Group B** is the harsher case: most of the time his position shows **more than sixty percent** loss against the trend — *"सिक्सटी परसेंट से ज्यादा ज्यादा लो पर"* — before the market finally begins to move his way — *"नजर आता है तब जाकर मार्केट उनके कोडिंग"* (⚠️ `उनके कोडिंग` = ASR damage for *his side*).
- Why he draws the split: the budget rules below are addressed to group A first (he says he belongs there — *"क्योंकि मैं भी इसी"*), and group B is handed an **extra construction** (the quantity split) precisely because its drawdown runs deeper before the turn.
- The distinction is not decoration — it decides *which* remedy you get: group A gets the budget and the frozen limit; group B gets the budget, the frozen limit **and** an entry built out of parts.

### The averaging budget — minimum one, maximum three, inside ten days
- The permission is stated with hard numbers. **Minimum one** attempt — *"तो आपको इस प्रकार इन करना है आपको मिनिमम"* — and a second one only in a genuinely rare condition — *"एक बार और बहुत ए रेयर कंडीशन में आप"* — you may average again inside the market — *"दोबारा ग्रेडिंग मार्केट के अंदर कर सकते"* (⚠️ `ग्रेडिंग` = एवरेजिंग, the recurring ASR corruption of *averaging*).
- The **window and the ceiling**: inside ten days — *"हो अगर आप 10 दिन के अंदर 10 स्प्रेड करें"* (⚠️ `10 स्प्रेड` is ASR for the averaging attempts, not a spread) — the maximum is three — *"ज्यादा से ज्यादा तीन हो सकते इससे ज्यादा"* — and past that you will not average at all — *"आप अपनी कि इनको अंग्रेज नहीं करोगे"* (⚠️ `अंग्रेज` = *average*; the same corruption appears twice in the clip).
- Why cap it at all: most of the time you are wrong and then right — the figure arrives corrupted as `1560` — *"क्योंकि आप मोस्ट ऑफ द टाइम 1560 परसेंट"* (⚠️ `1560` is an ASR mash of **50–60%**; the surrounding sentences carry the meaning — you are wrong roughly half to sixty percent of the time, your entry is therefore *about right*, and random averaging only puts you in the trap). If the entry point is fine — *"तो यह आपका एंट्री प्वाइंट"* / *"ठीक-ठाक चल है तो आप बेवजह Amazing करके"* — averaging is exactly what makes you stuck in the market.
- ⚠️ The `10 दिन` / 10-attempts / 3-max passage is where the ASR is most broken; the **numbers** survive (10, 10, 3), the connecting grammar does not. Recorded as spoken.

### Freeze the loss limit; the average is a probability play
- The one condition on which averaging is allowed: **only if you still have payment (ammunition) left to spend** — *"पेमेंट बाकी है तभी आपको यहां पर एवरीथिंग"*.
- The hard rule, stated twice and slowly, is that the loss limit you fixed at entry **must not move**: *"आपको लोन लेने वाला है जो लॉस आपने शुरुआत"* (⚠️ `लोन` = loss) — *"में डिसाइड किया था वहीं लोस आपको"* — *"कंटिन्यू रखना है उसको चेंज नहीं करना है"*. Averaging changes the quantity and the average price; it does **not** change the stop-loss number.
- Why the limit is frozen: because this is a **probability game** — *"क्योंकि यहां पर आप थोड़ा सा प्रॉपर्टी के"* (⚠️ `प्रॉपर्टी` = probability) — you already know the market will not fully recover — *"आपको पता कि मार्केट पूरा रिकवरी नहीं"* — there will be exactly one bounce — *"होने वाला एक उछाल यहां पर होने वाला है"* — and you exit at the target you fixed earlier. The purpose of the average is to catch that single bounce, not to wait for a full retrace.
- The target is decided **at entry**, not invented after the average, and the moment it prints you leave without waiting further — the paired limit-and-target of 2022-06-19, here with the limit explicitly frozen *through* the average.

### Group B: split the entry into three or four parts — and never average at random
- For group B the construction changes: split the entry quantity into three to four parts — *"जो क्वांटिटी उसको तीन से चार पार्ट्स में"* — and every time take a part, not the whole — *"हर बार अपने क्वांटिटी को तीन से चार बार"* — so the quantity you would have taken as one lot is taken as pieces — *"लेने वाले थे वहीं क्वांटिटी 25 25 करके"* (⚠️ `25 25` is the spoken illustration of splitting one quantity into two visible pieces, not a lot size).
- If the entry is proving wrong, **reduce** — do not add — *"लेकिन यहां पर क्वांटिटी को कम करके करना"* — because the entry itself is going wrong — *"पड़ेगा क्योंकि आप की एंट्री है वह गलत हो"*.
- The absolute line under all of it: **never average randomly** — *"लेकिन अगर आप रैंडमली ऐसे और इन करते गए"* — *"आपको रैंडमली नहीं करना चाहिए तभी भी ऐसे"*.
- **The same warning is extended to investing.** In investing too, keep away from averaging — *"लेकिन वहां भी एवरेजिंग से बचना चाहिए"* — because you do not have enough capital for it to fall again and again — *"क्योंकि आपके पास इतना पैसा नहीं होता कि"*. His reason: averaging needs an assumption of infinite capital, and capital is finite.
- **His personal worked example** (given so the rules can be seen in use): he had an entry, the market rose and he did nothing — *"पर मार्केट बढ़ने लगा कुछ नहीं किया आपने"* — because it went sixty, seventy, eighty percent up — *"क्योंकि जब साठ से सत्तर अस्सी परसेंट ऊपर"*; then when the market came down and started ranging he decided to act — *"यहां पर ट्रेंड करने लगा तब मैंने डिसाइड"* — he kept his loss limit exactly where it was — *"का लिमिट वहीं रख दिया आपको ध्यान होगा"* — took his points on the momentum — *"मुमेंटम किया हम निकल गए क्वाथ मार्केट"* (⚠️ `क्वाथ` = ASR damage for *the market*; the sense is *he left, and where the market went afterwards was not his concern*) — and exited. On that occasion he added **no** quantity, because his own rule did not sit there — *"मेरा यहां पर रूल नहीं बैठता कि मैं यहां"*.
- The verdict he draws from his own example: averaging is not wrong — *"बड़ा पैसा भी एवरीथिंग करता है लेकिन"* — *"एवरीथिंग से पैसा कैसे बनाना उसके लिए"* — you have to build rules for it — *"आपको कुछ रोल बनाने पड़ेंगे अब देखिए यहां"*.
- The closing instruction: apply this averaging technique anywhere in intraday — *"यह टेक्नीक अपनाएंगे तो आपको कभी भी"* — and you will never have a problem in the market — *"मार्केट में दिक्कत नहीं आएगी धन्यवाद"*.

### Keep permanently
1. **Averaging is a tool only with terms, conditions and a formula.** It is a slow poison by default; it becomes usable only when the rules below are attached. Never average as a reflex.
2. **Budget the average: minimum one, maximum three, inside ten days.** A second attempt needs a genuinely rare condition; past the ceiling, do not average again. This is his rule of thumb, not a measured edge.
3. **Average only while you still have payment left.** The decision to average is gated on remaining ammunition, never on conviction that the market must come back.
4. **Freeze the loss limit at the number you decided at entry.** Averaging changes quantity and average price; it must not change the stop. The limit is the universe the trade lives inside.
5. **Treat the average as a probability play, not a recovery plan.** The market will not fully recover; there is one bounce, and you exit at the target you fixed earlier. Do not wait for a full retrace.
6. **Group B: split the entry into three or four parts.** Build the position from pieces rather than one lot; if the entry is proving wrong, **reduce** the quantity — do not add to it.
7. **Never average randomly — and in investing, avoid averaging entirely.** Random averaging is what produces the big loss, and investing averaging fails because the capital is not infinite.
8. **The exit is at your number, not the market's maximum.** Once the pre-decided target prints on the bounce, take the points and leave; where the market goes afterwards is not your concern.
