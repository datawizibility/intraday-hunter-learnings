### Core idea
- The session's answer to “how do I decide quickly?” is not a speed technique. **A quick decision is a PRE-BUILT decision.** Every ingredient he names is something finished before the market opens: a written plan of execution, a defined setup, a small position size, a fixed trade count, and an image of how the move will actually arrive.
- **Speed is the output of preparation, not the input.** Left to itself, quickness works against you: *"तो जितना आपने क्विकली डिसाइड किया उतना ही हो सकता है जल्दी से आपका लॉस हो जाए।"*
- **Trading is not a daily-income source** — *"ट्रेडिंग डेली बेसिस पर इनकम करने वाला सोर्स नहीं है।"* The account moves in windows (his illustration: the first five-to-six days flat or slightly negative, the last three-to-four days covering the back and paying forward), so the mindset that must be installed before speed is the window mindset, not the daily one.
- **Revenge trading is the first thing to remove, and there is no alternative route.** *"इससे अलावा कोई दूसरा रास्ता है नहीं।"* A fast trade is wrong often; if a wrong trade immediately triggers a repair trade, the quick-decision mindset can never form at all.
- **A plan of execution is a precondition, not a nicety.** Without a setup the trader cannot even tell what to improve; the random trader may avoid a big loss and a big profit and simply burn time.
- **Full capital destroys the ability to decide.** *"फुल कैपिटल के साथ कभी भी ट्रेड नहीं करना चाहिए।"* The point is psychological rather than arithmetical: with everything deployed, the running loss is visible in your own account, and the mind that should be choosing a setup is instead watching its own capital shrink. A small portion is what makes the fast read possible to act on.
- **Over-trading is the gate that has to close.** The traders who take fast decisions *and* make money are, in his words, the ones far from over-trading — the decision is quick because the number of permitted trades is small and known in advance, not because the trader is quick.
- **Direction alone is not a decision.** Knowing the market will go up or down is what everyone knows; the fast decision needs the *how* — the structure/image the move will print — otherwise there is nothing to compare the moment against.
- **Quick ≠ gambler.** Fast decisions are not a licence to fire at anything; the gambler is defined here by having lost all sense of his own net — *"जो मिल गया उसी से संतुष्ट हो जाएंगे।"*

### Quick decision = pre-built decision (he refuses a trade that fails his own plan)
- The worked illustration is a **buy** he took (market was already moving up) — and the reason he gives is the reason the lesson exists: it was *not* taken to eat a seller's stop. *"अगर मेरा ऐसा प्लान है तो मैं यहां पे ट्रेड नहीं बनाऊंगा।"* Had his plan required seller stop-losses there, he says he simply would not have traded. That is a refusal branch operating inside a live decision.
- The read behind that buy: the area had been ground sideways at one place, so retail had not built a position there — neither side sat with quantity. His rule for that state: *"अगर रिटेल का पोजीशन वहां पे अवेलेबल नहीं है तो उनको हम टारगेट नहीं बनाते"* — no seated side means no target, so the market is free to continue the existing strength, and buying is the continuation trade.
- Two consequences he draws:
  - Recovery trading must go first, and after a fast loss the correct response is the *opposite* of speed — find a good opportunity slowly and carefully.
  - Without plan and setup, nothing can be corrected: *"आपके पास प्लान ऑफ एग्जीक्यूशन होना चाहिए।"* Profit and loss are not the teaching signal; the setup is, because only a defined setup can be reviewed and improved.

| State of the trader | What he can do |
|---|---|
| Plan + setup in hand | Read the market against the plan; improve the execution over time |
| No plan, no setup, random trades | Cannot improve, cannot tell right from wrong; big loss / big profit both avoided, and *"वो टाइम खराब होगा"* |
| Fast loss then a fast repair trade | The mindset never forms — revenge fuel, not experience |

### Position size decides decision speed (not just the loss)
- The two-trader illustration is about identical capital and different behaviour, and he uses it to isolate size alone:
  - Trader A works the whole stack at once. He cannot take the quick decision — and even if he does, he cannot handle the trade; a single red candle blocks the entry that his own written plan told him to take.
  - Trader B holds the same capital but works one or two lots — *"एक दो लॉट से काम कर रहा है 5000 से वहां पे वो रिस्क ले सकता है"* — so he can risk, decide, and still make money even when the market runs against him.
- So size here is not being taught as loss control. It is taught as the **enabler of the decision**: an unbounded-in-your-face loss switches the mind off, and a small committed portion leaves the mind available for the read.
- The underlying arithmetic is not restated in this clip; only the conclusion is — *"एक छोटे पोर्शन के साथ काम करना सीखना पड़ेगा।"*

### Fix the count: limited options are what make the decision both fast and good
- The mechanism is stated plainly: if the trader does not know whether he is a 10-trade, 11-trade or 15-trade trader, then being quick buys him nothing. When the mind knows that only two, three or four trades will be available, the decision becomes *"वो डिसीजन जल्दी लेगा लेकिन अच्छे डिसीजन लेगा."*
- Why a count changes the quality of a decision:
  - A move that is missed stops being a loss — *"उससे ज्यादा ट्रेड आपको नहीं मिलेगा।"* No scarcity, no rush, no repair need.
  - Without a count the tape does the deciding: the move is running, it is being missed, *"लालच आएगा डर लगेगा।"* — and in that state the mind stops checking right from wrong.
  - With a small counted number of options the filter tightens by itself, and the result improves with time — *"टाइम के साथ उसमें सुधार भी होगा।"*
- Contrast he draws: a trader who learns to build a trade on one candle has learned quickness, not profitability — long term there is no benefit until the options are limited.
- Over-trading therefore is not a discipline problem to be solved by willpower here; it is the thing that has to be **removed** before fast trading can pay: *"ओवर ट्रेडिंग से दूर-दूर रहना पड़ेगा।"*

### Know the HOW: direction is the easy half, the structure is the decision
- Setup: everyone already checks whether the market will go up or down. He adds the second question — *"लेकिन वह जाएगा कैसे?"* — and says that without it you cannot decide quickly either, because “I have to buy” is not a decision.
- The instrument is an internal chart image: *"एक स्ट्रक्चर बनना चाहिए चार्ट का।"* The trader has to pre-hold the shape by which the move will arrive.
- The concrete case he gives is momentum after a one-way run: if positive momentum has already printed and the market has gone straight up, further positive momentum cannot come directly the same way — *"यहां पे रिट्रेसमेंट होगा या फिर कुछ अलग मोमेंटम करेगा।"* — the market retraces, or builds resistance, or does something else first. Because that intermediate step is already in the trader's head, the decision at the moment is relaxed instead of panicked.
- Stated the other way: only the direction is held, but not how the move will be shaped — then the decision cannot be made quickly at all, because there is nothing to check the moment against.

### What “quick” must not become (the gambler test)
- *"क्विक डिसीजन लेने का यह मतलब नहीं कि हम ट्रेडिंग को गैंबलर की तरह लेना शुरू कर दें।"*
- The defining failure of the gambler in this clip is not the size of the bet — it is the loss of the net: capital went down or up and *"पैसा कम हो गया, ज्यादा हो गया उन्हें इस बात का कोई सेंस नहीं रहता कि नेट में उन्हें क्या मिल रहा है।"*
- The trader's counterpart is improvement: open the mind, keep correcting, and the count of trades stays small. Net effect is the scoreboard he keeps returning to — *"नेट में क्या होगा? कभी बन गया कभी रह गया।"*
- Inverted rule for a trader who has not yet installed plan + setup + size + count: speed is not owed to him — *"अदरवाइज मार्केट में क्विक डिसीजन नहीं लेना चाहिए।"*

### Keep permanently
1. **Quick decisions are pre-built.** Plan of execution, setup, size and count are fixed before the open; speed is the output of that preparation, never the input to a trade.
2. **Remove revenge trading before chasing speed.** After a fast loss the correct move is to slow down and search for a genuinely good opportunity; a repair trade is what makes the quick-decision mindset impossible.
3. **Never deploy the full capital.** Work a small portion so the running loss stays readable and the mind stays free for the read — size is a decision instrument, not merely a loss limit.
4. **Fix the trade count in advance, and keep it small.** A limited number of options makes the decision both faster and better, and it converts a missed move into something that was never yours to take.
5. **A missed move is closed business.** Do not chase it, do not recover it; scarcity plus greed plus fear is exactly the state in which right and wrong stop being checked.
6. **A setup must be able to refuse.** Write the plan with its refusal branch: if the situation does not match what the plan needs, no trade — that refusal is what allows an instant call.
7. **Know both halves: which way, and how it will get there.** Hold an image of the structure the move will print; direction alone is not a decision.
8. **Without plan and setup, nothing can be improved.** Random trading cannot distinguish right from wrong in review, so neither profit nor loss teaches you anything.
9. **Trade to improve, not to draw a daily income.** Judge over a stretch (his illustration: cover the back half of a two-digit day count), not on one day's profit or loss.
10. **Quick is not gambling.** If you cannot say what you are net accumulating, you have stopped trading and started gambling.
11. **Fast trading is available only to the trader far from over-trading.** Speed follows from a small count of permitted trades, not from fast fingers.
