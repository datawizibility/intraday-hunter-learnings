### Core idea
- The week worked because he **followed one trend that ran without a major retracement** — the rare tape where intraday trend-following pays continuously. His own framing: a positional trader rides that trend on a single good entry; an intraday trader must re-enter again and again, so every re-entry is exposed to a retracement day.
- **The trend was never declared in advance.** Each day's side was chosen by *who was still sitting in the market*, not by "a downtrend has begun". The downtrend was only confirmed mid-week, by a specific tell (below).
- **The flushes happened in order:** first the sellers who had positioned around Monday's close were cleared (that required a *buy*), and only then did the buyers — sitting at two places — become the target (that required the *sells* for the rest of the week).
- **Even while selling every day he kept a live buy plan**, and on the oversized gap-down day he actually took it: gap-down ⇒ buy (counter-trend) when the market stops falling and holds.
- **Following a trend costs drawdown.** One sell went to a decent loss and came close to his limit before the market fell his way; he held because the level had broken in his direction — patience, not an early stop, converted it.

### The retracement filter (before any intraday entry)
His stated pre-entry checklist whenever he wants to work *with* a trend:
1. **Is a retracement day forming?** A trend is only intraday-tradable while no major retracement has appeared — a retracement can reach **61%**.
2. **Is a chart high/low already built?** An existing high/low changes what the market does next.
3. **Is a round number available** at that spot?
- Quoted: *"दूसरा हमें यह भी देखना होता है कि चार्ट के अंदर कोई हाई लो जैसा कुछ तो नहीं बना हुआ है… कोई राउंड नंबर यहां पे अवेलेबल तो नहीं है."*
- Why it matters: intraday means repeated entries; a retracement inside a trend is exactly what invalidates the *entry*, even when the *direction* is right.

### How a downtrend is confirmed (the week's clearest new tell)
- His argument: *if the purpose were only to flush retail, the market would have moved a little and turned.* It did not — **it came down far more than the flush required**.
- Quoted: *"मार्केट काफी ज्यादा नीचे आ चुका है तो यह कंडीशन क्या बन रही है यह एक **ट्रेंड मार्केट** के अंदर हो सकता है… कंफर्मेशन मिलना शुरू हो चुका था कि यह जो मार्केट नीचे आया है यह **ज्यादा ही नीचे आ चुका है**."*
- Corollary he adds later: the lower side (a level that could have been the buyers' opportunity to push up) was **crossed** — *"जो लोअर साइड था जो बायर के लिए एक अच्छा मौका हो सकता था दोबारा से ऊपर ले जाने का वो भी मार्केट ने क्रॉस कर दिया"* ⇒ a *better* downtrend.
- And the operator inference: if the market were only after the retail sitting there, he would have expected it to rise again; because it fell **after** those retailers were gone, a big operator is on the sell side.

### The 52,800 / 53,000 read (round-number psychology at the close)
- *"मार्केट में 52800 के आसपास ही क्लोज मिला है… मार्केट कहीं ना कहीं **साइकोलॉजी को टेस्ट** करने की कोशिश कर रहा है."*
- His counterfactual: **if it had closed directly below 53,000, the read would have differed** — a clean break would have made other traders sell *and hold* their positions.
- But because **52,800** was the pivot (closing above, then below, then above), anyone who sold exited at a small profit, and **buyers sat down** believing the retracement was over and the market would rise again.
- Therefore: a close that keeps pivoting around a round number ⇒ the sellers have already covered, the buyers are the ones now holding the open risk ⇒ expect a bigger move. (His own caveat right beside it: retracement up to 61% is normal; beyond that is the danger bell.)
- Later the same logic reappears on **52,000** (a round-number support the close took before pushing up) and on **51,500** (whose breakout he waited through).

### Price-level first, trend second
- His rule for the analysis itself: *"सबसे पहले आपको क्या जाना पड़ेगा — प्राइस लेवल के ऊपर प्राइस लेवल सही चल रहा है, फिर आप काम कर सकते हो इंट्राडे के लिए; अदर वाइज अगर प्राइस लेवल **खराब** चल रहा है, भले ही आपका **ट्रेंड नेगेटिव** चल रहा है, तो उसके अकॉर्डिंग आपको डिसीजन लेना पड़ेगा."*
- Read as a gate: the trend only tells you *which side to prefer*; the price level's behaviour decides whether you may take the trade today.

### The counter-trend call trade (the week's exception)
- He wanted a gap-down, got a **very large one (~1,000 points)** — and took a **CALL (buy)** instead of the planned sell.
- Reasoning chain: after that much negativity the market may try to turn; he could see it had **stopped falling and begun holding**; a holding market will hand one upside pop — to let the traders who have been selling for 2–3 days book some profit, and to capture those who sold directly on the gap-down.
- He is explicit that this is **not confirmation**: *"यह ऐसा नहीं होता है कि यहां पे कंफर्म है… इसमें मैं **रिस्क** लेता हूं, रिस्क के अकॉर्डिंग पैसा बन जाता है, अदर वाइज लॉस होता है."* Confidence varies; the risk is taken to the situation.
- Mirror framing: **an oversized gap-down with a market that refuses to fall further is a risk-licensed counter-trend buy** — not a rule, a licensed exception.

### Trend-following needs patience (the drawdown trade)
- He sold with the trend and saw **a decent loss** — *"अच्छा खासा लॉस नजर आया… ऑलमोस्ट अपना लिमिट रीच ही होने वाला था"* — and did **not** exit.
- Why holding was defensible to him: the market had **broken out directly above 51,500**, and the trend was already negative, *"तो मार्केट ऊपर नहीं जा पाएगा"*. He waited with patience and the market fell sharply; he booked a decent profit.
- The trap inside this lesson: the drawdown *approached his limit*. Patience is only affordable when the level has broken in your direction **and** the position is sized so the trend's normal drawdown does not breach the limit.

### Look both ways (his closing instruction)
- *"अगर आप एक तरफ देखना शुरू कर दोगे तो दूसरी तरफ बिल्कुल नजर नहीं आएगा, अंधेरा हो जाएगा."*
- So even while the major focus is selling, the buy plan stays live and is re-tested daily — the oversized gap-down day is the proof that the other side must be kept written.

### Keep permanently
1. **Run the retracement filter before any intraday entry on a trend:** is a retracement day forming, is a chart high/low already built, is a round number available? Intraday needs repeated entries, so the retracement — not the direction — is what breaks you.
2. **A downtrend is confirmed by overshoot, not by the first fall:** if the market falls further than a retail flush would need (and crosses the level that was the buyers' chance), an operator has come in on that side — a trend market.
3. **Flush in order.** Clear the pool that positioned around the close first; only after that does the next pool (the momentum chasers) become the target.
4. **Positions taken around the close are the first pool addressed** — especially on a trending or breakout day.
5. **Round numbers carry the read.** A close that keeps pivoting around a round number means the sellers have already covered and the buyers now hold the open risk — expect the bigger move.
6. **Retracement up to 61% is normal; beyond that is the danger bell.**
7. **Price level first, trend second:** confirm the level is behaving before taking the trade; a negative trend does not excuse trading a badly-behaving level.
8. **An oversized gap-down + a market that refuses to fall further is a risk-licensed counter-trend buy** — taken small, explicitly as risk, because the recent sellers must be given their profit-booking pop.
9. **Following a trend means living through drawdown.** Hold only while (a) the level has broken in your direction and (b) the trend is with you — and size so that the trend's normal drawdown cannot breach your limit.
10. **Book the momentum, don't ride to the close** — intraday trend-following pays in repeated captures, not in one long hold.
11. **Always keep the other side's plan alive:** watch only one side and the other goes dark; the counter-trend call trade existed only because the buy plan was kept written.
