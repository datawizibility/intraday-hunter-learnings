**URL:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5ek3m09Vy-Y
**Transcript:** Hindi ASR of `5ek3m09Vy-Y` — `transcripts/2022-10-22-teaching-5ek3m09Vy-Y.md` (2834 words)

### Core idea
- ⭐ The opening observation is a **perception trap**: *"मार्केट के अंदर जो भी बोलोगे वही होगा"* — while your position is in
  **profit**, the chart appears to obey you (say green, green comes; say red, red comes). The moment your position is in
  **loss**, the same reasoning inverts: think resistance here → it breaks out; think support here → it breaks down.
- ⭐ His correction: *"मार्केट तो अपने कोडिंग ही चलता रहता है"* — the market runs its own process. Big positions exist
  that **cannot be fully cut even if someone wants out**, and the market does not travel up and down for one, two or three
  lots. The apparent personal targeting is an illusion.
- ⭐ The operational reading that replaces the illusion: *"जो मार्केट में ब्रेकडाउन हमारे डिसीज़न के अकॉर्डिंग नहीं आते,
  उनका रीज़न यह होता है कि जो हम डिसीज़न बना रहे हैं वो गलत बन चुका है"* — **a move that contradicts your plan is
  information about your plan, not about the market's intent.**
- ⭐ And the failure chain that follows from misreading it: the loss is taken → not cut on time → the trader keeps
  **sitting** in the market → *"बैठे रहने से हमारा लॉस बढ़ता रहता है और लॉस बढ़कर हमारा कैपिटल भी जा सकता है."*
- ⭐ The session's central instruction: **never build a position in order to recover a loss.** *"लॉस को रिकवर करने के
  लिए आपको पोज़िशन नहीं बनाना है — आपको पोज़िशन इसलिए बनाना है क्योंकि वहां पर आपको एक अपॉर्चुनिटी नज़र आ रही है."*

### Why an options trader loses (three causes, as he lists them)
1. **The decision was wrong.**
2. **The loss was not cut on time.**
3. **The rush to recover the first loss** — *"उसको रिकवरी करने की जल्दबाज़ी में जो हम डिसीज़न बनाएंगे वह भी मोस्ट ऑफ दी
   टाइम गलत होते हैं."* The third cause is the one this session exists to remove.

### The double-loss chain (his worked illustration of the crowd)
- Many traders had built a **call-side** position (*"जैसे मैंने भी बनाया था"* — he includes himself). When rejection
  started, the loss became visible to thousands at the same moment, and they cut.
- Then: *"पहले उन्होंने ओल्ड में लॉस लिया, उसके बाद उन्होंने फुट में लॉस किया"* — **loss in the calls, then a second loss
  in the puts.** Two losses in one session.
- The consequence is psychological, not financial: *"अब मार्केट में उनका जो साइकोलॉजी, उनका माइंडसेट है वो और भी ज़्यादा
  खराब हो गया"* — and from there they take *"कुछ न कुछ ट्रेड"* at **every** resistance or breakout, because the objective
  is no longer the setup, it is the loss. *"और जब हम ऐसा सोचने लग जाए कि आज की आज ही लॉस को रिकवर कर लेंगे, तो हो सकता है
  एक बहुत बड़ा लॉस हम लेकर मार्केट में निकल सकते हैं."*

### His own two sessions (as he narrates them)
**Day 1 — the loss he took, and the re-entry he refused.**
- Plan: **sell on the gap-up.** Reason as spoken: there was no sentiment suggesting buyers could arrive
  (*"कोई सेंटीमेंट नहीं था कि यहाँ सेल [करने] ... बायर मार्केट में आ सकते हैं"*).
- The gap-up opened and rejection began, but *"हो सकता है रिजेक्शन छोटा-मोटा है, मार्केट में ऊपर भी जा सकते हैं — क्योंकि
  हम गलत हो सकते हैं"* — so he **waited** instead of selling the first rejection.
- When the rejection was clear he built the trade. Then: *"मुझे ठीक-ठाक प्रॉफिट नज़र आने लगा था — 60,000 — और मुझे मार्केट
  के अंदर लॉस नज़र आने लगता है."*
- He held and waited (*"कम से कम या तो प्रॉफिट मिलेगा या लॉस मिलेगा"*). The market went back **up**, momentum returned,
  and a level was sitting close by — where even a small breakout would take **~₹2 lakh** away. So he cut near the high.
- Then the market **fell anyway**, without him: *"कभी-कभी ऐसा भी होगा, आपको रिग्रेट आएगा."* The point of the story is what
  he did **not** do: he did not rebuild a position to punish the market for that regret. *"एक दिन के रिग्रेट से अगर आपने
  दोबारा से पोज़िशन बनाना शुरू कर दिया तो हो सकता है बड़ा लॉस आप उस एक दिन के लेकर निकालो."*

**Day 2 — recovery through an opportunity, not through urgency.**
- Same plan (selling). Big gap-up, fast selling. He skipped the **first candle** because it was too fast
  (*"बहुत ही फास्ट कैंडल होता है"*), waited for a proper breakdown + a **red candle**, then built the trade.
- Then, in a hurry — *"दो दिन से लॉस ले रहे थे, तो ज़रूरी था कि यहाँ पे थोड़ा सा वेट करें, कंफर्म के साथ ट्रेड करें"* — he
  admits the hurry was the mistake; the correct response to two losing days is **more** confirmation, not less.
- He waited. Support appeared and **Nifty gave a breakout**, so the trade was initiated; momentum came, a good target came,
  and he exited. *"हमारा जो लॉस हो रहा था, दो दिन से, वो एक दिन के अंदर रिकवर हो रहा है."*
- Why he exited into the target instead of holding: a **cluster of levels sitting just above** (see ASR fidelity — read as
  Bank Nifty ~40,200 / ~40,000) — *"जब लेवल बीच में आ जाते हैं तो मार्केट ऐसी उथल-पुथल भी मचा सकते हैं."* Levels overhead
  are a reason to take the target, not a reason to widen it.
- ⭐ The sentence that closes the loop: **"जब आपके पास मौका मिले, तब आप रिकवर करने का प्लान बनाओ."** The recovery came
  from a setup he was willing to wait for — never from the need to recover.

### Over-trading, redefined
- ⭐ *"ओवर-ट्रेडिंग यह नहीं होता कि आपने एक दिन के अंदर 10 ट्रेड कर लिए, 20 ट्रेड कर लिए — वो कोई ओवर-ट्रेडिंग नहीं है."*
  If the trades are **found opportunities**, the count is irrelevant.
- Over-trading is the other thing: *"अगर आप अपॉर्चुनिटी को तो समझ नहीं रहे, लेकिन मार्केट में आपका लॉस हो चुका है, इस
  वज़ह से आप [पोज़िशन] बना रहे हो — तो वो गलत हो जाता है."* **The count is not the disease; the motive is.**
- His test, as spoken: if the reading is *"मार्केट को गिरना चाहिए, गिरना चाहिए … सेलिंग आएगा"* but the market is giving a
  loss, then he has to leave — *"हो सकता है मैं यहाँ पर गलत सोच रहा हूँ."*

### Two smaller rules stated in passing
- ⭐ **Do not take a trade whose stop sits around the closing price.** *"अपना स्टॉप लॉस अगर क्लोज़िंग प्राइस के आसपास बैठ
  रहा है, तो वहाँ से हम थोड़ा सा दूर रहने की कोशिश करते हैं"* — whatever the sentiment, if the chart does not support it,
  stay away; if conviction is there, size it.
- **Both sides are needed.** *"अगर मार्केट बार-बार गिरेगा, तो हो सकता [है] बायर मार्केट में न आएं — केवल सेलर से बात नहीं
  बनती."* A one-sided crowd is not enough; he wants the opposite side to exist as the target.
- **Expiry completed → the buy side is available again** (his stated reason for accepting a buy trade that day).
- Closing note on motivation: trade because the chart interests you. *"काफ़ी लोग केवल पैसे के लिए आ जाते हैं"* — if a person
  can sit in front of a chart all day, the interest is real; *"अगर पैसे के लिए आए हो तो मैं आपका कुछ नहीं कर सकता."*

  count-based version of the rule that most retail traders carry, and replaces it with an opportunity test.
- 🔵 **NEW — the perception asymmetry stated as a phenomenon**: in profit the chart obeys, in loss it contradicts — and the

  most common trigger for the revenge trade — the market moving the right way **after** you were stopped out.
- 🟡 **REFINED — cut when wrong** (2022-02-06 → 2022-09-18 → 2022-10-16) gains its **failure mode**: the loss that is

  है."*
- 🟡 **REFINED — the closing price as a hostile place to leave a stop.** 2022-04-02/2022-04-23 called *buying the support

### Keep permanently
1. **The market is not watching your position.** A move against your plan means your plan was already wrong —
   *"मार्केट तो अपने कोडिंग ही चलता रहता है"* — not that you are being hunted.
2. **Never build a position to recover a loss.** Build it because an opportunity is visible. If the only reason for the
   trade is the previous loss, the trade is wrong before it is placed.
3. **The recovery decision is the most reliably wrong decision there is** — because its motive is the loss, not the setup.
4. **Cut on time, and do not sit in an open loss.** A loss that is taken but *sat in* grows, and it can take the capital
   with it.
5. **The double loss is the trap**: cut the first side, then cut the opposite side in the same session, and the mindset
   degrades faster than the account.
6. **Over-trading is a motive, not a count.** Ten or twenty trades built on found opportunities is not over-trading;
   one trade built to recover a loss is.
7. **One day's regret is not a mandate to rebuild.** The market moving your way after you were stopped out is not a signal
   to re-enter — it is the market doing its own thing without you.
8. **After two losing days, ask for more confirmation, not less.** He names his own hurry as the error on the recovery
   day; waiting for a red candle and a matching index print is what made the recovery trade work.
9. **Keep away from setups whose stop sits around the closing price** — that is where the trade is easiest to break.
10. **A one-sided crowd is not a trade.** Only sellers is not enough: the opposite side has to exist as the target, and
    the completed expiry can be the reason that side becomes available again.
11. **Trade it because the chart interests you.** Money-only entry is the one motive he says he cannot help.
