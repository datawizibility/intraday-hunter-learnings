### Core idea
- Thesis: the **CPR (Central Pivot Range)** is a *range*, not one line, and that is its whole advantage over a plain support level. The plain tool, he says, gives only a centre — *"बारे में जो सपोर्ट लेवल के अकॉर्डिंग"* … *"में केवल आपको सेंट्रल सपोर्ट मिलता है"* — whereas the CPR hands you a band of support **and** resistance for the intraday session: *"लेकिन CR में आपको एक बेहतरीन सपोर्ट ठेर"* … *"रेजिस्टेंस मिल जाएगा इंट्राडे में"* (⚠️ `"CR"` = CPR; `"ठेर"` = और / and).
- One tool decides three things at once — direction, entry and exit — *"एंट्री और एक्जिट भी डिसाइड कर सकते हो और"* — and it also forecasts the *character* of the session: volatile, or a one-sided momentum day: *"कैसा रहने वाला है कि मार्केट वोलेटाइल हो"* … *"सकता है या मार्केट में मुमेंटम हो सकता"* … *"है वन साइड तो यह सभी बातें आपसी प्यार के"* (⚠️ `"प्यार"` = an ASR misspelling of **pivot**, recurring throughout).
- Its chief selling point is that it is **pre-computable** — the edge is knowing tomorrow's range before the open — *"और मिलेगा सीपीआर आपको एडवांस में पता लग"* … *"सकता है पता लग सकता है कि आने वाला समय"*, so no hand calculation is needed any more — *"उसके लिए आपको कैलकुलेशन करने की आवश्यकता"*.
- The session is a **tool walk-through**, not a market read: he installs the indicator from his own saved template and then narrates entry, stop and target rules off the chart.

### CPR versus a single level — why the range is the point
- The opening contrast is explicit: the ordinary support-level overlay hands you one central support and a lower level — *"बारे में जो सपोर्ट लेवल के अकॉर्डिंग"* … *"एक इसमें आपको पर लेवल मिल जाता है एक"* … *"में केवल आपको सेंट्रल सपोर्ट मिलता है"* — and that, he says, is why it is not always enough: *"जिसके कारण इतनी कुर्सी पर नहीं पाती"* (⚠️ garbled; plain reading: therefore it does not hold up as well).
- With the CPR you get a live support **and** resistance band for the intraday market — *"लेकिन CR में आपको एक बेहतरीन सपोर्ट ठेर"* … *"रेजिस्टेंस मिल जाएगा इंट्राडे में"*.
- It doubles as a trend and momentum filter — *"सीपीआर के थ्रू आप मार्केट का ट्रेंड भी"* — because the position of the range tells you the day's bias before you trade.
- Install path, from his own saved copy: go to the indicator section and search for CPR — *"जाना सीपीआर आप शर्त लगा लेना मैंने"* (⚠️ `"शर्त"` = search) — he saved his own, *"सीपीआर लाइक कीजिए इस गोमती शंकर इसको"* (⚠️ trailing clause is garbled; plain reading: I have it saved like this), and the point is that the levels show up *before* the session: *"तो इससे आपको पता लग जाएगा कि आने वाली"*.

### An untested pivot becomes your level
- He flags a distinction he wants remembered across the session — this paragraph of ASR is heavily damaged: *"इसके साथ-साथ आपको यह ध्यान रखना कि वरदान"* … *"शिपयार्ड इसमें होता है वरदान से प्यार"* … *"किसको बोलते हैं जहां पर मार्केट इस प्यार"* … *"को टैक्स नहीं करता था जब यह तो इसको"* (⚠️ `"वरदान"` is ASR-unstable — do not reconstruct; `"टैक्स"` reads as **टेस्ट / test**; plain reading: a pivot the market has **not yet tested** is the one that pays).
- The benefit is then stated plainly — *"पेयर बन गया इसका बेनिफिट आफ कैसे उठाओगे"* (⚠️ `"पेयर"` = पिवट / pivot, garbled).
- Worked sense: if price opens above and keeps falling into an untested pivot, that CPR gives a strong support — *"गिर रहा होगा यह वरदान सीपीआर आपको एक"* … *"बेहतरीन सपोर्ट प्रोवाइड करेगा इसके साथ"*.
- And the same pivot, sitting overhead, supplies resistance — *"क्वेश्चन है तो एक बेहतरीन रजिस्टेंस आपको"*.

### Entry logic — open relative to the CPR
- The governing rule of the tool: when the market **opens near the CPR**, it tries to come back and touch that pivot — *"डिसाइड करते हैं तो देखिए जब मार्केट नियर"* … *"बाय सीपीआर के ओपन होता है तो उसी प्यार"* … *"को अप्रोच करने की कोशिश करता है जैसे"*.
- From there the decision splits two ways. If price approaches the pivot and is *rejected*, a **reversal** trade can be planned on the next approach — *"इसी पर को अप्रोच किया तो इसकी अकोर्डिंग"* … *"आप एक रिवर्सल ट्रेन का भी प्लान कर सकते"* (⚠️ `"रिवर्सल ट्रेन"` = reversal trade).
- The stop-loss goes at the level, and deliberately a touch **beyond** it, because price can overshoot: *"मार्किट में पॉजिटिव मुमेंटम में"* … *"हुआ है आपको यहां पर स्टॉपलॉस रखना"*, *"स्टॉपलॉस यहां पर रख सकते हो देखिए यहां"*, and it should be able to act like a resistance level — *"पर आपको आर टो एक्जिस्टेंस की तरह काम कर"* … *"कभी-कभी आर टू गुड टच करता लेकिन थोड़ा भी"* (⚠️ `"आर टो"` / `"आर टू"` = **R2**, read as the second resistance; there is no TC/BC rail named by that spelling in this clip).
- The reason for the buffer is stated: price sometimes touches the level and then, after a small spike, turns down again — *"कभी-कभी आर टू गुड टच करता लेकिन थोड़ा भी"* … *"थूक लगाकर फिर नीचे आने लगता है तो आप"* (⚠️ `"गुड टच"` / `"थूक लगाकर"` are ASR noise; plain reading: sometimes R2 is touched and price dips through it slightly before falling).

### Stacked CPRs read the trend; previous-day CPRs are still levels
- The trend is read off the **stack** of successive CPRs: each range sits above the last — *"देख सकते हो आपको यहां पर नजर आ रहा है यह"*, *"वाला सीपीआर है उसके ऊपर यह वाला सीपीआर"*, *"है उसके ऊपर यह वाला सीपीआर हैं तो आप"*.
- But the bias to the higher side is **conditional, not automatic** — only when the market is a near buy and reacting off the range, *"दे सकते हो लेकिन कब दे सकते हो जब"* … *"मार्केट नियर बाई सीरियल ऐसा नहीं कि"* (⚠️ `"नियर बाई"` = near buy).
- A return that finds support at the pivot confirms the read — *"आ रहा है तो वापिस प्यार का घर में सपोर्ट"* … *"मिल रहा है तो आप एक पोस्टपेड और प्रीपेड"* (⚠️ the trailing clause is garbled and is not reconstructed).
- The session then generalises past the current chart: there are **two prior CPRs** on this picture and those also act as support/resistance for the next day — *"जा सकते हैं दूसरा देखिए इसमें दो"* … *"प्रिवेंट होते हैं सीपीआर जैसे आपको नजर आ"* (⚠️ `"प्रिवेंट"` = previous / पिछले), and he ties it back to what he already taught — *"हैं जैसे वृद्धि सीसीआर के बारे में मैंने"* … *"बताया था उसी तरह तो प्रिवेंट होते हैं वह"*.

### Confirmation rules — reaction, stop below the third level, volume
- A positive entry needs a **reaction** at the zone, not a print: he points to a W pattern as one such trigger — *"पैटर्न देख सकते हैं यहां पर कोई डब्लू"* — and a trendline/support as others.
- The stop rule: the stop-loss must sit **below the third level** of the range, *"होगा जब आप एंट्री करोगे तो आपका स्टॉपलॉस"* … *"के नीचे बैठना चाहिए जो आपको थर्ड जो लेवल"* … *"मार्केट थर्ड लेवल तक तो आ जाता है इसके"*; below that line the level behaves cleanly as resistance.
- On an **index**, volume is added as the tie-breaker — *"सकते हो लेकिन इसके साथ आप वॉल्यूम को भी"* … *"इंडेक्स के अंदर काम करना है तो आपको यह"* — and he says volume is available directly: *"डाइरैक्टली वॉल्यूम अवेलेबल हो जाएगा"*.
- The volume entry trigger is an **increment** on the candle: *"प्रॉपर आपके एंट्री हो जाती है क्योंकि"* — when a lower-timeframe candle expands its range/volume, the entry is clean.
- Target and R:R are set explicitly from the setup — *"अपने तारे पृथ्वी या आपने जो भी इसलिए और"* (⚠️ `"तारे पृथ्वी"` is garbled; plain reading: अपने target) — with a practical R:R ladder, *"में वन बाय टू में 153 में वन बाय फोर में"* (⚠️ `"153"` = spoken one by three, 1:3).

### Keep permanently
1. **Prefer a range to a single line.** One support number is a point; a band that carries both a central level and rails gives you support *and* resistance for the same session — the band is the instrument.
2. **Use tools that are computable before the bell.** If a level set can be known in advance, it removes on-the-spot calculation and lets you plan entries, exits and the day's expected character in advance.
3. **An untested level is worth more than a tested one.** A pivot the market has not yet reacted to is where a reaction is still available; once a level has been used, its edge is spent.
4. **Read the open against the range.** Near the range, price tends to come back and touch the pivot — that approach, and the reaction it produces, is the decision point, not the open print itself.
5. **Trade the reaction, not the level.** Enter when the zone produces a printed reaction (a W, a trendline hold, a support touch) — a touch alone is not a signal.
6. **Put the stop beyond the level, with a buffer.** Price can spike through a level before turning, so the stop belongs past the far rail, not exactly on it.
7. **Let the stack, not the last range, set the trend.** Successive ranges stacked on one side give the bias — but only when the market is reacting off the range, never blindly.
8. **Prior-day ranges are still live levels.** Yesterday's (and day-before's) ranges act as today's support and resistance; keep the recent ranges on the chart.
9. **Confirm with volume on an index.** Direction plus a range reaction is not enough; add the volume expansion on the entry candle as the tie-breaker.
10. **Strip the chart to the one tool that does the job.** Additional overlays add lines and density without adding information; simplify to the range and set stops and targets from it.
