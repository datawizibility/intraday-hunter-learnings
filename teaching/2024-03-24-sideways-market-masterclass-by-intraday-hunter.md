### Core idea
- A sideways market is **not random**: it is a **demand–supply state**. To read it you must see *how* demand and supply are behaving.
- **Balanced supply and demand at a level ⇒ trending momentum.** Buyers and sellers both keep arriving (for their own different reasons), get roughly matched, and the market keeps **eliminating one side** — momentum runs to the side where the **big money** is placed (*"जिस तरफ बड़ा पैसा लगने वाला है उसी तरफ ट्रेंड"*).
- **The moment the balance breaks — sellers arrive and buyers don't (or buyers arrive and sellers don't) — the market goes sideways.** Sideways is the *visible footprint of an imbalance*, and there are three causes.
- Practical consequence: in a range you trade the **levels** (fade the extremes), because the market takes important numbers **accurately** instead of breaking them.

### The three causes of sideways
1. **Demand low AND supply low.** After a **big momentum**, buyers feel they *missed it* (*"मेरे हाथ से मार्केट निकल गया"*) and sellers feel they *can't risk selling* an uptrend. Nobody trades ⇒ **2–3 days of sideways**, then fresh psychology and fresh money ⇒ momentum resumes. *"मार्केट एक बड़ा मोमेंटम करता है … दो-तीन दिन साइड वेज हो जाता है, उसके बाद दोबारा से साइकोलॉजी चेंज होता है."*
2. **Demand high, no supply at that level ⇒ ACCUMULATION.** A big trader wants a **large quantity bought inside one price range**, not scattered. Until his demand is complete he **holds the market in the range** — goes up **200–300 points**, brings it back, again and again — while sellers keep feeling *"I should sell"* and buyers stay confused. **When accumulation completes, the next momentum prints.**
3. **Supply high, no demand at that level ⇒ DISTRIBUTION.** Same engine on a higher level: a big trader wants a **large quantity sold around one price**, so he takes the market down and up inside a range until his supply is complete — then momentum prints.

### Root value first, setup second
- His stated purpose: *"मेरा फोकस रहता है कि आप रूट वैल्यू को समझ लो — स्टॉक मार्केट काम कैसे करता है."* Understand the root *why*; the method then follows.
- Why it matters: *"जो भी सेटअप होते हैं वो डिपेंड करता है किसने बनाया और उस पर्सन का माइंडसेट कैसा है."* **His setup is calibrated to his mindset.** If your mindset is not at that level, *"वह सेटअप आपके लिए काम ना करे."* So: root value → then either his setup works for you, or you build your own. This is the session's philosophical spine.

### Trending vs sideways — the round-number behaviour differs
- **Trending market, number in the middle of a move** (e.g. 48,000): the market first **breaks out, lets buyers work, ELIMINATES them, and only then does the actual momentum print.** Mirror for a down move: **breakdown → eliminate → momentum.** *"इसमें एक-दो चक्कर मार्केट एक्स्ट्रा निकाल सकता है"* — extra rotation to flush anyone already in profit at a mid number before the real move.
- **Sideways market, number in the range:** the market takes the number **accurately** as resistance or support. If a breakout happens **by chance**, the trader who wants a position **writes a call** (or a put if below) and *"दोबारा से उसी रेंज के अंदर मार्केट ले आएगा"* — no big momentum, just rotation. The option writer is the range's enforcer.
- **The fade rule he repeats in his daily analysis:** *"ऊपर मिल जाए तो नीचे के लिए काम कर लेना, नीचे मिल जाए तो ऊपर के लिए कर लेना."* In a sideways tape you work **against** the extreme, not with a breakout.
- Stray-breakout handling: if the market comes **back under** the range after a false breakout, start working — **above-range ⇒ sell side, below-range ⇒ buy side**.
- Missed the entry? He notes the big trader's tell: after a **big fast candle** into resistance, other traders can't sell fast enough, so *"बड़ा ट्रेडर आराम से कॉल राइट करके बैठ जाता है"* — the writer's read is available before the move.

### Fast candle vs slow momentum — when does the market break a level?
- **After a big/fast candle the market does NOT need to break the level** (his examples: 47,800 resistance after a fast candle; the 46,000 support held after a fast candle). Psychology is already changed; nobody is left comfortably seated.
- **After slow momentum the market MUST break down/up once** — to eliminate the side sitting comfortably at the visible support (46,000) who *"आराम से बैठा रहता है."* *"एक बार के लिए उसे ब्रेकडाउन में कन्वर्ट कर देता है; ब्रेकडाउन करने के बाद जब वो दोबारा घूमता है, फिर परेशान नहीं करेगा."*
- So a **one-off** break-and-rotate in a sideways tape ≠ trend; but a repeated up-down-up pattern (e.g. a 47,000 breakout → down → up) is the sign that **trending momentum is starting**.

### Sideways ends — how you know
- **While sideways:** no gap-up/gap-down; important numbers get taken accurately; a stray breakout returns to range and you trade the rotation.
- **When trending momentum is coming:** gap-up/gap-down opens appear (a trend *wants* to skip the level, not fight it), or an important number in the middle gets **broken, flushed, and then a real move follows**.
- Sideways therefore has a shelf life: *"साइड वेज कब खत्म हो जाएगा — जब मार्केट 47,000 का ब्रेकआउट करेगा, फिर नीचे जाएगा, फिर ऊपर जाएगा … तब समझ लेना कि ट्रेंडिंग मोमेंटम हो सकता है."*

### Expiry-day read (his worked example)
- On the **expiry** session the market rotated completely instead of letting the 46,200-position crowd out; it **broke 46,000 directly** — because *"उनको निकालने की आवश्यकता नहीं थी"*: the profit-sitting traders believed the market would keep falling, so they stayed, and the market took their money anyway. **Gap-down opens are what trouble the buyers sitting at 47,000 resistance.**
- Sideways rule restated: *"साइड वेज मार्केट में ऊपर मिल जाए तो प्रॉफिट बुकिंग, नीचे मिल जाए तो फिर से बाइंग, और फिर से ऊपर सेल."*

### Keep permanently
1. **Sideways is a demand–supply state, not randomness.** Balanced supply/demand ⇒ trend; imbalance ⇒ range.
2. **Trend runs to where the big money is placed** — the other side gets eliminated, repeatedly, at each new level.
3. **Sideways has three causes:** (a) demand low *and* supply low (after a big move, nobody dares) — 2–3 days then momentum; (b) **accumulation** — demand high, no supply, big buyer ranges the market until his quantity is full; (c) **distribution** — supply high, no demand, big seller ranges it until supply is done.
4. **In a range, trade the levels — fade the extremes:** *"ऊपर मिले तो नीचे के लिए, नीचे मिले तो ऊपर के लिए."*
5. **The option writer enforces the range:** a stray breakout is written back into the range; in sideways the number is taken **accurately**, and a one-off break-and-return is a rotation, not a trend.
6. **Trending vs sideways, seen through a mid-number:** in a trend the number is broken, the side working there is **eliminated**, and only then does the real momentum print; in a range the number simply holds / reverts.
7. **Fast candle ⇒ no break required** (psychology already changed). **Slow momentum ⇒ the market must break once** to flush the traders sitting comfortably at the visible level — and then it won't trouble you again.
8. **Sideways ends when the pattern changes:** gap-up/gap-down opens appear, or a mid-number draws breakout → down → up (repeated). That is the signature of trending momentum beginning.
9. **Read the squeeze:** in accumulation the buyers look confused and sellers keep feeling *"I should sell"*; in distribution the reverse. The range is someone's unfinished business.
10. **Root value before setup.** A setup is calibrated to its maker's mindset; learn *how the market works* first, then either adopt the setup or build your own — and only fund it once it is understood.
