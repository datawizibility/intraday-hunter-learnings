### Core idea
- The session is a **chart-reading masterclass**, not a pattern library: how price actually moves, what the market's psychology is at each point, and how to *project* the next move from that.
- The stated main focus is the **distorted open** — a very large gap open (made worse when it follows a holiday). In that situation normal position-building logic breaks down, because the market has been "switched off" and re-prices over the break. Positions and stop references no longer sit where they did.
- The teachable skill is therefore: **when there is no obvious position or stop-loss set-up on the chart, you must read what the market was doing *before* the open** — the trend, who was trapped, who is in profit. The gap is only the trigger; the market's prior behaviour is the instruction.
- His central doctrine: **the market repeats its behaviour, not its chart.** A down-trend will still go down after a gap-down open — but the picture will look different. So learn *who reacts and when*, not the shape.
- He closes by dismissing support/resistance as the first thing to learn: *first* learn how the chart travels and why (the mechanism behind the momentum); S/R then becomes obvious rather than memorised.

### The distorted gap open — what it forces
- **Time decay over a holiday:** with a two-day holiday, option premiums fall hard. Traders who were *holding bought options* (calls bought on the fall / longs) come back with their position already damaged — and when the market opens with a large gap against them they must liquidate in panic. They have to *get their goods out*; profit booking is no longer available.
- **Trapped long stock/position holders:** anyone who bought near the earlier support saw a profit zone once, then faces a gap-open against them. That is where the day's panic selling comes from — not from the chart pattern.
- So the distorted open is not a random distortion: it is the mechanism that **converts already-loss-making positions into market-order exits**, and those exits are the fuel the other side trades against.
- He notes the gap-down open was *supposed* to be matched by panic selling and was not, and asks why — the answer is the prerequisite in the next section. A big gap alone does not produce panic.

### Momentum needs market orders (why some falls have no momentum)
- His explicit mechanism: **momentum appears only where market orders are placed.** If holders keep unwinding with *limit* orders position by position, price drifts — no momentum print appears.
- The green candle that forms after a gap-down comes from two separate seller groups:
  1. **Immediate stop-hit sellers** — their stop is gone, so they exit without any consideration of whether the market reverses.
  2. **Waiters** — they first watch the reaction, and only when momentum builds does their courage fail and their selling start.
- Result: *the momentum in that candle is the sum of exits (booking) + the momentum of whoever is buying*. This is the same stop-pool logic as earlier videos, but here he splits the *order type* — a refinement of "fills only happen where counterparty quantity exists" (2022-02-06).

### Panic selling has a prerequisite (the discipline point)
- A big gap-down open does **not** by itself produce panic selling. Two conditions must already exist:
  1. a **proper, well-formed prior trend/up-move** (not a single day's print) — the market must have had momentum first; and
  2. **participants sitting in positions** with profits that can be damaged, i.e. a side with stops and unrealised gains to protect.
- He is blunt about the timeframe: *"you cannot take yesterday's trend and call it a trend"* — the run must have lasted some days.
- **Timing anchor:** panic selling appears only **after a specific point/zone breaks** — the point whose crossing starts the down-trend. Until that line/zone is crossed there may be minor stop-making and nothing more. Before the break: no major selling. After the break: the down-trend starts and panic supply arrives.
- Corollary for instruments: **the index leads.** Panic selling will not appear inside the stocks until the *index* crosses its own line/zone — individual stock levels will not "dance" on their own until the index gets there. So watch the index level to know when the stock's move becomes real.

### Trend rules (the two sides)
| Market state | What happens at the level | What is done |
|---|---|---|
| **Up-trend** | A gap / dip brings price back toward good support | Buying is done — the pullback is bought |
| **Down-trend** | A bounce brings price back up to a resistance | **Selling** is done — not buying; every return to resistance is sold |

- His reason for the down-trend rule: whoever was caught by the positive candle sells again on the retracement, and whoever buys the bounce is trading against the trend's inventory.
- He repeats it as a mandatory rule: *after a market has gone into a down-trend, whenever it comes back near a resistance, it is sold — not bought.*
- He also flags the **engineered round number**: the people who move the market push price *slightly past* the round/psychology level, so that the psychology point actually breaks and selling is then visible. Expect the small overshoot; it is deliberate, not a failure of the level.

### Rare prints are not rules
- He identifies a candle on the chart that would have changed the whole read if it had formed, and says explicitly such prints are **rare — perhaps one or two in one or two years**.
- His instruction: do **not** build your process on that rare print. He asks you to study how the market *mostly* behaves.
- Related line: *"the market does not burn by words"* — a print or a narrative does not stop the market; it continues in its own direction regardless of what is said about it.

### Learning sequence he prescribes
- Do not start with support/resistance. First learn **how the chart travels**, how the market delivers momentum, and **what is actually behind the move** — then the S/R read stops being a struggle.
- Self-reliance is stated as a condition of progress: as long as you act only on what others say, nothing works. You must analyse the chart yourself.
- He uses the prior-trend + gap + trapped-position sequence as the worked example and offers the multi-day stock example to show that the same logic runs in a single stock as in the index.

### Keep permanently
1. **The market repeats its behaviour, not its chart.** Learn who reacts and when — not the shape of the previous example.
2. **Panic selling has prerequisites:** a well-formed multi-day trend *and* participants sitting in damaged-profit positions. A big gap-down open alone is not panic.
3. **Momentum requires market orders.** Position unwinding done with limit orders shows drift, not momentum — the print tells you which is happening.
4. **Split the sellers:** immediate stop-hit exiters (deaf to reversal) versus waiters who sell only when momentum builds. The second group is why momentum accelerates.
5. **Trend decides the level's job:** up-trend → the dip toward support is bought; down-trend → every return to resistance is **sold**, never bought.
6. **Wait for the point that starts the move.** Panic selling appears only after the anchoring line/zone breaks; before the break there is only minor stop-making.
7. **The index leads the stock.** A stock's panic move waits for the index to cross its own line/zone — do not expect stock-level panic before the index gets there.
8. **Round-number psychology breaks are engineered.** Expect a small push past the round level; that overshoot is the mechanism, not a failure of the level.
9. **A distorted open (large gap, especially after a holiday) forces trapped holders to liquidate** — time-decayed option buyers and trapped longs must get out, and their exits are the other side's fuel.
10. **Do not build rules from rare prints** (extreme candles, ~1–2 per 1–2 years). Trade how the market *mostly* behaves, and analyse it yourself rather than on others' word.
