### Core idea
- A weekly recap in which he re-reads five of the week's charts and reports **one big win and two full losses** — including one loss on a chart he was *sure* about. The recap's spine is not the wins; it is the answer to *why the losses did not become damage*: he let the market decide the outcome instead of deciding it himself.
- **You are the trader; profit and loss are the market's decision.** Doing the analysis and then sitting inside the trade waiting for the market to comply is the mistake, in both loss and profit.
- **A lower-risk read: the seated side is not the target.** When a run has already flushed a side (or left it far in profit), the remaining pool — the opposite side with fresh stop-losses — is who the market is coming for. This drives the open-type plan.
- **The week's genuinely new observation:** when **two** indices have already taken their breakout (round number or closing price), the **third (lagging) index does not take its own** — and the reason is crowding, not weakness.

### Case 1 — a positive run flushed, then continuous selling (the sellers were already gone)
- Chart: a strong positive momentum, then an equal amount of selling. His read of the crowd: when the market goes up and then turns down, traders' mindset does not change fast enough — they only become fully positive near the top, and they do not buy the *breakout* itself.
  - *"जब मार्केट ऊपर जाकर नीचे आता है या फिर नीचे जाकर ऊपर जाता है तो वहां पे ट्रेडर का माइंडसेट इतना चेंज नहीं हो पाता"*
- They then average down, hoping it comes back — so the market falls further, and retail's selling portion was never properly built. The benefit he took was exactly that: because the crowd was focused on the buying side, the continuous selling had no counterparty resisting it.
- **Conclusion he drew:** do not target these sellers — they are not the fresh pool. On the next day's **gap-up / flat-to-gap-down**, follow the same downward momentum:
  - *"अगर हमें गैप अप मिलता है, फ्लैट टू गैप डाउन ओपन होता है। इसी मोमेंटम को हम कंटिन्यू फॉलो कर लेंगे"*
- Execution: the next day opened gap-down with rejection from the first candles; he built the **put** side and captured a large momentum move. He notes the gap-down was on the larger side, but he worked with the market and booked a good profit — a "very simple" trade once the psychology was understood.

### Case 2 — the closing-price support that turned (a loss taken in full confidence)
- Chart: continuous selling again. The plan was flat-to-gap-down ⇒ go *with* the market, because anyone who sold into the retracement has already run — or is deep in profit — so they are not the target:
  - *"जो भी यहां पे सेल वगैरह करके बैठा है, रिट्रेसमेंट वगैरह में भाग गया होगा या फिर काफी अच्छे प्रॉफिट में बैठा है। तो इसको हमें अपना टारगेट नहीं बनाना है"*
- For a gap-up there was **no plan** at all that day. The market gave only a slight gap-up (Sensex and Nifty a little more), and his read was that few sellers would still be holding on. No momentum appeared in Nifty/Sensex, so he expected a small range.
- He did **not** trade the open. He waited until Bank Nifty started **taking support at the closing price**, and only then built the put side:
  - *"जब ये क्लोजिंग प्राइस का सपोर्ट लेना शुरू कर दिया था ना तब मुझे लगा कि मैं ठीक समझ के बैठा हूं"*
- The breakdown did happen — but it did not extend, it turned, and he took a full loss. His closing line on it: wherever you *expect*, the market does something else:
  - *"जहां पे उम्मीद करते हो वहीं पे मार्केट कुछ ना कुछ अलग करता है"*
- **The counterfactual he spells out is the lesson:** had he instead sat waiting — the sellers' stops have to come, if not from here then from there — or averaged, **both** would have been wrong. Accept the loss the market gave and leave.

### Never sit in a trade (his most repeated line of the week)
- *"बैठे नहीं रहना है। मार्केट ने आपको लॉस दिया उसको आप स्वीकार करके चले गए। प्रॉफिट दिया उसको आपने मार्केट के अंदर एग्री किया और आप निकल गए"*
- *"ट्रेडर मार इसलिए नहीं खाता कि उसे ट्रेड करना नहीं आता। ट्रेडर मार इसलिए खाता है कि वो ट्रेडिंग में बैठा रहता है"* — and the profit side is the same instruction: *"वह लॉस में बैठा रहेगा। प्रॉफिट में भी नहीं बैठे रहना चाहिए"*
- His explanation of the "sure" trade that lost: knowing a lot about the trade does not make it safe. Pain the *next day* is normal — the benefit of leaving a losing day alone showed up as a good profit the following day.
- *"एक ट्रेड के पीछे चिपक के मत बैठ जाओ"* — do not glue yourself behind one trade, and do not try to clear a whole week inside one day.

### Case 3 — expiry day: flat-to-gap-down ⇒ buy, gap-up ⇒ sell
- Chart: a sudden turn, a light breakdown, then an attempt upward. His pool read: if a few buyers are still sitting, their stops will be **below 56,000** — so those buyers are not the target.
- The plan he states explicitly, as a two-sided open gate:
  - *"फ्लैट टू गैप डाउन में हम क्या करना चाहते थे? बाय करना चाहते थे। हां, गैप अप में हम मार्केट के अंदर सेलिंग करना चाहते थे"* — in a gap-up, sell, on the possibility that the market dips to trouble the buyers.
- That day was **expiry for both Bank Nifty and Nifty**, so he expected no big momentum — an upper and a lower range — and therefore took the buy when the market came to him at the lower end. What followed was a big momentum and a big profit, on a trade he expected little from.
- Framing: *"आप मार्केट के ऊपर छोड़ दो"* — take the risk, leave the outcome to the market. Sideways, momentum — the market chooses.

### ⭐ Case 4 — when TWO indices take the breakout, the THIRD refuses
- Setup (28 April): Sensex and Nifty went up hard and **both** took their breakout — Nifty worked straight off its closing-price cross, and both had a **round number available at 77,000**. Then the turn came and Bank Nifty sold off far more than the "small range" he had expected.
- Setup (27 April, the one that seeded the rule): Nifty and Sensex were rising fast off round-number breakouts — *"इवन उन्होंने दोनों के पास राउंड नंबर अवेलेबल था 77,000 का"* — while Nifty worked directly off its **24,000** breakout: *"अगर आप निफ़ी को देखोगे 27 अप्रैल को तो यहां पर भी डायरेक्टली 24,000 का ब्रेकआउट होके काम होना शुरू हो चुका था"*
- The rule he states, and the level it applies to:
  - *"अगर दो इंडेक्स ने ब्रेकआउट कर दिया है तो थर्ड इंडेक्स जो पीछे रहता है यानी कि बैंक निफ्टी वो 56500 के लेवल का ब्रेकआउट नहीं कर पाएगा"*
- **Why** the third one refuses — this is the mechanism, and it is a crowd mechanism, not a weakness:
  - *"तो थर्ड इंडेक्स में ब्रेकआउट नहीं होता है। क्यों नहीं होता है? क्योंकि वहां पे दूसरे लोग बाय कर नहीं पाते तो वो क्या करते हैं? बैंक निफ्टी में आकर बाय करना शुरू कर देते हैं"* — traders who missed the first two breakouts pile into the lagging index expecting *its* breakout; that expectation is the fuel that fails.
- He was positioned for the **positive** side and used the rule to wait — then, when the market came down, took closing-price support and went up, he built the **put** side and booked a good profit. His one caveat: the market *can* occasionally print a sudden rejection then a sharp move up, but small momentum will not sustain a breakout — it falls back and has to change psychology before it can really pass.
- Process note he attaches: seeing the chart once is not seeing everything — re-read the situation at the point where you are building the entry and ask whether the market *can* go up from there.

### Case 5 — the missed level invalidated the target (56,500)
- Chart: continuous selling, so sellers were seated and the plan was a **buy** targeting them: downside had no buyer stops, seller stops were up top. He was sure — but the trade only works *through* the level:

- Positive momentum did come but never crossed the level; then the market turned hard and the position went to a full loss. His own audit of the mistake is one line: *"हमने 500 के लेवल को मिस किया"* — the level was the precondition for the whole idea, and it was not checked as a precondition. His description of how the level sat fixed inside the trade: *"जब तक 500 के लेवल का क्रॉस ना हो"* — no crossing, no exit.
- The generalisation: a right direction with a wrong entry still loses — *"कभी-कभी क्या होता है? हम डायरेक्शन के अकॉर्डिंग सही बैठे होते हैं लेकिन एंट्री गलत बन जाता है"*

### Stop-loss hunting is only as good as the psychology behind it
- He is open that he follows stop-loss hunting seriously — *"मैं भी एसएल हंटिंग को बड़े तरीके से फॉलो करता हूं"* — and the week gave him the caution that comes with it.
- *"एसएल हंटिंग का यह मतलब नहीं होता कि आप बैठे रहो"* — SL hunting is not a licence to sit and refuse to be wrong. The big traders know the background psychology; retail frequently does not, and one missed detail (who traded, at what size, in that momentum) is enough to be wrong.
- The protection is a pre-committed loss barrier: *"इसीलिए एक लॉस का हमने बैरियर बना रखा है। एक लिमिट बना रखी है कि अपना ट्रेड वारा कट करके चले जाएंगे"* — *वारा* is ASR damage for **वगैरह**.
- Verdict line: *"अगर आपकी साइकोलॉजी सही होगी, एसएल हंटिंग आपको मार नहीं खाने देगा"* — correct psychology makes SL hunting protective; the "I cannot be wrong, if not from here then from there" version is what caused his own big damage.

### Keep permanently
1. **You trade; the market decides the P&L.** Do the analysis, take the risk, then leave the outcome to the market — deciding the result in advance is what produces both the bad hold and the bad exit.
2. **Never sit in a trade** — neither in a loss nor in a profit. "The stops have to come, if not from here then from there" is not a plan; averaging on that belief is the same error twice.
3. **Accept the day's loss and trade the next day.** One bad day is not cleared inside one day; the benefit of leaving it alone showed up the following session.
4. **Write the plan as open-type → pool, never as direction.** Flat / small gap / gap-up each select *which side is seated*; the side is the plan.
5. **Never target a side that is already flushed or already deep in profit.** Look for the pool with fresh stops — that is the market's fuel.
6. **A level is a precondition, not a detail.** If the idea only works *through* 56,500, then no cross means no exit-free trade, regardless of how good the analysis felt. Missing the level is the whole loss.
7. **Two indices already broken out ⇒ the lagging third does not take its own breakout.** Traders who missed the first two crowd the lagging index — that crowding, not weakness, is why it fails.
8. **A right direction with a wrong entry still loses.** Audit the entry, not just the direction.
9. **SL hunting without correct psychology is the biggest damage source.** Correct psychology makes it protective; "I cannot be wrong" makes it a reason to sit inside a losing trade.
10. **Carry a pre-committed loss barrier.** One limit, decided in advance, that cuts the trade and walks — the barrier is what makes the hunting survivable.
