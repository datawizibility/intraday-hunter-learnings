### Core idea
- A trade is **mostly mental**. *"अपना जो 80 पर जो काम होता है, वो माइंडसेट के अंदर ही हो जाता है; और जो 20 पर काम होता है, वो हमें आउटसाइड नजर आता है."* The **80%** (chart read → plan → entry & quantity → target and stop arithmetic) happens **inside the mind**; only the **20%** (the physical entry/exit) is visible. Leave the 80% incomplete and the trade breaks.
- ⭐ **The single stabiliser: decide BOTH the profit and the loss before entry.** Deciding only one leaves the mind unstable and the account exposed.
- **Patience** is redefined: not "waiting a long time", but **trading only when the setup has formed** — and then deciding profit and loss.

### The 80/20 rule of a trade
- Inside (80%): reading the chart, forming the plan (*"मुझे यहाँ एंट्री करनी है, इतनी क्वांटिटी लेनी है"*), and computing the profit/loss at a level. Only when this is **complete in the mind** do you take the physical entry.
- The failure mode when the 80% is left half-done: you had decided **5k quantity** but took **10k** (or cut to **2k**) — *"ये आपके लिए प्रॉब्लम क्रिएट कर सकता है."*

### Decide BOTH profit and loss (the core behaviour)
- Many traders decide the **loss** (*"लॉस डिसाइड करना है"* on repeat) but skip the **profit** — *"चलो देख लेंगे"*. Then the first red candle inside an up-move triggers a **panic book**.
- Others decide the **profit** but not the **loss** — and this is where the market's **biggest disease** comes from: *"चित की कमी"* — running out of money.
- **His option rule:** target ≈ **15–20% of the premium** (*"टारगेट लगभग 15 से 20 पर जो प्रीमियम के मुझे मिलते हैं, उसके अकॉर्डिंग"*). Near expiry it may be a little bigger/smaller. More than that = a **positive** impact; less = a **negative** impact.
- **Worked example (his):** profit running **₹1 lakh** → a big red candle cut it to **~₹60,000** → the market turned back → he exited with **≥₹3 lakh** profit. Without a decided target you would book **neither ₹1 lakh nor ₹50k** and walk away with **₹25k** — *"वो कोई काम का नहीं; इससे अच्छा लॉस हो जाता."*
- **Why it works:** once profit and loss are fixed, the mind's oscillating process **stabilises into a straight line**; fear reduces as experience grows, and the wave flattens.

### Patience = trade only when the setup forms
- He entered a **put trade 2–3 minutes after the 9:15 open** — *"तो यह पैशंस है मेरे साथ, या मैं ओवर-कॉन्फिडेंट होकर पहले ट्रेड ले रहा हूँ?"* His answer: it is fine **if the setup was formed**. If the setup was not formed, *"चाहे 10 बजे हो, चाहे 11 बजे हो"* it is wrong.
- **Option-selling setup example:** decide the trade **window** — *"10 बजे से लेकर 2 बजे तक नॉर्मली मार्केट थोड़ा स्टेबल रहता है, तो वहाँ मैं काम करने की कोशिश करूंगा."* That window **is** the setup; entering even one minute before it is the mistake.
- **Second meaning of patience:** after the entry, **decide the profit and the loss** — that is the patience that holds the mind still.

### ⭐ Round-number target booking
- His put trade: entered, market wobbled, held around **53,000**. Many traders then start thinking *"ब्रेकआउट होने वाला है"* or *"बहुत अच्छा रेजिस्टेंस ले रहा है"* and **add quantity on hope** — that becomes a **habit** and a problem.
- **The rule:** *"जब राउंड नंबर अवेलेबल हो — ऊपर हो, नीचे हो, कहीं भी — टारगेट बुकिंग कर लेना चाहिए."* When a round number is near **and a decent profit is visible**, do **not** be greedy.
- **Worked instances (his own week):** call position → **52,000 breakout** → he booked the target (market ran further; that day could have matched a month's profit — *"इसका रिग्रेट नहीं करना है"*); put → **51,200 breakdown** → booked; put → **52,500 breakdown** → booked. Each time *"अगर निकलते नहीं, तो मार्केट कंप्लीट घूम जाता."*
- **Closing-price rule:** when the **closing price breaks**, **book the target** — *"जैसे क्लोजिंग प्राइस का ब्रेक डाउन हुआ, हमने टारगेट बुकिंग कर लिया."*
- The **habit of NOT booking at round numbers** is what turns into the big problem; booking there is the profitable habit and does not need regret.

### Don't chase others — patience + effort decide the outcome
- *"कोई कितना ही प्रॉफिट बना रहा है … उसके पीछे नहीं जाना है; हमें अपनी तरफ देखना है."* Chasing breaks the patience system.
- **Income-first, patience-carrying trader beats the urgent one:** a person with **₹10 lakh and a job** doing it **part-time with patience** is more likely to succeed than a person with **₹1 crore** trying to double/triple in 2–4 months. *"जो इंसान पैशेंस से काम करेगा, उसको मार्केट पैसा देगा ही देगा — यह रूल है."*
- **Learning curve:** patience + effort can compress learning to **4–5 months** (or 6 months / a year depending on mindset).
- **Trade count:** decide the day's number of trades (**1 / 2 / 5**) and stay inside it. *"आपका सेटअप बन नहीं रहा, बीस ट्रेड कर रहे हो — वो गलत होगा."* Trade less: intraday money is small, and option money is lost faster. His 2025-warning: *"ट्रेड थोड़ा कम कीजिए."*
- **Don't chase money:** *"जितना पैसे के पीछे भागो, स्टॉक मार्केट से उतना ही दूर होते जाओगे."*
- Those who **quit a job to jump in** often had to **return to a job**; the patient part-timer's market life is the fruitful one.

### Keep permanently
1. **80% of a trade is mental** — complete the plan (level, size, target, stop) *inside* before taking the 20% physical entry.
2. **Decide BOTH the profit and the loss before entry** — deciding only one destabilises the mind; the missing-loss case breeds *"पैसे की कमी"*.
3. Set an **option target** (his: **≈15–20% of premium**); a decided target replaces impulse booking.
4. **Patience = trade only when the setup has formed** — an early entry *inside* a ready setup is fine; entering *before* the setup is not.
5. **Book the target at round numbers** — do not add quantity on hope; not doing so becomes a habit that costs you.
6. **A closing-price break is a booking trigger.**
7. **Judge monthly** — a single day can be a fluke; never chase another trader's profit.
8. **Decide the day's trade count (1/2/5) and stay inside it** — over-trading is not a method.
9. **Patience + effort beat capital size** — a patient part-timer's ₹10 lakh can outperform a hurried ₹1 crore.
10. **Don't chase money; the market pays the patient.**
