### Core idea
- The session exists because of one repeated audience question: *"काफी यह क्वेश्चन किया था कि सर हमें भी बता दीजिए कि आप कौन सा इंडिकेटर यूज करें ताकि हम भी यूज कर पाएं"*. He answers it — **reluctantly and on purpose**.
- **Indicators can make money — but only inside a method.** Against the common claim that nobody earns from indicators, his position is explicit: *"काफी लोग यह भी मानते हैं कि इंडिकेटर से पैसा नहीं बनता लेकिन मैं उन लोगों में से नहीं हूं"* and *"मैं यह मानता हूं कि अगर आप सही में सेट का यूज करोगे तो आप इंडिकेटर से भी पैसा बना सकते हो"*. The condition is method + discipline, not the indicator: *"इंडिकेटर से पैसा तो बनता है लेकिन उसके लिए आपको एक मेथड अपनाना पड़ेगा"*.
- **He names his set but hides it on the chart.** The indicators are not secret knowledge; the *hiding* is deliberate — they belong to his portfolio-management team, not to his intraday work: *"जो इंडिकेटर है अपना जो पोर्टफोलियो मैनेजमेंट जो टीम है वह इसको यूज करते हैं"*.
- **One indicator gives false entries; two or three cancel them out.** The practical rule of the session is disagreement-as-filter, not confirmation-as-filter.
- **The indicator supplies the entry; the trader supplies the exit.** The SL and the target are set from your own risk-reward — if the indicator is left in charge of the exit, the trade is not yours.
- Framing line of the session: *"देखिए मार्केट में प्रॉपर्टी का गेम है और मार्किट में डिसिप्लिन का आगे हैं"* — a probability game, fronted by discipline.

### The false-entry filter (use two or three, not one)
- Diagnosis first: *"इंडिकेटर मैं आपको प्रॉब्लम सबसे ज्यादा तब नजर आता है"* — the failure mode is a **false entry**, printed on a retracement.
- The fix is cross-checking, not more conviction: *"लेकिन अगर आप थोड़ा सा दो या तीन इंडिकेटर यूज करोगे तो उसी तो फोर्स एंट्री होगी वह निकल जाएगी"*.
- The second benefit is fewer whipsaw entries: *"और जो आपको बार-बार स्ट्रेट करने में दिक्कत आती है वह भी निकल जाएगी और आपको एक पर्टिकुलर होकर एक एंट्री मिलेगी"* — the filter tightens entry count to one clean setup.
- Worked example he narrates: one indicator (e.g. RSI-type) says *go long*; a second (e.g. a supertrend) has not activated yet — the second one is what keeps you out of the false entry.
- **Do not hand the decision to the indicator**: *"आप इंडिकेटर पर यह तय करने लग जाते हो इंडिकेटर भी एकजुट करने लग जाते हो तो वहां पर आपका काम बन नहीं पाता"* — the indicator is a filter, it is not the decision.

### Who owns the exit (the indicator gives the entry, you give the SL and target)
- Entry from the indicator is fine; the exit must be re-owned by the trader: *"एंट्री होने के बाद 20 और रिवॉर्ड होगा वह इंडिकेटर से होटल ना करके आप अपने अकॉर्डिंग हैंडल करना शुरू कर दोगे"* (⚠️ `होटल` is ASR noise; context = *hold*, i.e. do not let the indicator hold the trade).
- The sizing language is stated in a simple rupee illustration: *"आप यहां पर ₹10 का रिस्क ले वह बीस रुपए का प्रॉफिट ले रहे हो"* — risk ₹10 to make ₹20, i.e. a **1:2** relationship held by the trader, not by the study.
- Why the exit matters more than the entry: if you keep managing size and risk yourself, a single wrong entry does not end the position — *"लेकिन आपका एयरसेल का लिमिट अभी भी रहता है मार्केट के अंदर तो आपका दोबारा से संपन्न होगा"* (⚠️ `एयरसेल` = ASR for *SL*; `संपन्न` is garbled — the sense is a re-fill / profit resuming).
- The same "own your own exit" idea is what makes trailing workable later; here it is only an entry/exit split.

### The accuracy ladder (price action vs indicators vs candles)
- His ranking is stated as a gradient, not a rule: *"आप अगर सपोर्ट रेजिस्टेंस को फॉलो करेंगे थोड़ा सा ज्यादा मिलेगा को इंडिकेटर को फॉलो करोगे वापिस थोड़ा कम मिलेगा"*.
- Then extended to candles: *"पैसा तो बनेगा लेकिन वहां पर एक्यूरेसी ऊपर नीचे रहता है"* — the money can be made either way; what moves is accuracy.
- Therefore mastery is a choice, not a checklist: *"तो डिपेंड करता है कि आप अपने आपको मास्टर किसमें करना चाहते हो"* — pick one and master it, then the other does no harm.

### Keep permanently
1. **An indicator is a filter, never the decision.** *"आप इंडिकेटर पर यह तय करने लग जाते हो … तो वहां पर आपका काम बन नहीं पाता"*.
2. **Use two or three indicators so they can disagree** — a lone indicator gives false entries; the disagreement cancels them and leaves one particular entry.
3. **The fix for false entries is cross-checking, not more conviction.** Tightening the entry to one clean setup also kills the repeated-whipsaw problem.
4. **Entry from the tool, exit from your own risk-reward.** The SL and target are yours (his illustration: ₹10 risk to ₹20 reward); never let the study hold the trade.
5. **Know the difference between which indicator and which method.** He answers the first unwillingly because only the second pays: method + discipline is the condition attached to the disclosure.
6. **Indicators are neither useless nor sufficient** — he rejects the claim that nobody earns from them, and equally rejects using them without method.
7. **Keep your own visible chart work (support/resistance) in the loop** — on his own ladder it sits slightly above indicator-following on accuracy.
8. **Pick one thing and master it**, instead of splitting attention between price action, indicators and candles: *"तो डिपेंड करता है कि आप अपने आपको मास्टर किसमें करना चाहते हो"*.
9. **The session's frame is probability fronted by discipline** — not a promise, and not a system to copy wholesale.
10. **Hiding indicators is a legitimate choice** — an unexplained indicator on someone else's chart is noise to you, and confusion about "which indicator for which job" is a real cost.
