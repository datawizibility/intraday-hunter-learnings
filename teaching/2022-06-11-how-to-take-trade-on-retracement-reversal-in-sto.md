### Core idea
- The session answers one question — **how do you tell a retracement from a reversal, and how do you trade each?** — and the answer is not a pattern. Two charts that look **almost identical on candle shape** are shown side by side: one was a reversal, one was a retracement. ⭐ **The shape does not decide. The participants decide.** This is 2022-06-05's "read the other person" applied to the single hardest live decision.
- ⭐ He opens by naming the **demonstration gap**: in a live session he tells you some things *one minute before* and some *two minutes after*, and when both are not put together the read is lost — *"कुछ डॉट मिला नहीं पाते।"* So this session is his own head-work made explicit: *"वो बातें जो मैं अपने आप से करता हूं"* — the reasoning he normally does silently.
- ⭐ The working order he states: **before taking an entry or setting a target you must already know how far the market can go up and how far it can come down.** Entry, target and stop are all downstream of that one number — and that number comes from where the stops are, not from the shape.

### The first control: support and resistance
- His base statement: **the market's lower points work as support, the highs work as resistance** — the level framework of 2022-03-06 carried forward unchanged.
- What he refuses to do with it: take it as a *reason*. Support held, therefore long — no. The framework tells you **where to look**; the participants tell you **whether it will hold**.

### ⭐ The retracement test — the four things he actually checks
- ⭐ **1. Who built the position, and how well.** On the retracement he is reading, *"जो सेलिंग कर रहा है"* — the side that was selling — built that position **deliberately**, not accidentally. A considered position is not abandoned at the first pullback.
- ⭐ **2. Distance = safety from the hunt.** *"मार्केट में डिस्टेंस है, वह बहुत ही ज्यादा है"* — the distance is large, so the market **cannot reach them to track them**. Large distance ⇒ that participant cannot be hunted today ⇒ the pullback is not their forced exit.
- ⭐ **3. They already have their target.** The position side booked at a **round number** (*"उसने टारगेट आलरेडी राउंड नंबर ले रखा है"*) ⇒ *"वहां पर उसे डरने की कोई चिंता नहीं"* — they have no fear left. Fearless money does not capitulate on a pullback.
- ⭐ **4. Did the market take support where it should not have?** This is the trap tell he actually acts on. If the market truly had to go up it should have **printed the breakout candle first** and *then* retraced — that would have been sustainable. Instead the market **took support** at a level and trapped the people who bought it: *"अगर मार्केट को अपसाइड जाना चाहिए था… पहले ब्रेक डाल देता, फिर [ट्रेंड] सस्टेनेबल था। लेकिन मार्केट ने ऐसा नहीं किया, यहां ट्रैपिंग किया।"* **A support taken instead of a break printed is bait, not demand.**
- From those four he draws the conclusion: *"यहां से मार्केट में एक रिवर्सल आ सकता है"* — a reversal can come from here; and this is a **retracement**, not the start of a new trend.

### ⭐ The 100–150 point retracement rule
- ⭐ On the trade he walked through, the market had **already delivered its momentum** (he describes a move of roughly **300–350 points**). His rule for entering the continuation side: *"अगर मुझे [कॉल साइड] का पोजीशन बनाना होता तो मुझे लगता है कि सौ से डेढ़ सौ प्वाइंट मार्केट को [रिट्रेस] करना चाहिए"* — **the market should first give back 100–150 points.**
- ⭐ And the discipline on top of it: *"यह वाला प्लान नहीं किया था कि यह पूरा नीचे आ जाएगा… वह हमारा प्लान रहता है कि सौ से डेढ़ सौ [प्वाइंट] मार्केट में रिट्रेसमेंट चल जाए, वहां पर हम डिसाइड कर पाएं कि मुझे टारगेट बड़ा करना है"* — **he never plans a size for the whole move; he plans a retracement band, and re-decides the target after the band arrives.** That is 2022-04-24's "unreadable moves are legitimate" and 2022-04-10's "no fixed lookback" finally given a **number-shaped workflow**.
- He adds the mechanics of why waiting is better: if he had taken the position **before** the print, the print itself would have triggered that first side's exit (*"यह वाला निकल जाता, क्योंकि यह सोचता कि जो मैंने सोचा था वह मार्केट करने वाला"*), the level's breakout would have come, and a reversal could have followed — target would not have been met. **The buyer who arrives early pays for the starter's exit.**

### ⭐ Target booking when there are no stops ahead
- ⭐ His exit rule, stated flatly: *"मार्केट में अगर [कोई लेवल] डायरेक्टली टेस्ट करे और नीचे का स्टॉपलॉस नहीं है, तो वहां पॉसिबल है कि रिवर्सल या मार्केट साइडवेज़ हो सकता है — तो वहां बुकिंग कर सकते हो।"*
- ⭐ Reading: **a continuation needs remaining inventory ahead of it.** When the next level is tested and the stop inventory beyond it is thin or absent, the *reason for further movement* has expired — so booking is correct even under your own stated target. This is 2022-04-24's fuel-bounded exit, restated as an explicit test.
- He applies it to a trade where the target **was met** and he still did not extend it: *"दोनों पॉसिबल थी — इसलिए टारगेट को ज्यादा नहीं बढ़ाया."*

### The gap-fill as a cut level
- ⭐ The one operational statement about gaps in this session: *"कौन सा लेवल है? जब कोई गैप फिल हो जाए — जहां पर मार्केट दिन गया — उसको प्रेस करे, तब जाकर अपनी पोजीशन काट सकते हैं."* — **where a gap fills and where the market had gone, pressing that level is where positions get cut.**
- 📌 This is the first **use** given to the gap-fill idea that 2022-06-05 recorded only as a low-fidelity lead ("people perceive a filled gap will act as resistance"). The perception has become a **stop location**. ⚠️ The ASR sentence is compressed and the level is unnamed — recorded as a rule-shape, not a level.

### Intraday's structural advantage, and the trap sequence
- ⭐ *"यही एक इंट्राडे में काम करने का बेनिफिट होता है कि आप कहीं भी किसी प्रकार की पोजीशन बना सकते हैं, चार्ट को देखकर"* — the intraday case is that **you can build the position from anywhere in the day once the chart prints the read**, instead of being committed to an open thesis.
- ⭐ He states the trap sequence as a standing expectation: *"मार्केट में ब्रेकआउट हो जाए तो उसका रीटेस्ट का वेट कीजिए"* — after the good break, the market **comes back, tests the last time, and takes the stops** of the early buyers; only then does the real momentum arrive. When his own first read did not confirm, he **waited and re-decided** instead of defending it.

### Discipline and the homework
- ⭐ *"डिसिप्लिन मेंटेन किया — और डिसिप्लिन ही आपको प्रॉफिट बना कर देता है."*
- ⭐ Instruction does not transfer, again: *"केवल मेरे सिखाने से आप नहीं सीख सकते… अपने आप पर कॉन्फिडेंस लाना पड़ेगा, [चार्ट] पर देखना पड़ेगा कि यह चीजें एक्चुअली काम करती हैं"* — he cannot explain every minute of his own reasoning, so the confidence has to be built chart-by-chart by the trader.

### Keep permanently
1. **The shape does not identify the retracement; the participants do.** Two identical candle structures can be a reversal and a retracement — ask who built the position, not what the pattern is called.
2. **Before any entry or target, know how far the market can go up and how far it can come down.** Entry, target and stop are all derived from that one number.
3. **A pullback is safe to buy only when the position behind it cannot be hunted**: large distance to their stops, a deliberately built position, and a target already booked at a round number (no fear left to trigger a capitulation).
4. **Support taken instead of a breakout printed is a trap, not demand.** If the market wanted to go up it would print the break first and retrace after.
5. **After a big momentum print, require the retracement before entering the continuation — a stated band (he uses 100–150 points), not a hope.**
6. **Never plan the whole move.** Plan the retracement band, then re-decide the target once the band arrives.
7. **Entering before the retracement pays for the first side's exit.** The starter's forced exit is exactly what pushes price to take your stop.
8. **When a level is tested directly and no stop inventory remains beyond it, book** — the reason for further movement has expired, and reversal or sideways is live.
9. **After a good breakout, wait for the retest**; the market returns to test the last time and takes the early buyers' stops before the real momentum arrives.
10. **Intraday's advantage is that the position can be built from anywhere once the chart prints the read** — but the read still has to be produced by you; the teaching can only give direction.
