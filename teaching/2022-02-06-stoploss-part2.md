### Core idea
- Part 1 asked *whose stop-loss sits where*. Part 2 asks the same question at a different level of the market: **which level the crowd reacts at, what sentiment is doing, and what the trader does with his own stop once the position exists.**
- The Part-1 mechanic is restated in Part 2's own words: the large candles are *"केवल और केवल स्टॉपलॉस"* — only and only stop-losses. No news, no "bank momentum", no wave of real buying: *"ऐसा कुछ नहीं होता, मार्किट में केवल और केवल स्टॉपलॉस मिलते हैं"*.
- Part 2's chosen conclusion is **de-complication**: *"हमें ज्यादा दिमाग लगाने की आवश्यकता नहीं… जितना आप ट्रेडिंग को कंपलेक्स करोगे उतना आप पैसा नहीं बना पाओगे"*; *"नार्मल चीजों पर काम करो, सिंपल रखो ट्रेडिंग"*.
- The session moves from **identification → mitigation**: he explicitly pivots to *"इसके बचने के क्या उपाय हैं"* (what are the ways to protect yourself from this), which is the second half of the video (sentiment, position size, stop discipline).

### Scope stated up front — intraday was last week, positional is added today
- Part 1 said *"यहां पर हम केवल इंट्राडे के लिए बात करेंगे, पोजिशनल में नहीं जाएंगे आज"*. Part 2 opens the other way: intraday has already been covered, so today positional is brought in — *"पहले मैं पोजीशन… हम बात करेंगे थोड़ा आज क्योंकि इंट्राडे में तो मैंने पहले भी बात की थी"*, *"पोजीशन और… का भी देख लीजिए"*.
- Same SL logic, same charts; what changes is **whose stop-loss is being counted**: an intraday trader's candle-level stop vs a positional holder's premium / point stop.

### The "psychology level" — his level type of this session
- Definition as spoken: if the market gives a **breakout above the psychology level**, the market stays bullish; if it comes to the **downside** of it, a bearish move becomes likely — *"उसकी अगर मार्किट ब्रेकआउट दे तो मार्किट में तेजी रहती है, और अगर उसके डाउन साइड आ जाता है तो… मंदी आने की संभावना होती है"*.
- He presents this as something he **heard/saw on social media** rather than a rule he authored — i.e. it is described as the crowd's rule, which is exactly why the crowd's stops sit around it.
- The case: after the Budget the market approached the psychology level, everyone read it as "market will go up" and bought — *"सभी ने यहां पर बाय-बाय-बाय"* — and the market then **fully reversed**, eating that crowd's stops. His stated reason for the reversal is tied to a level above it (ASR-garbled — see fidelity section).
- Read with Part 1's mechanism, the psychology level is simply the place where a **large, obvious crowd parks entries and therefore stops**.

### Expiry-day mechanics (the time-of-day factor he adds)
- The reversal day was an expiry day; the market looked slightly negative, and the momentum came **after 12 o'clock** — expiry-day premium softening after noon is given as the reason the tape behaves differently.
- Positional holders' stops are described as **small / tight** (*"उनके… स्टॉपलॉस छोटे होते"*; the ASR prints "Store" where "stop-loss" belongs).
- Sequence he narrates: the market crossed the high → **new positions began forming there** (*"जब मार्किट इस हाई को क्रॉस करने लगा तो यहां पर फादर [further] पोजीशन बनना शुरू हो जाती है"*) → that fresh crowd's stops became the fuel → the market delivered a continuous reversal.
- Mirror case from earlier in the video: on a **psychology-level breakdown** the market reads "weak / bearish is coming", the crowd rebuilds in the **next expiry** — puts, short futures (*"नेक्स्ट एक्सपायरी में… पुट वगैरह… फ्यूचर वगैरह को शूट [short] किया जा सकता है"*) — and the market then reverses and gives continuous momentum into that build.

### Big candles are only stop-losses — with the identification tell
- He drops to a **1-minute chart** (*"मैं आपको वन मिनट पे ले चलता हूं"*) and points at candle size: a stop-loss-driven candle is **bigger than the timeframe you normally use**, and it carries that bullish (or bearish) momentum with it.
- That oversize candle is the tell that not only your stop, but **the whole side's stops, were consumed**.
- His diagnostic: do not invent a story for such a candle ("some bank momentum came", "there is very heavy buying") — in the market only and only stop-losses are being taken.
- Same read applied to option positions: those who built put positions had their stops eaten, and that move too was only and only stop-loss momentum.

### Why a retracement is waited for — and what you pay when it does not come
- He asks the reader's own question (why wait for a retracement?) and answers mechanically: **if the retracement does not come, the stops at the end of the move get collected anyway** — the wait is a wait for stops to become available.
- Liquidity mechanism as he states it: no sellers in an area means no stops there and **no fills** there; quantity executes **only where the counterparty quantity exists** — *"वहां पर एक्जीक्यूट होगी जहां पर [seller] अवेलेबल है"*. He explains oddly instant / "free" fills the same way: buyer-seller quantity imbalance — place a size the other side does not have and **price travels** to where that size is.
- Honest self-report: **he himself waited for the retracement and the market never gave him one** — *"मार्किट में मुझे मौका ही नहीं दिया, कंटिन्यू… मोमेंटम करता गया"*. He was not fighting the top; he wanted to buy on the retracement because the trend was good, and the market simply kept going.
- For a **positional** position he frames the job as: know how far the positional crowd's stops can be eaten — *"उसके स्टॉपलॉस कितने पॉइंट तक खा सकते हैं"* — how much premium a call holder can give up, or where the stop of a short-futures holder would sit. He insists this estimate be built **in the live market** (*"यह आपको लाइव मार्केट में फ्रंट [prep] करना होगा"*), i.e. preparation, not hindsight.

### Sentiment — how he says to read it, and the rule attached
- Rule: **never buy because of sentiment** — *"कभी भी आप सेंटीमेंट के कारण बाय ना करें"*. Example: the Budget, when everyone expected an up-move "because 2021's Budget had looked like that", and everyone was standing ready to buy.
- Sources he lists for sentiment: the **previous Budget's outcome** (market fell in this Budget → the next one carries a slightly negative sentiment; positive → positive), and **what the TV commentators are advising** ("are they telling people to buy or to sell").
- The only condition he accepts: if sentiment **and** the market are both positive, it *can* work for you somewhere — sentiment is a filter, never a trigger.

### Discipline: your own stop, your own size, and only two exits
- Once a position is on: **finalise your stop-loss and do nothing else** — *"अपना स्टॉपलॉस फाइनल कर लो… केवल जो पोज़िशन बना ली वह बना ली… अपने स्टॉपलॉस पर निकाल दिया पोजीशन को, अगर टारगेट आ गया तो टारगेट में निकल जाए"*.
- The anecdote he gives: a trader asked him to review a trade — positions in an **auto-company stock** and a **sugar** stock, with market momentum running opposite — the question was *"सर मैं क्या करूं"*. His answer is the rule: **exit at the stop-loss at the moment the chance to exit comes**; if you already believe the position is wrong, there is nothing to wait for. (The sentence is ASR-garbled; the direction is not in doubt.)
- **Fear is handled by size, not hope:** *"डर लगने लग जाए तो… अपनी पोजीशन साइज को कम कीजिए"* — reduce size; and **keep your own stop noted** so you know where the exit is, because then "there is not much to fear", and the market can be worked — but done with learning.
- Borrowed conviction is the fear generator: *"दूसरों के कहने पर ट्रेड करोगी तो आपको डर लगेगा ही"* — learn to read **your own chart** first, then trade it.
- The complexity rule: more complication, less money — *"जितना आप ट्रेडिंग को कंपलेक्स करोगे उतना आप पैसा नहीं बना पाओगे"*. His own described behaviour: he let the market move, waited until a reversal became visible, and sold where **resistance became available to him**; the rest (both the psychology-level short and the other side) had their stops eaten by the market.

### Chart-only method (the compressed version he gives near the end)
1. Mark **support / resistance** on the chart.
2. **Near support or resistance, hunt for the stop-losses** — *"कोई भी अगर सपोर्ट या रजिस्टेंस हो तो उसके नियर… आपको स्टॉपलॉस ढूंढने हैं"*.
3. If those stops are being taken and a break / breakdown prints, you can take the benefit of that move.
4. But a printed break is **not automatically valid**: a candle can break the level directly and the market can still reverse — *"मार्केट रिवर्स भी कर सकता है… मार्केट में एवरीथिंग इज़ पॉसिबल"*.
5. So attempt the trade **only where you judge it high-probability** — *"जहां पर आपको लगे कि… हाई प्रोफाइल [probability] ट्रेड मिल रहा है, वहीं पर कोशिश करें"* ("probability" is ASR'd as "प्रॉपर्टी").

### Learning method (his instruction to the viewer)
- **Never dry-run your skill in the live market** — *"कभी भी आप लाइव मार्केट में अपनी स्किल को ड्राई ना करें"*. Work on charts that have **already formed**: understand *why* that chart formed, then check the moves that came after it — prediction (projection) slowly improves that way.

### Keep permanently
1. **Big candles are only and only stop-losses** — do not invent a news or institutional story for an oversize candle; an SL candle is bigger than the timeframe you use, and that oversize print means the whole side's stops were consumed.
2. **The psychology level is a crowd level** — breakout above it / downside of it is the crowd's rule, and that is precisely why the crowd's entries and stops are parked around it. Read the level as a pool location, not as a signal.
3. **Never buy because of sentiment** — the Budget / 2021-Budget memory / TV commentary are context, not triggers; sentiment is usable only as a filter, and only when sentiment and market are both positive.
4. **Level crossing creates new positions** (a cross of the high starts fresh positions whose stops become the next fuel), and **after a psychology-level breakdown the crowd rebuilds in the next expiry** (puts / short futures) — that build becomes the next target.
5. **A retracement is waited for because the stops are there** — if the retracement never comes, the end-of-move stops get collected without you; so prepare, in the live market, the estimate of **how many points the crowd's stops can be eaten** (premium given up on calls / short-futures stop).
6. **Liquidity rule:** orders execute only where counterparty quantity exists; if it does not, price travels to where it does. This is why an instant fill and a non-fill can both happen at the same level.
7. **A printed break is not automatically valid** — the market can break a level directly and still reverse; "everything is possible", so attempt the trade only where you judge it high-probability.
8. **Two exits only: stop-loss or target.** Finalise your stop with the position and stop improvising; if you already believe the position is wrong, exit on the stop-loss at the first chance — do not wait.
9. **Fear is handled by size and by ownership:** reduce position size, keep your own stop noted, read your own chart, never trade on others' levels — and keep trading simple (the more you complicate it, the less you earn).
10. **Never dry-run your skill in the live market** — practise on charts that have already formed: understand why that chart formed, then examine the moves that followed.
