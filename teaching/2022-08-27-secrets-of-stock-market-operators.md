### Core idea

- A **week-review of the operator**, not a new tool. His own framing sentence: *"आज हम बात करने वाले हैं कि इस वीक में ऑपरेटर ने किस प्रकार काम किया"*. The week's novelty is that the operator used **both positional and intraday power wherever he got a chance** — *"जहां पर भी उसे मौका मिला उसने अपनी पावर का यूज किया है"*.
- The reason the operator had to work so hard is the crowd's new competence. Retail has become smart — *"हम यहां पर समझदार हो गए हैं"* — and now runs one mechanical rule: *"अगर मार्किट इस रजिस्टेंस के ऊपर निकलेगा तो मैं यहां पर बाइक कर लूंगा अगर मार्किट इसके नीचे देख लेगा तो मैं सेल कर लूंगा"* (`बाइक` = buy, `रजिस्टेंस` = resistance). Because the rule is public, his only route is to **build a level that triggers it and then go the other way**: *"अगर हम जितना समझदार होंगे मार्केट के अंदर उतना वह ऐसा काम करेगा"*.
- The read he teaches is deliberately **single**: where the market sits above one price point, and how much buying is stacked above it versus selling below it — *"मार्केट किसी एक पर्टिकुलर प्राइस पॉइंट के ऊपर आया हुआ है उसके बाद बाएं की कितनी प्रॉपर्टी है से लिंग की कितनी प्रॉपर्टी है"* (`बाएं`/`से लिंग` = buying / selling; `प्रॉपर्टी` = the position, ASR). Any operator, in any market, is answered by this one question.
- Three concrete rules fall out of it: **cut the position** when the market opens near the *same* level where the selling/stops showed yesterday; expect the operator to buy in the **background** and only then lift the market; and expect **momentum only** when he trades opposite the crowd.
- A separate block is about **expiry day**: that day's action is about the option writer, the writer is the one carrying the greatest risk, and the reason is behavioural — he sells cheap premium, thinks in small rupees (`1000` / `2000`), and one expiry day erases a whole month of collected premium.
- He flags a personal directional expectation of his own (`65 percent`) and closes on the one non-negotiable: the whole thing is learnable **only with chart work**.

### The operator's problem: retail learned the mechanical rule

- The teaching opens by describing what the operator *has to do* rather than what he wants: let two days of other people's money build, then cover his own and take the rest out of the market. This session he did it on both horizons.
- **The crowd's rule is the operator's constraint.** Once retail reads the obvious thing mechanically — buy above, sell below — the operator cannot simply ride it; he must **manufacture the trigger** and then stand on the other side. The sentence is a scaling law, not a one-off: *"अगर हम जितना समझदार होंगे मार्केट के अंदर उतना वह ऐसा काम करेगा"*.
- The instrument scale is deliberate — this is not a minor name being pushed around. ⚠️ In *"इंडेक्स भी छोटा नहीं है बैंक लिट्टे का"* the ASR writes Bank Nifty as `बैंक लिट्टे`; the point survives (the index is not small, yet the manipulation is still visible in it), so it is recorded as the clip's one mention of a *named* index — and it is nowhere near any number.
- He also pre-empts the reader's objection: it is not only one hand and not only one index — ⚠️ the clause *"वह किसी एक इंडेक्स के अंदर हो रहे हैं"* is ASR-damaged and its polarity is not recoverable, so nothing is inferred from it beyond "the activity is spread across instruments".
- His conclusion for the learner is that this is *readable*, not mystical: *"ऑपरेटर की चाल को समझना इतना भी मुश्किल नहीं है"*.

### The single read: how much sits above, how much below

- He reduces the whole method to one frame — think in the *balance* of positions sitting around a price. *"हमें एक ही चीज को फॉलो करना है वह है बैंक बैलेंस का कांसेप्ट"* (`बैंक बैलेंस` = the balance-of-book concept, ASR).
- The question, verbatim: *"मार्केट किसी एक पर्टिकुलर प्राइस पॉइंट के ऊपर आया हुआ है उसके बाद बाएं की कितनी प्रॉपर्टी है से लिंग की कितनी प्रॉपर्टी है"*. Get this right once, he says, and any operator in any market can be traded around — not predicted, answered.
- **Who is seated where is visible on the tape.** ⚠️ In *"वहां पर सैलरी बैठे हैं जहां पर बांध देखने मिलता है वहां पर बाहर बैठे हैं"* the ASR carries `सैलरी` = seller, `बांध` = buying and `बाहर` for buyer (the last is damaged); the sentence's load is the pairing rule — the side you can *see* printing is the side that is *seated* there.
- **The no-fuel-above read.** During the trade he says he told the room his own position had nothing above it to hunt: *"मेरी मार्किट में ऊपर कोई स्टॉपलॉस नहीं है इसलिए कुछ या फिर रिडक्शन आ सकता है"* — no stop inventory above means no fuel for a clean upside run, so a reduction/misfire is possible. It is the **absence of stops above** that is being read forward, which is the mirror of the corpus's usual "stops below" reading.
- **The operator buys in the background.** *"ऑपरेटर हमेशा बैकग्राउंड पर लाइक करता है"* (`लाइक` = buy), and only then does he lift — *"तो उसने बाइक किया उसको बाद में फिर ऊपर लेकर"*. The visible lift is the *end* of his accumulation, never the start.
- **Support↔resistance role flip.** ⚠️ In *"जो स कुड़ता को रजिस्टेंस बन गया रजिस्टर था वह सपोर्ट बन गया"* the ASR is mangled (`स कुड़ता`, `रजिस्टर`); the meaning carried by the surrounding sentences is the standard flip — the level that was resistance became support, and the break point is where the trendline forms and only then the buy fills.
- **Momentum is a consequence of opposition.** The climactic rule of the clip: *"जब वो ट्रेड ऑपोज़िट करेगा तब आपको मुमेंटम देखने को मिलेगा ही मिलेगा हंड्रेड परसेंट"*. Momentum is not something the market gives a crowd — it arrives when the operator is on the *other* side of it.
- He explains a specific no-momentum print with exactly that logic — the move did not run because only stale positions were sitting there and had been eaten: *"यह जो मुमेंटम होता है यहां पर इससे लिंग यहां पर नहीं हो तो यहां पर होते हैं यहां पर नहीं हुई होती"* ... *"यहां पर नहीं हुई होती क्योंकि यहां पर तो केवल और केवल"*.

### The cut: opening at yesterday's same level

- **The single hardest practical rule of the session.** If the market opens around the *same* level where the selling (and therefore the stops) was seen the previous day, the position is cut — the operator has already shown his hand: *"अगर मार्किट जैसा ओपन दे रहा है उसी लेवल के आसपास स्थित ओपन हो रहा है जहां पर उन्हें सेटिंग नजर आया था तो डेफिनेटली आप अपनी वह पोजीशन काट देंगे"* (`सेटिंग` = selling, ASR).
- He frames it as a **confirmation**, not a guess: once the operator has played the same level well, the open *itself* is the evidence. In his own case the position was cut on that basis; the point for the learner is that "hold or cut" is answered by *where the open sits*, not by feelings about the profit already visible.
- **The read is prepared one day earlier.** He presents the same-level cut as the continuation of the earlier lesson where he marks where buyers were activated and where they failed — the level's character (was it prepared? was it only stops?) is what makes the next open decisive.
- ⚠️ The supporting sentence *"क्योंकि ने बहुत अच्छा खेला डिपो की नजर आ चुका था"* is damaged (`डिपो` unresolved); the surrounding sentences carry the meaning — the operator played the level well and that was already visible, so the open seals it.
- The cut is stated as a **general article**, not day-specific: it does not depend on which side won or on the size of the move; it depends only on the open sitting on the same shelf as yesterday's selling.

### Expiry day belongs to the writer

- He notes the day explicitly before the chart point — this was an expiry session: *"एक्सपाइरी का दिन था और एक्सपाइरी के दिन आप थोड़ा नोट कर लीजिए कि मार्केट ऑप्शन राइटर के लिए ज्यादा काम करता"*. The market's action that day is **the writer's action**.
- ⚠️ The next clause is ASR-damaged (`ऑप्शन बातें`), but the two sentences that follow fix the sense: that day the option **buyer's** danger is *smaller* and the option **writer's** danger is the *greatest* — *"ऑप्शन रायटर को सबसे ज्यादा खतरा होता है"* (ASR spells it `रायटर` here). Recorded as spoken; a note in the fidelity section keeps the ambiguity visible.
- **Why the writer is the exposed party:** he sells premium cheap. ⚠️ *"वहां पर ₹10 का नोट मिल गया बीस रुपए का अटूट मिल गया"* (`नोट`/`अटूट` are ASR damage) is the cheap-money sale — a ₹10 premium, then a ₹20 — and the ladder is restated later in the clip: *"जो फ्रूट ₹10 कहते हैं वहीं rs.20 ₹40 ₹40"* (`फ्रूट` = premium, ASR).
- **He thinks in small rupees.** *"1000 2000 रूपए यहां पर बन जाएंगे"* is the writer's arithmetic — a few thousand rupees collected per expiry — which is exactly why he stops defending himself.
- **One month versus one day.** The market's counter to that arithmetic: *"मार्केट को पता है कि एक महीना पैसा बना ले लेकिन एक दिन ऐसा आएगा"*. However many small premiums a writer banks across a series, one expiry day can hand it all back.
- **The visual threat.** He walks the tape to the point where the market has gone *through* the writer's shelf and quotes the operator's implied taunt — *"मार्केट देखिए यहां तक आया बोलो मैं छोड़ूंगा नहीं"* — and the crowd's counter-positioning is named directly: *"किसी ने बेचारे ने पुट राइट यात्री किया होगा और ऑपरेटर को याद आ गई"*.
- **The buyer's own obligation.** He does not ban expiry-day buying — he bans borrowing the decision: *"कब आपको निकलना है वहां पर डिसीजन खुद का होना चाहिए ना कि ऑपरेटर का"*, and he names the failure mode of the trader who waits on the operator's mercy: *"ऑपरेटरों पर डिसीजन बनाएगा तो आप यहां पर यह सोच रहे थे इतना नीचे तो क्या करें"*.
- **The `65 percent` flag.** He states his own directional lean as a probability, not a level: *"मुझे लगता है कि आप सिक्सटी फाइव परसेंट मार्केट को नीचे आना चाहिए"* — his logic wanted more time open before the downside, and he says so as an expectation to be re-read, not a target.
- **The close.** Everything above is learnable, but only from the chart and only with volume of reps: *"थोड़ी सी मेहनत लगा लीजिए थोड़े से चार्ट होना चाहिए थोड़ा सा पार्ट को याद रखना चाहिए"*, and *"जिस दिन काम करना शुरू कर दिया जिस दिन चार्ट खोल कर बैठने शुरू कर दिया"*. He says the effort is the entry ticket — *"मेहनत बेशुमार करनी पड़ेगी इतनी मेहनत करनी पड़ेगी कि रातों में भी आपको देखना पड़ेगा"* — and gestures to a separate solution video: *"मैंने आपके लिए एक सलूशन बनाया था"*.

### Keep permanently

1. **Read one question: what sits above, what sits below.** Find the price the market is sitting above, then weigh the buying stacked above it against the selling below it. Any operator, in any market, is answered by this — not predicted.
2. **The operator buys in the background and lifts afterwards.** The visible move is the *end* of his accumulation. Never treat the visible print as his entry.
3. **Momentum comes from opposition, not from the crowd.** Expect momentum only when the operator is trading opposite the side the crowd has taken — he says it happens every time.
4. **A level built to trigger the crowd is a trap, not a signal.** Because retail now buys above resistance and sells below support mechanically, the operator's cheapest route is to *build* that trigger and go the other way.
5. **Cut when the open sits on yesterday's same level.** If the market opens around the exact shelf where yesterday's selling/stops appeared, the position is cut — the level's character was already settled the day before.
6. **No stops above means no fuel above.** Absence of stop inventory on a side is a read on whether a clean run can happen at all — not a promise that it will.
7. **The support↔resistance flip is the buy trigger, not the break.** A resistance that turns into support, with a trendline forming on it, is where the buy fills.
8. **On expiry day, know whose day it is.** The action that day is the writer's action; the writer is the exposed party because he sells cheap premium and thinks in a few thousand rupees — one expiry day repays a month of his collection.
9. **Never hand your exit decision to the operator.** Expiry-day participation is allowed; borrowing the decision from the operator is not — decide your own exit before you take the position.
10. **It is all chart work.** No shortcut: the material is only learnable with reps on real charts, at volume, over time.
