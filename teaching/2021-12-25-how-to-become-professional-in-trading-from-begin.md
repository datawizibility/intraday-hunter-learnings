### Core idea
- **"Professional" is defined by the quality of the STYLE, not by the size of the P&L.**
  *"प्रोफेशनल का मेरे लिए मीनिंग रहेगा कि जब हम किसी कार्य या ट्रेडिंग वगैरह को दूसरों से अच्छा कर
  रहे हो"* — doing the work better than others, in a better manner. Making money is one thing; if
  the money is being made with a bad style, he does not call that professional.
- **The retail style is complex; the professional's style is simple.** The same chart is used by
  both — the difference is how many things the trader has loaded on top of it.
- **Three disciplines make the style professional:** (1) reduce the number of indicators,
  (2) fix ONE time frame and stay on it, (3) decide one trading style and continue it. Do these and
  a **system** forms by itself — *"आपके जो ट्रेन किस टाइम में आपको एक सिस्टम मिल जाएगा"*.
- **The professional is not doing something secret — he is following his own system.** *"जो
  प्रोफेशनल करते हैं वह क्या करते हैं वह भी एक अपना सिस्टम फॉलो करते हैं"* — that is why he can
  live off it. The system is also what stops random trading: *"रेंडम ट्रेडिंग से आप दूर हो जाओगे"*,
  and because you know where the stop-loss belongs, a news headline no longer produces an instant,
  panicked position.

### Discipline 1 — cut the number of indicators
- *"जो नंबर ऑफ इंडिकेट रहते हैं वह कम कर दीजिए"* — the more indicators loaded, the more confused the
  style becomes: *"अगर आप जितना ज्यादा इंडिकेटर यूज करोगे आप अपने ट्रेडिंग स्टाइल में उतना ही ज्यादा
  कंफ्यूज हो गए"* — more false signals are generated, and therefore more trades are taken.
- **He does not ban indicators.** He explicitly says indicator-based trading can be excellent when
  it is run as a system ("if this indicator turns up, I enter; if that one turns down, I exit" is a
  very good trading style), and he records that he has seen indicator traders who make good money
  in a better manner.
- His stated reason for valuing indicators so highly: **an indicator is algorithmic, so it carries
  no human error** — *"जो इंडिकेटर होता है वह यह कि एल्गो ट्रेडिंग कह सकते हो आप उसको थैंक्यू
  ट्रेडिंग में मिस्टेक नहीं होता"* — whereas support/resistance is **drawn by a human**, so the
  mistake risk sits with the person drawing it. (⚠️ Worth marking: this is a **pro-indicator**
  statement of 2021-12 — see the evolution section.)
- The real failure is not indicator use but **not seeing price at all**: when so many studies are
  loaded that the candles are no longer visible and only the indicator outputs are read, the style
  has to be fixed. That is the case he wants corrected.
- **The starter's indicator set** he names as commonly used by beginners (ASR-garbled names — see
  the fidelity section) is exactly the pile he is telling the viewer to **trim**, and he says he has
  drawn price action on the chart alongside them deliberately, for comparison.

### Discipline 2 — fix ONE time frame
- *"आपको एक टाइम प्रेम को चूज करना है"* — choose a time frame and operate on it.
- The problem is never the chosen frame, it is the **switching**: *"जहां पर आप कभी 5 मिनट में ट्रेंड
  करोगे और कभी शिफ्ट में"* — a SuperTrend on the 5-minute gives *"वह बाइक का सिग्नल दे रहे हैं"*
  (a buy signal) while the same study on the 3-minute gives the **sell** signal, and the trader is
  left "confused" and reads noise as a setup.
- His evidence for consistency: *"अगर उसी पर ऑपरेट कर रहे हैं ना तो आपको कोई प्रॉब्लम नहीं हो रही
  है"* — if you keep operating on the frame you chose, there is no problem at all.
- Useful addition he makes: along with your trading frame, **looking at a higher time frame for the
  overall market view** is a good addition — the point is a fixed pair, not constant switching.

### Discipline 3 — pick one style (and stop mixing)
- *"पहले आपको एक ट्रेडिंग स्टाइल डिसाइड करना है"* — investing and intraday are both allowed, but
  mixing them by mood is what prevents a style from ever forming: doing investing for one month,
  intraday for the next, then back again, will not build anything.
- The specific error he names: moving from investing to intraday **because the investing position
  is in loss** — the switch is then an emotional exit dressed up as a strategy change. His rule:
  either drop the thing you do not like and continue the one you do, or keep both but **continue
  each of them properly**.
- Payoff: once the style is fixed and followed, the trader can tell which kind of trading actually
  suits him — the answer only appears over a long horizon, not from a week of switching.

### Keep permanently
1. **"Professional" = the quality of the method, not the size of the profit.** A profitable trader
   with a chaotic style is not the model to copy.
2. **The professional's edge is a system, not a secret** — he follows his own rules, and that is why
   the work is repeatable.
3. **Keep the tool count small.** More indicators ⇒ more generated signals ⇒ more trades ⇒ more
   confusion; if the candles are no longer visible on your chart, the chart has a problem.
4. **Indicators are legitimate** and have an advantage — they are computed, so they cannot make a
   careless human mistake; the risk moves to the person drawing the hand-made levels.
5. **Choose one time frame and operate on it.** The damage comes from switching frames mid-read, not
   from the frame you chose; a higher frame for overall context is an addition, not a substitute.
6. **Pick one trading style and continue it.** Switching between investing and intraday (especially
   to escape a losing investing position) is an emotional exit dressed as a strategy change.
7. **Let the system place the stop-loss**, so a news event cannot produce an instant reflexive
   position — the discipline is what absorbs the shock.
8. **Follow your chosen method long enough to learn which trading actually suits you** — that answer
   only appears over a long horizon, never from a week of switching.
9. **Do not judge a method by its marketing or by someone else's preference** — judge it by your own
   consistent results on your own fixed frame.
