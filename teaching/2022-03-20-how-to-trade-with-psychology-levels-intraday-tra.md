### Core idea
- The session is about **projection** — projecting the next chart from how the current one was built — and specifically about **psychology (round-number) levels as stop-loss pools**: how the market decides which side to go to, and how to trade around a psychology level.
- The direction rule he states outright: **the market goes to the side where MORE stop-losses are available.** Availability on both sides is not enough — he answers a hypothetical critic ("there were stops above too, why didn't you buy?") by saying the decision is comparative, and the market heads for the **bigger** pool.
- Because a psychology level is the **easiest place for the market to eat stops**, price is pushed into/toward it; and when price approaches a psychology level, his action is to **sell** (in the March-2022 context of the chart shown).
- The second durable rule: **never set a target on the side whose stops have already been consumed.** Once a pool is eaten, that side is no longer the objective.
- He also names a trap in reading direction: a move that is really **operators unwinding their positions is not buying** — do not take a trend from it — while it *is* usable as evidence for planning a sell when nothing on the other side justifies a rise.
- And the closing (strongly repeated) meta-rules: **back-study the completed chart after the close**, and **practise projection yourself** — a teacher's effort does not transfer. Expect months-to-years, not one video.

### Projection — measuring the hunt in points
- His working tool: take the momentum move that took the stops and **measure the point-distance it covered** — from the point where selling started down to the lower point that the move reached.
- On the archive chart he measures **116 points** of selling on that candle, then re-measures from point to point on the same candle to confirm the 116 points; the point count is how he sizes **what has been consumed**.
- After that 116-point move, two large green candles appear **without any position stop-loss behind them** — movement with no fresh pool. His comment: somebody may have bought, *"I have no concern with who bought"* — the print has no stop inventory behind it, so it does not create a target.
- Later on the next chart he repeats the same exercise and measures **290 points** around the selling zone. The tool is consistent: **distance in points = the size of the pool that was taken.**
- Consequence he draws: after the stops were eaten, the market reversed and began **creating fresh stop-losses on the other side** — the pool rebuilds, and the next move is aimed at the new pool.

### Which side does the market go to? (the comparative rule)
- With pools on both sides, the market still goes **up** here — because below there is a *psychology level*, and he reasons the market knows that if price comes back down to it, **selling will be seen** there. Going down would force the market to rebuild prices/stops from scratch, with no psychology level, event or sentiment nearby to justify it.
- His phrasing of the mechanism: the market's **easiest possible place to eat stops is its own psychology level** — so that is where it pushes.
- The mirror case on the next chart: price runs up **without any visible stop pool**, and crucially **with no psychology level, no event and no sentiment** to support an up-move. His conclusion: with nothing on the upside, the market's best option is the **down-side stops** — and the market then made a price action, hunted those stops, and continued down. He presents this as the sequence you should be able to *project*, not react to.

### Psychology-level rules
- He marks a **psychology level (round number ~36,500)** with a line and states the rule: **as price approaches the psychology level, we attempt a sell.**
- He then refers to where the market went and where the sell attempt sits (spoken numbers in the 36,500–36,540 band and a ~36,7xx high). ⚠️ The ASR in this passage is unstable — see the fidelity section; the *rule* is clear, the **numbers are not usable**.
- The management instruction that follows: **never target** the zone whose stops the market has already taken. The target has to be the **unconsumed** pool.

### Operator unwinding is not buying
- The momentum that returned after the reversal was, in his reading, **operators exiting their goods (unwinding)** — not buying.
- His instruction: **never consider that a buy**, and **never take a down-side trend based on it**. The correct use is the opposite: because there is (a) no position stop available on that side, (b) no psychology-level reaction, and (c) no event/sentiment pushing up, you **plan a sell** — the market must go to the down-side stops.
- This reproduces the smart-money-unwinding mechanism taught on 2022-02-20, but here it is turned into a *direction filter*: unwinding momentum on the wrong side is not a reversal signal.

### Holding discipline (do not bolt on a small profit)
- If you have built a position and you see a small profit, and then momentum appears against you — **you do not exit.** His stated belief: the market should return in the same momentum.
- He admits the market sometimes proves him wrong (he refers to seeing something like "4,000" continue to convert, ASR unclear) and says that does not trouble him — **"sometimes, but not always"** — and that if you follow the chart the market will not keep proving you wrong.
- The point is not stubbornness: the exit decision comes from **the chart's structure**, not from the size of the unrealised profit or the sight of an opposing candle.

### Learning rules (his closing)
- **Back-study:** you must come to the completed chart and work out **how it was built and where the selling happened**, otherwise watching live charts alone will never teach you how the market works.
- **Self-effort:** the projection has to be practised by the trader, on the chart, at home. His effort cannot substitute. If he explains and you do not draw, that is your failure, not his — he is explicit that he will not take responsibility for that.
- **Nothing is complete:** he states plainly that what he has shown is **not the whole of price action** — if one or two videos were enough, everyone would have learned it. Time frame for learning is the trader's own: a month, a year, ten years.

### Keep permanently
1. **The market goes to the side with MORE stop-losses available.** Stops on both sides is not the question — compare the pools and expect the bigger one to be taken.
2. **Never target the side whose stops are already consumed.** After a pool is eaten, the objective moves to the **unconsumed** pool.
3. **A psychology/round-number level is the market's easiest stop-pool** — that is why price is pushed into it; as price approaches the psychology level, attempt a **sell** (reaction style), and expect the small engineered overshoot.
4. **Measure the hunt with projection:** the point-distance of the momentum move that took the stops (116 / 290 in his examples) tells you the size of what was consumed.
5. **Operator unwinding is not buying** — never read it as a buy and never take a down-side trend from it; use it to plan a sell when the other side has no pool, level or event.
6. **A reversal needs a reason.** With no psychology level, no event, no sentiment and no available position stop on a side, price will not travel there — continuation toward the existing stops is the path of least resistance.
7. **Do not bolt on a small profit.** A modest gain or an opposing momentum print is not an exit signal; exit on the chart's structure, and accept being occasionally wrong without abandoning the read.
8. **Back-study every completed chart** — work out how it was built, where the selling happened, and what the market was targeting; live watching alone teaches nothing.
9. **Practise projection yourself.** The method only transfers through your own chart drawing; the teacher's effort cannot substitute, and it may take months to years.
10. **Nothing here is complete.** Treat each lesson as one input to a process you are building, not a finished system.
