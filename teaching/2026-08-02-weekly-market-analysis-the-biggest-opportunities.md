### Core idea
- **The week is not read off candles — it is read off two mindsets.** His whole method reduces to one input: *"रूट वैल्यू क्या होती है कि हम बयर और सेलर इन दोनों को देखेंगे"*, and he closes the session with the same two-value frame — *"यही दो वैल्यू को आपको समझना है। ज्यादा अगर हम देखेंगे मार्केट के अंदर यह भी देख लें वो भी देख लें तो उससे हो नहीं पाएगा।"* Read the crowd's position, not your own chart.
- **Pick the arena first.** Investing, intraday or positional — decide which of the three you actually execute well **before** going deeper into charts: *"अगर आप यहां पे क्लियर हो गए कि आपको क्या करना है? इन्वेस्ट करना है, इंट्राडे में ट्रेड करना है या फिर पोजीशनल ट्रेड करना है।"*
- **Every plan is an open-type gate whose output is a *pool*, never a direction.** The plan is written in advance — flat / gap-up / small gap-down / big gap-down — and each branch names *whose stop-losses* are reachable, then the setup is hunted on that side.
- **Greed is the tool, not the accident.** The market first hands the seated counter-side a chance to escape or average, that side adds size, and *then* it is targeted. All of the week's setups are this one mechanic read through different open types.
- **A correct direction can still book a loss.** He lost on the Bank Nifty leg while Nifty and Sensex closed positive all week — the read was right, the single SL was simply eaten. One day (or week, or month) not paying is not a reason to change the method.
- **Options force a 1:1 risk-reward.** Because time decays a bought premium, he cannot keep a tiny stop and a huge target — he holds one-by-one and buys his accuracy instead of his payoff.

### Pick your arena before you read charts
- He opens by splitting traders by **where they execute well**, and says to locate yourself honestly first: some are good at investing, some intraday, some positional. His own self-location: *"मैं छोटे मोमेंटम को अच्छे से समझ लेता हूं।"*
- Once the arena is fixed, the chart has to be understood at its **root** — and the root is not the candle but who is sitting.
- The alternative to a narrow arena is a widening search for the trade that always pays: he refuses that — *"मार्केट के अंदर एक ट्रेडर को नहीं देखना है। हजारों लोगों ने क्या किया होगा?"* The unit of analysis is the crowd that traded, not one trader.

### Root value: the market has only two inputs (buyer + seller)
- **Root value = the buyer's and the seller's mindsets** — nothing else is in the market: *"ये दो ही तो चीजें हैं। इनको आप समझ लो हाथ लग जाएगा सब कुछ आपके।"*
- Concretely he asks: *"बयर और सेलर किस प्रकार का माइंडसेट बना के यहां पे ट्रेड बना के गए होंगे"* — and says that question is exactly what lifts his own accuracy.
- **One absent input cancels the trade.** Where he cannot tell which side holds the bigger quantity, he refuses to name a target and follows the market instead: *"केवल मार्केट के साथ चलने का। फ्लैट मिल जाए, गैप अप मिल जाए। हम बाइंग का सेटअप ढूंढेंगे। छोटे-मोटे गैपडाउन में कंफ्यूजन रहेगा।"*
- Root value is the **gate on accuracy**, and accuracy is the gate on risk-reward (below): if the root read is weak, the risk-reward dial must be turned up — and in option buying that dial is nearly stuck.

### Accuracy and risk-reward are two dials
- His framing of the baseline: *"मार्केट आपको यह बोलता है कि 50% एक्यूरेसी आपकी मेंटेन हो जाएगी।"* A plain setup with a plain stop gives roughly a coin-flip.
- With **50% accuracy you must keep at least 1:1** to stay alive: *"अगर हम इस एक्यूरेसी के साथ वन बाय वन रिस्क रिवार्ड रखकर काम करेंगे तो हम ब्रेक इवन पर पहुंच पाएंगे।"*
- So there are two routes — raise accuracy, or raise the ratio. He takes the accuracy route: *"मैं रिस्क और रिवार्ड वन बाय वन रखता हूं।"*
- **Why he cannot simply widen the ratio:** he buys options, so time is a cost — *"मुझे रिस्क रिवार्ड यह रखना पड़ता है क्योंकि टाइम का बड़ा मैटर रहता है।"* A small stop and a big target gets the stop taken while the target never prints.
- Reading the crowd's mindset is what lets him run the low ratio safely: high accuracy plus 1:1 still pays.

### The greed mechanic (how the seated counter-side is built, then taken)
- The market does not take a side cold — it **first gives that side a chance to add**: *"पहले वो सेलर को लालच देता है कि मैं नहीं जा रहा हूं ऊपर।"*
- The concrete picture he builds: a put seller is already sitting; the market opens a gap-up and appears to fall, so the seller reads it as an escape and averages instead of exiting — *"मार्केट जरूर उसे लालच देगा कि थोड़ा सा ओवरेज कर लो मैं गिर रहा हूं"* ⇒ *"तो वो और ज्यादा सेल करेगा। फिर मार्केट मे भी उसको टारगेट बनाए।"*
- The same in words on the gap-up branch: the market **will not rise directly**, precisely because it owes the seated seller that temptation — *"अगर हमें मार्केट के अंदर गैप अप मिल जाता है तब भी डायरेक्टली बढ़ेगा नहीं मार्केट क्योंकि थोड़ा लालच देगा।"*
- Escalation is the tell for how much fuel a greed move carries: a *small* gap-up can still tempt the seller, but a *very large* one cannot — *"तो यहां पर तो सेलर को लालच भी नहीं दे सकता था।"* When the temptation is impossible, the seated side has no reason to add, and the pool dries up.
- He owns the trade as a captured temptation — *"तो थोड़ा लालच जरूर देगा और वही लालच हमने कैप्चर किया था।"*

### The retracement and the seated-side read (Friday's chart)
- Friday's sudden recovery inside a selling tape is the setup he unpacks first: *"अगर देखिए सेलिंग हुआ और रिट्रेसमेंट हुआ तो दो बातें यहां पे नजर आ रही है"* — momentum had been sell-side, then a sharp pop.
- **The pop is not buyers arriving — it is profit-taking.** The buyer who got a sudden gift is gone: *"अब यहां पे किसी ने बाय किया होगा वो तो निकल ही गया होगा"* ⇒ *"तो हम बायर के ऊपर तो भरोसा कर नहीं सकते थे"*. So the buyers are explicitly **not** the target.
- What the retracement *does* create is a fresh sell crowd — traders who now get the sell entry they missed on the way down.
- Rule he draws: **follow the retracement, and read the target off who is seated** — flat/small gap-down hands the sellers to you; a big gap-down puts those same sellers straight into profit and kills the target — *"वैसे ही प्रॉफिट में आ जाएंगे"*.
- He names the discipline the retracement demands — do not flip sides on the pop, do not re-read one candle.

### Risk-reward arithmetic in option buying
- The wishful trade he rules out with a point example: *"छोटा सा एसएल रखूंगा 10 पॉइंट का और वहां पे मैं 100 पॉइंट निकाल लूंगा। तो ऐसा तो हो नहीं पाएगा।"*
- The trade that **does** work is the modest ratio: *"ऐसा हो सकता है आप 50 पॉइंट का एसएल रख लो। 100 पॉइंट का टारगेट रख लो।"* — a 1:2 priced off a realistic stop, not a 1:10 fantasy.
- The rule under both examples: **the stop distance must be honest for the instrument**, and inside options the premium clock punishes any stop that is too tight relative to the time spent.

### Keep permanently
1. **Read the crowd's two mindsets, nothing else.** Root value = the buyer's and the seller's position; if you cannot name which side holds the bigger quantity, you have no target — follow the market instead.
2. **Pick your arena before you pick your charts.** Decide whether you execute best at investing, intraday or positional, then study at that scale; drifting between the three on the strength of one loss is what keeps accounts flat.
3. **Every plan is written as open types, and each branch names a POOL — never a direction.** Flat / gap-up / small gap-down / big gap-down in advance; the setup is then hunted on the side whose stops are reachable.
4. **Greed first, target second.** The market hands the seated counter-side a temptation to add before it takes that side — so a move that refuses to run directly is information about who is sitting, not confusion.
5. **Temptation size is the fuel gauge.** A small open can still recruit the seated side (pool alive); an open too large to tempt it leaves no pool — read the open's size as the pool's size.
6. **A gap-up pop is not buyers arriving.** The buyer handed a sudden profit leaves; the pop manufactures a fresh sell crowd. Do not flip to the buy side because price moved.
7. **One index diverging opens the trade; all three agreeing closes it.** When all three run together, only one side is arriving and the continuation is unavailable — the lagging index is where the trade lives.
8. **Keep risk-reward at least 1:1 and buy accuracy instead of payoff.** With ~50% accuracy, 1:1 is break-even; in option buying time charges rent, so a tiny stop with a huge target cannot work — a realistic stop with a ~1:2 target can.
9. **Set the stop honestly for the instrument, then accept the single SL.** A correct read can still pay a stop first; one day, one week or one month not firing is not a reason to change the method — only an un-understood situation is.
10. **Judge the week, not the day — and once it starts paying, do not let it go.** Direction found correctly across a week shows up as a scorecard of small wins plus one controlled SL, not as a perfect streak.
