### Core idea
A weekly price-action review built on one question — whose stop-losses are seated — rather than on candles and drawn levels. He replays several days of the same week: a day when all three indices printed large positive momentum after a selling day, a day that opened exactly flat and was traded on the put side, and a Friday chart on which the first big fall had left sellers seated. The spine is that a candle-plus-support/resistance read only makes the mind flip from side to side — *"जब हम चार्ट को देखते हैं, कैंडल को देखकर काम करते हैं, केवल सपोर्ट रेजिस्टेंस लगाते हैं, तो हमारा माइंड बार-बार चेंज होता है"* — whereas naming which side is seated gives the direction. Why the week matters at all: *"इस वीक का प्राइस एक्शन हमारे लिए इंपॉर्टेंट क्यों हो जाता है"* — the tape kept turning (up, down, up, gap up, then a reversal), so the only stable read is the seating map. Nothing here is a live call: the analysis is written on the previous day's chart, the **opening type** (gap up / flat-to-gap-down / exactly flat) selects *which* pool may be targeted, and where no retail position is actually readable the market is simply followed — *"अदरवाइज वहां पे इग्नोर कीजिए। मार्केट को कंटिन्यू फॉलो कीजिए। यह बड़ा सिंपल रूल है एसएल एंटिंग का"* (⚠️ `एसएल एंटिंग` is ASR for **एसएल हंटिंग**, which also occurs correctly in the same clip).

### Candles alone flip the mind; the seated side sets the direction
- The opening complaint is about what a chart-only method does to the trader, not about its accuracy: watching candles and drawing support/resistance makes the read swing every few minutes, because a support/resistance chart can always be argued either way — *"जब हम चार्ट को देखते हैं, कैंडल को देखकर काम करते हैं, केवल सपोर्ट रेजिस्टेंस लगाते हैं, तो हमारा माइंड बार-बार चेंज होता है"*.
- What replaces it is a single either-or question — *"अगर हमें पता हो कि बयर को टारगेट करना है या फिर सेलर को टारगेट करना है"* — from which the direction falls out almost automatically: *"डायरेक्शन का पता चल जाता है"*.
- He is careful about the claim: the candle-plus-level method does produce trades, just not understanding — *"अकेले कैंडल देखकर अकेले सपोर्ट रेजिस्टेंस को देखकर ट्रेड आपको मिल सकती है। लेकिन ट्रेड का लॉजिक नहीं मिल पाएगा"*. The pay-off he names is psychological, not predictive: *"वो चीजें आपके लिए काम करेंगी और दूसरा सबसे ज्यादा इंपॉर्टेंट आपका माइंड बार-बार चेंज नहीं होगा"*.
- The order of the two skills is stated as a dependency: applying stop-loss hunting without the seating read is the failure state — *"अगर आप मार्केट के अंदर एसएल हंटिंग को फॉलो नहीं करते हो और अगर आप यह नहीं समझते हो कि बयर या फिर सेलर कहां पे बैठे हो सकते हैं"*. His closing instruction for price-action learners is the same sentence in the imperative: identify where retail buyers' and sellers' stop-losses can sit, and everything else (selling or buying) follows from it.

### SL hunting is not the automatic opposite — the retail-portion test
- The mistake is named as a habit of reading momentum as an instruction — *"एसएल हंटिंग को आप बहुत गलत तरीके से देखते हो"* — and then spelled out and reduced to absurdity, because a market where every momentum is immediately reversed could not move at all: *"आपको ये लगता है कि ये मोमेंटम आया तो इसमें हम बायर को टारगेट बनाएंगे। ये मोमेंटम आया तो हम सेदर को टारगेट बनाएंगे। तो ऐसे तो मोमेंटम मार्केट के अंदर हो ही नहीं पाएगा"* (⚠️ `सेदर` = सेलर; the ASR's `सेलर`/`सैलरी` substitution is the standard one in this corpus).
- The replacement test is quantitative and stated as the whole rule: how much retail *position* actually got built at that spot — *"हमें ये देखना होता है कि रिटेल में मार्केट के अंदर कितना पोर्शन बना है"*. If it is readable, the target is licensable — *"अगर वो आपको नजर आ रहा है तो आप उसके अकॉर्डिंग टारगेट बनाने की कोशिश कीजिए"* — and if it is not, the instruction is to drop the reversal and join the trend: *"अदरवाइज वहां पे इग्नोर कीजिए। मार्केट को कंटिन्यू फॉलो कीजिए। यह बड़ा सिंपल रूल है एसएल एंटिंग का"* (⚠️ `एंटिंग` for हंटिंग).
- The general form follows from that condition — reversal is a licensed exception, not the default — *"तो हमेशा ऐसे नहीं कि अपोजिट ही काम करना होता है"* … *"हम मार्केट के साथ भी चल सकते हैं"*. A caution kept from the same reading: on the day he describes, retail thinking was simply absent from the move and therefore unreadable — *"अगर रिटेल ने काम नहीं किया क्योंकि मार्केट ने क्या किया? नेगेटिव से पॉजिटिव गया"* ⚠️ (ASR subject/negation is garbled in `रिटेल ने काम नहीं किया`; context — a negative-to-positive day on which few had built positions — carries the meaning, and the clause is quoted exactly as spoken, not repaired).

On the gap-up day the logic of the branch is stated in one move — if the market gaps up, the crowd's absence of positions means the gap extends, so the plan is to ride the continuation: *"हम मार्केट के साथ चलते हुए कंटिन्यू मोमेंटम को फॉलो करेंगे"*.

### The gap-up day: the buyers already in profit are not the target
- The day opened with a gap up, and the decision that follows is the corpus's profit filter applied to the option buyer's side: you cannot take a gap-up as permission to target the buyers who are sitting long — *"बायर को हम डायरेक्टली टारगेट नहीं बना सकते"* — and the market therefore keeps travelling in the same direction, because there is no crowd position to eat — *"तो ऐसे में उसी डायरेक्शन में मार्केट चलता रहेगा क्योंकि वहां पे रिटेल का पोजीशन अवेलेबल नहीं है"*.
- The conclusion is stated flatly, and it is the reason his own plan did not sell into it: *"इन बायर को हमें टारगेट बनाना नहीं है"*. The arithmetic of that immunity is walked through on a **stated hypothetical** call:

| the prices the market was trading at when those calls were bought — **chart read** | *"जब मार्केट यहां पे ट्रेड कर रहा था 76791 पे 76900 पे 76700 पे जो कॉल के प्रीमियम थे"* ⚠️ (values as ASR; index unnamed — see Numbers) |

| …and what they do instead | *"पोजीशनल उन्होंने बाय किया तो थोड़ा प्रॉफिट वगैरह भी मार्केट के अंदर कैप्चर करने की कोशिश करेंगे"* |

- He then bounds his own claim with the retail trader's realistic error — the filter protects the side only until greed is exercised — *"ज्यादा ही लालच कर लेता है। फिर मार्केट जब घूमता है तो वह बच नहीं पाता"* — and adds the honest caveat that the market *did* give that temporary profit: a gap-up winner who does not overreach can still bank something. The whole paragraph is a filter on *who* may be targeted, not a promise about direction.

### The day after the heavy selling: the seated seller is the target
- The setup day is shown as one thing only — a first big fall after a run of strength — *"बहुत बड़ा सेलिंग हुआ। बहुत बड़ा सेलिंग हुआ।"* — and the pool is read off the habit that the fall creates: long rising markets teach the crowd to sell tops, so anecdotal sellers are left in place when the fall arrives — *"टॉप पर सेलिंग करने की आदत होती है"* … *"तो जैसे ही गैपडाउन मिला होगा थोड़े बहुत यहां पे सैलरी बैठे रह गए होंगे"* (⚠️ `सैलरी` = **सेलर**).
- Why those sellers are licensable rather than un-targetable, in his own mechanism: a continuously rising market gives the other side the *courage* to pick the top, which is what builds the pool — *"जब मार्केट कंटिन्यू किसी भी डायरेक्शन में चलना शुरू कर देता है ना दूसरे साइड के ट्रेडर वहां पे हिम्मत करना शुरू कर देते हैं"*.
- With the pool read, the flat-to-gap-down branch is a buy, and he says so twice: *"तो ऐसे में जब हम प्लान मार्केट के अंदर बनाएंगे तो इन सेलर को टारगेट बना सकते हैं"* and *"फ्लैट टू गैप डाउन में हमने मार्केट के अंदर इन सेलर को टारगेट करने का प्लान बनाया था"*. Execution is reported in one line, with no size or premium: *"ऐसे में हमने बाय किया और एक अच्छा मोमेंटम कैप्चर करके निकल गए"*.
- The opposite branch is the load-bearing insight of the session — in a gap-up, a seller-trap trade does not exist at all: *"क्योंकि गैप अप में हमारे पास सेलर को टारगेट करने का स्पेस ही नहीं रहता"* ⚠️ (recorded as spoken; compare the contradictory earlier dated statements in Evolution).
- On the Friday chart of the same week the same logic is scaled by the *size* of the gap: a small gap up or small gap down on that chart keeps the plan positive (targeting the seated sellers), while a large gap down means the crowd could not build trades at all and the plan flips to the selling-side setup — *"हां गैप डाउन ज्यादा होता है तो हम समझ जाएंगे मार्केट के अंदर ज्यादा लोग ट्रेड नहीं बना पा रहे हैं"* … *"इन सेलर को टारगेट बनाते हुए पॉजिटिव का प्लान बनाएंगे"*.

### Sideways days, decayed premium, and letting a missed trade go
- The flat-open day is the session's honest cost lesson. Retail behaviour was missing from that tape altogether, so a sell-side trade was written and it lost the big move: the position was a put — *"यहां पे हमने ट्रेड भी पुट का बना रखा था"* — and he exited before the momentum arrived — *"तो हम पहले ही निकल गए तो मार्केट के अंदर मोमेंटम बाद में हुआ"*. The named reason is why an option buyer cannot sit through a flat index — *"साइडवेज मार्केट में क्या होता है? कुछ ग्रीन कैंडल बने वैसे ही आपके ऑप्शन बाइंग में प्रीमियम कम होना शुरू हो जाते हैं"* … *"उसमें फायदा नजर आता नहीं"*.
- The exit is audited rather than defended: the loss was booked and the read of the day was not, in his words, wholly wrong — *"हालांकि हमने थोड़ा सा एंट्री जल्दी बना लिया था जिसमें कोई बड़ा प्रॉफिट हम नहीं बना पाए थे"*.
- The two laws he attaches to that: this psychology is only built **after** the fact — *"यह साइकोलॉजी बाद में बनती है"* — and a trade that leaves without you is simply let go — *"कोई ट्रेड आपके हाथ से निकल जाता है तो निकल जाता"*, stated again on the day's missed put: *"हालांकि ये ट्रेड बड़ा अच्छा मिस हो गया हमसे"*.
- The session's most quotable day-specific judgement is about the flat open that dangles a long: a market that offers you the buy *and* the target is not the real one — *"इतना अच्छा मार्केट नहीं होता कि आपको बाय करने का मौका दे और फिर आपको बहुत अच्छा टारगेट बना के दे"* — hence the flat-to-up move is read as a turn rather than a gift: *"मार्केट यहां पर डेफिनेटली घूमेगा"*, which is why the day's trade was on the put side.

### Keep permanently
1. Read **who is seated** (buyer or seller) before drawing anything; the candle plus level alone will make your mind flip from side to side all session.
2. SL hunting is **not** the automatic opposite of momentum. It is licensed only where a retail **position** is actually readable at that spot.
3. Where no pool is readable, drop the reversal and **follow the continue** — *"अदरवाइज वहां पे इग्नोर कीजिए। मार्केट को कंटिन्यू फॉलो कीजिए।"*
4. **Never target a side already sitting in profit.** It books at its own entry instead of panicking, so it supplies no fuel — use it as a reason to follow, not to reverse.
5. Write the plan on the previous day's chart as **open type → pool**, and leave the direction to the open — never as a fixed direction for tomorrow.
6. **An exactly flat open gets no plan.** If the chart then supplies a reason (a rejection after a flat open), the trade is taken off the chart, not off the plan.
7. On the flat open, remember the option buyer's cost: a sideways index decays the bought premium while the profit does not appear — that is why the position is exited before the momentum.
8. A **gap up has no room for the seller-trap trade** as stated in this session (in a gap-up the seated side is already paid) — record it against the earlier gap-up readings, do not silently average them (see the contradiction above).
9. A trade that leaves without you is let go — *"कोई ट्रेड आपके हाथ से निकल जाता है तो निकल जाता"*. The psychology that would have caught it is built only afterwards.
