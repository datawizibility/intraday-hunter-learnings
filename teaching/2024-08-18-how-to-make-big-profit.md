### Core idea
- **The asymmetry that caps everyone's profit:** in a **loss** trade a trader happily **adds quantity** (*"लॉस में तो हम शेयर बन जाते हैं"*), but in a **profit** trade he refuses to add or increase — *"इसी क्वांटिटी को रखते हैं."* ⇒ **The fear is actually felt in PROFIT, not in loss.** *"जो हमें डर लगता है वो लगता है हमें प्रॉफिट से"* — that is why losses swell and profits stay small.
- Big profit is **not** made by adding quantity; it is made by **fixing the mindset** — *"प्रॉफिट को इंक्रीज़ करने के लिए आपको अपने माइंडसेट को सुधारना पड़ेगा."*
- **Profit is not secure either:** *"प्रॉफिट में भी दूसरे का हो सकता है, अगर हम उसको सही से मैनेज नहीं करेंगे तो प्रॉफिट दूसरा ले जाएगा."* (The loss you take is always your own; the profit you leave is taken by the other side.)

### Mindset rule #1 — a big profit cannot be pre-decided
- You **cannot** say *"कल मार्केट ओपन होते ही बड़ा प्रॉफिट बुकिंग करूंगा."* The normal target you can always keep — but a **big / double / triple target depends only on the SITUATION**: *"उस टाइप की सिचुएशन नहीं मिल रही तो बड़ा टारगेट नहीं."*
- **Do not target big when the situation is bad:** entry poor, **no breakout**, candles not forming well, **option-chain problem**, **news problem**, support/resistance not working → *"जस्ट टारगेट बुकिंग करना है."*
- Big target is allowed **only after the situation is understood** — never decided in advance (*"10 दिन में… एक महीने में…"* is not a target that can be set).

### Mindset rule #2 — stop the calculator in the head
- Do **not** compute inside the head: *"अगर यह 20 के का 50,000/1 लाख बन जाए…"* — *"कभी भी अपने दिमाग के अंदर कैलकुलेशन मत चलाना."* Two-four minutes nothing happens, then **next minute you apply it** and the trade is destroyed.
- **Big target is earned by LOGIC, not by wish:** news turning positive, a **50,000 breakout**, option chain + support/resistance confirming — *"मैं इतना तो सोच ही रहा था, मुझे ये और मिल रहा है, चलो इसे कैप्चर करने की ट्राई करते हैं."* If it is only the mind saying *"आज तो बड़ा करते हैं"* → **that game must never be played.**

### Mindset rule #3 — the day after a loss, don't chase a big target
- If **yesterday was a loss**, avoid a big target today: *"लॉस हुआ तो कोशिश कीजिए उस दिन टारगेट बड़ा ना करें"* — because *"आपकी साइकोलॉजी के ऊपर प्रेशर ज्यादा — ये थोड़ा सा वीक पॉइंट है."*
- He grades this rule **softer**: ~**50%** applicability, depends on how long you have traded and how stable your mindset is. Rules #1 and #2, by contrast, are *"सभी को फॉलो करने हैं."*
- The trap he describes: two days you booked the target and the market ran further; on the third you decide *"आज बड़ा टारगेट करते हैं"* — and **that is the day the market turns.**

### WHERE a big target is possible — the difficulty zone first
**The "operator zone" = where BOTH sides have traded (do NOT target big):**
- Chart: market went **up → down → up again**. In that middle zone *"किसी को लगा 800 का सपोर्ट ले आया → बाइंग; किसी को लगा 50,000 का रेजिस्टेंस → सेलिंग"* — **both call-side and put-side traders were entering.**
- His read of that zone: **retail is SELLING, the operator is BUYING.** *"इसमें रिटेल सेल कर रहा होता है, ऑपरेटर बाय कर रहा होता है, और उसको पोजीशन मिल भी रही होती है."* That is why the market could hold and move up — the operator **built full position** here.
- ⇒ **Never use a both-sides zone for a big target:** *"जहां दोनों साइड के ट्रेडर आ रहे थे, उसको बड़ा टारगेट करने के लिए कभी यूज नहीं करना."* Inside this zone just **take the visible profit and leave** — *"प्रॉफिट लेके निकल जाओ और नज़र मत आना."*
- A rare chance exists (even a bigger operator can target the operator's zone) — but *"रेयर चांस के चक्कर में हमें प्रॉफिट बड़ा नहीं करना है."* Target big only where **probability is highest**.

### WHERE a big target IS possible — the easy zone
- **🔑 The whole distinction: DIRECT break vs HOLD-then-BREAK.**
  - **Direct breakout / direct breakdown ⇒ book and go, no big target.** When the market takes a level **directly** (e.g. breaks **50,000** and goes straight down) it simply **flushed** the traders sitting there — *"जो भी सेल वगैरह करके बैठे थे उनको निकाल दिया"* — and with the other side already gone there is **no fuel left below**: *"इसके नीचे जाने की कोई भी गुंजाइश नहीं है."* You take your profit and leave; *"और गिरेगा"* is greed.
  - **Hold-then-breakout ⇒ big target.** When the market **holds a level** (takes resistance once, twice — *"होल्ड कर रहा है, होल्ड कर रहा है"*), lets **sellers build positions**, and **then** breaks out → *"वो चला जाएगा, कोई प्रॉब्लम नहीं"* — big target is allowed. The clean break is *"रेजिस्टेंस लेकर ब्रेकआउट"*.
  - **Same on the downside — "रुक-रुक कर" (step-by-step) fall ⇒ big target.** If the market falls **pausing at levels** (e.g. pausing at **50,200**, letting buyers enter, then falling again), it is a *hold* pattern → big target. A **direct** fall with no pauses = **intraday benefit only**, just book.
  - **Downside gap**: a **gap-up that is "extra"** means the market will come down only once and **won't complete** — intraday benefit only. If the operator wanted the buyers as the target, he would give a **small opening and then break down** (so buyers feel the threat). An extra gap-up is a different trap.
  - **Upside gap-down:** market gives a **gap-down**, takes resistance, **breakout**, takes resistance again, **breakout** → that staircase = **big target allowed.**
- **Direction first, then entry, then target:** *"अगर मैं डायरेक्शन में सही हूं तो मैं ढूंढ लूंगा कि कहां एंट्री करना है और कहां प्रॉफिट बड़ा करना है."* If the **direction is wrong**, both the entry **and** the big target are wrong — *"डायरेक्शन के अकॉर्डिंग गलत हो गए तो एंट्री भी गलत होगा, प्रॉफिट बड़ा करना भी गलत होगा."*

### His two live examples (day-specific, Aug 2024)
- **16 Aug — the direct breakdown (booked):** he had a **put**; the market broke **50,000 directly** and fell. He **booked profit** as it broke below 50,000 and did **not** get greedy (*"और गिरेगा"*) — because the retail buyers above 50,000 had been **flushed on the direct break**, and a flushed seller leaves **nothing below**: *"इसके नीचे जाने की गुंजाइश नहीं है."* He says he also told viewers **not to buy positional puts** there — a 2–3 day recovery was possible.
- **13 Aug — the read that failed (a loss):** he waited at **50,500** for a breakout, because the market had taken **exact resistance at the closing price** (his normal breakout cue). But *"मार्केट का गेम अलग था — पहले नीचे जाऊंगा, फिर ऊपर आऊंगा."* He built a **call**; it showed profit, then the market **turned** — *"मेरा डायरेक्शन उस दिन गलत था"* → loss. On the chart the fall was **step-by-step (रुक-रुक कर)**: the market **paused at 50,200**, let buyers in **slightly before the level** (impatient buyers getting trapped), then **took support at 50,200** and turned up.
- ⚠️ Two charts (13 Aug vs 16 Aug) looked **identical** ("दोनों एक जैसे नज़र आ रहे हैं") — what separated them: **hold + step-by-step pause (big target, turn) vs a direct break (no big target)**.
- He closes: this works *"हर बार"* neither always nor never — *"80 से 90 पर चांस"* of seeing such setups — and asks you to **back-test in charts** and tell him the result. *"आप इसको 100% कभी मत मान लेना."*

### Keep permanently
1. **The fear is in profit, not in loss.** Traders add to losers and refuse to add to winners — big profit comes from **fixing that mindset**, not from adding quantity to a working trade.
2. **A big profit cannot be pre-decided** — keep the normal target always; let the **situation** decide whether the target goes big.
3. **No big target when the situation is bad:** weak entry, no breakout, poor candles, option-chain/news problem, S/R not working ⇒ just book the normal target.
4. **Never run the calculator in your head** ("if this becomes 1 lakh…"). Big target is earned by **logic** (news + breakout + option chain + S/R), not by wish; the head-game must be switched off.
5. **The day after a loss, don't chase a big target** (~50% rule) — your psychology is under pressure. Rules #2 and #4 are for everyone.
6. **Identify the operator zone (both call- and put-sides active = retail selling, operator buying) and NEVER target big inside it** — take the visible profit and leave; rare big moves there are not worth chasing.
7. **DIRECT breakout/breakdown ⇒ book and go.** A direct break only **flushes** the traders sitting there; with the other side gone there is no fuel beyond — *"और गिरेगा/बढ़ेगा"* is greed.
8. **HOLD-then-BREAKOUT ⇒ big target allowed.** Level taken once or twice, positions allowed to build, **then** the break — and a **step-by-step "रुक-रुक कर" drop** (pausing at levels) is the same hold signal on the downside.
9. **An "extra" gap-up means intraday-only** — the market comes down once and won't complete; if the operator wanted buyers as the target he would give a **small opening then break down**.
10. **Order of operations: direction → entry → target.** If the direction is wrong, both the entry and the big target are wrong; fix the read first, find the spot second.
