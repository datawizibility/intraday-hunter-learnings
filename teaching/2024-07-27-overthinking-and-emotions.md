### Core idea
- **Same chart, one year apart, different crowd.** He puts up a **2023** chart (market rising from a bottom, continuous momentum, one big momentum day, then a **flat open that continued up again**, with a **round number ≈44,000** nearby) beside a **2024** chart with the *same structure and another round number nearby*.
- ⭐ The structure repeated — **but the traders' thinking / emotional part had CHANGED**: *"उसी प्रकार का स्ट्रक्चर बना हुआ है … लेकिन जो थिंकिंग है, एक ट्रेडर का जो इमोशनल पार्ट है, वह यहाँ पर चेंज हो चुका है."* So a formula that worked on the 2023 chart **cannot simply be applied** to the 2024 chart — read *today's* emotional state, not last year's pattern.
- ⭐ **Over-thinking belongs to the trader with NO position; emotion belongs to the trader WITH a position.** *"ओवरथिंकिंग वो ट्रेडर करता है जो ट्रेड नहीं ले रखा होता"* — *should I take it / is my setup formed?* Once the trade is on, the question changes to **handling**: the loss growing, the profit turning into a loss, the big red candle and the fear. ⇒ **The seated side (the side holding a position) is the emotional side — and that is the side you target.**

### The three plans (works the crowd's emotions, not a direction call)
With **buyers seated** (market rose, then rose again — so buyers hold positions), he builds a *buy* plan and shows where each open type changes it.

**1. Gap-down open (with buyers seated)**
- Gap-down + the market falls straight through a level (he spoke a **51,000 breakdown**) ⇒ **do NOT buy.** A trader who bought on a bottom already sits in a negative trend; add a gap-down and a level-breakdown and the fear-exit is unquantifiable — *"डर के मारे कितनी तेजी से भागेंगे, यह अंदाज़ा मार्केट भी नहीं लगा सकता."*
- **Buy only when the market, after coming down, goes back UP and breaks out above its level.** The breakout does two things: it *"बायर को स्ट्रेंथ मिल जाएगा"* and it gives you a **stop-loss at the lower point**. Once the weak hands are flushed, the strong hands stay → buy the breakout with SL under the low.
- Also from that read: don't chase the target — after a breakout *"आराम से बाइंग मिल जाएगा"*, because the others keep waiting for the **closing-price resistance** that the market has already eaten.

**1b. Gap-down with a SELLING plan** — the mirror, and it needs **NO wait**: sell immediately, **SL above the closing price**, define the target down. Reason: the gap-down has already **removed the buyers' (emotional) stops**, so *"मार्केट खा भी जाएगा."*

**2. Flat open**
- Flat open + a direct breakout still **gives buyers confidence**, so at least a small momentum is likely. But there are **no SLs above** ⇒ the market can hold/repeat, so it is **riskier (~50-50)**.
- Therefore: on a *flat-open, direct-momentum* trade that goes wrong, **do not over-trade**. (The same flat-open psychology is what answers the second over-thinking question: *do I take the next trade or not?*)
- On this open type the market can also cheat you: it may not give a big target because *"डायरेक्टली एसल वगैरह नहीं पड़े यहाँ."* Once the buyers have been flushed and the market then goes up, the trade is much more likely to be right.

**3. Gap-up, then the market falls straight down**
- There are **no SLs above** on the gap-up ⇒ after the open an early **green candle** forms (someone who sold at the close gets their position cut) **but the market cannot go up** — *"एसल है नहीं तो मार्केट ऊपर जा नहीं पाएगा"* — it **must come back down to CREATE the SLs**.
- So it breaks down, flushes the **weak-handed buyers** (the regular, stable ones who sit far away stay stable), then **holds**; sellers get **greedy** on the breakdown, sell more (he spoke the market possibly going below the closing price with a **51,500** level), and then the market **turns up** — ⭐ **that is the call-side entry**: *"यही मौका होगा जब आपको कोल साइड का काम करने का मौका मिलेगा"* — and here there is **no fear and no over-thinking**.
- **Do NOT sell the breakdown that follows a gap-up rise** — that breakdown is only *removing weak-handed buyers*, and the market then goes back up, with a high probability of returning **near the closing price**.

**3b. Gap-up, then the market falls straight down and does not recover**
- Now the **other side's** setup is what matters: the gap-up open + sudden fall leaves the **seller** with **no visible setup** ⇒ ⭐ *"जब दूसरे को कोई सेटअप नज़र न आए, वही हमारा सेटअप होता है"* ⇒ **sell immediately**, SL above the high.
- The trigger: fear on the **buy-side profit erosion** — his illustration *"1 लाख प्रॉफिट → 60,000 → 50,000 → 40,000 → 30,000"* ⇒ the seated buyers panic ⇒ *"डर के साथ हमेशा मार्केट गेम खेलता है"*.
- If you already hold that trade when it turns: hold some part, **book some part** for the experience; *"हर चीज़ हमेशा काम नहीं करेगी"* — one bad instance does not invalidate the tool.

### Over-thinking as a tool — the operating rule
- **Do not lean on the emotional side while it is being pushed:** *"जहाँ ट्रेडर इमोशनल हो रहे हैं, वहाँ उनका भरोसा नहीं करना; वहाँ वेट करना है."* Buy after the **breakout**, i.e. after they are emotionally strong again.
- Where the other side is **NOT** emotional and the market is going straight for them, you can **act immediately**.
- **Choosing trades by experience:** the two *high-probability* beginners' trades he names — (a) market goes **down then breaks out up**, (b) market goes **up, breaks down, then holds**. The **"gap open then straight down"** trades are ~50-50 while learning and should be **skipped**; only a trader with profit/experience should take them (with an SL).
- **Over-trading control:** when a trade of this kind goes wrong, the number of trades must stay **in control** *"नंबर ऑफ ट्रेड आपको कंट्रोल में ही रखनी चाहिए."*

### Keep permanently
1. **Over-thinking = the trader with no position; emotion = the trader with a position** — identify which one is in the tape; the seated (emotional) side is your target.
2. **Same structure at a different time carries a different crowd** — never transfer last year's (or last week's) formula; re-read today's emotional state.
3. **Never buy into the falling side while it is emotional** — wait for the **breakout** (that is when they turn emotionally strong) and put the SL at the low.
4. **Gap-down + level breakdown + buyers already in loss ⇒ no buy**, because the panic-exit cannot be quantified; buy only the breakout that follows.
5. **With a selling plan, no wait is needed on a gap-down** — sell immediately, SL above the closing price; the buyers' stops are already gone.
6. **A gap-up with no SLs above cannot rise** — the early green candle is a cut position, not fuel; the market must come **down to create SLs**, and **that** breakdown-then-turn is the call-side entry.
7. **Don't sell the breakdown that follows a gap-up rise** — it only removes weak-handed buyers; the market tends back to the closing price.
8. **When the other side cannot see any setup, that IS your setup** (gap-up + straight fall ⇒ sell at once, SL above the high).
9. **Flat-open direct momentum is ~50-50** (no SLs to exploit) — take it small or not at all, and **never over-trade** it.
10. **While learning, trade only the two high-probability shapes** — down-then-breakout-up, and up-then-breakdown-then-hold; skip the "straight down after the open" trades until you have experience/profit, and always keep the **number of trades controlled**.
