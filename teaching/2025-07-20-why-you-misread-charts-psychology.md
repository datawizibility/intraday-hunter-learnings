### Core idea
- A week-review session taught as a method: one losing day and four trades in profit in the same week, and the difference between them, he insists, is how clear the **observation** was — not luck, not the setup.
- The chart is read through three things, and they are a **build order**, not a menu: the candle makes the momentum, and candle + momentum together make the psychology. *"कैंडल से क्या होता है? मोमेंटम बनता है। और मोमेंटम और कैंडल को मिलाकर साइकोलॉजी बनता है।"* So psychology is not a mystical third input you need a special tool for — it is the observation you are already forming.
- Psychology is defined operationally: work out **who is sitting where, and in what condition** — the trader who sold / bought puts / shorted Bank Nifty futures is sitting in good profit because the market gave a large down-side momentum.
- **The side sitting in good profit is never a target** — buyer or seller. It is excluded before direction is even discussed.
- Direction is then a **funding question**: to buy you must be able to take the seated sellers' money, and their stops sit in a queue around the round number above. Until that number is crossed, the sellers are untargetable and the plan has to be continuation, not reversal.
- The week's loss came from a **wrong projection** — he assumed retail would not have built positions on a support the market kept taking. Retail had been recruited there, and then the market fell. The closing discipline: 100% does not exist; cut quickly on the loss day, try to make the profit day big.

### The build order: candle → momentum → psychology
He opens with the question students actually ask — of candle, momentum and psychology, which matters most? — and refuses the ranking. They are not parallel inputs; they produce each other. Psychology is therefore not something separate to hunt for: *"साइकोलॉजी समझना आसान होता है।"* If you are watching the momentum and the candles, the observation you form **is** the psychology; there is nothing extra to look at.

The worked example is a profit seat. On the chart under discussion the trader who sold — whoever bought puts or shorted Bank Nifty futures — is in good profit, *"जो सेल करके बैठा है वो ट्रेडर अच्छे प्रॉफिट में बैठा है।"* He then asks how you knew that without seeing his account: you saw the candles, you saw the size of the down-side momentum, and you inferred it. That inference is the whole skill — nothing more was needed.

From the profit seat follows the exclusion rule. *"उसको हम डायरेक्टली टारगेट करने का प्लान नहीं बनाते"* — we do not plan to target him directly — and the mirror for the other side: *"जो प्रॉफिट में आ जाता है उसको हम टारगेट नहीं बनाते"*. A trader who is comfortable and in profit is not the fuel; the money is on the uncomfortable side. This is why he treats the read as a simulation rather than a chart exercise — *"यह केवल और केवल माइंडसेट का गेम होता है।"* — and why the exercise at the end of the session is to ask what **you** would have done sitting in that seat.

### The two-day holiday: count who carried risk
The chart sat in front of a **two-day holiday** — *"2 दिन का हॉलिडे रहने वाला है।"* His reasoning for why this matters is practical: two days is a long interval for a trader, news keeps arriving across it, and the market can open with a large gap either way. So the question that actually decides the next trade is not "where is the trend" but a **census**: *"कितने ट्रेडर ने यहां पे रिस्क लिया होगा"* — how many traders took risk by buying into the break? He calls it the biggest question of the session, and says its answer unlocks the rest: *"अगर यह क्वेश्चन अपना क्लियर हो जाए तो अगला ट्रेड हमें कैसे बनाना है?"*

Once the count is settled, the plan becomes almost mechanical. With buyers seated before the holiday and the sellers sitting in good profit, *"बायर को हम डायरेक्टली टारगेट बना सकते हैं।"* — and his stated tolerance for the open type is wide: a gap-down, a small gap-up, or a flat open all keep the same target. The one branch that changes the arithmetic is a **direct gap-up**, because that open reaches and pays the seated sellers' stop-losses; then the buy is the funded side. Otherwise the buyers' entry is the unfunded one, and the market should continue selling. The open type is where you find out which of the two branches is live.

### 57,000: the wall, the queue of stops, and a market with no capital of its own
The session's spine is a single round number. Asked where the seated sellers' stops will be found, he answers with the queue: *"उसके एसएल कहां पे मिलेंगे आपको आसपास राउंड नंबर अवेलेबल है 57000 का इसके ऊपर मिलेंगे"* — the round number is available, their stops sit above it; some keep the stop a little here, some a little there, and *"कुछ लोग एग्जैक्टली 57000 का रेजिस्टेंस बनाएंगे"*, some will take the exact number as their resistance. That clustering is what turns a number into a gate: *"जब तक मार्केट इस लेवल को क्रॉस नहीं करता है इन सेलर को टारगेट नहीं बनाया जा सकता"* — until the level is crossed the sellers cannot be targeted, their stops cannot be eaten.

Why a *buyer* must care about someone else's stops is the zero-sum half of the lesson, stated as a law: *"मार्केट का अपना कोई कैपिटल नहीं होता।"* There is no market money — *"एक का पैसा जाएगा और वही पैसा दूसरे को मिलेगा।"* One participant's loss pays the other's profit. Hence the entry test: if the sellers' stops are far above and cannot be reached, *"अगर सेलर का पैसा नहीं आने वाला तो बाइंग करने में कोई भी बेनिफिट नहीं है।"* — there is no benefit in buying. The plan therefore becomes continuation, and the far side of that continuation is spelled out: *"जो भी यहां पे बाइंग वगैरह करेंगे वो ट्रैप हो जाएंगे"* — whoever buys here gets trapped.

He deliberately leaves the route open. *"कैसे सेलिंग बनाएगा मार्केट वो मार्केट के ऊपर छोड़ देते हैं।"* The plan fixes the side to be trapped, not the path to it; if the market does not intend to sell, the opening and the first momentum will say so — either a direct gap-up, or an operator arriving from the other side to lift it.

### The operator is not the plan: numbers, and retail's missing unity
The operator appears here mainly to explain what numbers are *for*. He does not need the level for his own trade — *"वह नंबर को नहीं देखता।"* — he can put money to work anywhere. The number is a psychology device: *"नंबर वो क्या करता है? साइकोलॉजी बनाने के लिए यूज़ करता है"* — to make retail find the market interesting, to get them to enter, and to move them out. *"उन्हें ट्रेड बनाने से इन नंबर का कोई लेना देना नहीं होता।"*

But even the large player has to maintain that psychology, and this is the part that stops the number from being decoration: he too takes a resistance and a support off a number, *"अगर डायरेक्टली ऊपर लेकर चले जाएंगे, नीचे लेकर चले जाएंगे तो उनको कुछ पैसा नहीं मिलेगा।"* — take it straight up or straight down and there is no money in it, because nobody was positioned to be harvested. He also warns against building the trade out of an operator story: *"हम यहां पे ऐसा नहीं समझते कि ऑपरेटर बैठे होंगे"*. What follows is offered as understanding, not as a plan input — *"ऑपरेटर इन नंबर से क्या करता है? आपकी साइकोलॉजी को चेंज करने की कोशिश करता है।"* — with the motive named: *"अगर आपको यहां पे बेनिफिट उठाने का मौका मिल जाए तो अगले दिन ट्रेड कौन करेगा?"* A trader who is allowed to profit does not need to come back tomorrow. The two statements sit next to each other in the clip; the caution governs the plan, the explanation sits behind it.

The week's other structural point is **why the crowd cannot turn a market**. Retail has plenty of money, but it never lands at the same time — one buys, another sells, one's stop is hit, one books early — *"इस वजह से हमारी एकता बन नहीं पाती"*. So a turning point is not a crowd event: *"कोई अच्छा ट्रेडर ही घुमाता है।"* Only a holder of big capital can deploy in one shot and give momentum. And the confrontation that follows is not with you: *"कभी-कभी क्या होता है कि यहां पर भी ऑपरेटर बैठा है। सामने से भी बड़ा ऑपरेटर आ जाता है।"* — an operator met an operator, and *"रिटेल तो मिडिल में रहता है।"* Retail sits in the middle. His own rule is stated twice: he does not target the large player, he trades along his side — *"मैं तो उसके अकॉर्डिंग ट्रेड लेता हूं।"* · *"ऑपरेटर को हम टारगेट बनाते भी नहीं।"*

### The loss: projecting your own patience onto retail (and the 100% ceiling)
The losing trade was a **buy**. The chart showed up-side momentum, it looked good, he was following the trend — and the market fell from the open and went into continuous selling: *"उसके बाद अगर नीचे चला गया तो हम लॉस वगैरह लेकर चले गए"*.

His own diagnosis is the most valuable line of the session, because it accuses the method's own premise: he read the psychology **through himself**. *"मैं तो अपने ऊपर लेकर मानता हूं कि मैं कैसा काम करूंगा अगर मुझे यहां पे काम करना होता।"* Watching the tape, he concluded traders would not step in so quickly — *"मैं तो वैसे देख रहा था ओवरऑल मोमेंटम को कि नेगेटिव मोमेंटम है।"* — and, believing retail had little position, expected the market to go up.

What he had under-read was the **recruitment**: the market had taken the exact 57,000 support again and again — *"देखिए यहां पे एग्जैक्टली आपको 57,000 का सपोर्ट ले रहा था बार-बार मार्केट।"* — and a repeatedly defended number is exactly where buyers arrive: *"तो यहां पे बायर आना शुरू हो जाते हैं।"* His restraint was not retail's restraint. The crowd built positions at the number, and when the market turned down they were the pool that got taken. He also notes the layer above the crowd — an operator sitting there, and a bigger one arriving against him.

The next day the read was repaired and paid, and the repair is instructive: the buyers who had come in at the number were removed by the breakdown — *"जो बायर आ रहे थे उनको रिट्रेसमेंट करके निकाल दिया तो अगले दिन अपसाइड का मोमेंटम होना चाहिए"* — so the up-side momentum could be expected, and it came. Buying after a confirmed 57,000 breakdown, once the crowd that had bought the number was gone, produced the week's profit.

The week outcome and the two disciplines that close it: *"तो एक दिन लॉस हुआ चार ट्रेड अपने प्रॉफिट में गए।"* — and he is honest that the winning day could easily have gone the other way, because a market of this size can print one or two more candles for no reason: *"लाखों करोड़ों का मार्केट है।"* Hence the asymmetry: *"लॉस होगा जिस दिन हम क्विकली निकल जाएंगे।"* · *"जिस दिन प्रॉफिट होगा बड़ा करने की कोशिश करेंगे।"* · *"यही आपका डिसिप्लिन है।"* — and the ceiling over all of it: *"यह कभी भी मत मानना कि मेरा 100% प्रॉफिट ही होगा।"*

He attributes a bad stretch — week or month — to observation and nothing else: *"जितनी हमारी ऑब्जरवेशन सही होंगी, उतना ही हम सही होंगे।"* · *"पूरा वीक खराब हो सकता है, पूरा मंथ खराब हो सकता है।"* The homework is the same drill he used on himself: *"आपने सेलिंग किया होता या फिर बाइंग किया होता उसके अकॉर्डिंग डिसीजन लेने की कोशिश कीजिए।"* And the session's own summary of why the week worked: *"हमने अपने माइंडसेट को अपने ऑब्जरवेशन को क्लियर रखा तो अच्छा कर पाए।"*

### Keep permanently
1. Read the chart as three things in **build order** — the candle makes the momentum, and candle + momentum make the psychology. *"कैंडल से क्या होता है? मोमेंटम बनता है। और मोमेंटम और कैंडल को मिलाकर साइकोलॉजी बनता है।"*
2. **Never target the side sitting in good profit** — buyer or seller. *"जो प्रॉफिट में आ जाता है उसको हम टारगेट नहीं बनाते"*
3. **There is no market capital** — one participant's loss funds another's profit, so an entry is only valid if the seated side's stops are reachable. *"मार्केट का अपना कोई कैपिटल नहीं होता।"*
4. **A round number carrying a stop-queue is a gate, not a target**: the seated side cannot be traded against until its number is crossed. *"जब तक मार्केट इस लेवल को क्रॉस नहीं करता है इन सेलर को टारगेट नहीं बनाया जा सकता"*
5. **A repeatedly defended number is a recruitment device, not a floor** — that is where the opposite side was built, and it becomes the next pool.
6. **Numbers exist for psychology**: the large player does not need the level for his own entries; he needs it so the crowd will work there. *"नंबर वो क्या करता है? साइकोलॉजी बनाने के लिए यूज़ करता है"*
7. **Plan from price, not from an operator script** — do not write "the operator did this" into a setup (his own caution in this clip).
8. **Retail cannot act in unison** — many capitals land at different times, so a turning point takes one big single-shot deployment. *"इस वजह से हमारी एकता बन नहीं पाती"*
9. **Do not project your own discipline onto the crowd**: simulating the seat is the method, but assuming the crowd shares your restraint is how this week's loss was made.
10. **Asymmetric exits + the ceiling**: cut fast on the loss day, try to make the profit day big — *"यही आपका डिसिप्लिन है।"* — and never assume 100%; a bad week or month is explained by observation quality, not by the market. *"जितनी हमारी ऑब्जरवेशन सही होंगी, उतना ही हम सही होंगे।"*
