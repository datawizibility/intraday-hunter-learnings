### Core idea
- The question he answers: **how much price movement is needed to hit all the stop-losses on one side?** The answer does two jobs — (a) it sets the **target** of an SL-hunting trade, and (b) once the SLs are known to be *done*, you can consider an **entry on the opposite side**.
- Plain answer (his numbers): normally **~300 points** hits the intraday SLs; **~500 points** usually needs a **retracement** (300 pts strike the first pool, a little psychology builds, then ~200 pts more flushes the rest); **~1000 points** needs a *reason* — **news** or **expiry** — where option writers get targeted.
- **But the number is not fixed:** if a **round number** sits close by, the crowd's SLs may be only **100–150 points** away, so a small breakout/breakdown is enough. The skill is **reading where people actually keep their SLs**, not memorising 300.

### The 300 / 500 / 1000 ladder
| Movement | What it does | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| ~300 pts | hits the intraday traders' SLs directly | the *normal* intraday hunt |
| ~500 pts | 300 to hunt + a small psychology build + ~200 more ⇒ the market "takes 500 out easily" | usually travels **with a retracement** |
| ~1000 pts | **news** or **expiry** (option writers being targeted) | not a daily occurrence |
- On **expiry**: writers sell **₹1 / ₹5 / ₹10** options in huge quantity; an operator bigger than them targets them and the market keeps hitting them *"नीचे से ऊपर तक"* ⇒ the outsized one-day moves. *"कभी-कभी मार्केट का दिल करता है कि उनको भी हम टारगेट करें."*
- **The gap shortcut:** if a big operator wants to trap a *profitable* buyer (who would never *give* an SL while in profit), the cheapest way is a **direct gap-down** — *"1000 का गैप डाउन, 600 का, 700 का"* — no waiting needed.

### The other end of the ladder: 100–150 points
- If the crowd's SLs sit just below a **round number** (e.g. 51,500), a **100-point** move is enough — *"जरूरी नहीं कि मार्केट 300 पॉइंट नीचे निकले."*
- Where the SLs sit is decided by **structure**: if a *high* has formed, people shift the SL **above that high** (52,000 → 52,100), so from ~51,800 the market may need **~300 points**; if a call buyer has no visible support below, the SLs cluster right under the level ⇒ **100 points** finishes it.
- *"हमें सिचुएशन के अकॉर्डिंग देखना होता है कि मार्केट को कितना मोमेंटम करना पड़ेगा एसल को हिट करने के लिए."*

### Keep permanently
1. **Normal intraday SL-hunt distance ≈ 300 points** — a starting estimate, never a rule.
2. **~500 points** usually means 300 to hunt + a small psychology build + ~200 more, and normally travels **with a retracement**.
3. **~1000-point moves need a reason** — **news** or **expiry** (option writers being targeted). Don't expect them daily.
4. **Round numbers shrink the distance** — if the crowd's SLs sit under a round number, only **100–150 points** may be needed; the number is a read of *their* SL placement, not a constant.
5. **Where SLs sit follows structure:** after a high forms, people move the SL **above that high** (52,000 → 52,100) and the distance grows; with no visible support below, SLs cluster right under the level and a small move clears them.
6. **Gaps are the operator's shortcut** — to trap a buyer comfortably in profit, a direct **gap-down (600/700/1000)** does what 300 points of selling cannot.
7. **Expiry mechanism:** writers selling ₹1/₹5/₹10 options in big quantity become the target of a larger operator ⇒ outsized expiry-day momentum.
8. **Set the target just short of the complete SL** and book a little before it — the full pool can sit above the obvious round number (SLs above 52,000 ⇒ watch toward 52,160).
9. **Knowing the SLs are finished opens the opposite side** — that is the second use of the same count.
10. **Read which pool is still un-hit:** a held put whose level the retracements never crossed is *still* a live target.
