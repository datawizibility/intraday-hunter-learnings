### Core idea
- The 2025 frame is **balance, not fear**: the same method must govern loss and profit, and neither panic nor carelessness may drive a decision.
- **Stop blaming the market.** It only makes momentum; the trade was *yours*. Profit is filed as "made by me" and loss as "given by the market" — that asymmetric story is the first thing to change.
- **Find the easiest and best trade** — the one where your setup *agrees* with the market's direction. A setup that fights the tape works rarely (his figure: ~3–4 times in 10), so skip it even when it looks tempting.
- **Never cover a loss**, and never inflate quantity to do it. The classic wipe-out is a 10–15-session winning streak erased in 3–4 days by over-confidence → over-trading → an 8×–10× size jump.
- Keep a **written rule-set** for how you buy and how you sell, and act only where the rule speaks; where it says no entry, ignore the rest — including your own urges.

### Balance is the 2025 theme (loss and profit sized alike)
- Loss is a normal part of the activity (*"लॉस होता है एक ट्रेडिंग का पार्ट"*). The rule is balance: whatever loss you are prepared to take, take a profit of the same order.
- Two workable styles: keep losses small and grow slowly (he calls this correct), **or** take larger losses and also take larger profits. Both can work if the two sides match.
- The failing style: small losses (*"लोस हम छोटा-छोटा रखेंगे"*) but bolting the moment profit shows — or a big loss taken and then a quick snatch at a small profit. His verdict: *"यह ज्यादा दिन तक नहीं चलेगा क्योंकि यहां पे... बैलेंस नहीं बन पा रहा है"* (it won't last, because no balance forms).
- Balance also means **neither stressed nor carefree**. He rejects both the "my money isn't being made" panic and the "money will be made anyway" complacency; every loss and every profit gets analysed.

### Attitude = the whole game (do not blame the market)
- The complaint he keeps hearing: *"मार्केट गलत हो गया / मार्केट ने आज गलत मोमेंटम दे दिया"* — "the market went wrong / gave wrong momentum today". His reply: the market's only job is to make momentum.
- *"225 के अंदर सबसे पहले आपको यह काम करना है कि मार्केट को लेकर आपको एटीट्यूड चेंज करना पड़ेगा"* — in 2025 the first project is to change your **attitude** toward the market.
- The same trader says *"मैंने प्रॉफिट बना लिया"* on a good day and blames the market on a bad day. The market neither makes your money nor hands you the loss: *"मार्केट ना ही तो पैसा बना के देता है ना ही मार्केट आपको लोस देता है"*.
- The market never told you to buy or sell; you read a situation and chose a side. There is total freedom in the choice — so a wrong trade is your mistake, not the market's. Momentum can come up or down, loss or profit can follow; the work is to run it, not to blame it.

### Find the easiest, best trade (the setup must agree with the market)
- The one 2025 work item: *"सबसे आसान और अच्छा ट्रेड हमें फाइंड आउट करना है"*.
- If the market is going up **and** your setup says buy → take it. If the market is going up **but** your setup says sell → **ignore it**, however attractive, because the error probability is higher when the setup fights the tape: *"इसमें गलती होने की प्रोबेबिलिटी ज्यादा है... इसमें गलती होने की प्रोबेबिलिटी कम है"*.
- Setups will sometimes offer a profitable-looking counter-move; skip those too. *"जितना आप अपनी ट्रेडिंग को इजी रखोगे उतना ही ज्यादा आप आगे जाओगे"* — the easier you keep your trading, the further you go.
- He concedes the counter-trend trade pays **bigger** when it works — but it works rarely: *"मार्केट में 10 में से होगा तीन या चार बार आपको मौका मिले"* (~3–4 in 10); the rest of the time it just hands you a loss.
- Momentum-identification note: a fast up-move can still be followed by a large fall. That read is only available if you had **planned for rejection** and had the chance to capture it — if there was no chance to capture it, the miss *is* the mistake.

### Never cover a loss; never inflate quantity
- Stated plainly as the next 2025 rule: with a loss on the book, do not trade to cover it. Two or three trades a day is fine; a trade taken *because* you lost is not — *"लॉस को कवर करने के लिए एक भी ट्रेड आपको नहीं करना है"*.
- And specifically: *"लॉस को कवर करने के लिए कभी भी क्वांटिटी को इंक्रीज नहीं करना है"*.
- The wipe-out mechanism he describes: a trader runs 10–15 sessions of continuous profit, becomes **over-confident** (*"मार्केट का हमें सब कुछ पता लग गया... अब मैं गलत नहीं हो सकता"*), starts **over-trading** and **raises quantity** — his illustration: base 100 quantity, then roughly 800, roughly 1000 (an 8×–10× jump) — and the 10–15 days of profit vanish in 3–4 days when the loss finally comes.
- His point: money *is* made in the market; the failure is that it is **not kept**. Raising quantity is not wrong by itself — doing it 8×–10× straight off a short win-streak is.
- Over-trading can forfeit the same day's gains; without over-trading the profit is made and stays.

### Change with time (instrument focus shifts)
- *"बैंक निफ्टी के अंदर वीकली एक्सपायरी खत्म हो चुका है"* — with Bank Nifty's weekly expiry gone, he is deliberately rebuilding his habit on **Nifty 50** and **Sensex**, where the momentum now is, instead of staying locked into Bank Nifty (his earlier focus).
- General instruction: a trader must **change with the times**. "I always traded stocks, so I'll always trade stocks" is wrong; if there is a chance to trade options or futures while it lasts, take it — try every segment while you can, and only step back if a real problem appears.
- Reason he gives: when all three indices' weekly expiries ran together, such large momentum was rarely seen; now the market gives big momentum, so put money where the opportunity currently is.

### The market is a cycle (loss runs do not define you)
- Sometimes 3–4 days run continuously in loss. The wrong reaction is despair: *"मेरा लॉस चल रहा है अब मैं क्या करूं... अब तो मैं लोस में जाने वाला हूं"*.
- The market is a cycle (*"मार्केट का क्या होता है एक सर्कल होता है"*): continuous profit at times, heavier losses at others. Manage the situation and keep the mindset balanced and it works out.
- The mirror trap: even with 4–5 days of profit banked, never go carefree. Risk is lower with profit on the book, but "anything can happen tomorrow" — *"कभी भी ऐसा नहीं करना है कि चार से पाच दिन का ट्रेडिंग का प्रॉफिट हो गया अगले दिन हमारा कुछ भी हो जाए"*.

### Rules over mood
- What to build for 2025: a **rule-set** — what you look at to buy, what you look at to sell.
- Act only where the rule speaks: enter where it says entry, ignore where it says no entry; neither stressed (money not being made) nor carefree (it'll be made anyway).
- Capital advice: for a good trader capital never truly runs short — *"कभी भी कैपिटल की कमी होती नहीं"*; focus on *learning trading* first, and the capital follows. Some capital of your own helps you learn faster; going straight into options with it just means bigger losses.

### Keep permanently
1. **Size the profit to the loss.** Whatever loss you are prepared to take, take a profit of the same order — small/small or large/large, but never small-loss-and-run-from-profit, nor big-loss-and-snatch-small-profit.
2. **Never blame the market.** It produces momentum; you produce the trade. If the trade is wrong, the attitude is the thing to fix.
3. Trade the setup that **agrees** with the market's direction; **skip** the setup that fights it, however tempting — error probability is higher there, and the counter-trend win comes only ~3–4 times in 10.
4. **Never take a trade to cover a loss**, and never raise quantity to cover a loss.
5. Do **not** 8×–10× your quantity after a short winning streak. Raise size slowly and only after sustained results; a 10–15-day win can be erased in 3–4 days.
6. Money is made and **not kept** when over-confidence becomes over-trading and size inflation — protect the winning streak the way you protect against a loss.
7. Build a **written rule-set**: what you look at to buy, what you look at to sell. Enter only where the rule speaks; ignore the rest, including your own urges.
8. **Acclimatise to the market's cycle.** Loss runs will come; keep the situation managed and the mindset balanced — never despair in a loss run, never go carefree in a profit run.
9. **Cut the loss when it appears.** Do not hold a losing position on a story ("the operator is sitting, so the market must go up").
10. **Change with the times.** Shift instruments/segments as circumstances change rather than locking into one (his example: Bank Nifty weekly expiry ended → focus moved to Nifty/Sensex).
