### Core idea
- The week traded **both ways** — buys and sells — and made money, yet the whole session circles one question: why does the market keep running *after* momentum already looks exhausted?
- **Continuation needs a momentum threshold.** A small move (~200–300 points) does not carry the market on; it takes a large displacement (~600–800 points, he narrates a 708-point momentum) plus a trigger — an established downtrend, running bad news, and a crowd already positioned to sell.
- The market's real product is **psychology**: it hands greed to the seller (a resistance to sell into), confusion to the buyer (a big gap-open that feels unbuyable), then runs up — a trap built for both sides.
- **Entry is decided target-first, not price-first.** Before buying, the minimum 70–80% of the target must be reachable; the market builds the last ~20% itself. The stop-loss and target set the entry, never the reverse.
- Honest loss of the week: a sell-side entry that failed because he stopped asking *why* the market was going up — and the rule he draws is that the loss must not be allowed to grow.
- **Do not deploy capital suddenly.** Use large capital only once the stage is right, or the same mistake recurs even from a profitable seat.

### Momentum thresholds — when a move is allowed to continue
- Small momentum is not continuation: *"अगर आपको 200 300 पॉइंट के मुमेंटम मिले तो वहां पर ऐसा कुछ नहीं हुआ कि मार्केट कंटिन्यू भक्त आ जाए"* ⚠️ (ASR: `भक्त` for `भाग`).
- Only the large displacement counts: *"जब 708 पॉइंट मुमेंटम कर देता है"*, and the generic band *"जब 600 800 पॉइंट का मुमेंटम आ जाता है तभी आपको इस प्रकार के मार्केट में मुमेंटम देखने को मिलेंगे"*.
- Conditions he requires before such momentum can appear — not just the number: an already-running downtrend, live negative news, and a crowd convinced it is going further down. The threshold is a **read**, not a trigger by itself.
- The payoff of a threshold read: he treats big displacement as the market **managing your psychology** — a green surprise is printed precisely because positions are parked against it.

### Operator psychology — the two-sided trap
- Rain without clouds: *"कि बिन बादल बारिश हो जाती है"*.
- The operator buys **because** sellers are parked: *"हर्षद मेहता क्या करता था वह बाइक करता था मार्केट को"* and *"उसे पता था कि मार्केट में अपसेट शैलर बैठे हैं"* — when those sellers are forced to cut, momentum arrives and the operator's money is made. He does not care about taking the market up or down; only about his own book.
- The market feeds the seller **greed** so the fuel keeps forming: *"लेकिन सैलरी को लालच आना चाहिए क्योंकि अगर लालच नहीं आएगा मार्केट बढ़ता ही जाएगा"* and *"तो मार्किट उसको लालच बना कर देगा उसे ट्रैफिक बना कर देगा"*.
- The market feeds the buyer **confusion** at the same time: *"कंफ्यूज किया कि मार्केट में इतना बड़ा गिफ्ट ओपन हो गया"*.
- Give the other side a target: *"जो सेटिंग करते बैठे इनका भी प्रॉफिट कहा जाए"* ⚠️ — the move is engineered so that the *other* trader's profit closes it, which is why the market *"मार्किट डाइरैक्टली प्रॉफिट नहीं बना कर देगा"* (it does not hand you the profit directly).

### Building the entry — target first, stop second
- The 70–80% gate before entry: *"मिनिमम मेरा सत्तर से अस्सी परसेंट टारगेट अचीव हो जाना चाहिए 20 पर सेंट मार्केट अपने आप बनाकर दे देगा"*.
- Asking both questions first: *"यह बहुत ही इंपोर्टेंट शुरुआत होती है इलस्ट्रेटर को एंट्री बनाने के लिए"* ⚠️ (ASR `इलस्ट्रेटर`).
- If resistance sits immediately overhead, a buy cannot run — the nearby sellers will activate there. So the reachable-target test rules out entries where the room to move is already used up.
- He is explicit that a correct read does not mean a mechanical entry: he often wants the setup but the *room* is missing, so he passes.

### Levels as instrument — round numbers and named triggers
- Round numbers are treated like an indicator input, not a mechanical trigger: *"मार्केट में यहां पर इसके आसपास रॉन्ग नंबर यह राउंड नंबर को इग्नोर कर देते हैं"* ⚠️.
- The one level he actually reads off a chart this session — a level he set near 36,500: *"मैंने सेट किया यहां पर एक 36500 का लेवल तो आसपास जब इस लेवल का"*; the breakout above it is what triggered his switch to selling (he says he could not stop himself). The index is **not** named in that sentence.
- He then reads the printed structure — a big green candle after the gap — as the crowd's confusion, and reasons from who is trapped rather than from the shape alone.

### Risk, size and psychology
- Keep the loss small — after a loss, re-ask why the market moved: *"जब मेरा लोस हो चुका था मैंने यह नहीं सोचा कि मार्केट ऊपर क्यों"* ⚠️ and *"यह बहुत ज्यादा इंपोर्टेंट होते कि से आप स्ट्रेट करोगे तो आपका लोस बड़ा नहीं होना चाहिए"* ⚠️.
- Capital discipline: *"मार्केट में आप बड़ा कैपिटल मत यूज के लिए कीजिए जब तक आप एक अच्छे स्टेज पर ना पूर्ण जाएं"* ⚠️ and *"अचानक से जितना भी पैसा होता है वह लगाना शुरू कर देते हैं तो वह गलती का भी निकलना"*.
- The rope analogy for fear-of-the-familiar: *"एक आदमी तू बार-बार रस्सी पर चलते उसको डर नहीं लगता है"*, and its application *"इतनी बार कर चुका हूं कि इस राशि पर चलने में मुझे डर नहीं लगता"*. He notes he had given this example before (*"एक चीज को मैंने आपको बताया था"*).
- Confidence and fear: *"कॉन्फिडेंस की सेट करना है डरना नहीं है"* ⚠️, *"मैं बोलता हूं आपको ही मार्केट को समझने की कोशिश कीजिए मार्किट से डरिए मत"*, and — someone is always running it — *"इसे की भी कोई ना कोई इंसान चला रहे"*.

### Keep permanently
1. Build the entry from the **target and the stop first** — never let the entry decide the exit.
2. Require the **minimum reachable target (~70–80%)** before entering; accept that the market builds the last ~20% by itself.
3. **Size momentum before trusting it:** below the momentum threshold a move is not continuation; only a large displacement under a stated trigger carries the trend on.
4. Assume the trap is **two-sided**: greed installed in the seller, confusion installed in the buyer — trade the opposite of the crowd's emotion, not the emotion.
5. Buy only where the crowd's **stops are parked**; the operator buys because parked sellers must eventually cut.
6. Treat round numbers as a **read type** (like an indicator input), never as a mechanical trigger.
7. If your profit requires the **other side to keep arriving**, that is a warning, not a confirmation.
8. **Keep the loss small** — after entering, never carry a loss without re-asking why the market is moving against you.
9. Do **not deploy large capital suddenly** — only at the right stage; abundant capital is a discipline problem, not a profit guarantee.
10. **Fear of a setup drops with repetition**, not with size; familiarity is what removes hesitation.
