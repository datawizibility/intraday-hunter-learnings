### Core idea
- The session's first sentence is its thesis: **on a chart you can draw support/resistance anywhere** — *"अगर आप चार्ट पर जाओगे तो कहीं भी सपोर्ट-रेजिस्टेंस लगा सकते हो"* — **the skill is the filter, not the drawing:** *"लेकिन कौन सा सपोर्ट रेजिस्टेंस ज्यादा इफेक्टिव रहने वाला है, इस पर आपको [फ़िल्टर] बनाना चाहिए"*.
- He names three separate sources of a *usable* level in this one session: **(a) the chart** (swing structure, break-then-react, closing print), **(b) public disclosure data** — **bulk/block deals on the NSE website** — i.e. *where a large investor actually transacted*, and **(c) the counterparty's own state** — *is his position still there at all?*
- ⭐ That third one is the actual masterclass: a level is not a line, it is **somebody's open position**. Before trusting a level you ask whether the person who created it is **still in it** or has already booked and left.
- This is a **level-sourcing** session, not a trade-setup session. **No entry, no exit, no size, no outcome, no P&L** appears anywhere in it.

### ⭐ Source (b): bulk/block-deal data — where the large money actually transacted
- He sends the viewer to the **NSE website** for the disclosure: *"आपको यह भी पता होना चाहिए कि ब्लॉक डील… यह इंफॉर्मेशन [NSE] की वेबसाइट से भी ले सकते हो"*. (ASR renders the term loosely; see fidelity.)
- What you can read off it: **the date and the price at which a large participant bought** — *"यहां पर आपको नज़र आ रहा है कि 5 अगस्त के दिन इस बॉक्स के अंदर इन्होंने बाय [चालू] किया है — किस प्राइस पर, कि यह भी आपको पता लग गया"*.
- Then the reading rule: **that transacted price is a candidate support/resistance** — *"यह प्राइस हमारे लिए सपोर्ट या रेजिस्टेंस की तरह काम कर सकता है"*. He walks a stock chart to show the market reacting around that price, and says: whichever way it behaved, the point is now a reference — *"जो भी प्राइस आपने देखा, वह आपके लिए [सपोर्ट या] रेजिस्टेंस बन गया"*.
- ⭐ **A block deal is not a market price.** *"यह प्राइस आपको [मार्केट में] नहीं मिलेगा — यह [पहले से] डिसाइड होता है कि कौन कहां पर [बेचने] वाला है. लेकिन एक बड़ी क्वांटिटी पर बाय मुश्किल होता है, तो यह [प्री-डिज़ाइड] होता है."* The deal exists **because** size cannot be absorbed in the open market — which is exactly why the print is informative.
- He names the counterparties as examples of *who* leaves such footprints and instructs the viewer to identify them: **Rakesh Jhunjhunwala** in **Tata Steel** and **Tata Motors**, with the buying entity he tells you to research — *"यह [Rare Enterprises] कौन है, यह आपको पता लगाना होगा"* (ASR-corrupt; see fidelity). His point is not the name — it is that **the name behind the print is researchable, and the price is public**.
- Also on the same data set, a handful of stocks where such a print occurred on **5 August** (he names one he cannot pronounce cleanly, plus **NMDC**) — *"यहां पर कुछ ऐसे स्टॉक हैं जहां 5 अगस्त के दिन… [बड़ी डील] हुई है"*. His concession: these prints *"जितना [भी] इफेक्ट करते हैं"* — the effect is visible around that price, not a guarantee.
- ⭐ **Business reading:** price-levels left by disclosed institutional trades are a *public* footprint of an otherwise invisible participant — a free, auditable level source. 2022-07-10 taught that manipulation lives in low-valuation single stocks; this session shows the opposite instrument-side tool: **in the high-quality names, the big player's hand is on the record.**

### ⭐ Source (a): what makes a *chart* level high-quality
He builds the chart-side rules in this order:

1. **Swing structure first — the major extremes.** *"जिसमें [स्विंग] हाई बना, वह हमारे लिए अच्छा रेजिस्टेंस होगा, और जिसमें [स्विंग] लो बना, वह हमारे लिए [अच्छा] सपोर्ट होगा"*, and: *"इस प्रकार हमें सपोर्ट-रेजिस्टेंस को मेजर लेवल पर देखना चाहिए"* — **major levels only**, which is the same control established in 2022-01-30 and taught as drawing technique on 2022-03-06.
2. **Break-then-react.** A point where the market broke a level **and then reacted** (came back to it) becomes a working support/resistance: *"अगर हमें बहुत अच्छा रिएक्शन मिला हो, कोई ब्रेकआउट होने के बाद रिएक्शन मिला, तो वह पॉइंट हमारे लिए सपोर्ट-रेजिस्टेंस की तरह काम कर सकता है"*. Mirror case: *"कोई ब्रेकडाउन हुआ था और वहां से मार्केट दोबारा ऊपर चढ़ने लगा, तो वह पॉइंट बेहतरीन सपोर्ट की तरह काम कर सकता है."*
3. **Break with immediate one-way selling = deliberate.** *"अगर कोई ब्रेकआउट हुआ और डायरेक्ट सैलिंग आने लगा, तो वहां पर डेफिनेटली किसी ने जान-बूझकर उसको ब्रेक [किया] है"* — the break was not noise, it was a decision, so the point is loaded.
4. **The closing print is the arbiter.** Levels work *"क्लोजिंग प्राइस"* as the reference; if the level is *broken/disrespected on a closing basis*, it loses standing — *"यह क्लोजिंग प्राइस है… [ब्रेक] हो गया तो मतलब यह [अब] सपोर्ट-रेजिस्टेंस नहीं है, तो इसको थोड़ा सा इग्नोर कर सकते हैं"* (he allows a *small* trade there instead of a position). This is the same **closing print as the market's own statement** that 2022-07-09 introduced as a rule.
5. **Position underneath is the level's quality.** Where a large player built, *"उन्होंने बहुत ही सोच-समझकर यहां पर पोज़िशन बनाया होगा"* — which is *why* the level holds. Where price later closes **beyond** it, that is not just a line break — the positioned party is now in trouble (see the stop-loss section below).

### ⭐ Source (c): position availability — has the big player already left?
This is the section that makes the session a *master course* rather than a drawing lesson:
- After the price ran, he asks the viewer to check **whether the position is still live**: *"आपको देखना कि [उसकी] पोज़िशन अभी के लिए अवेलेबल है या नहीं है."*
- The test he gives: **if the move already delivered a large profit, assume the position is gone.** *"इन्होंने बहुत अच्छा प्रॉफिट भी बना लिया… तो अपनी पोज़िशन बुकिंग करके भाग गए होंगे"* — therefore *"यह पॉइंट हमारे लिए अब ज्यादा काम करने का नहीं"*.
- Practical consequence: at that price, **a new position will have to be built from scratch** before the market responds again — *"यहां पर अभी अगर मार्केट [आएगा] तो यहां पर पोज़िशन बनना शुरू होगी, क्योंकि हमारे [पास] [पुरानी] पोज़िशन नहीं है."*
- ⭐ So the same line can be a **wall** (position live, protected) or **empty air** (position booked, market must rebuild there). The line does not tell you which; the **profit already taken** does.

### ⭐ Why a large investor's prints behave the way they do
He explains the counterparty's mechanics in plain business terms — the reason such prices become levels:
- **You cannot accumulate size into a falling market.** *"अगर मुझे बल्क में कोई क्वांटिटी [खरीदनी] है, तो मैं ऐसे थोड़ी [करूंगा] — मार्केट गिरता जाएगा, मैं यहां [खरीदता] जाऊंगा? क्योंकि अगर मैंने [ऑर्डर] शुरू कर दिया, तो मार्केट और नीचे चलना शुरू हो जाएगा, तो वह दिक्कत हो जाएगी."*
- **So he sells into strength — and gets a good price point doing it.** *"लेकिन अगर मैं बढ़ती हुई मार्केट में [बेचूंगा], तो मुझे प्राइस पॉइंट बहुत अच्छा मिलेगा."*
- **Therefore the level to look for is where such activity left a mark** — *"वह पॉइंट हमारे लिए बेहतरीन सपोर्ट या रेजिस्टेंस की तरह काम कर सकता है"* — and the level's strength comes from the fact that the decision behind it was made deliberately.
- His framing, closing the section: the market is a **quality game** — *"मार्केट में प्रॉपर्टी का गेम है, क्वालिटी का गेम"* — *which* price point you stand at matters more than how many points you trade.

### Stop-loss placement relative to the level
- *"मिनिमम अपना स्टॉपलॉस [लेवल] के आसपास होना चाहिए."*
- And the cascade he explains: if a candle **closes beyond** the level where the buyer built, *"उसकी भी पोज़िशन में प्रॉब्लम हो जाएगी; और अगर उसने अपनी पोज़िशन में चेंज करना शुरू कर दिया, तो मार्केट में और भी [गिरावट] आ सकती है."* — one participant's stop is the next participant's fuel. (Same "stops are the fuel / consumed in order" spine as 2022-01-30 and 2022-02-06.)
- ⭐ **Practical:** place the stop **around the level**, not a comfortable arbitrary distance from your entry — because the level, not your entry, is what the other side is defending.

### Discipline and the closing note
- Having taught the identification, he puts the burden back on the viewer: *"मैं जितने पॉइंट [लर्निंग के] प्रोवाइड कर दूंगा, लेकिन फिर भी आप तभी काम कर पाओगे जब आप [खुद] सीखना शुरू करोगे."*
- **Practice = your own hand-drawing.** *"जब आप [अपने हाथ से] लगाना शुरू कर दोगे इन सपोर्ट-रेजिस्टेंस को, तब मेरी [मेहनत] काम आएगी."* Only support/resistance *seen* from a video does not transfer.
- **Discipline is a separate skill from identification.** *"डिसिप्लिन भी एक [मुश्किल] काम होता है, उसको हैंडल करना आना चाहिए"* — and so is handling **momentum**: *"अगर मार्केट ऊपर चढ़ने लगे, थोड़ा नीचे आने लगे, ऊपर जाने लगे — वह वाला मुमेंटम भी आपको थोड़ा सा हैंडल करना आना चाहिए"* — plus the choice between **taking a small profit and thinking big** *"अगर आप छोटा प्रॉफिट लेते हो, बड़ा सोच लेते हो"*.
- He also shows, live on the charts, how he **trims** when a level can't be targeted: after the market continued falling, *"अगर थोड़ा सा नीचे आने लगता है तो मैं थोड़ा बहुत [प्रॉफिट बुक] कर सकता [हूं]"* — i.e. the level read also manages an open position.

### Keep permanently
1. **You can draw a level anywhere; only the filter makes it tradeable.** The work is not line-drawing, it is deciding which of the many lines is *effective* — start from **major swing highs/lows**.
2. **A level is somebody's open position — check whether it is still open.** If the move already delivered a big profit, assume the position is booked and the line is empty air; the market must rebuild there before it responds again.
3. **Look for the big player's disclosed prints.** Large-deal (bulk/block) data on the exchange website shows **the date and the price at which size actually changed hands** — a public, auditable footprint of the invisible participant.
4. **A block deal is deliberately a non-market price.** Size cannot be absorbed openly, so the deal is pre-decided — that is exactly why the printed price is worth marking on the chart.
5. **A break with immediate one-way selling was intentional.** If a level broke and the market ran without a fight, someone decided that; treat the point as loaded rather than as noise.
6. **The closing print is the arbiter.** A level respected or rejected on close is the market's own statement; if it is broken on a closing basis, ignore it (and at most take a small quantity there).
7. **Put the stop around the level, not around your comfort.** The other side is defending the level; if a candle closes beyond it, the positioned trader is in trouble and his exit becomes the next leg's fuel.
8. **A large investor cannot accumulate into weakness.** His own order moves the price, so he sells into strength — which is why his prints sit at extremes and why those prices become references.
9. **Do not confuse identification with discipline.** Reading a level and holding a position through its momentum are two different skills; both must be practised — and so is the small-profit vs big-target decision.
10. **Your own hand-drawn levels are the only ones that transfer.** Watching him mark levels teaches nothing; practice marking them yourself, then his material starts paying.
