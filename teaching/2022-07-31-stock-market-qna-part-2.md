### Q1 — Can one particular setup keep working for 2–3 years, or must it be changed?

- It depends on *what kind* of setup was built. A price-action / technical setup (levels, the way the trade is structured) does **not** need changing — *"बना रखा है तो अगर इस प्रकार का आपका कोई"*, then *"शैटर पर तो आपको चेंज करने की आवश्यकता"* (⚠️ `शैटर` = setup). The instruction is blunt: *"अपना सेट अप चेंज नहीं करना होता"*.
- Experience, not replacement, is what makes a setup pay: *"रखता है अपने रूल को वह मार्किट से पैसा"*. The switcher fails — *"बनाना सीख जाता है और जो अपने सेट अप को"* is the contrast he draws, i.e. the one who changes it again and again does not easily make money from the market.
- The single difference between a trader being made and not being made is this one thing: *"एक ट्रैक्टर बनने में दो क्विड नहीं बनने"* (⚠️ `ट्रैक्टर` = trader; the second clause is ASR damage). One abandons the setup, the other continues it.
- If the trend changes, build a **separate** downtrend rule-set: *"आपको डाउनट्रेंड की अलग रोल बनाने पड़ेंगे"*, and define it explicitly — *"डिफाइन करना पड़ेगा कि डाउनट्रेंड मार्केट"*.
- **Genuinely new here:** he names *how* to change safely — not the setup, only one rule inside it; if a trend made money and then stopped, change something within the same setup, or add the downtrend rule-set on top.

### Q2 — The trader who doubled capital but once risked 25% of it: how to avoid that mistake?

- The case: a viewer doubled capital with discipline, then slipped — *"अपना टैबलेट ब्लास्ट मन के अंदर डिसिप्लिन"* — and gave back **25% of capital** in one trade: *"इन्होंने कैसेट ले लिया था जिसमें ट्वेंटी"*, *"फाइव परसेंट कैपिटल का सोच नई ने उसके बाद"* (⚠️ `ट्वेंटी फाइव परसेंट` = 25%; an illustration of the error, not a level).
- Restated plainly: *"उन्हें अपने कैपिटल का ट्वेंटी फाइव पर"*.
- **Find the mistake in your own record first**, before blaming the setup — *"अपनी मिट्टी को फ्रंट कीजिए"*. The two mistakes he names are the classic pair: quantity jumped on seeing profit — *"प्रॉफिट को देखकर क्वांटिटी को इंक्रीज कर"* — and the losing trade was never cut — *"दिया था या फिर अपने लूज इन पेडो को काटा"*.
- The forgiveness budget and its limit: the market forgives roughly twelve mistakes — *"आपकी मार्केट माफ कर देगा लेकिन बार-बार"* — but a **repeated** one is terminal: *"वाली मिस्टेक मार्केट आपकी रिस्पेक्ट खत्म"*, after which it starts punishing instead of forgiving.
- **The fix is sequencing.** Bank first, then size up: *"होने दर्द होने दीजिए और उसको अपने घर ले"* (⚠️ the opening clause is ASR damage — the sense is to let that money season a while, then take it home). Only what is left raises the quantity — *"रहता है उसकी ऊपर क्वांटिटी बढ़ाने की"* — the purpose being a visible reserve outside the trading account.
- Cut the loser and never patch it, and keep the mistake logged: *"कभी भी आप लूजिंग ब्रेड को एवरेज"* (`ब्रेड` = trade), and *"और अपने लूज सिगरेट को काटना पड़ेगा अपनी"*.
- **Never tell yourself the setup is perfect:** *"सोचिए कि आपका सेट अप हंड्रेड परसेंट काम"* — the sentence continues *करता है रविवार को ऐसा 19 सोचने की*, i.e. do not think it always works and that the market must therefore go your way.

### Q3 — Can a strategy be built that starts working at 3 and exits after 3:15?

- Yes, and it lives inside the **15-minute window**: *"है जिसमें तीन मार्केट में काम करना शुरू"*, *"तो जस्ट में 15 मिनट के ऊपर डिपेंड"*.
- His reason for the window is that the close can pay — *"ज़्यादा प्रॉफिट हुआ और दोस्तों उसके बाद"* — after which you are to leave.
- **Make it mechanical and stop thinking.** Read candle colour, not the tape's story — *"चाहे मेरा मैं 5 मिनट का यूज कर हूं चाय"* — on a 1/3/5-minute frame, so that green becomes a buy and red a reverse — *"आपकी बाइक कर लूंगा अगर मुझे रेट बनता नजर"* (`बाइक` = buy).
- The whole rule is: do not use your brain — *"आपको अपना दिमाग ना लगाएं"* — and the mechanical form is stated outright: *"मैं देखूंगा कि बजे मार्केट अगर ऊपर है तो"* ... green means take it, red means leave.
- **Risk:reward is a ladder you walk down, then flip.** Start at 1:2, then 1:1 — *"उसके बाद मैं वन बाय वन रखकर देखूंगा"*; if the higher rung works, good — *"वन बाय टू में काम कर है तो बहुत ही अच्छी"*. If neither works, invert the whole thing — *"उल्टा कर दूंगा क्योंकि वन बाय वन में"* (if one side does not work, take the opposite side).
- He frames this as *how to build any strategy*, not a one-off: pick the tools visible in your favour, write rules on them, and keep following the same rules so the read needs no daily decision.

### Q4 — Traders who wait for the stop-loss but cannot hold for the target

- The diagnosis: disciplined on the stop, undisciplined on the target — *"वेट करते हैं बहुत अच्छी बात है लेकिन"*. It worsens when the target is undefined — *"मुझे पता नहीं कि टारगेट मुझे रखना कर इसे"* — and the reported result is the classic inversion: small profit, bigger loss — *"वह कम होता है उन लास्ट बड़ा हो जाता है"*.
- **His remedy: use a trailing-profit method** — *"प्रॉफिट का मेथड यूज करें"* — but first reduce size while learning it: *"थोड़ा पहले आप इग्नोर लेवल पर तो"* ... quantity down a little.
- **Keep a written record.** *"आप अपना प्रॉपर इसको रिकार्ड मेनटेन"* — write what you actually took: *"था मगर मैंने ₹10 का रिस्क लिया था क्या"*, *"मैंने ₹10 का टारगेट रखा था तो उस टारगेट"* (⚠️ **₹10 risk / ₹10 target** = a spoken illustration of the record form, not a level).
- **Order of learning:** first practise exiting **at** the target and leave trailing aside until that is reliable — *"के अकॉर्डिंग निकलना ट्रेनिंग वगैरह छोड़"*, *"कोड निकलना देखिए टो अप्रोच किया कोड"*. Only once exiting at the target has become confident and the account is making something do you add trailing.
- Closing line: never let the profit stay smaller than the loss — *"कभी भी पॉकेट छोटा मत होने"* ... देना है नोट से ज्यादा.

### Q5 — How do you read what is in the other trader's mind — greed or fear?

- The question is answered without any indicator — a two-tell structure read on the tape. *"शर्म कैसे पता लगाएं"*, and *"लालच आ रहा है या डर लग रहे हैं"* is the thing a reader has to be able to see (`शर्म` = the viewer's question, ASR-damaged).
- **In a downtrend, when the market suddenly jumps up near resistance, it is the sellers' greed that traps them** — *"अचानक से कुर्रे आपके आसपास आकर मार्केट"*, then *"सैलरी को लालच का यार को डालेगी का"* (`सैलरी` = seller; the second clause is ASR damage — the sense is that the sellers' greed draws them in).
- **Mirror in an uptrend: a hold at support frightens the buyers** — *"इसी तरह मार्केट अगर आप ट्रेन में चलें और"*, *"को लाइक आयुष्य को थोड़ा डर लगेगा"* (`ट्रेन` = trend, `वायर` = buyer; `आयुष्य` unresolved). Seller greed at a resistance jump, buyer fear at a support hold — that is the whole tell.
- **The catalogue behind it:** *"बनाए रखें मार्केट को समझने के लिए 21"*, *"प्रकार की सिचुएशन में क्या-क्या मार्केट"* — **21 defined situations** for reading the market, which he narrates when reviewing his own trades.
- The payoff is a skill that grows with use: *"सभी पहचान पाओगे जब आप यह समझ पाओगी"* — read the psychology, then trade.

### Keep permanently
1. **Keep the setup, change the rule.** A price-action / technical setup lives for years; what evolves is one rule inside it. Switching setups destroys the knowledge; continuing one builds it.
2. **Build a separate downtrend rule-set.** When a trend setup stops paying, do not discard it — define explicitly how you *see* a downtrend market and add it alongside.
3. **Take the profit home before you raise quantity.** Booking a visible reserve outside the trading account is what stops the account from tempting you into a bigger, undisciplined position.
4. **Never average a losing trade** and never leave a loser uncut — the two mistakes in the clip's case study are always the same pair (quantity jumped on profit; loss not cut).
5. **Never believe a setup is 100%.** Every working setup contains a loss branch; treating it as perfect is what breaks it.
6. **A first mistake is forgiven; a repeated one costs respect.** Roughly twelve errors are absorbed, then the market stops forgiving and starts punishing — so log and repair the error, do not rerun it.
7. **In the close window, be mechanical.** A 15-minute end-of-day strategy (green means take it, red means leave, 1/3/5-minute frame, exit after 3:15) must not use the brain; write the rule and follow it.
8. **Risk:reward is a ladder: 1:2 → 1:1 → invert.** Test the higher rung first, step down, and if one side does not work take the opposite side.
9. **Master exiting at the target before adding trailing-profit.** Reduce size while learning it; use the record (₹10 risk / ₹10 target form) to see whether profit is being cut short.
10. **Read greed and fear from structure, not indicators.** Seller greed at a resistance jump in a downtrend; buyer fear at a support hold in an uptrend — and keep a written catalogue of the situations (21 here) that make the read repeatable.
