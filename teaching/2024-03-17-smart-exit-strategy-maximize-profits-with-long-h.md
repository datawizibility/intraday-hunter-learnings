### Core idea
- He defines the two words before answering the question. **Big profit is not a rupee figure** (*"1 लाख … 2 लाख … 5 लाख"* are all rejected) — it is **extra points beyond your own normal**: if your normal capture is **50 points**, then the day you hold and take **70 / 80–90 / 100** is your big-profit day.
- **The smallest loss is exactly the loss you pre-decided — no more and no less.** Giving the market *extra* points (decided 60, took 70/80) is a **big loss** and is never to be done. But cutting *early* (decided 60, took 40) is **also wrong**, because the market could have dipped a little more and then delivered the target — *"बेवजह ही लॉस बुकिंग कर लिया."*
- Having stated the definitions, the session's real subject is **exit**: decide *how* to exit first, because exit quality is what turns a normal day into a big-profit day.

### Exit is a function of two inputs
He splits every exit into: **(a) price level** — where the market is working relative to round/important numbers — and **(b) momentum type**:
| Momentum | What it looks like | Trader psychology |
|----------|--------------------|-------------------|
| **Fast** | sudden 200–300 point move | the losing side's SL is hit → **mindset flips**, they exit |
| **Slow** | up/down, small candles, grind | mindset does **not** flip → the losing side **averages** |

### Fast-momentum playbook
- Fast momentum eliminates the opposite side — whoever sold into it has SL hit and exits; the buyer sees profit. ⇒ the fuel that was driving the move is **spent**: *"जिसका SL हिट होना था SL हिट हो गया, जिसका माइंडसेट चेंज करना था मार्केट माइंडसेट चेंज कर चुका है, अब कहीं से भी घूम सकता है."*
- Therefore: **book by default.** If you want to risk more, look for a **round level 30–40–50 points away** (1000s and 500s) and wait only up to that level — *"उससे ज्यादा आपको रिस्क नहीं लेना चाहिए."* If you do hold, use the split: **book ~75%, keep ~25% running.**

### Slow-momentum playbook (the session's big-money insight)
- In slow momentum psychology doesn't flip, so the losing side **averages** — and *"जो ट्रेडर अवरेज करता है वो सबसे ज्यादा पैसा देता है मार्केट को, और वही पैसा आपको मिलने वाला है."* New sellers also step in at the visible level. **Their stop-losses are the target.**
- **If slow momentum AND no round/important level is nearby** → the market must keep going to reach where the averagers finally cut ⇒ **hold for a big target**. His worked example: buyers averaging above a level will only cut **below** that level, so *"जब तक उस लेवल का ब्रेकडाउन नहीं होगा … मार्केट को नीचे आना ही पड़ेगा."*
- **If slow momentum AND a round level is nearby** (e.g. 46,500 / 47,500 reached) → **book** (or 75/25, or leave): after such a level there is **no averaging, only over-trading** — one side exits, the other enters, and *"अचानक से मोमेंटम नहीं मिलेगा."* Around these levels *"मार्केट परेशान ही करेगा आपको"* — it chops you.

### Which levels actually matter for the averager
- The **averaging trader does not cut at 200 / 800 levels** — he watches **round psychology numbers: the 1000s and the 500s** (47,000 / 46,000 / 47,500 / 46,500). ⇒ **for a big target, aim at the round number**, because that is where the average-and-lose crowd finally exits and where *"बड़ा प्रॉफिट"* is available.
- Consequence: big targets are **rare, ~1 in 10** — *"10 में से एक बार"*. He refuses the temptation to over-size on the hope: *"एक बार के चक्कर में हम नौ बार बार-बार लॉस … नहीं कर सकते."*

### Entry: the planned scale-in (and the line you must not cross)
- On entry he allows a **deliberate scale-in**: if you would take 10 lots, start with **~5**, add **~3** on the small dip, add **~2** on a little more, and the slightly-wrong entry point **self-corrects** — then hold the **same target** you set at the start. Because positions almost never go straight to profit — *"कभी लॉस नजर आएगा, कभी प्रॉफिट नजर आएगा"* — the scale-in buys a bigger target.
- **Hard condition:** this is only for someone **already trading multiple lots**. Doing it for the first time breaks psychology.
- The line: *"एवरेजिंग करेंगे हम एंट्री के अंदर; और जब लॉस नजर आ रहा होगा वहाँ पे एवरेजिंग कभी भी नहीं करना."* Planned entry scale-in is legitimate; **averaging a showing loss is the market's way of taking the most money from traders** — never do it.
- Implementation advice for beginners: don't abandon the method after one bad try — **keep doing what you already do, and only place a 25% slice into this structure**, verify, and then automate the habit.

### Expiry note
- He attaches the caveat that **expiry days are sideways by nature**, so the same reading still applies but must be handled knowing sideways is built in.

### Keep permanently
1. **Big profit is relative to your own norm:** normal capture = baseline; holding for the extra points is the big-profit day. Rupee amounts (1L / 2L / 5L) are not a definition.
2. **The smallest loss is the one you pre-decided — exactly.** Extra points given = big loss (never); cutting *early* = also a loss (the target was still owed to you).
3. **Exit first, entry second.** Exit quality is what converts a normal day into a big day.
4. **Classify momentum before you exit:** fast momentum spends the opposing side's fuel (book; wait only to the next round, max); slow momentum does not flip psychology — the opposing side **averages**, and their SLs are your target.
5. **Slow + no round level nearby = hold for a big target** — the market *has* to travel to where the averagers finally cut (breakdown of their level).
6. **Slow + round level nearby = book / go 75-25 / leave.** Around these levels there is no averaging, only over-trading — the market will chop you.
7. **Aim big targets at the 1000s and 500s, not the 200s/800s** — the average-and-lose crowd only cuts at the round psychology numbers.
8. **Big targets are ~1 in 10.** Don't over-size for one; you cannot pay for nine losses with one hit.
9. **Entry scale-in is legitimate — averaging a showing loss is not.** Start ~50% of the intended lots, add on the small dip; only for existing multiple-lot traders; keep the original target.
10. **Never average when the loss is showing.** Averaging at entry is structure; averaging a live loss is how the market takes the most from traders.
