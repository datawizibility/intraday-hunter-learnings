### Core idea
- A pure **loss-avoidance / mistake-audit session**: no chart, no setup, no level. He opens on the familiar complaint that a trader can do everything right and still lose — *"हम चार्ट को भी सीख रहे हैं हम कुछ इंडिकेटर पर लगा रखिए मैंने एक सिस्टम भी बना रखा है फिर भी हम एलओसी होता है"* — he learns charts, puts indicators on, has even built a system, and still keeps losing.
- Two answers are promised. **(1)** He names why losses survive good effort (over-trading, the wrong kind of post-mortem, an unowned system, a good read entered badly). **(2)** He prescribes a **research book** — run every change as a *virtual* experiment and let it prove itself before it touches the live account.
- The headline expectation is modest and stated plainly: never 100% — *"आप 100 पर्सेंट एक्यूरेसी लेना आपके लिए थोड़ा सा मुश्किल होगा"* — but a good accuracy is buildable. The claim is *improvement*, not perfection.
- The frame throughout is **time-spent is not the answer**: *"टाइम भी हो गया मार्केट के अंदर फिर भी मैं कोशिश कर रहा हूं"* — elapsed years are not the fix, so the fix has to be a change of *behaviour* (what you record, what you test, what you keep off the live account).

### Why the losses keep coming — over-trading and the post-mortem trap
- **Loss is a function of trade count, not of a single bad trade.** *"लॉस होता है 10 दिन के अंदर अब इस दिन के अंदर आप कितनी ट्रीट करते हो"* — loss is made inside a stretch of days, and how many trades you take in that stretch is yours to decide: *"कोई पांच करता है कोई 10 करता है"* (his own per-trader count, not a rule).
- ⚠️ The recurring garbled numeral-phrase *"10 की द स्टेट"* / *"10 की स्टेट"* / *"10 की डेसपरेट"* / *"10 रेट"* / *"10 प्रेड"* / *"10 की डेट"* (six spoken spellings, one phrase) is reproduced as heard and **left unresolved** — a plain reading is *a fixed batch of trades of which a few lose*, but the digits and the noun do not survive ASR, so **no rule is built on it**. The one place the phrase meets a full sentence is *"आपकी 10 की द स्टेट में लो हो रहा है"*.
- **The cause named is over-trading, and the mechanism is a conversion.** *"हम ओवर ट्रेडिंग करते हैं और जो मारा प्रॉफिट होता है उसको लोस में कंवर्ट कर देते हैं"* — the profit that was made gets converted back into loss by the trades taken after it. The setup is not blamed; the extra trades are.
- **The post-mortem trap.** After a loss the trader goes back to the chart and re-reads the trade he *should* have taken — *"चार्ट में जाते हो और आप देखेंगे कि मुझे यहां पर कॉल ने लेना चाहिए था"* — but a loss cannot be analysed that way: *"रूस को इस प्रकार एनालाइज नहीं किया जा सकता"*. Marking *where* you should have entered is not a diagnosis; it only re-imports the regret into the next trade.
- **The unowned system.** If you trade because someone told you to, there is nothing to repair: *"आपके पास अपना एक सिस्टम होना चाहिए कि आप रीड क्यों करते हो किस प्रकार करते हो"* — and if you take trades on someone else's word, *"वह सिस्टम को पूरा चेंज करना पड़ेगा"* is the only honest answer.
- **Good accuracy, wrong entry.** His sharpest diagnostic: the stop is hit and *then* the trade goes your way — *"आपकी एक्यूरेसी तो अच्छी है लेकिन आप की एंट्री गलत हो गई है"*. Accuracy and entry quality are two different numbers, and a loss can belong entirely to the second.
- The same split is stated as a batch: *"उसमें हमें दो या तीन रेट में तो प्रॉफिट होता है लेकिन यह छह या सात फ्रेंड ऐसी होती है जिसमें हमारा लो हो जाता है"* — a working method still carries a losing majority; the job is to move that mix, not to pretend it away.

### The prescription — research it in a book, not in your account
- **Two kinds of people enter the market** — this is his opening taxonomy: *"मार्केट में दो तरह के लोग होते हैं"* — *"वह केवल पैसा कमाना चाहते हैं लेकिन कुछ लोग अजय को मार्केट को समझकर पैसा कमाना चाहते हैं"* (⚠️ *"अजय"* is ASR noise, not a name). To the first group his advice is blunt: *"आप अपना फंड मैनेजर करने के लिए दे सकते हो"*, because *"मार्केट को समझोगे नहीं तब तक आप भी पैसा नहीं बना पाओगे"*. The second group is the audience for the rest of the session.
- **The centrepiece: virtual trading only.** Any change you want to test is run as a *virtual* book, never inside the live system — *"आपको केवल और केवल आपको वर्चुअल ट्रेडिंग करना है"* — and the cost is the point: *"रिसर्च में केवल आपका टाइम लगने वाला है"*, and *"कोई पैसा लगेगा ना आपकी कोई ट्रेडिंग स्टाइल इफेक्ट होगा"*. Research spends time; a live experiment spends money **and** corrupts your live psychology.
- **Write it down, one trade at a time.** *"आपको वन बाय वन रिकॉर्ड करके देखना है"*, kept either in a book or in a sheet: *"यह आपको कहीं नोट बुक में या एक्स्सल के अकॉर्डिंग आपको देखना है"*. The record is what turns a feeling about your trading into a countable fact.
- **The reversal experiment.** If the recorded losing set stays losing, invert it as an experiment — *"आपको बिल्कुल उल्टा कर देना है कि बाइक करते वहां पर चल कर देना"* (⚠️ *"बाइक"* / *"चल"* render **buy / sell**, both garbled) — and it is research, not a strategy switch: *"आपको यह नहीं है कि आपको रियल में करना है"*, *"आपको जस्टिस को रिसर्च करना है"* (⚠️ *"जस्टिस"* = जस्ट). He gives the benchmark out loud: *"करते थे 100 पर्सेंट प्रॉफिट"*.
- **Wait for the stop before you study the trade.** The research posture he wants is passive-until-outcome: *"आपको वेट करना है कि मार्केट आप का स्टॉपलॉस कब खाएगा"* and, more fully, *"आप मार्केट में वेट करो जब मार्केट आपको स्टॉपलॉस खाने के लिए तब आप रेट बनाना शुरू कर दो"* — let the stop be *hit* on paper, then note how much momentum you would have captured. Patience is prescribed as a *method*, not as a virtue.
- **Watch how he takes a trade to learn what he saw, and keep the real system untouched.** Don't copy a mentor's entry; ask what he was reading — *"वह जब पार्ट को देख रहा था वह क्या सोच रहा था मैं क्या सोच रहा था"*. And the closing instruction holds the two books apart: *"अगर आप एक रिसर्च शुरू करोगे तो आपकी ट्रेडिंग भी इफेक्ट नहीं होगी"*, with the expected payoff *"आप दो चार महीनों में ऐसा इंप्लीमेंट करो कि आपका ट्रेडिंग रिजल्ट पूरा ही चेंज हो जाएगा"*.
- **Diagnose yourself.** The findings are supposed to be self-generated, not received: *"यह सभी बातें अपने आप से आपको पता होना चाहिए"*. And he warns against staring at the *instruction* instead of doing the audit — *"ऐसा नहीं होता कि इसके ग्लास के जाकर आप यहां पर कैंडल देखने लगा"* (⚠️ *"ग्लास"* is ASR noise) — because the loss is removed by the audit, *"यह वही चीज है लॉस को इलायची इस प्रकार"* (⚠️ *"इलायची"* renders **eliminate**).

### Accuracy is not entry — the option clock and the ₹1-lakh tank
- **Options add a per-minute cost that the chart does not show.** *"ऑप्शन में आपको इस टाइम बेसन रहता है"* (⚠️ *"टाइम बेसन"* = **time-based**, garbled) — the same move pays differently depending on *when* it arrives: *"अगर यह 1 मिनट के अंदर सॉफ्ट मुकर गया है"* versus a profit removed an hour later. For an option position the clock is a position input, exactly as the level is.
- **Fund the book properly before you trade it.** Instead of feeding small amounts at the ticket window — *"हजारों रुपए देते वह ₹5000 देते हो ₹10 दिल्ली देते ऑप्शन के अंदर"* (⚠️ the final fragment is garbled; plain reading a **₹10,000**-scale example) — he instructs: *"आपने फंड को क्लिक कीजिए आप 1 लाख रुपए को इकट्ठा कीजिए और फिर आप टमी ट्रेडिंग कीजिए"* (⚠️ *"क्लिक"* = collect, *"टमी"* = intraday, both garbled). The tank is collected first, the trading is done second — sizing is a **capital decision, not a premium-price decision**.
- **A cross-book check on options**: when trading options, also note what the futures trade would have done, so the option trade is judged against a direction read and not just a premium flicker — *"आभूषण में जाकर एक वर्चुअल ट्रेडिंग के अकॉर्डिंग अपना नोट बुक में एक्सेल में बना सकते हो"* (⚠️ *"आभूषण"* is the ASR's rendering of **option**).
- **The exit is not a candle colour.** He explicitly rejects the mechanical read that the record is meant to replace: *"रेड सैंडल एंट्री नहीं लेनी चाहिए ग्रीन कैंडल पर ही नहीं लेनी चाहिए"* (⚠️ *"सैंडल"* = कैंडल) — the slogan is cheap; the audit is what tells you whether it was ever true for *you*.

### Keep permanently
1. **Keep a written trade record, one trade at a time.** A decision you cannot see on the record cannot be corrected — the log is the instrument, not a formality.
2. **Test every change in a research book, never in the live account.** Research spends time; a live experiment spends money *and* disturbs the live psychology you need to read the market with.
3. **When a recorded batch of trades keeps losing, invert it on paper before you rewrite the method.** The counterfactual is free; the rewrite is not.
4. **Over-trading is the converter — it turns booked profit back into loss.** The losing trade is rarely the setup's fault; it is the extra trade taken after the profit.
5. **Separate accuracy from entry quality.** A good read with a bad entry is still a losing trade, and the entry is the part you can fix.
6. **Own your system in writing — why you read it and how you read it.** A method you cannot state is a method you cannot diagnose or change.
7. **Let the stop be hit on paper before you judge the trade.** Waiting for the outcome converts impatience into information.
8. **For an option position, time is a cost paid per minute.** The identical move pays differently at one minute and at one hour; price the clock, not just the level.
9. **Collect and separate the capital before you trade it, and size to the capital — not to how cheap the premium looks.** Small premiums invite oversized positions.
10. **Look at a mentor's trade to learn what he saw, not to copy what he did** — and never let the copy overwrite the system you already own.
