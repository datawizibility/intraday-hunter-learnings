### Core idea
- Almost every index was trading near an **all-time high (ATH)** at the time of this session. Near an ATH **both obvious actions trap you**: sell the first rejection and you get trapped (the market covers back up); buy the strength and it stays difficult (every pullback looks like a top).
- The session supplies the missing rule: **a top is made by SMOOTH selling, never by FAST selling** (mirror: a bottom by smooth buying). *"कभी भी मार्केट के अंदर जो टॉप होता है ना, वो फास्ट सेलिंग करके नहीं होगा."*
- Mechanism he gives: **fast momentum flips the crowd's mindset** — and the market never wants the mindset to flip at the point where it is building its top/bottom. It wants the crowd to keep believing *"market will go up"* while it quietly hands the market lower. Hence a top prints with **smooth, patient selling**, not a sharp break.
- Practical split: **fast selling near a high ⇒ not a top** (the market will cover it back up) — tradeable only for a **small** target. **Smooth selling near a high ⇒ the top's signature** — that is where the sell side and a **big** target are licensed.

### The top-formation rule — fast vs smooth
- *"अगर सेलिंग होकर मार्केट ने ऑल टाइम हाई के आसपास से गिरना शुरू कर दिया — वो टॉप नहीं लगेगा ... मार्केट फिर से उसको कवर अप करेगा, मार्केट फिर से बढ़ने वाला है."*
- Why: a **fast momentum changes the trader's mindset**. The market refuses to change the mindset exactly where it wants to make its top — it wants traders still thinking up while it drifts down. So **when a top forms you see smooth selling**, and *"जब ऐसा सेलिंग आ जाए तब आपको बाइंग करने से थोड़ा डरना है."*
- **But a rejection is not smooth selling.** When a rejection comes — *especially* while the market trades **above** the ATH — the rejection can be big or small, but: *"उसके ऊपर आपको सेलिंग में पैसा नहीं लगाना है; वहां आपको बाइंग के ऊपर ही ट्रेड आइडेंटिफिकेशन करना है."* On the worked chart the rejection **flushed the buyers** (their SLs were taken); the market then gave only a **small** rejection, covered up and continued — so *"यहां आपको बाइंग करने में कोई ज्यादा तकलीफ नहीं होगी"* and it paid.
- **Selling a fast move is not forbidden — it is only small.** In a fast momentum, if a level breaks down you may get an intraday opportunity, *"लेकिन उसका टारगेट बड़ा नहीं रखना है."* Conversely, if you already **hold** a trade and the market is moving with you, *"हमेशा टारगेट बड़ा रखो — वहां बड़ा रिवर्ड मार्केट दे सकता है."*
- Reinforced at the close: a **fast** sell (e.g. an abrupt gap-down that dumps 1,000 points) *"ज्यादा लंबा काम नहीं कर पाता"* — it cannot hold; only the patient, smooth decline persists.

### The ATH playbook — which pool is seated decides the side
- *"बायर-सेलर तो हमेशा मार्केट के अंदर रहते हैं ... लेकिन प्रोबेबिलिटी कि हमें कौन से सेलर/बायर को टारगेट करना है — वो पता लगानी पड़ती है."* Buyers and sellers always exist; the **work is choosing whose stop-losses are the target**.
- Worked sequence on the chart:
  1. A **rejection above the ATH** flushed the buyers — their SLs were hit. Pool empty on that side ⇒ **look for buying**. Next day the market delivered **buy-side momentum**, giving money.
  2. Later, with the market back at a high, the buyers who had bought at support were left **sitting in loss** ⇒ the pool is now **those seated buyers** ⇒ **sell side, targeting the buyer**. He wanted that because *"अगर सेलर बैठा है और टॉप लगेगा, तो सेलर बैठा रह जाएगा (अच्छे पैसे वाला होगा) और बायर को मार्केट परेशान कर सकता है."*
- This is why he insisted on only two opens for that day: **flat or a gap-down** — the opens that let the market **take the seated buyers**. A gap-up would have ruined the setup.

### The open-type gate near an all-time high
- **Smooth-selling state (top forming) — expect flat → gap-down, and sell there.** *"जब ऑल टाइम हाई के आसपास स्मूथ सेलिंग आ चुका है, तो अगले दिन फ्लैट या अच्छा गैप डाउन ही देखने को मिलेगा."*
- **Gap-up ⇒ the setup is cancelled ⇒ no plan, wait.** He had already told his viewers he had **no plan** for a gap-up: *"अगर गैप ओपन होता है, तो मेरा यहां कोई प्लान नहीं ... हमें वेट करना होगा."* A gap-up would only spoil the market's own situation.
- **Flat open — the concrete branch (day-specific):** *"जैसे ही 48,800 के नीचे जाएगा, डेफिनेटली हमें सेलिंग करना है; अगर फ्लैट ओपन होता है और 49,000 को या हाई को क्रॉस करता है, तब हमें बाय करना है."*
- **Positive-close state (total positive close above the ATH) — buy side only.** *"जब तक मार्केट में पॉज़िटिव क्लोज़िंग हो रहा है, सेलिंग करने को इग्नोर करो; जितना भी दिमाग लगाना है, बाइंग में लगाओ."* Reason: above an ATH with a **clean positive close and no resistance taken**, *"सेलर के एसएल अवेलेबल नहीं हैं"* — the seller does not come; there is nothing to hunt on the upper side.
  - In that state a **good gap-up** or a **small gap-down** both license buying — *"अगर पॉज़िटिविटी देख रहे हैं तो गैप अप ज्यादा होना चाहिए; अगर नेगेटिविटी देख रहे हैं तो गैप डाउन ज्यादा."* A **big** gap-down, though, is the danger — *"अगर बड़ा गैप डाउन ओपन हो जाएगा तो जो बाइंग करके बैठे हैं उनको प्रॉब्लम हो जाएगी."*
- **General ATH open preference: big gaps are your friend, flat is the hard one.** *"जब मार्केट ऑल टाइम हाई के आसपास हो, बड़ा गैप डाउन और बड़ा गैप अप ही ज्यादा यूज़फुल होते हैं; फ्लैट के चांसेस बहुत कम रहते हैं."* Flat is awkward because your SL zone is unclear and the tape becomes a range; *"फ्लैट में हमारे लिए मुश्किल हो जाएगा."*

### Opportunity cost — the reason a setup is never 100%
- *"सेटअप जब बनाते हो, वो 100% काम नहीं करते ... क्योंकि आपको मार्केट के अंदर अपॉर्चुनिटी कॉस्ट देना पड़ता है."* You must pay the market an **opportunity cost** (a stop-loss) to locate a positional top's day. That is why the working band lands **≈80–90%**, not 100.
- And it is a **repeated process, not one attempt**: *"ऐसा नहीं कि एक ही दिन में मिल जाएगा — एक दिन स्मूथ सेलिंग मिले, फिर मार्केट परेशान करने चला जाए, आपको फिर ट्राई करना पड़ेगा."* Once the opportunity cost is exhausted and **smooth-selling keeps appearing**, keep working it — *"जिस दिन टॉप लगेगा और मोमेंटम आएगा, बड़ा पैसा बनाओगे."*
- **His own worked example:** the first sell below 48,800 (after the gap-down open and re-cross) **took the SL** — he names it *"अपॉर्चुनिटी कॉस्ट"*. On the **second smooth selling**, once the level was re-crossed the way he wanted, he re-entered and it paid.

### Once the top is made — the level is the flip-stamp
- *"अब एक बार मार्केट में टॉप लग चुका है — चाहे छोटे टाइम फ्रेम के लिए रहे — अब हमें बाइंग में रिस्क नहीं लेना है ... जब तक मार्केट 48,800 क्रॉस न करे."* While the top holds, work the **sell side only**; buying before the cross would be *"सिर्फ किस्मत"* (luck), not a setup.
- Only if the market **rotates back and crosses the level** do you refocus on the buy side. *"जब तक ये अपना टॉप बना रखा है, तब तक सेलिंग ही ढूंढेंगे; गैप अप मिलेगा — आसान; गैप डाउन मिलेगा — आसान; फ्लैट में मुश्किल."*

### Net summary (as he closed it)
- A top is **never** made by fast selling. Wait for **smooth** selling.
- A small rejection, or a clean positive close ⇒ **focus on buying**.
- While the market keeps **closing positive** above the high, there are **no seller stop-losses available** ⇒ do not sell; buy.
- Near an ATH, **gap-up / gap-down = easy; flat = hard**.
- **Smooth selling ⇒ big target. Fast selling ⇒ small target.**

### Keep permanently
1. **A top is made by SMOOTH selling, never by FAST selling** (mirror: a bottom by smooth buying). A sharp drop off a high is a cover-up candidate, not a top — the market will take it back up.
2. **Fast selling is only tradeable small.** Take a sell into an abrupt fall or a level break-down and keep a **small** target; a **big** target is licensed only when the market is already moving with your side.
3. **A rejection is not a top.** After a rejection near an all-time high, do **not** fund the sell side — the rejection has just flushed the buyers; look for **buy-side** trades.
4. **Ask whose stop-losses are seated before choosing a side.** Buyers flushed ⇒ buy. Buyers left sitting in loss after a rejection ⇒ **sell and target the buyers**. Both sides always exist; the skill is picking the pool.
5. **The next open is the gate.** After smooth selling near a high: **flat → gap-down = sell**; **gap-up = setup cancelled, no plan, wait** — never force the plan.
6. **A clean positive close above a high ⇒ buy side only.** With no resistance taken overhead, the **seller's stop-losses are not available**; a good gap-up *or* a small gap-down licenses buying, but a **big gap-down** endangers the seated buyers.
7. **Near a high, big gaps are the useful opens; flat is the hard one** — flat blurs your stop and turns the tape into a range.
8. **Setups are not 100% because of opportunity cost.** You pay the market a fee (an SL) to find a positional top's day; expect to try more than once — smooth-selling days that keep reappearing tell you to stay with it.
9. **Once a top is made, the level is the flip-stamp.** Work the **sell side only** until the level is crossed; buying before the cross is luck, not setup.
10. **While the market keeps closing positive, ignore selling** — put all of your reading on the buy side.
