### Core idea
- The session explains **how a mindset gets installed in a trader by the chart itself**, and what he did with the trading week
  that followed: he sold back-to-back, aggressively enough that his trade was built by **about 9:15** — *"चाहे मार्केट के अंदर गैप अप
  हो, गैप डाउन हो, कैसा भी कैंडल बन रहा हो, हम सेलिंग करने का ही प्लान बना रहे थे."*
- His opening law, stated as the whole method:
  ⭐ *"जब मार्केट में दूसरे लोग अपोजिट करने के लिए तैयार हों, तब आप मार्केट के साथ-साथ चलने के लिए तैयार हो जाओ; और जब दूसरे लोग
  मार्केट के साथ-साथ चलने के लिए तैयार हों, तब आप अपोजिट करना शुरू कर दो."* Stated as the **symmetric operating form** of the
  2022-04-23 stop-pool law, most of the time it pays.

### The brainwash mechanism (how the 10–15 day chart installs the belief)
- He walks a 10–15 day chart in which the market **covered every gap**: *"कितना भी बड़ा गैप ओपन हो जाए, मार्केट फिर से ऊपर चलने लगता
  था"* — while the global market was clearly negative. Result: *"पूरी तरह से ब्रेनवॉश हो चुका था."* Any stock fell → the trader
  bought → it rose → he booked. Repeat, for two weeks.
- The belief sentence that got installed: **"स्टॉक मार्केट और इंसान तो ऊपर ही जाता है."** What he could not know: *"ऊपर जाने से पहले
  वो नीचे कितने दिन तक रहता है."* So they bought *"भर-भर के"*, made good money, and their mindset was **completely** set:
  the market can never fall, and any fall will be covered.
- Then, when selling started, *"ट्रेड इस वाले चार्ट को नहीं देख रहा था — वह 10 से 15 दिन वाले चार्ट को देख रहा था"*: he expected
  every selling to be covered in 2–3 days, so he accumulated and averaged into a falling market.
- ⭐ **The causal claim that makes this an operator story, not a psychology story:** the market knew he would do exactly that —
  *"मार्केट को पता होता है कि वो ऐसा करने वाला है … क्योंकि मार्केट में चार्ट इसीलिए बनाया था, ताकि वो ऐसा कर पाए."* The
  covering chart was **built to install the belief** that would later be spent. A thousand points down, two thousand down —
  *"कहां तक वो अपनी पोज़िशन रोक पाएगा"* — and at the exact point the trader gives up and concedes *"अब तो मार्केट गिरने वाला
  is"*, *"तब मार्केट ऊपर चलना शुरू कर देता है."*
- This is why he planned **only** selling: *"मुझे ऐसा लगता था कि काफी लोग यहां पर इस प्रकार के चार्ट को देखकर बाय करने का प्लान
  बनाएंगे, तो मैं यहां पर कंटीन्यू सेलिंग करूंगा जब तक मार्केट में सेलिंग नज़र आ रही है"* — and therefore they did not even
  *discuss* a buy plan, whatever the opening was, *"क्योंकि तब तक सेलिंग में पैसा बन रहा था."*

### The comfort rule (the load-bearing new idea)
- ⭐ *"जहां पर आपको कंफर्ट लगने लगे कि मार्केट ऐसा अब करेगा, तो वहां पर मार्केट काम कर ही नहीं सकता — क्योंकि वहां पर सभी लोगों को
  कंफर्ट आ जाता है."* Where the crowd becomes comfortable, the market cannot pay — *"जहां पर कंफर्ट आने लग जाए, तो वहां पर काम
  बनना थोड़ा सा मुश्किल हो जाता है"* — unless the market has a bigger plan that requires it.
- Applied immediately: *"सेलिंग का कोई भरोसा नहीं होता कि रिप्लेसमेंट होगा या नहीं होगा"* — **there is no guarantee of a
  retracement**; and even if it comes, *"आपको बैठने नहीं देगा"* — it will not let you sit in the comfortable version of the
  trade (sell the retracement, ride the next leg). Everyone was waiting for exactly that.
- The same logic, said about his own entries: a **sharp / vertical** move is hard for the market to cover back — *"कोई भी शार्प
  सेलिंग या शार्प [मूव] आता है, उसमें मार्केट को वापस कवर करना थोड़ा सा मुश्किल हो जाता है"* — which is why he was comfortable
  being aggressive in that window.

### Aggression scales with how public the move is
- ⭐ *"मार्केट में एग्रेशन कब कम करना है? जब मार्केट में सभी को नज़र आने लगे कि अब मार्केट में सेलिंग हो सकती है — फिर आप अपना
  धीरे-धीरे जो [साइज़] था वह कम करते जाइए"*, because at that point up-down momentum becomes likely. *"जैसे-जैसे मार्केट गिरता गया,
  हमारा एग्रेशन कम होता गया; शुरुआत में हम थोड़ा सा फास्टली पोज़िशन बनाते गए."*
- Entry aggression is therefore **inversely related to how visible the trade has become** — the same variable as the comfort rule.

### The trap has a second round — the rebuilders
- Sequence on the chart: continuous selling into a resistance where many were waiting to sell — *"लेकिन मार्केट ने कब तक सेलिंग
  किया? जब तक हमारा मंथली एक्सपायरी कंप्लीट नहीं हो गया"* — and after the monthly expiry, the Friday momentum came.
- Buyers waiting for the high-cross bought. **Next day opened on a gap-down**, and everyone sitting long-and-waiting-for-a-long-
  retracement *"उनको मार्केट ने कंप्लीटली निकाल दिया"*; the market also built a level and broke it down — the breakdown that made
  the greedy buyer *"वह भी डर जाता है, वह भी अपनी पोज़िशन काट लेता है."*
- Then the second round: those who had failed on the buy side did not have money to show — so *"उनको ऐसी आदत होती है कि दोबारा से
  अपनी पोज़िशन बनाने की कोशिश करें — मार्केट ने ब्रेकडाउन दिया है, तो क्यों न मैं यहां सेलिंग करने का प्लान बनाऊं."* And the very
  next day: a good gap-up — the side that has just been rebuilt gets punished.
- His plan on that gap-up, as spoken: the **writers' distance** becomes too large and the sellers are already trapped, so that
  side cannot be worked — *"यहां पर हम काम नहीं कर पाएंगे; हमें यहां पर सेलिंग के लिए नहीं, बाय के बारे में सोचना पड़ेगा"* — and he
  waited for the **vertical momentum to actually appear** before buying. *"सोचने से कुछ नहीं होता, मार्केट में नज़र आना चाहिए."*
- The vertical-momentum rationale: while the market ran vertically, buyers were afraid to buy at each level, *"लेकिन रिट्रेसमेंट के
  बाद ऊपर जाएगा तो यहां पर कोई भी बाय कर सकता है"*; he held the position, and exited when the market started coming down.
- Then small breakouts that failed repeatedly gave the sellers courage (*"चलो, रिजेक्शन दिखा रहा है, मैं यहां सेलिंग कर लेता हूं"*),
  and *"अगले दिन मार्केट ब्रेकआउट देता है"* — the failed prints were building the next pool of sellers. The plan after that:
  do not target the buyers on that side; keep buying on a flat open, and buy even on a gap-down open.
- Broadly stated: read what the **overall** market is doing and what others are thinking. If they want to move *with* the market
  (gap-down ⇒ plan per the gap) you may plan a little opposite; **when they want the opposite** — waiting for a retracement of a
  selling market — *"वहां पर हम रिट्रेसमेंट के लिए वेट नहीं करेंगे, हम वापस सेलिंग के लिए ट्राई करेंगे"*; and while they think an
  up-move must come down, *"हम मार्केट में बाय करते जाएंगे."*

### Cut when wrong — averaging is the mistake
- *"हर ट्रेड कहीं न कहीं गलती कुछ न कुछ कर देता है, लेकिन गलत होने के बाद भी गलत होते रहना — वो बात गलत होती है."* When the loss
  appears, cut; *"वहां पर अंग्रेजी के बारे में नहीं सोचना चाहिए"*; never argue *"मार्केट इतना गिर चुका है, अब रिकवरी होनी
  चाहिए / इतना बढ़ चुका है, अब गिरना चाहिए"* — *"मार्केट का अता-पता नहीं होता कि कहां से कहां तक चला जाए."*

### Why learn at all
- *"बिना सीखे मार्केट में एक-दो दिन पैसा बना लोगे, दो-तीन दिन, एक महीना, दो महीना — लेकिन ओवरऑल मार्केट को आप समझ नहीं पाओगे
  कि मार्केट काम कैसे करता है. एक साल बाद वही रहेंगे."* You will keep drawing support/resistance without knowing **why** S/R
  works, why the market falls, or why it sometimes falls vertically. *"जब इन बातों के क्वेश्चन आपके माइंड में आने लगेंगे, तब आपको
  मार्केट भी समझ में आने लगेगा."*

### Evolution vs earlier
- 🔵 **NEW (load-bearing)** — ⭐ **the comfort rule**: *profit exists only where the crowd is not comfortable* (unless a larger
  plan requires the market to pay there). This is a genuinely new **filter on setups**, and it is the reason he refuses to plan
  the *comfortable* trade — "sell the retracement and ride the next leg." It also supplies the reason behind 2022-09-18's
  *the visible trade is never the trade*: the visible trade is the **comfortable** trade.
- 🔵 **NEW (load-bearing)** — ⭐ **the chart as the instrument that installs the belief**: 2022-09-18 named *print → belief →
  parked stops*; this session supplies the **longer build** (10–15 days of covered gaps) and the admission that *"मार्केट में चार्ट
  इसीलिए बनाया था, ताकि वो ऐसा कर पाए"* — the covering chart existed **to create the average-down crowd**. The trap has a
  construction phase, not just a trigger.
- 🔵 **NEW (load-bearing)** — ⭐ **aggression scales inversely with how public the move is**: start fast, *reduce* size as the

- 🔵 **NEW** — ⭐ **the second-round trap (the rebuilders)**: after the buy side is destroyed, the failures *rebuild the losing

### Keep permanently
1. **Go with the market when others prepare to go against it; go against it when others prepare to go with it.** That is the
   operating form of the stop-pool law — and it pays most of the time.
2. **Where the crowd is comfortable, the market does not pay.** Refuse the comfortable version of the trade (sell the
   retracement, ride the next leg), unless a bigger plan requires the market to pay there.
3. **The chart that installs the belief is built for that purpose.** A run of covered gaps manufactures the average-down crowd;
   the covering print is the preparation, not the accident.
4. **There is no guarantee of a retracement, and no comfort even if it comes.** *"सेलिंग का कोई भरोसा नहीं कि रिप्लेसमेंट होगा"* —
   never build the plan on the retracement you are *expecting*.
5. **Scale aggression inversely with visibility.** Enter fast while the move is not obvious; reduce size as the crowd starts
   seeing the same direction.
6. **Read the traders who just failed — they are the next pool.** After one side is destroyed the failures rebuild the losing
   side, and the next gap punishes them.
7. **A sharp, vertical move is hard for the market to cover.** That is the window where aggression is safe and where the
   retracement may never come.
8. **Do not work a side whose distance has already been made impossible** (writers too far, sellers already trapped) — look at
   the other side instead.
9. **Decide the side, then wait for the print.** *"सोचने से कुछ नहीं होता, मार्केट में नज़र आना चाहिए"* — plan earlier, execute only
   when the chart shows it.
10. **Cut when the loss appears; never average, and never argue from how far the market has already travelled.** *"मार्केट का
    अता-पता नहीं होता"* — and a trade that made an error is normal; staying wrong after the error is the actual mistake.
