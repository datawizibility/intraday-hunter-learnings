### Core idea
- Price action is not a collection of candle patterns. In this framework, it starts with one question: **where are other traders' stop-losses?** He says price action without stop-loss identification has no sense.
- A setup cannot compensate for uncontrolled behaviour. Before applying his setup, the trader must control **trade frequency, psychology, position size and risk–reward**.
- His setup is explicitly two-sided: **buy when sellers' stop-losses are available above; sell when buyers' stop-losses are available**. The trade is against the pool that can fuel the move, not against a prediction or borrowed opinion.
- The framework now classifies stop pools into **intraday, positional and sentiment-based**. Continuous one-way movement can be the market *creating* the stops it will hunt later.

### Five gates before the setup
1. **Discipline / trade count:** price action may show many apparent opportunities, but they should not all be taken. Decide the maximum number of trades in advance and reduce it as far as practical.
2. **Independent psychology:** if a friend or Telegram channel stating the opposite makes you reverse your position, your psychological readiness is too low for the setup.
3. **Position size for learning:** size must be small enough that profit and loss feel materially similar. If a loss hits psychology, the size is wrong. Do not increase size for profit; choose it so learning can continue.
4. **Risk–reward:** the reward must remain larger than the risk often enough to keep the process viable over time. He does not mandate one ratio; he mentions alternatives and asks the learner to maintain as much favourable reward as practical.
5. **Stop-loss identification:** until the trader can identify where stops are, his setup will not become usable or profitable.

### The setup's two-sided root
- **Buy-side condition:** he initiates a buy when sellers' stop-losses are available above price.
- **Sell-side condition:** he attempts a sell when buyers' stop-losses are available.
- He says he works on these two conditions regardless of the market situation. This is the first compact statement in the chronology of the setup as an executable two-sided rule.
- The target is **someone else's stop**, consistent with Parts 1–2; the trader's own stop remains a risk-control device, not the object being hunted.

### Three classes of stop-loss pool
- **Intraday:** stops belonging to traders positioned within the day.
- **Positional:** stops belonging to carried positions; this continues the scope expansion introduced on 2022-02-06.
- **Sentiment-based:** pools created around news or crowd narratives—examples spoken include the Budget, an RBI policy, or a market that has been continuously rising/falling.
- The transcript around support/resistance breakout/breakdown conditions is garbled, but the stated lesson is clear: these categories take repeated observation, including live-market explanation; they are not learned in one or two days.

### How the market creates the pool before hunting it
- If price keeps falling and no obvious stops appear available, his interpretation is that the move may be **preparing stop-losses for a later move**.
- Example narrated: roughly two days of continuous decline followed by roughly two days of gap/upside recovery. His conclusion is not that the first move "failed", but that positions/stops were created and then hunted.
- Traders who repeatedly short while the market rises keep adding seller stops; price can continue upward while consuming them. This develops Part 2's idea that a level crossing forms new positions.

### Discipline vs borrowed conviction
- The friend/Telegram example is a direct test: a buy should not become a sell merely because another person announces a sell.
- The desired psychology is not confidence theatre; it is **small enough size plus ownership of the original analysis**.
- Learning—not immediate P&L—is the objective at this stage. A setup cannot be evaluated cleanly when position size makes every fluctuation emotionally decisive.

### Risk–reward — principle, not a fixed ratio
- He contrasts taking a larger loss and a smaller profit with a sustainable favourable payoff; the ASR corrupts the sample amounts.
- He audibly mentions **1:1** and **1:2** as possible choices, then says the trader may use what suits the style while keeping reward as favourable as possible.
- Therefore this video does **not** establish a mandatory universal ratio. The permanent rule is to prevent repeated small winners from being overwhelmed by larger losers.

### Chart example — usable mechanism, unusable exact sequence
- He describes a recent chart with negative global sentiment, trapped buyers/sellers, and a trade initiated around an early green candle.
- His stated logic was high confidence that price could not keep moving to the upside because the required stop pool was not available / would cost too much to reach; therefore he expected reversal.
- The ASR alternates buy/sell words (`बैंक`, `सेलेक्ट`, `शिवलिंग`, `सिगरेट`) and does not preserve the exact entry direction or candle sequence reliably. **No trade outcome, entry or level is claimed here.**
- The transferable element is only the pre-trade question: *which stops are actually available, and how much effort/liquidity would be required to reach the other pool?*

### Price action vs patterns and indicators
- He distinguishes an indicator setup from price action and says he labels indicator-based material as such.
- His strong claim: learning candlestick patterns without learning where stops are does not amount to learning price action.
- Practical study—observing charts and the stop pools formed—is presented as more valuable than assuming a paid course can supply the understanding automatically.

### Keep permanently
1. **Limit trades before the session.** Price action may display many opportunities; taking all of them destroys discipline and makes even a valid setup hard to evaluate.
2. **Do not reverse a position on borrowed conviction.** If a friend or Telegram message can flip the trade, psychology and ownership are not ready.
3. **Size for learning, not profit.** If ordinary loss movement hits psychology, reduce position size until learning can continue without emotional compulsion.
4. **Maintain sustainable risk–reward.** No single ratio is mandated here; the rule is not to repeatedly risk more than the reward the process captures.
5. **Price action begins with stop-loss identification.** Candle patterns or indicators without knowing where other traders' stops sit are not this method.
6. **Two-sided setup:** seller stops available above support a buy-side hunt; buyer stops available support a sell-side hunt. Availability—not opinion—is the trigger context.
7. **Classify the pool:** intraday, positional or sentiment-based. News/narrative is relevant because it creates crowd positioning and stops, not because it predicts direction by itself.
8. **A trend can manufacture its own future fuel.** When no stops look available, ask whether continuous movement is building the positions that a later reversal will hunt.
9. **Never convert garbled archive narration into a level or outcome.** Preserve the mechanism; discard unrecoverable entry direction, numbers and candle sequence.
