### Core idea
- The question he answers is the one every losing trader asks: *"I have already given the market six months / one year / two years and I still cannot take profit out of it — how much longer?"* His answer is a **number and a decomposition**, not encouragement.
- ⭐ His headline figure: **1 to 1.5 years minimum**, *"आराम से"* (comfortably) — with today's resources available and **continuous focus** on the market. Realistically he puts the ceiling at **1.5–2 years**.
- ⭐ The number is not one block — he splits it into three phases: understanding the *tools and segments*, then *maturing your judgement of information*, then *building your own setup*. The third phase is where setups die and get rebuilt, and it is the phase nobody budgets for.
- ⭐ The sharpest claim in the session is causal, not motivational: **the single biggest variable in how long it takes is not talent or setup — it is whether you have a backup plan (income outside the market).** No backup ⇒ you trade for money ⇒ you cannot enter late, cut fast, or hold a stated target ⇒ time stretches. Better backup ⇒ time collapses.
- ⭐ Second practical claim: **sitting in front of the chart is not study.** Two or three passive hours prove nothing; one focused hour spent asking *why my setup did not work today, and when does it work* moves the process. He admits this about his own desk habit.

### The timeline he actually gives — phase by phase
| Phase | His duration | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Tooling | **2–4 months** | Which segment (cash / futures / options / stocks), how it is traded, how much quantity, what the reward is |
| 2. Judgement / maturity | **~4–5 months** (ASR says "मिनट" — see fidelity) | What to work on — indicators vs projections; whether to take a piece of a move; who is telling you right and who wrong |
| 3. Your own analysis & setup | **5–6 months, even a year** | Building your own setup, then discovering it breaks when the market condition changes |
| **Total** | **1–1.5 years** (his floor), **1.5–2 years** realistic | |

- He warns that **the order matters**: if you do any of these in reverse, the process gets longer, not shorter.
- ⭐ The mechanism that eats the months: **a setup is market-condition-dependent.** His example — you build a short setup in a market that is *continuously falling*, it prints money, you conclude you have found it and start ignoring everything else; a year later the market turns sideways (up-and-down) and the same setup simply does not work. You could not have known earlier, because the discovering condition (a different regime) had not arrived yet. Then the setup has to be changed, and that costs more months.
- Note the shape of the claim: the loss of time is caused by **regime change plus attachment to a working setup**, not by a lack of knowledge. Two to four months of learning ends with a setup that works — it is the *assumption that it will keep working* that buys the extra year.

### ⭐ Psychology — why the setup is not the bottleneck
- His stated reason a tested setup still fails in a trader's hands: **"there is no backup plan."** Setup gives the signal; the trader cannot follow it.
- What the missing backup concretely breaks (his list): you cannot **enter quickly**, cannot **cut quickly**, cannot **set the target properly** — and then you start **deciding your profit by looking at other people's profits**.
- ⭐ The inverse is stated as a promise too: *"the one I am telling you takes two years — with a good backup plan you can start working in 1–1.5 years"*, and at that point even **small setups start paying**.
- This makes **psychology a capital-and-income question before it is a mindset question** — the session's most useful reframe of the corpus's earlier "psychology is the hardest part".
- Corollary he pushes hard: **having your own income outside the market is not a distraction from learning; it is the thing that buys the patience to learn.**

### Do not quit the job/business the day profit starts
- ⭐ Explicit instruction: even if profit starts coming, **do not drop the other work** — give the setup time inside the market so it is **properly tested**, and maintain a **proper record** first; only then take the decision.
- His reason: going straight from "first profits" to "I quit the job / I leave the business" is itself a decision that will trouble you later (the setup has not been seen across conditions — see the regime problem above).
- This is the corpus's first **full-time / part-time decision rule**, and it is gated on *evidence* (documented record over time), not on confidence.

### Don't buy someone else's setup and expect it to work
- He names the belief — *"let me take someone's course, or someone's setup, and I'll benefit"* — and rejects it: it cannot work as handed over, because **you have to do it according to your own reading**.
- The mechanism he gives is *trust*, and it is not sentimental: **you do not trust a setup you did not work out yourself**, so you will not hold it, will not enter on it fast, and will not wait. As soon as you have *worked on that particular setup yourself*, trust appears — and then one or two minutes are enough to take the trade, and the market pays.
- Practical conclusion: **be self-motivated, not a follower**, and expect to adapt any external setup to your own settings before it can be traded.
- This is the same boundary as an *exam*: he frames learning scoping/selection as process, matching the earlier "answer the question you know first".

### Time spent ≠ time learned
- He rejects the arithmetic of hours outright: *giving the market two hours, three hours, four hours makes no difference by itself.*
- ⭐ His honest admission: he himself sits watching the chart and is **doing nothing** — the market is not moving, and attention drifts. That is why the hours prove nothing.
- His prescription: use even **one hour** on the *analysis of your own setup* — *why did my setup not work today, and at what time does it work?* — observe, take notes.
- ⭐ Related small-print claim (ASR-garbled, see fidelity): changing a parameter of the setup a little — the level moving **up or down** — can change the strategy materially. So the answer is not always a new setup; sometimes it is a small adjustment.
- He also flags the opposite error: **over-thinking / excessive conviction** does not make you profitable — sometimes a **small shift** is what makes it work. (Spoken loosely; recorded as his direction, not as a method.)

### Keep permanently
1. **Budget the time in phases, not as one hope**: tooling (segment, quantity, reward) → maturing judgement (what to work on, whom to believe) → building your own setup. Each phase has its own cost, and the third one is the expensive one.
2. **Your income outside the market is a trading variable, not a distraction.** No backup plan ⇒ you trade for money ⇒ you cannot enter late, cut fast, or hold your stated target. A good backup plan is what actually shortens the time.
3. **Never trade to make money from the market** — his own reason the time stretches.
4. **A setup is valid for a market condition, not forever.** Build it in one regime, and expect it to fail in the next; budget the rework instead of believing the setup has betrayed you.
5. **Do not quit your job or business on first profits.** Test the setup properly, keep a proper record, decide from the record — not from confidence.
6. **A setup you did not work out yourself cannot be traded by you.** Trust is what makes you enter on time and hold to target, and trust is not transferable — adapt anything you take from someone else to your own reading first.
7. **Screen time is not study time.** 2–3 hours of passive chart-watching prove nothing; one hour of *"why did my setup fail today, and when does it work"* is the learning.
8. **When a working setup stops working, try the small adjustment before the new setup** — a small change of level/parameter can change the outcome materially.
9. **Do not reverse the order** — the phases stack; doing them out of order lengthens the process.
10. **Don't make your profitability dependent on someone else's proof.** Watching other people's profits is exactly what a missing backup plan pushes a trader into.
