He explained the exact risk: when a lagging index (BN) has sellers accumulated and a **round number is just above**, the market can break out to hit those stop-losses → the loss gets bigger. Both conditions (BN going positive; a round number above) would enlarge the loss.
- He then **cut the trade** once the loss limit was done and the market was rising again — "we will not look at more loss than this."

### Keep permanently
1. **When a positive momentum is followed by a rejection, the retail sellers are NOT sitting** — most couldn't participate, so there is no seller pool to hunt; the day is a *continuation* read, and continuation without real inventory is fragile.
2. **Trade handling > trade creation.** "Anyone can make the trade; how you handle it when the loss or profit comes is what matters."
3. **Once a loss is running, read the loss, not the chart.** Do not match the chart to the loss ("now it will fall"); the position's loss is the operative information — sit only *inside* the limit.
4. **A lagging index that turns positive first, with a round number just above, is the classic loss-enlarger.** If the quantity-heavy index (BN) goes positive while the others stay negative, a round-number breakout can hit the stops and blow the loss; cut — don't wait for the laggards to follow.
5. **Option buying vs option selling is a segment fit, not a value judgement.** Buying captures momentum (premium expands fast); selling captures time value (pays in sideways); which one a trader uses depends on the day and on their own psychology — the differentiator is trade management, not the instrument.
