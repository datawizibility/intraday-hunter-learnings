### Keep permanently
1. The **speed of rejection** matters: after a gap-up, a fast rejection may be a trap and can justify a quick recovery trade; a slow rejection through small candles needs patience because it can keep grinding down.
2. A valid narrative does not override the loss limit. Even if the thesis feels “100% right,” the trade must be cut when the predefined loss boundary is reached.
3. Do not apply creativity to a losing trade: no averaging, quantity changes, new support/resistance stories, or endless scenario-building to avoid the exit.
4. The more work you do to defend one losing position, the more emotionally attached you become and the harder it becomes to leave.
5. Spend analytical effort on **profitable trades**—how to manage and enlarge a winner. Handle losing trades mechanically according to the limit.
6. For a fast-rejection recovery setup, the market should recover promptly. **No recovery plus a closing-price breakdown** invalidates the setup; do not keep waiting for the original story.
7. Small losses become large and create a recurring recovery-loss cycle when traders average or postpone the cut.

### Psychology / discipline
- He acknowledged the thesis and the actual price action were diverging: the expected immediate recovery never came.
- He resisted common loss-side interventions—reducing or adding quantity, averaging, or finding new levels to justify staying.
- He cut once the loss limit and structural invalidation were reached, despite still being able to explain why the original buy thesis had looked reasonable.
- Core psychology rule stated repeatedly: **use your mind in profit; do not sit and overthink in loss**.
