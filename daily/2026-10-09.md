### Keep permanently
1. **A gap-up into a seller-heavy chart flushes the sellers — so the continuation-down trade has no fuel.** When the pre-plan's *buy* branch (flat/gap-down ⇒ target the seated sellers) is denied by a gap-up, the sellers run on the positive print; a put then has **no stop pool left to hunt**. The gap-up ⇒ SELL branch exists, but it is the **weak branch** and must be sized/managed as such.
2. **"A small retracement we can bear; more than a normal retracement = the thesis is dead."** The kill-signal for a follow-down trade is a **positive momentum larger than a retracement**, not a price level — the moment the market covers the point where the fall started, direction has changed.
3. **Don't apply more brain to a wrong trade.** Once the entry is invalidated, the work is done: cut inside the limit. Looking for a reason to stay (*"it will stop at the round number"*) *is* the loss.
4. **Being handed a worse entry is acceptable if the miss-risk is real — but only with a pre-stated loss limit.** IH entered slightly low deliberately (*"there is also the risk that if we don't trade here, we miss it"*) and explicitly accepted that a lower entry is covered **inside the limit**. (Reinforces Bible §8 / §9.)
5. **Friday after a long negative run is reversal-prone** — the pre itself flagged that the gap-up ⇒ SELL branch is the weaker one, and the session played exactly that out. Treat Friday/expiry-adjacent gap-ups into a stretched down-trend as **no-trade-or-small** (Bible §4 gap-size nuance, §7; P91).
6. **The "no entry = miss" and "wait for a better entry" pulls are symmetric costs.** IH names both: entry lower costs money, waiting risks missing. The resolution is **not** to avoid the trade but to pre-decide the **limit**, so either outcome is bounded.
