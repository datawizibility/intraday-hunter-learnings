### Keep permanently
1. **The round-number support test decides the trade, and its breakdown is the trade's own kill-switch.** IH read "most people will not buy at this support ⇒ the breakdown should not happen ⇒ buy where others won't" — a sound **anti-crowd entry** — but the entry is only valid **while the round number holds**. *"If it goes below the round number we will cut the trade."* The condition is pre-stated, not improvised.
2. **Cut on breadth, not on your own index.** *"Whether or not Bank Nifty breaks its level, the loss limit should not be crossed."* Bank Nifty was holding; Sensex and Nifty were bleeding ⇒ *"we cannot hold our trade further."* A laggard that holds is **not** evidence that the thesis is live.
3. **Before entry, think as much as you want; after entry, more thinking is worthless.** *"Once the position starts running a loss, do not think 'I should have sold.'"* — the corridor of decisions closes at entry; after that the only decisions are *hold* or *cut to the limit*.
4. **Understanding psychology > reacting to momentum.** *"When we build a trade, it is not just that selling momentum is coming so we buy — overall we understand the psychology, and if we are going wrong according to the psychology, we cut the trade."* Momentum is the cue; the psychology (who is trapped, where their stops are) is the decision.
5. **"This rejection is making sellers add" is a hypothesis that must be paid for in price.** His alternative read — *"maybe the market did something to add sellers, and after that, as sellers come, the market covers slowly and goes up"* — is exactly the sort of story that keeps a losing trade alive. He named the story, then still cut when the price did not confirm it.
6. **Anti-crowd entries are high-value, and rare — insist on the "others aren't buying" test.** *"Where others buy, there is a problem; where the chances of others buying are low, you can build an entry and make profit."* This is the week's most explicit statement of the entry-edge criterion.

- **Differed (the decisive difference — agent's advantage *and* the trader's error):** the reconstruction's risk note implied the *gap-up-into-a-crowded-long* tape is the dangerous one, i.e. it would have been **more cautious**; IH read the same rejection as an *anti-crowd entry* (people won't buy at the round support) and **bought at full week's size**. This time the agent's caution was right and the anti-crowd read was early. The rule that survives is IH's *own*: the anti-crowd entry is only good **while the round number holds** — and it didn't (Nifty/Sensex broke, BN closed below 55,000).
- **Underweighted (agent):** the **breadth exit rule**. IH cut because Sensex/Nifty led the fall "in net", before BN's level broke. An agent reconstruction focused on levels would have waited for the level — IH's rule is *loss limit across the basket*, which fired earlier and cheaper.
- **What the chart actually did (index OHLC, verified):** Bank Nifty 55,350 / 55,416 / 54,709 / 54,918 — opened a slight gap-up, **tagged 55,416 just under the 55,500 resistance**, then fell to 54,709 (one point off the 54,700 support) and closed **below the 55,000 support**. - **Learnings:** the pre-plan's branch logic is decided at the open, but the **entry test must be re-run in the first 5–10 minutes** — the market can print a non-negative open and *then* go hard negative, which is precisely the state in which the buyers become the inventory. The 12 Oct loss is the mirror image of 09 Oct: on 09 Oct a gap-up **flushed the sellers** (buy failed); on 12 Oct a gap-up **left the buyers exposed and then flushed them** (buy failed too). The durable content is not "gap-up ⇒ buy" or "gap-up ⇒ sell", it is **"whichever side is crowded after the open is the side the market will hunt — and the round number tells you where."**
- My takeaway: use IH's own invalidator chain — *(1)* don't be on the crowded side after the first 10 minutes, *(2)* the round number is the entry's life-line, *(3)* breadth/loss-limit beats level-watching for the exit. And note the cost of getting the *entry* right and the *exit rule* wrong is what turns a good idea into a small loss instead of a bigger one.
