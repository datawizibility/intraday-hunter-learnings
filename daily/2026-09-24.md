His first statement was that the already-built chart **cannot be followed** in this case: everybody who bought or sold into it is already trapped, or already in deep profit — so the old read is void and the **new chart** has to be followed. - Why a **PUT** and not a call, once the tape opened: he saw a small rejection with only a little buying, and positive momentum should *not* appear here. Around the open there is no stop-loss pool available to hunt, but once a **breakdown** prints, everyone who "invested" (bought) gets targeted — that is his profit. Over a big gap-down he weighs two possibilities: (a) profit-booking / gap-cover rally, which he did not see, and (b) continuation, because the sellers who wanted to leave had already left during the previous 2–3 days' retracement and *nobody* books profit inside a big gap-down. He also noted a breakdown had **not** happened yet, so one was still available.
- Session sequence: entries showed moderate profit almost immediately, then losses in Sensex/Nifty while Bank Nifty carried the trade. He waited because momentum (Bank Nifty) was with him; Bank Nifty had been in selling from the start, whereas Sensex and Nifty did their heavy selling only at the end.
- On support: he noted the market was taking support, and in a negative market **nobody looks at support** — very few people buy there — but the market can sometimes *use* that support to cover more. Since the target was already achieved he booked rather than testing that branch.
- On exit timing he was emphatic: for an option buyer, one adverse candle can wipe out **₹2.5 lakh** of profit — the exit is 2–3 candles-worth of decision, not a price level. He took the target and left, saying greed after a booked target is pure added risk.

### Trade outcome (IH)
- **Win — target booked** on the put-side basket (Bank Nifty leg carried most of the P&L; Nifty 1,430 qty; Sensex leg in expiry). No rupee profit figure was stated; he booked on target with momentum in hand.

### Keep permanently
1. **A very large gap-down voids the previous chart.** Both sides' overnight positions are already trapped or already in profit, so yesterday's inventory map is worthless — wait at the open and read the *new* chart before trading.
2. **One day's opening must never set greed or strategy.** The market can open completely against your read; what pays is a pre-stated risk and following discipline, not the gap.
3. **In a large gap-down inside an existing negative trend, do not wait for a profit-booking recovery.** Whoever wanted to exit already exited during the earlier retracement, and nobody books profit inside a gap-down — so the sell side may be followed, but only after the first momentum and a printed breakdown.
4. **For an option buyer, exit *timing* is the trade.** One adverse candle can give back ₹2.5 lakh; when momentum has paid, take the exit instead of holding for a better level.
5. **Support-take in a negative market is mostly ignored** (few participants buy it) — but the market can sometimes use that support to cover, so once a good target has been achieved, book rather than leaning on the level holding.
6. **Wait for the first momentum after a huge gap** — entering blind at the open in a gap-down means paying the worst premium with no analysed structure.
