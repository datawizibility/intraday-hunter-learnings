### Keep permanently
1. **"No direction" is a first-class market state, and it is bought, not traded as a trend.** *"Big momentum came, small momentum came — net there is no direction."* The response is not to guess the next leg: it is to **follow the running chart in the direction that has no inventory against it** (here: call side — there were no seller SLs above, and buyers had already booked).
2. **The operator's motive is SL inventory — if there is none, there is no reason for a direction move.** *"If retail SLs are not available here, why would the operator turn the market?"* ⇒ When a momentum leg has been fully **booked**, the pool is empty; what remains are only *small traps to add new sellers*, which are **not** direction moves. Trade the direction only when the counter-side inventory exists.
3. **Gap-up ⇒ the seller's SL is unavailable ⇒ sell-side; flat/small gap-down ⇒ the buyer's SL is unavailable ⇒ buy-side.** This is the cleanest statement in the corpus of *how the open type tells you which side has no cover* — and it **replaced the 12 Oct phrasing** (which made the flat/gap-up open a buy). Both readings are really the same test: **find the empty side**.
4. **Small breakdowns are only "good" in the right context.** *"Sometimes a small breakdown is a good thing — here it is not."* In a held positive chart a small breakdown can **change the direction**; in a crowded-negative chart the flush is the entry. Never carry the "small breakdown = good" slogan without its context.
5. **One index can carry a three-index basket — and the weak legs only need to not break.** IH's stated wish was *"Sensex and Nifty should not break their level"* — not that they should pay. *"We do not mind that Sensex and Nifty are negative… if the total turns negative, the problem becomes big."* ⇒ Judge the **basket's total**, and give the strongest index room to deliver the target.
6. **Option buying's payoff is momentum, not time.** *"Small sideways momentum gives no money… when the market makes such a candle, profit runs with you."* A range day still pays if one index releases a leg — but the day's expectation must be **"a normal target"**, not yesterday's big target.
7. **Do not re-write the trade afterwards with candles you did not trade.** *"In the trade you will only get as much profit as you normally take."* Back-fitting a bigger candle to a closed position is pure regret, and it degrades the rule that made the trade.
