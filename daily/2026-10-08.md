### Keep permanently
1. **Yesterday's SL-hit counter-side will not repeat today.** When the counter-side (here: sellers) was already **SL-hit by yesterday's recovery**, they will not sell again the next day even into a fresh big red candle — *"because yesterday the market recovered, they fear the same recovery."* That absence of the crowd is itself the continuation signal, and it is what makes the entry available.
2. **The entry test: "can others also sell/buy where I am selling/buying?"** Build the trade where the crowd will *not* participate. Where they sold yesterday, they will not sell today.
3. **The market must manufacture the buyer before it flushes him.** With no seller pool to hunt, the day's selling arrives as: **hold or small retracement → a few buyers are invited in → their stops are taken → the market continues down.** Expect the retracement, and size for it (it may cause a visible loss inside the limit).
4. **"Selling changes to buying only when the market covers its higher point"** — the *start* of the fall, not a resistance number, is the reversal gate. Until that is covered, small upside momentum is noise.
5. **Companions-only profit ⇒ book the normal target; don't wait for the quantity index.** Third-index lag (and even a turn) after two indices have delivered their momentum is the classic way a good P&L is given back. (Reinforces Bible §6 / §7 exit #24; same rule as 05 Oct.)
6. **Fear in a live position is downstream of the loss book, not the chart.** A run of losses or one oversized loss creates the fear that then destroys trade management — so keep the loss rule hard (*"we will not lose more than X"*) and never scale up into a losing patch.
7. **Direction decision and entry decision are separate steps.** Decide the direction from inventory/structure first; decide the *entry* from "where will others not trade". Both must agree before a ticket goes on.

The agent had the *side* right and the *exit* right but treated the day as a straightforward follow-down; in reality the profit engine was **the two companions** with **Bank Nifty visibly behind**, and the discipline that banked the day was **booking before the laggard turned** — not holding for the mapped continuation.
- My takeaway: after a failed-recovery day, sell the flat open — but enter only where the crowd isn't, expect to *lose first* while the retracement invites buyers, and treat the companions-only P&L as the exit clock.
