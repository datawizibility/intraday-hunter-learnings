### Keep permanently
1. **In a multi-day negative market, a flat open is the highest-probability seller-SL hunt.** When consecutive sessions have sold off, the crowd is short and seller stops sit just above the round numbers; a **flat / barely-positive open** means *nothing flushes at the bell* ⇒ the market can drive into those stops and pay the buyer. IH: *"flat opening was seen, so we could make the sellers our target… today there is no trap; today the market is making the seller its target."*
2. **Gap-up flushes the trade at the bell; flat lets you build the whole target.** *"Had it opened gap-up, most would have run away first; but in flat we got the chance to build the target nicely."* ⇒ prefer the flat branch for inventory-hunt trades.
3. **A no-retracement, continuous tape is the point-maximiser — and the exit is "when profit reduces," not a fixed price.** Stay while the direction holds; exit the moment a **rejection candle starts reducing the profit**; book the **normal target** if the leader index hesitates.
4. **Wait patiently through the loss; press the target only on a good trade.** *"When a loss is happening wait with patience; when a good trade comes you get the chance to enlarge."* **Never sit long in a losing trade** — that is where the damage compounds. (Mirrors 12 Oct: he **cut** the losing call; 13 Oct: he **stayed** in the winning call ⇒ "the market paid yesterday's too.")
5. **First vs second momentum in a negative market.** A trader tolerates the **first** momentum but cannot tolerate the **second** (sharp/abrupt) — sellers almost all run on the second leg. Read the *second* momentum as the payoff leg; don't chase it late.
6. **Round-number closes leave seller inventory.** A close *below* a round number leaves sellers sitting with stops above ⇒ the next flat/gap-up open is a target-generator (a levels-visibility edge).
7. **Don't over-greed a great momentum, and don't measure yourself against others' P&L** — discipline + accuracy is what makes a trader (repeats the 12 Oct monologue).
