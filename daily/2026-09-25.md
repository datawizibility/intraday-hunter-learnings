He acknowledged small rejections have to be tolerated — the market never moves straight in your favour and sometimes the irritation is bigger — and he explicitly guarded psychology: do not wreck your mindset with "this has gone into loss, now the market will only fall"; the market first creates stop-losses and gives the sellers a chance to re-enter, then slowly moves on. Rule he voiced: **as long as the loss limit is not exhausted, sit calmly** — but once it is exhausted, there is no second option.
- What actually decided the exit: the **market printed a breakout but did not continue it** ("ब्रेकआउट तो निकाला, लेकिन कंटिन्यू फॉलो नहीं किया"). He reasoned that if Sensex and Nifty had been the ones rejecting while Bank Nifty held up, he could have absorbed it; but a sudden rejection in *all three* means the market is going to stay **sideways**, and in a sideways market the loss will not be covered — and if it keeps selling instead, the loss only gets bigger. Both branches are bad for an option buyer, so he **booked the loss and exited** ("the boat has sunk, sitting in it is of no use").
- Recap in his own words: the gap-down + continuous selling one day earlier meant sellers should be there to target; the market did print a breakout — but it did not follow through, and Bank Nifty, having given the positive momentum first and the rejection after, was the main cause of the problem. ### Keep permanently
1. **A printed breakout is not a trend — wait for follow-through.** The market took out the level and then refused to continue. Without continuation, the "target the trapped sellers" thesis is invalid and the right response is to cut, not to hope.
2. **Bank Nifty must lead *and hold*.** 23 Sep taught that the basket works when Bank Nifty carries it. Today adds the other half: if Bank Nifty is the index that prints the momentum and then produces the rejection, it is the *cause* of the failure. A lagging BN caps the target; a reversing BN kills the trade.
3. **Sudden rejection in all three indices = sideways = exit for an option buyer.** When all three reject simultaneously there is no divergence left to hide in; sideways means the loss will not be recovered and continued selling means it grows. Both outcomes are losses for a buyer, so both point to the same action.
4. **Flat is not the same as promising.** A flat open in the "flat-to-gap-down → buy" branch still needs real positive momentum. A flat open alone is not a confirmation; without momentum the sellers' confidence never breaks.
5. **Honour the loss limit — and nothing beyond it.** Small rejections must be tolerated, and the market must first create stop-losses and give sellers a re-entry chance before it moves. Sit calmly *until* the limit; when the limit is exhausted, "there is no second option" — cut and leave.
6. **Never let one loss rewrite your bias.** The rule he stated live: don't tell yourself "now the market will only fall because I'm in loss." Journal the rejection, not the prediction.
7. **The overnight-seller read is a *thesis*, not a gift.** After a gap-down + continued-selling day the counter-trade is legitimate, but it must be earned by momentum at the open — same structural read as 23 Sep's winner, opposite outcome, because the momentum never arrived and the breakout was not held.
