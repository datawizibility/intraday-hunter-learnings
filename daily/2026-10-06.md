### Keep permanently
1. **A slight/flat gap-up with the closing price NOT crossed = buy the buyers, not the sellers.** When the gap is only small and the closing price holds, the buyers' stop-loss is never triggered, so net momentum stays positive. A **huge** gap-up or a flat open would be different — sizing of the gap is the input.
2. **After a momentum-trap day, expect the reverse-flush.** Yesterday's momentum buyers got trapped ⇒ today no fresh buyers commit ⇒ the surface looks seller-heavy ⇒ the market rises **gradually** to flush those sellers. *"The momentum that was due yesterday arrives today."*
3. **Rejection → rise → rejection → rise.** On a day with no *direct* seller SL available, the market invites sellers on each rejection and then lifts again. This drip-feed flush is the engine of the day — not a single clean breakout.
4. **Don't demand a bigger target after a round-number breakout.** Once the round number breaks, other people start buying, so the market can go **sideways or reverse** — a fresh positive push can itself be a **trap** (exactly the 05 Oct pattern). Book a decent, *earned* target instead of holding for more.
5. **Buyer risk is defined by the closing price.** While the closing price holds, anyone long stays in and the market won't be given a reason to hit their SL — so risk on the long side is genuinely small and the trade can be taken *with* the market.
6. **The open decides the branch (mirror of the pre-market plan).** Pre-market said flat/gap-down ⇒ sell-side, gap-up ⇒ buy-side. The actual open was a **slight gap-up with the closing price uncrossed** ⇒ buy-side — the pre-plan's gap-up branch fired, and IH executed it.
