### Keep permanently
1. A **large** retracement before a fall makes further falling hard for the market: when traders have already been given a proper retracement, everyone sells into the fall, nobody is left to buy, and the decline cannot continue — recovery is the higher-probability path.
2. The pre-market "target the sellers" plan is for **small** gap-downs. A very large gap-down breaks that setup; you must re-read the **new chart** rather than forcing the old plan.
3. In a **large gap-down after continuous selling**, the trapped short sellers become the target — buy the recovery rather than joining the continuation selling.
4. Define the **lower point** before entering: until it is crossed, positive momentum is comfortably possible; once crossed, only sellers come and no intraday buyer will buy.
5. Buying against the opening trend is acceptable only with a pre-defined risk: prepare for the market to resume falling and hold true to the exit rule.
6. **Exit on the breakdown or on loss beyond the limit** — those are the only two exits once the position is on; a growing loss alone is not an exit if the structure still supports the thesis.
7. Sellers get added in a falling market one after another (each "a retracement is done, let me sell"); the market eats their stop-losses, which is what fuels the recovery leg.
8. Book on the **first good recovery** when the round-number breakout has happened — holding for the second leg only buys extra risk.
9. In an already-negative market, stop-losses for the *other* side (sellers) are the liquidity that pays you; identify who is trapped before choosing a side.

### Psychology / discipline
- Sat through a substantial and growing loss without panic-exiting, because the reason for the loss (a small rejection inside a momentum buy) did not invalidate the thesis — and he had stated the invalidation in advance (breakdown / loss beyond limit).
- Accepted the risk before entry: he openly assumed a possible ~₹5 lakh loss if the market fell, rather than discovering the risk after being caught.
- Did not revenge-trade or flip sides when the loss grew; he stayed with the single call-side thesis.
- Was willing to wait, explicitly noting "time will be taken" because the loss had to be covered first, and that the loss should not happen in this trade.
- Took profit instead of maximising it: profit had already run higher than his exit, but he accepted less to avoid the added risk after the round-number breakout.
- After the exit he explained why the momentum would need time again (seller stop-loss build-up, rejections) — he stopped rather than re-entering immediately.
