### Keep permanently
1. After losses—or when a trade shows a large profit—emotional stability must come from following predefined rules, not from changing the directional read out of fear or excitement.
2. Use position size/capital that creates enough seriousness to enforce discipline but is still comfortable enough to hold through normal adverse movement; oversized risk causes panic exits, while trivial size does not train discipline.
3. Separate a directional market from a volatile range. Stop-loss-location logic is more reliable when the market is capable of directional expansion; a two-sided range can cause losses even when the stop-loss read is reasonable.
4. Prior losses do not automatically invalidate today's setup. Reassess today's structure and confirmations independently, then execute the rule-based plan.
5. After a difficult week, booking a valid available profit can be preferable to forcing an extended target in a volatile market.
6. Which index moves first matters for the P&L: the index carrying the largest quantity (normally Bank Nifty here) is the one that decides the outcome.
