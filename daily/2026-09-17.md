In that kind of recovery two things happen — sellers arrive on the retracement, but because the market then goes sideways they cut their trades and leave. So the read is conditional: if the retracement was a **trap for sellers** → flat/gap-down → identify **SELL-side** setups; if it was a **trap for buyers** (the round number and the day's high were not crossed, so a good trader could have bought quietly while others stayed out) → gap-up → identify **BUY-side** setups.

### Keep permanently
1. A **sideways hold** after a breakdown-and-recovery means counter-traders have already cut — there is no stop-loss pool left, so follow continuation instead of expecting to target them.
2. The opening confirms which pool the market chose: flat/small gap-down with sellers flushed → sell-side; a gap-up would have meant buyers were still trapped.
3. Levels decide the day's side: while they are not crossed the bias stays; once one index breaks out, the situation has changed — do not keep defending the old read.
4. When an index **breaks out against the position and the loss limit is spent, exit**. Waiting for the laggard indices to follow is not a reason to hold (on this day Bank Nifty ran while Sensex and Nifty stayed behind).
5. Emotion control is a by-product of following rules. Every extra scenario built while in a loss degrades the decision; more thinking in loss = worse decisions.
6. Expiry-day index can be slower and retrace more, so targets must be read accordingly.
