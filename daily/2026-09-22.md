### Keep permanently
1. **Time is a cost in option buying.** A long no-momentum hold plus one opposite candle converts a small loss into a big loss — the trade's clock matters as much as the level. If the market has spent a lot of time without momentum, exit is the higher-probability action.
2. **Loss symmetry / mirror rule.** Do not accept a loss bigger than the profit this setup would plausibly have produced. Without that cap, a single trade can run to lakhs of loss. Once the defined limit is finished, cut — no waiting for the SL hunt to complete.
3. **Continue-follow only where SLs are absent.** Continuation without a fixed target is allowed when the opposite side is not holding (their SLs are not available) — but it still needs a defined loss limit and a time stop.
4. **Slow grinds create SL fuel.** When a move grinds slowly, later buyers enter with stops near the closing price / the level; the market commonly drives into those stops before deciding. Small rejection + large time = the warning sign that this flush is coming.
5. **A lagging index under its round number is the cap on the trade.** Two indices taking out their highs while the third cannot cross its round level is a warning that the breakout may fail.
6. **Only sit while the path is right.** Direction never guaranteed — trade probability and the asymmetric payoff; if the market's path has turned wrong, capital protection outranks conviction.
7. **Capital discipline over one day.** Repeatedly re-deploying capital across the same session destroys net profitability even if individual entries were fine.
8. **Three-index trading is two-sided** — it smooths profit when one index runs, but drags net P&L when one lags.
9. **Expiry day (Nifty weekly)** adds nuisance value, but Bank Nifty momentum is the confirming driver for the other indices.
