### Keep permanently
1. Read the psychology of a flush: when a market holds a level for a long time and then breaks, positional sellers are shaken out and their stops disappear — continuation can then run without a pool of sell-side stops overhead.
2. A market that only falls *after* first holding (not an immediate reversal) draws few fresh sellers, so there is no seller stop-loss to target — follow the broader momentum instead of hunting stops.
3. Continue a trend only while the other side genuinely has no stop-loss available; if stops are available above, expect the market to reverse and take them.
4. When two indices lead and one lags (Bank Nifty behind Sensex/Nifty), a brief breakout in the laggard can flush late/high-quantity sellers before continuation resumes — allow time rather than exiting on the counter-move.
5. With expiry and premium/time-decay in play, an adequate profit is worth booking instead of holding for a stretched target.
6. After a losing day, protecting capital and psychology by taking a decent profit beats over-risking for a bigger one.
