- Why buy: yesterday was a continuous sell day with the retracement only around 2:00 pm, and the market closed back down — so the sellers **definitely held their positions overnight**. The overall trend had been negative for many days, which means sellers re-activate on every dip. Those reactivated sellers were the target, hence the call-side trade.
- His caveat on this pattern: sometimes sellers *do* run away in a retracement — but only when the trend is not negative. Here the trend was strongly negative, so they stay and get activated on dips. That is what made the trade worth taking.
- Yesterday's loss was explicitly isolated: "if we get a genuinely good trade in the market today, only then should we work." And separately — do not try to cover yesterday's loss; leave yesterday behind whether it was a profit or a loss. If a continuous loss streak is running, be careful only about not making a *mistake* — not about increasing quantity or capital. "The more you complicate things, the more difficult it gets for you."
- Mid-session divergence: **Bank Nifty showed continuous positive momentum** while **Sensex and Nifty kept rejecting** and repeatedly tried to go below the closing price. He watched for a closing-price breakdown on Sensex/Nifty; if that happened, negative momentum would make the problem bigger — but no chance of a breakdown was visible, so he waited. Since Bank Nifty was moving in his favour and three-index momentum is normally roughly the same, he judged the basket would work.
- Then profit began to increase: Bank Nifty performing well, Sensex/Nifty not in profit but their loss shrinking on recovery → **net profit rising**. If all three had broken out, the target could have been bigger — "when all three indices move, more profit is made."

He called it heavy psychological pressure and his counter-measure was to **look at the trade less and watch the chart instead of the premium**, because the premia were swinging a lot. "The more you keep watching, the more the mind suffers." He also flagged the Bank Nifty **56,540 breakout** ("the 540 one") as the thing that would have made the move better, or Sensex/Nifty covering.
- Exit: **his target was achieved → he exited.** "We won't be greedy" — even if the market has more upside, he would not wait, because risk increases from here. On positive momentum it feels good to hold, but one rejection halves the profit. With the momentum still running and Sensex and Nifty printing a few green candles alongside, he took the exit. Staying would have been a problem if a retracement came.
- Closing recap: Bank Nifty gave massive momentum; had Sensex and Nifty travelled along, they would have extended the target. They had gone down earlier, but the recovery came back decently, so the target was met. He also noted the asymmetry that matters for his process: **waiting patiently works when Bank Nifty is the one moving; if Sensex/Nifty move up while Bank Nifty lags, the trade does not work as well.**

### Keep permanently
1. **After a losing day, trade only if a genuinely good setup appears.** He stated it plainly before taking the trade, and it is the cleanest expression of post-loss discipline: no trade is also a decision; the day after a loss the entry filter gets *tighter*, not looser.
2. **Yesterday's overnight sellers are a target on a flat/gap-up open.** Strong sell day + retracement only in the afternoon + a weak close ⇒ sellers held overnight. A flat-to-up open traps them, and any dip re-activates them. Their covers/stops are the fuel for a **call-side** trade — this is the mirror image of the 22 Sep SL flush that caught the call buyers.
3. **Never carry yesterday's P&L into today.** Leave yesterday behind whether it was profit or loss; do not try to recover it, do not increase quantity or capital to make it back. Complication is itself the loss mechanism.
4. **Self-diagnose the actual gap.** Are you wrong on direction, wrong on entry, or over-trading? Fix only the one that applies. Sitting on the same mistake without naming it is why money doesn't get made.
5. **Pick the segment that suits your discipline.** Some traders do better in option *selling*, others in option *buying*; stocks are also legitimate. Choose the segment where you can follow rules — do not fight the clock in a segment that breaks your discipline.
6. **Divergence rule (three-index mechanics).** Momentum normally arrives in all three indices together, so a call basket pays best when all three participate. When one index (here Bank Nifty) diverges and moves with you, the trade still works — but the target stays normal, not ambitious. And critically: **Bank Nifty momentum carries the basket; if Sensex/Nifty lead while Bank Nifty lags, the trade does not work.**
7. **Watch the chart, not the premium — especially when pressure is high.** When premia swing hard, watching the P&L creates the urge to exit or to overstay. Less screen time on the position, more attention on the index chart.
8. **Target achieved → exit. No greed.** Risk rises the moment the defined target is met; the next rejection gives back half the profit. Exit on plan, not on hope.
9. **If all three indices refuse to break out, cap the expectation.** Divergence or a single index carrying the move = normal target; a clean three-index breakout is the only case where the target can be enlarged (this is why 56,540 mattered).
